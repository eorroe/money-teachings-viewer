# Money Teaching Title

Your Brain Is Keeping You Poor: How To Think Like The Rich

## Overview

This teaching exposes the invisible mental programming that keeps most people trapped in employee thinking and earning only earned income from employment without systems generating passive or equity-based returns. It provides five actionable frameworks—the $4 Test, the 321 Rule, the 72-Hour Rule, chasing leverage over money, and reframing "I can't afford it" to "How can I afford it?"—to help you rewire your financial mindset and build systems that generate wealth continuously rather than trading time for money.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you feel stuck in a cycle of working harder without getting ahead financially
- When you want to shift from earning income to building automated income systems
- When you're considering a new business idea but keep getting stuck in research or doubt
- When you catch yourself saying "I can't afford it" instead of looking for creative solutions
- When you want to stop trading time for money and start building leverage

## Steps

### Step 1: Install the New Question—Replace "I Can't Afford It" with "How Can I Afford It?"

Every time you face a purchase, investment, or opportunity, consciously stop the default "I can't afford it" response. Instead, force your brain to answer "How can I afford it?" This single shift moves you from a scarcity, closed mindset to a solution-oriented, ownership mindset. The goal is not to spend recklessly, but to train your brain to seek opportunities and returns rather than defaulting to denial.

### Step 2: Apply the $4 Test to Reframe Every Purchase

When you encounter any expense—whether it's a $4 coffee or a $400 course—calculate what that money could generate if invested at an average 7% annual return or turned into leverage instead of being spent. A $4 daily coffee invested at an average 7% annual return compounds to $146,000 in 30 years. Use this lens not to deprive yourself, but to think like an owner: stop obsessing over small costs and focus on building systems that generate returns many times larger.

### Step 3: Build Your First Income System Using the 321 Rule

Identify 3 specific problems that people will pay you to solve. For each problem, develop 2 distinct monetization methods. Then build 1 automated system to deliver the solution without requiring your ongoing time. For example, if the problem is meal planning, monetize through a weekly meal prep service ($200/week) and a digital meal plan product ($20/month), then automate delivery through a simple website or app.

### Step 4: Test Ideas in 72 Hours, Not 6 Months

When a new business idea or opportunity arises, commit to testing it within 72 hours instead of researching endlessly. Poor thinking says "I need to research this for 6 months." Rich thinking says "I need to test this in 72 hours." Take imperfect action fast, gather real feedback, and iterate. Perfect is the enemy of rich.

### Step 5: Chase Leverage, Not Money

Stop optimizing for hourly income or salary. Start designing your efforts to generate asymmetric or continuous returns. Leverage comes from labor, capital, or products—anything you create once that pays you repeatedly. Build one piece of content, one product, or one system that generates revenue while you sleep. One hour of effort should not equal one hour of pay.

### Step 6: Automate Your Finances and Routines to Free Up Bandwidth

Automate your savings, use calendar blocks to batch similar tasks, and create routines around meals, workouts, and budgeting. Every decision you automate is one less decision that drains your willpower and cognitive bandwidth. Freeing up mental energy allows you to focus on high-leverage activities such as building systems, creating products, or acquiring assets that generate returns without requiring your ongoing direct time input.

## Examples

### Example 1: The $4 Coffee Reframe

You see a $4 specialty coffee. Your default employee brain calculates $1,460 per year and decides it's wasteful. Your new rich brain reframes it: $4 invested daily at an average 7% annual return compounds to $146,000 over 30 years. The lesson is not to skip the coffee—it's to stop fixating on small costs while ignoring the larger opportunity to build systems that generate $146,000 or more in returns. Shift your focus from cutting $4 expenses to creating $400+ value streams.

### Example 2: The 321 Rule in Action—Meal Planning

Problem: People hate meal planning and waste time and money figuring out what to eat. Monetization Method 1: Offer a weekly meal prep service for $200 per week per client. Monetization Method 2: Sell a digital meal plan subscription for $20 per month with automated weekly delivery. Automated System: A simple membership site or email sequence that delivers meal plans every Sunday without your ongoing involvement. Three problems, two income streams per problem, one system to deliver them.

### Example 3: Amazon and the 72-Hour Rule

Jeff Bezos started Amazon not by researching the book business for 6 months, but by building a minimal online store listing books for sale and testing it quickly. He chased leverage—an online platform that could sell books while he slept—rather than chasing a salary. The initial site was incomplete, but it was live, and that was enough to start gathering data, iterating, and scaling. Perfect is the enemy of rich.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Replace every "I can't afford it" thought with "How can I afford it?"
- ✅ Use the $4 Test to build an ownership mindset around every expense by comparing each cost to what the same amount could generate if invested or deployed as leverage
- ✅ Identify 3 real problems and build 2 monetization paths for each
- ✅ Automate at least one delivery system so income is not tied to your time
- ✅ Test new ideas within 72 hours instead of researching for months
- ✅ Chase leverage (products, capital, systems) rather than hourly income
- ✅ Automate savings, scheduling, and routines to protect your willpower
- ✅ Think in terms of infinite or asymmetric returns, not linear time-for-money trades

## Keep In Mind

- Your brain processes risk and opportunity differently depending on what mental software is installed—you can consciously upgrade it
- Financial stress can shrink your IQ by 13 points, impairing decision-making
- Loss aversion makes losses feel roughly twice as painful as equivalent gains feel good
- Wealthy people did not get rich by working more hours—they got rich by building systems that pay continuously
- Most people stay broke because they research for 6 months instead of testing for 72 hours
- The employee mindset was intentionally designed by historical systems to produce obedient workers, not wealthy free thinkers

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always verify business ideas with real market feedback before investing capital
- Legitimate wealth-building takes time and systems—avoid any scheme promising instant riches
- Keep personal financial details, account credentials, and sensitive data private and never share them publicly
- When building automated systems, use payment processors licensed and regulated in your jurisdiction, and follow platform security standards such as TLS encryption and PCI DSS compliance where applicable
- Understand the income tax, sales tax collection, business licensing, and self-employment tax obligations for any monetization method before launching, and consult a qualified tax professional if your projected annual revenue exceeds the IRS self-employment tax threshold

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You stay stuck in research and never launch. Poor thinking demands 6 months of preparation. Rich thinking tests in 72 hours.
  **Solution:** Set a hard 72-hour deadline to launch a minimum viable version of any idea, gather concrete user behavior data such as customer sign-ups, purchases, or engagement rates, and iterate from that data.

- **Problem:** You chase money directly through higher salaries or hourly gigs instead of building leverage. Linear income caps your wealth because one hour always equals one payment.
  **Solution:** Reframe every project around leverage: What can I build once that earns returns repeatedly without proportional additional effort—such as a digital product sold to multiple customers, a written piece of content viewed thousands of times, invested capital, or an automated delivery system?

- **Problem:** Every small purchase feels like a failure because you're focused on cutting costs rather than building income. Deprivation mindset drains willpower and stalls progress.
  **Solution:** Apply the $4 Test not to guilt yourself into skipping purchases, but to remind yourself that the same $4 (or $400) could be deployed into a system that generates returns greater than the original amount spent. Focus on increasing total income rather than reducing every expense.

- **Problem:** You ask "I can't afford it" and close every door before opening it. Scarcity language programs your brain to see only limits.
  **Solution:** Make "How can I afford it?" your default question. Write down 3 distinct actionable answers every time you face a cost, and you will begin to see paths you previously missed.
