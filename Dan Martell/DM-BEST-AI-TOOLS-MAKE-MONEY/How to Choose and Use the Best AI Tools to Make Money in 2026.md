# How to Choose and Use the Best AI Tools to Make Money in 2026

## Overview

This money teaching ranks the most popular AI tools into tiers (S, A, B, C, F) based on their ability to make money, ease of use, and long-term relevance. The core behavior is to prioritize high-ROI tools that integrate into existing business workflows rather than chasing flashy but low-value tools. S-tier tools like ChatGPT, Granola, your.com (Atlas), Zapier, and Gamma.app are non-negotiable foundations for any modern business, while F-tier tools like Apple Intelligence should be avoided entirely.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to choose which AI tools to invest time and money into for your business
- When working on automating business processes, content creation, or client deliverables
- When the user asks how to make money with AI or which AI tools are worth using in 2026
- When you feel overwhelmed by the number of AI tools available and need a clear prioritization framework
- When building a system to scale your business output without proportionally scaling your time

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Your Business Needs and Match to the Right Tier

Start by listing the core activities in your business that could be enhanced by AI: content creation, automation, voice/audio, coding, research, or customer communication. Then consult the tier rankings to identify which tools serve those needs. S-tier tools (ChatGPT, Granola, your.com, Zapier, Gamma.app) should be adopted first as foundational infrastructure. A-tier tools (Claude, Gum Loop, N8N, Buddy Pro, 11 Labs, Perplexity) are powerful but serve more specific use cases. B-tier tools (Runway ML, MidJourney, Hey Jen, Leonardo.ai, Lovable) are niche and valuable for particular industries. Avoid C-tier and F-tier tools unless you have a very specific, proven need.

### Step 2: Install, Set Up, and Force Habit Stacking

Once you select a tool, spend the next two weeks installing it, setting it up, and forcing yourself to use it on real projects. The key insight is that technology adoption is not about whether a tool can do the task, but whether you remember to use it. Habit-stack the new AI tool to an existing habit in your workday so it becomes second nature. For example, if you already check email every morning, stack a quick ChatGPT prompt session right after. If you use Notion, enable Notion AI within your existing workspace. If you use Lovable to build apps, use ChatGPT to write the prompts you feed into Lovable.

### Step 3: Monetize the Tool Through Services or Internal Efficiency

To make money, either use the tool to deliver services to clients or use it internally to free up time for higher-value work. For client services: use MidJourney to create brand packages and website designs, use Hey Jen to create training videos and product explainers for businesses, use Gamma.app to create pitch decks and proposals for clients, or use your.com (Atlas) to set up AI phone systems for companies. For internal efficiency: use Zapier to automate business processes, use Granola to capture meeting notes automatically, use 11 Labs for voiceovers instead of returning to the studio, or use ChatGPT for writing and coding tasks. Agencies that implement Zapier and Gum Loop to automate client workflows can charge premium rates for the automated results.

## Examples

### Example 1: Freelance Designer Using MidJourney and Gamma.app

A freelance designer takes on a client who needs a full brand package including logo concepts, color palettes, and a pitch deck. The designer uses MidJourney to generate visual concepts and brand assets based on text prompts, then uses Gamma.app to turn those assets into a polished, professional pitch deck. What would have taken days of manual design work is completed in hours, allowing the designer to take on more clients at the same quality level. The designer charges premium rates for the speed and visual quality, using the AI tools as a force multiplier.

### Example 2: Agency Using Zapier and Gum Loop to Automate Client Workflows

A marketing agency manages recruiting and financial workflows for multiple clients manually. The agency implements Zapier to connect 6,000+ apps and automate repetitive tasks across their clients' stacks, and uses Gum Loop to build visual AI workflows for financial automation and recruiting processes. Because the automation runs without manual intervention, the agency can serve more clients without hiring additional staff. The agency charges setup fees and monthly retainers for the automated systems, creating recurring revenue. Multiple agencies have publicly documented using Zapier for this kind of money-making automation.

### Example 3: App Developer Using Lovable and ChatGPT to Build and Sell Disposable Business Apps

A non-technical entrepreneur has an idea for a custom internal tool that would help a small business run its operations. Instead of hiring a developer, the entrepreneur uses ChatGPT to write detailed prompts describing the app, then feeds those prompts into Lovable, which builds the app automatically. The entrepreneur sells the finished app to the business for $10,000 to $15,000. This pattern is repeated across multiple businesses, creating a scalable service where the entrepreneur acts as an intermediary between business needs and AI-powered app generation. The future vision is that businesses will use these "disposable apps" instead of spreadsheets to run departments.

### Example 4: Content Creator Using 11 Labs and Hey Jen to Scale Video Output

A content creator needs to produce training videos and product explainers but lacks the time and budget for professional voice actors and on-camera presence. The creator uses 11 Labs to generate hyper-realistic AI voiceovers, eliminating the need to return to the studio for pickups or re-recordings. For videos requiring a human presenter, the creator uses Hey Jen to generate AI avatar videos from simple text scripts. This allows the creator to scale output significantly, take on more client projects, and reduce production costs. The creator can also offer these services to other businesses, charging them to set up and manage the AI video production pipeline.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Prioritize S-tier tools first (ChatGPT, Granola, your.com, Zapier, Gamma.app) as non-negotiable foundations for any business
- ✅ Habit-stack new AI tools to existing workday routines so they become automatic rather than optional
- ✅ Spend two weeks installing, setting up, and actively using a new tool before judging its value
- ✅ Use ChatGPT to learn how to use other AI tools: ask it for guidance on prompt engineering, setup, and workflows
- ✅ Use open-source tools like N8N if you want to audit the codebase and avoid licensing costs
- ✅ Focus on tools that save time or generate revenue directly, not tools that are merely flashy or novel
- ✅ If you use a tool like Notion, enable its native AI features within your existing workspace rather than switching platforms
- ✅ Use visual workflow builders like Gum Loop when you want a more intuitive interface than Zapier for complex automations

## Security & Safety Notes

- Granola.ai records meetings on your local device without other participants being notified. This is designed for personal note-taking and recap generation, not for covert recording. Ensure you comply with local laws and workplace policies regarding meeting recording before using it.
- When using open-source tools like N8N, you can audit the codebase yourself, which reduces trust risks compared to closed-source alternatives. If you self-host N8N on your own servers, you also avoid licensing costs and keep data in your control.
- Be cautious when feeding sensitive company information into third-party AI tools. Evaluate the tool's data handling policies, especially for tools that train on user data.
- Avoid using tools that have not been vetted for data privacy, particularly when handling client information, financial data, or proprietary business knowledge.
- If you are setting up AI phone systems (your.com/Atlas) or customer support bots (Buddy Pro), ensure you have proper disclosure and consent mechanisms in place for the people interacting with the AI.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Adopting too many tools at once and feeling overwhelmed, leading to none being used consistently.
  **Solution:** Pick one tool that seems most relevant to your business and spend two weeks mastering it before adding another. Habit-stack it into your existing routine.

- **Problem:** Forgetting to use a new tool because it is not tied to an existing habit.
  **Solution:** Connect the tool to a daily or weekly activity you already do. For example, run a quick ChatGPT prompt session right after your morning email check.

- **Problem:** Using F-tier tools like Apple Intelligence because they are from a familiar brand, wasting time on inferior products.
  **Solution:** Stick to the tier rankings. If a tool is ranked F, avoid it entirely and use any S or A-tier alternative instead.

- **Problem:** Generating branded visual material with Leonardo.ai and getting inconsistent or hallucinated results.
  **Solution:** Use Leonardo.ai only for concept art and exploratory design, not for final branded deliverables where consistency is critical. Pair it with a tool like Gamma.app or MidJourney for polished outputs.

- **Problem:** Using Lovable without knowing how to write effective prompts, leading to poor app outputs.
  **Solution:** Use ChatGPT to write and refine your prompts before feeding them into Lovable. The quality of the Lovable output is directly tied to the quality of the prompt.

- **Problem:** Relying on C-tier tools like Fixer.ai or Notion AI for critical workflows when better alternatives exist.
  **Solution:** Evaluate whether the tool is solving a real, recurring problem or just a temporary curiosity. If you already use Notion, its AI is convenient, but do not justify switching to Notion solely for its AI features when ChatGPT or Claude can handle the same tasks.
