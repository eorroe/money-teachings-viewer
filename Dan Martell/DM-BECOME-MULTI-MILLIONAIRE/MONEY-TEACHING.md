# How to Become a Multi-Millionaire: Focus on Intention, Assets, and Serving Others

## Overview

This money teaching distills the core principles Myron Golden used to go from broke to making $15 million in a single year and building an eight-figure business. It exists to teach entrepreneurs how to shift their mindset from survival to wealth creation by focusing on intention over distraction, solving expensive problems for the right people, building assets instead of chasing income, and reinvesting in mentorship and self-education. These principles are framework-agnostic and apply regardless of industry, business model, or current income level.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to escape the cycle of living paycheck to paycheck or trading time for money
- When you are building a business and need to identify the right market and pricing strategy
- When you feel overwhelmed by distractions and need a framework to prioritize high-leverage activities
- When you are ready to shift from "working hard" to "building assets" that generate passive or scalable income
- When you want to improve your sales and marketing without feeling manipulative or pushy
- When you are considering investing in mentorship, coaching, or education and want to know how to evaluate ROI
- When you are entering a new economic era (such as AI) and want to position yourself for outsized wealth creation

## Steps

### Step 1: Hyper-Focus on Intention and Eliminate Distraction

Wealthy people hyper-focus on intention and ignore distraction. Intention is anything that moves the needle in your favor. Distraction is anything you focus on that does not move the needle. To implement this, write down the five activities that directly generate revenue or build assets for your business. Every day, audit your calendar and eliminate or delegate anything that does not fall into those categories. Protect your focus ruthlessly. If an activity does not grow your business, improve your skills, or strengthen key relationships, it is a distraction. Stop doing it or pay someone else to do it.

### Step 2: Reframe the Purpose of Money

Poor people believe money exists to pay bills. Middle-class people believe money exists to maintain good credit so they can buy things they cannot afford to impress people they do not know or like. Rich people believe the primary purpose of money is to turn into more money. To adopt this mindset, every time you receive a dollar, ask yourself: "How can I turn this into $10, $100, or $1,000 before I spend any of it?" Reinvest profits into assets first. Only spend personal income after your business is sustained and your asset base is growing.

### Step 3: Solve Other People's Problems to Solve Your Money Problem

To solve your money problem, you must solve someone else's money problem. You cannot be focused on your own money problem while you are solving it. Instead, identify a specific, expensive problem that a specific group of people have, and become the person who solves it. The key is to perceive what is valuable to other people. This requires empathy and market awareness, not just desire for money. Interview potential customers, study their pain points, and build or position your offer around the outcome they want.

### Step 4: Sell to People Who Want to Buy, Not People Who Need to Buy

Most people sell to the wrong audience: they try to sell to people who need their product but cannot afford it or are not ready to buy. Myron Golden teaches that you should only sell to people who already desire to buy. There are millions of people in the world who would love to buy what you love to sell if they only knew you existed. Instead of hunting for someone to sell to, make yourself findable. Position your messaging so it attracts the right buyer and repels the wrong one. If you sell high-ticket coaching, put your price upfront. The people who cannot afford it will disqualify themselves, and the people who can will respect your clarity.

### Step 5: Solve Expensive Problems for People with Money

It takes no more energy to sell a $1,000,000 program than it takes to sell a $1,000 program. The only difference is who you are selling to. If you want to get rich, sell to rich people. They have the money to pay, and it is easier for them to pay because the money is not a stressor for them. To implement this, identify industries or customer segments where people already have capital and are actively looking for solutions: business owners, executives, real estate investors, or high-income professionals. Tailor your offer and messaging to their specific, high-stakes problems.

### Step 6: Define Sales as Uncovering Value, Not Persuasion

Most people think of sales as talking someone into buying something they do not want, do not need, and cannot afford. Redefine sales as uncovering the value of what you have to offer so well that people are happy to exchange the money they have in their pocket for the value you have revealed. When someone says no, do not lower your price. Raise the value. Offer more. Deliver more. People will happily pay when they understand the transformation your product or service creates.

### Step 7: Build Relationships Before Asking for the Sale

People need to know, like, and trust you before they will invest significant money with you. Prove to people that you can help them by actually helping them. Give value freely through content, answers, and small wins. People will pay you with their attention first. If you earn their attention long enough, they will eventually pay you with their assets: their money, their referrals, or their partnership. Myron Golden's YouTube channel is an example of this: he gives massive value for free, and viewers who consume enough of his content become customers and clients because they trust his expertise.

### Step 8: Keep Expenses Low Even When Income Is High

Myron Golden made millions, lost it all, and rebuilt. One of the biggest reasons he lost everything was upgrading his lifestyle too soon when his income increased. The scripture says, "When riches increase, so do they that consume them." To avoid this, structure your finances so that you can live on 10% or less of your revenue or income. If the bottom falls out, you can survive for at least two years. Reinvest the rest into assets, business infrastructure, and cash reserves. The saying "stay small enough long enough and you'll be big enough soon enough" captures this principle perfectly. The more money you have and the better you understand how to turn money into more money, the faster it compounds, as long as you are not constantly pulling it out to fund lifestyle inflation.

### Step 9: Focus on Building and Buying Assets

Income follows assets. Stop focusing on working hard, eight hours for eight hours pay, and paying bills. Start focusing on putting more assets in your asset column. You can buy assets or build them. Examples of assets: real estate, books, courses, intellectual property, software, businesses, investments, and crypto. Every book Myron Golden writes is an asset because it pays him every month for the rest of his life after a single writing effort. Evaluate every decision through the lens of whether it increases your asset base or simply trades time for money.

### Step 10: Invest Heavily in Mentorship and Self-Education

Mentorship is the ultimate shortcut. Someone has already spent their whole life figuring out how to do something, and all they will do is charge you to teach you how to do it. Myron Golden has spent $1.7 million on mentorship, books, courses, and coaching programs, and he has made exponentially more than that in return. The investment in your own mind is the highest-ROI investment you can make. To implement this, identify mentors who have already achieved the results you want, buy their programs, join their masterminds, and do what they tell you to do even if it seems uncomfortable or counterintuitive.

### Step 11: Set Objectives, Not Goals with Deadlines

Myron Golden does not believe in setting goals because a goal is an objective with a deadline, and he does not control deadlines or the times and seasons. Instead, set objectives for input and outcomes, but do not attach rigid time limits. If you force a deadline and you do not hit it, you will feel like a failure. That feeling of failure can last days or years and may even cause you to quit. If you had to produce 7 million ears of corn by next Wednesday, there is nothing you could do. Certain outcomes require the right timing. Let it happen when it happens. Focus on the daily actions that move you toward the objective, and trust the process.

### Step 12: Think Bigger and Perceive the Abundance Around You

People think they lack access to abundance, but they actually lack access to awareness of abundance that is already all around them. Myron Golden's mentor, Robert G. Allen, once told him in front of 700 people, "You're doing really good, but there's a level where $30,000 a month is not a lot of money." That shifted Myron's trajectory because it forced him to realize he was thinking too small. To think bigger, ask yourself: "What would this look like at ten times the scale?" Study people who have already achieved the level of success you want. Absorb their mindset, their decisions, and their perception of what is possible. Surround yourself with people who think bigger so your standard rises.

### Step 13: Enter the Right Economic Era

Wealth is created most easily by entering the right economic era. From the beginning of time to the mid-1700s was the agricultural age, where land equaled wealth. From the mid-1700s to the mid-1900s was the industrial age, where machines equaled wealth. From 1955 to 1978 was the distribution age, where outlets equaled wealth. Right now, we are in the AI age. The people who got started in AI from 2020 to 2025 will create the wealth of the future. To implement this, identify the dominant technology or infrastructure shift of your era and build your business or career around it. Do not fight the tide.

### Step 14: Serve the Masses to Achieve Greatness

One of the greatest lessons from the Bible that applies to business: "He who will be greatest among you, let him be servant of all." You can be great even if you are not the most talented if you are willing to serve others at scale. Greatness is your ability to serve the masses. Build systems, content, and offers that allow you to serve more people simultaneously. Scale your impact by multiplying your reach through media, automation, or delegation.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Broke to $15 Million a Year by Selling to the Right People

Myron Golden was the brokeest person in his mastermind. Everyone there acted like his ideas were the best they had ever heard. He realized he had been selling to the wrong people his whole life: people who needed his help but could not afford it or were not ready to buy. Instead, he started selling to the people in that mastermind who already had money and wanted what he knew. Those same people later became his clients. The lesson: position yourself where the buyers already are, rather than trying to convince people who cannot afford to say yes.

### Example 2: Rebuilding Wealth After Losing Everything by Keeping Expenses Low

Myron Golden made millions in his forties, upgraded his lifestyle, and then lost everything due to a series of tragedies. He rebuilt by keeping expenses extremely low even as his income grew again. He lived on a small percentage of his revenue and reinvested the rest into assets. Today, he has set up his finances so that even if the bottom falls out, he will be okay for years. The example shows that it is not how much you make, but how much you keep and reinvest, that determines long-term wealth.

### Example 3: Using Mentorship to Accelerate Sales Skill

Myron Golden's first mentor, Jerry, taught him how to sell from stage one-to-many. The concepts seemed unbelievable at first, but because Jerry was his coach, Myron did it anyway. The first time he implemented the strategy, it worked so well that he had to hold back hysterical laughter. He realized the wealth he was missing out on was not because of what he knew, but because of what he did not know he did not know. The mentor provided the shortcut that saved him years of trial and error.

### Example 4: Building Assets Through Books and Content

Every book Myron Golden writes pays him money every month for the rest of his life after a single writing effort. This is the definition of an asset: you build it once and it generates income repeatedly without proportional additional work. The example demonstrates the power of intellectual property as a wealth-building vehicle. If you have knowledge that solves a problem, package it into a book, course, or program and sell it repeatedly.

## Security & Safety Notes

- When investing in mentorship or masterminds, verify the credentials, track record, and reputation of the mentor before paying. Myron Golden paid $1.7 million over a lifetime, but not all mentors deliver proportional value.
- Do not upgrade your lifestyle or take on fixed personal expenses until your income and asset base are stable and diversified. Lifestyle inflation is one of the most common causes of business failure after initial success.
- When building an AI-based business or investing in crypto, understand the regulatory environment, tax implications, and volatility of the assets you are acquiring.
- Protect your personal finances by maintaining an emergency fund and avoiding personal guarantees on business debt whenever possible.
- Be cautious when giving away free value publicly. While it builds trust, ensure you are not revealing proprietary strategies or competitive advantages that competitors can exploit without compensation.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Getting distracted by shiny objects and low-leverage activities instead of focusing on what moves the needle.
  **Solution:** Write down your five highest-leverage activities every morning and do them first. Delegate or eliminate everything else.

- **Problem:** Upgrading lifestyle too quickly when income increases, leaving no buffer for downturns.
  **Solution:** Commit to living on 10% or less of your revenue. Reinvest the rest into assets and reserves for at least two years before increasing personal spending.

- **Problem:** Selling to people who need your product but cannot afford it or are not ready to buy.
  **Solution:** Position your messaging to attract the right buyer and repel the wrong one. Put your price upfront and focus on people with the means and desire to pay.

- **Problem:** Trying to talk people into buying by lowering your price instead of raising your value.
  **Solution:** When someone says no, ask what additional value or outcome would make the offer irresistible. Raise the value, not lower the price.

- **Problem:** Setting rigid goals with deadlines and feeling like a failure when you do not hit them on time.
  **Solution:** Set objectives for input and outcomes without attaching deadlines. Focus on daily actions and trust that results will come in the right season.

- **Problem:** Focusing on working hard and trading time for money instead of building assets.
  **Solution:** Audit your income sources. If your income stops when you stop working, you do not have an asset yet. Prioritize building or buying things that generate income independently of your time.

- **Problem:** Thinking too small and not perceiving the abundance already around you.
  **Solution:** Study people who are ten times more successful than you. Ask what they see that you do not. Raise your standard by surrounding yourself with bigger thinkers.

- **Problem:** Ignoring mentorship and trying to figure everything out alone through trial and error.
  **Solution:** Find mentors who have already solved the problems you are facing. Invest in their programs, do what they tell you, and compress years of learning into weeks or months.
