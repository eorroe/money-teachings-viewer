# SMB PROP-DESKS

## Overview

This document explains how proprietary trading desks like SMB Capital classify every trade setup into distinct grades (A+, A, A-, B, C) and match risk allocation to each grade. The core insight is that traders who risk the same amount on every trade find that losses on B-grade and C-grade setups can significantly reduce the net profit generated by A+ and A setups. By implementing a graded risk system tied to daily, weekly, and monthly limits, you can scale your account by raising your maximum daily risk limit, limit the damage from compounding losses during sessions where you are not reading the market accurately, and concentrate risk exposure on A+ and A setups.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you have a defined playbook and can identify the variables that make one setup higher-quality than another
- When you want to grow your account without changing your decision-making approach
- When you are taking too many B and C setups that erode monthly profit-and-loss (P&L)
- When you need hard limits to stop compounding losses during sessions where you are not reading the market accurately
- When you are reviewing past trades to improve future grading accuracy

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Your Trade Grades and Corresponding Risk Percentages

Create a risk grading system that maps each trade quality level to a percentage of your maximum allowable daily risk. SMB's model uses A+ at 80%, A at 40%, A- at 20%, and collapses both B and C down to 5% each. You can adjust these percentages to fit your own playbook, but the key is having different risk levels for different trade qualities rather than risking the same amount on every setup.

### Step 2: Set Daily, Weekly, and Monthly Risk Limits

Establish a hard daily stop (for example, $1,000 maximum loss per day). This limit acts as a hard stop to prevent you from continuing to trade after repeated losses during sessions where you are not reading the market accurately. When you hit your daily loss limit, stop trading and return the next session. This limit also serves as the scaling instrument: when you are ready to risk more, simply raise the maximum daily risk amount while keeping the same percentage allocations.

### Step 3: Grade Each Setup Before Entering

Before entering any trade, assess its quality relative to your playbook criteria. Use mental cues such as "potentially the best trade of the quarter" for A+, "one of the best trades of the month" for A, "high-quality core trade of the week with a clear edge" for A-, "situational trade that may not meet your highest standards" for B, and "experimental test of a new approach" for C. Consider opportunity profile, market context, positive factors supporting the setup, catalyst strength, and market environment. Do not rely on a rigid formula; grading combines quantified criteria with pattern recognition developed from experience.

### Step 4: Adopt the Right Trader Persona for Each Grade

Once a trade is graded, shift into the corresponding trading persona. The A+ trader is offensive, expressive, willing to take multiple entries, and comfortable with active recovery and re-entry. The A trader is willing to get stopped out if all checks in favor are present, and is willing to swing the trade for a larger price move. A- trades have a consistent, measurable edge. They are not as exceptional as A or A+ trades, but they still carry a real edge. The B trader is patient and selective, only taking the highest-quality B setups and protecting risk rather than entering haphazardly. The C trader is experimental and strictly limited in size, used for testing new plays. If you identify a setup as B-grade, you must behave like the B-trader persona even if you have experienced success or increased your account size.

### Step 5: Execute With Grade-Appropriate Sizing and Tactics

Enter the trade using a position size that reflects the trade's grade percentage of your max daily risk. For an A+ trade, express it freely—buy puts on the open as a hedge, build into the swing trade on dips to volume-weighted average price (VWAP), use a time-weighted average price (TWAP) execution program, give yourself multiple entries, and be willing to get stopped out. For a B trade, wait for a very specific pattern, avoid pre-market or open entries, and keep the position small. Do not apply A+ tactics to a B-grade setup.

### Step 6: Track Running Stats Per Grade

Keep detailed records of win rate, misgraded trades (misrades), and running profit-and-loss (P&L) for each grade. Review these stats monthly. As Tim Beldin notes, each grade represents a distinct trading persona: the A+ trader persona, the A trader persona, the A- trader persona, and the B trader persona. If your B-trader stats show a consistently low win rate and no consistently profitable edge (the B-trader approach would not be profitable), respect that data and remain defensive. If you discover that B trades are generating losses despite profitable A and A+ months, impose a monthly cap on the number of B trades you take. Use the stats to continuously refine your grading accuracy.

### Step 7: Scale Without Changing Your Process

When you are ready to increase position sizes because of strong performance, do not change your trading approach or psychology. Instead, raise your maximum daily risk limit and continue risking the same percentage of that new limit on each grade. For example, if your daily stop moves from $1,000 to $2,000, an A+ trade is now 80% of $2,000 instead of 80% of $1,000, but mentally you are still risking the same percentage of your max daily risk that you always have. This prevents the subtle shift in your thinking that comes from thinking you are suddenly trading "bigger."

## Examples

### Example 1: Gold Overextension Short (A+ Trade)

In episode 10 of the Trading Floor Podcast, gold gapped up significantly into a Thursday session with extreme overextension across multiple timeframes—above the Keltner channels (Kelners) on all four timeframes, large average true range (ATR) extensions from the five-day low, well above moving averages, and unfilled gaps. SMB's team identified this as an overextension short setup and a potential A+ opportunity because all positive factors supporting the setup aligned. As the A+ trader, Garrett Dryen used the full 80% risk allocation: he bought puts on the open as a hedge, built into the swing trade on dips to volume-weighted average price (VWAP) using a time-weighted average price (TWAP) execution program, and remained willing to get stopped out because the risk-reward was strong.

### Example 2: FSLY Earnings (A-Grade Trade)

FSLY earnings was graded as an A+ catalyst (a major inflection quarter with strong acceleration in the edge computing theme) but was downgraded to A because the broader market was in a steep decline. SMB's team bought on dips to volume-weighted average price (VWAP) using a time-weighted average price (TWAP) execution program and let price action confirm the thesis.

### Example 3: B Trade Overtrading Warning

A trader who increased their position sizes might see too many B-grade trades and feel compelled to trade them larger and more frequently because they believe they have advanced to a larger account. This is a pitfall: the B-trader stats still show a consistently low win rate and no consistently profitable edge (the B-trader approach would not be profitable). Taking too many B trades cuts into gains from A+ and A trades. The fix is to cap B trades monthly and remind yourself that trading with larger capital does not change the fact that the B-trader approach does not have a statistical edge.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Grade every setup before entry using your own grading criteria based on opportunity profile, positive factors supporting the setup, and pattern recognition developed from experience
- ✅ Keep daily, weekly, and monthly risk limits as hard limits and scaling mechanisms
- ✅ Treat passing on a trade as a valid decision that risks zero allocation
- ✅ Maintain separate running P&L stats for each grade and review them monthly
- ✅ Keep your trading goals narrowly focused (for example, "nail my grading") rather than scattering attention across many objectives
- ✅ Scale by raising your max daily risk limit, not by changing your percentage allocation or psychology
- ✅ Play tight: only take A+, A, and A- trades; avoid B and C trades unless they are your best setups
- ✅ Review historical parabolic short setups and catalyst plays to calibrate your grading intuition
- ✅ Cap B trades monthly to prevent small losses from accumulating—B trades do not make up monthly P&L but can cut into gains from A+ and A trades if overtraded
- ✅ Aim for an inverted triangle distribution of trade frequency where A- trades represent the highest-frequency tier, A+ and A trades are less frequent but carry the highest conviction, and B trades are the most highly selective

## Keep In Mind

- The market does not always give you the perfect entry you envision, especially on A+ scenarios. Be willing to express the trade in multiple ways rather than waiting for one ideal spot.
- Your emotional state can lead to inaccurate grading.
- B trades appear every day; A+ trades are relatively uncommon. Structure your trading frequency around this pattern.
- Being classified as an advanced trader or trading with larger capital does not change the statistical edge of each grade.
- If you cannot yet distinguish between grades, risk the same dollar amount on every trade until your playbook and experience allow you to differentiate.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never exceed your daily, weekly, or monthly risk limits regardless of the setup's apparent strength.
- Do not use leverage or position sizing that could produce a loss beyond your predefined max risk.
- If you are using a proprietary trading (prop) desk or funded account, understand the specific drawdown rules and compliance requirements that apply to your account before implementing this grading system.
- Keep your trading journal and grade stats private; sharing them publicly can reveal strategy details that others could exploit.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Risking the same dollar amount on every trade regardless of grade.
  **Solution:** Implement the percentage-of-max-risk model so that A+ trades receive 80% of your max daily risk while B and C trades each receive only 5% of your max daily risk.

- **Problem:** Changing your decision-making approach when scaling up, leading to taking too many or oversizing B setups.
  **Solution:** Keep the same percentage of your max daily risk in mind. If you were risking 20% of your max daily risk before, tell yourself you are still risking 20% of your new max daily risk even though the dollar amount is larger.

- **Problem:** Paralysis from thinking you must find the perfect 80% entry for an A+ trade.
  **Solution:** Trade with the mindset and tactics appropriate for an A+ grade. Let the trade develop, scale in as price action confirms, and avoid fixating on a single idealized entry point.

- **Problem:** Taking too many B trades because they feel familiar or "could be something."
  **Solution:** Impose a monthly cap on B trades. Trust your stats: B trades do not make up monthly profit-and-loss but can cut into gains from A+ and A trades.

- **Problem:** Misgrading due to recency bias or emotional attachment to a catalyst.
  **Solution:** Compare the current setup against past examples with similar variables. Review your misgraded trades at the end of each month and adjust your criteria accordingly.

- **Problem:** Using a black-and-white formula that ignores nuance.
  **Solution:** Remember that grading depends on your specific playbook and involves judgment based on experience. A trade might have an A+ catalyst but a B-grade overall market environment, landing it at A. Allow market conditions to influence the final grade.
