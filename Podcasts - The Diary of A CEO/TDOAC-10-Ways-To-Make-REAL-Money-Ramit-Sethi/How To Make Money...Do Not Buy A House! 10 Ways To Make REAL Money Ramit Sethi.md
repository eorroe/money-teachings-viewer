# How To Make Money...Do Not Buy A House! 10 Ways To Make REAL Money: Ramit Sethi

## Overview

This money teaching distills Ramit Sethi's framework for building real wealth and living a "rich life" from The Diary Of A CEO interview. It challenges conventional wisdom about homeownership, exposes the psychology behind money mistakes, and provides a practical investing and income strategy that any W-2 earner or freelancer can implement within 90 days. The core message is that you do not need an IQ above 130 or a winning lottery ticket—you need intentionality, automation, and a written description of what your rich life actually looks like on a typical Saturday.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to build wealth without following a restrictive 50/30/20 budget or feeling guilty about discretionary purchases
- When you are deciding whether to buy a house or continue renting in a high-cost metro area
- When you feel paralyzed by investing jargon and do not know which brokerage account to open first
- When your annual income has stayed flat for two or more consecutive years and you want to increase your earning power
- When you want your monthly spending to match your personal values rather than your Instagram feed or neighborhood expectations
- When you are preparing for a major life transition involving a partner, such as moving in together, engagement, or marriage

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Your Rich Life in Specific Detail

Instead of vague goals like "I want freedom" or "I want to do what I want when I want," write down exactly what your rich life looks like in 2026 dollars. Specify destinations (e.g., "first-class to Tokyo twice per year"), experiences (e.g., "private chef dinner monthly"), daily routines (e.g., "laundry service picked up and delivered for $120/month"), and relationships (e.g., "weekly dinner with my three closest friends"). Ask yourself: What does my perfect Saturday look like? What tasks do I never want to do again? Use money to solve specific problems like never doing laundry again ($120/month) or never dealing with airport luggage ($200/year for a luggage-forward shipping service). This clarity becomes your compass for every financial decision, including whether to take a higher-paying job that requires 10 extra hours per week of travel.

### Step 2: Know Your Four Key Numbers

Track the four percentages of your take-home pay:
- **Fixed costs** (rent or mortgage, debt payments, utilities, groceries, car payment and insurance): 50–60%
- **Savings** (emergency fund, down payment, car replacement): 5–10%
- **Investments** (where real wealth is created through compound returns): 5–10%
- **Guilt-free spending** (travel, dining, hobbies, relationships, entertainment): 20–35%

Take 15 minutes to estimate your current numbers using your last two pay stubs and a checking-account transaction export. If your spending does not align with what you claim your rich life is, that gap between your actual spending and your stated rich-life vision is the exact budget item to adjust first—not your coffee budget.

### Step 3: Set Up Automatic Investing in a Target Date Fund

Open a low-cost brokerage account at Vanguard, Fidelity, Schwab, or Hargreaves Lansdown in the UK. Find a target date fund based on the year you plan to retire (e.g., Vanguard 2065 Target Retirement Fund). Start with an initial deposit of at least $1,000 (or one month of your take-home pay), then set up an automatic monthly transfer of 5–10% of your take-home pay. The fund is already diversified across approximately 3,000 U.S. and international stocks and automatically rebalances as you age. Check your account only every 3–6 months, log in, see the balance, and walk away. Investing should be as exciting as watching paint dry.

### Step 4: Run the Numbers Before Buying a House

Owning a house is often sold as the ultimate investment, but after factoring in inflation, maintenance, property taxes, interest, and opportunity costs, it has essentially matched inflation over 100 years according to data from the Case-Shiller Index. Compare the true monthly cost of owning (mortgage plus phantom costs like roof repairs, labor, and interest—add roughly 50% to the stated mortgage payment) against renting the same property in the same neighborhood. If you must buy, plan to stay for at least 10 years to spread out transaction costs typically totaling 5–10% of the purchase price in closing fees, realtor commissions, and closing costs. Never feel guilty for renting if the numbers do not work out in your favor.

### Step 5: Increase Your Income by Moving to a Scarce Market

Your income is your greatest wealth-building tool. Double your revenue by: (1) asking existing clients for referrals to fill your calendar to capacity, (2) increasing average lifetime value per client by packaging add-ons (meal planning, group sessions, extended plans at $200–$500/month extra per client), and (3) moving your skill set to a market where it is rare and highly valued. A graphic designer in Manchester earning $50–$100 per flyer can move to Dubai and earn $10,000–$50,000 per branding project by serving luxury brands. Look for discontinuous jumps in hourly or project rate, not incremental $20/month increases in a saturated market.

### Step 6: Follow the 10 Money Rules

1. **Emergency fund:** Keep one year of essential expenses in a liquid high-yield savings account.
2. **Save 10%, invest 20%** of gross annual income before taxes are taken out.
3. **Pay cash** for large lifestyle expenses like engagement rings, weddings, and trips so you never pay 15–25% interest on depreciating purchases.
4. **Never question spending** on books, professional development courses, health-related expenses, or donating to a friend's verified 501(c)(3) charity fundraiser where you can see exactly where the money goes.
5. **Business class** on flights over four hours.
6. **Buy the highest-quality version of items you use daily**—mattress, laptop, car—and keep them for 7–10 years rather than replacing them every 2–3 years.
7. **No upper limit on spending** for preventive healthcare, medical procedures, and formal education that increases your earning power or qualifies you for a higher-paying career.
8. **Set your income target high enough** that you can refuse clients, employers, or collaborators who are unethical, disrespectful, or misaligned with your values.
9. **Automate all bill payments, investment contributions, and savings transfers** so you spend fewer than 60 minutes per month actively managing your finances.
10. **Marry someone whose financial values, spending habits, and long-term goals align with yours**, and discuss income, debt, and financial expectations at least monthly throughout the relationship.

### Step 7: Avoid Common Psychological Traps

- **Status spending:** Buy things for genuine personal or professional utility, not to impress strangers on social media. External validation leads to financially inefficient choices such as buying a mansion 90 minutes from your friends and family.
- **Counterintuitive costs:** A 1% financial advisor fee on a $100,000 portfolio over 30 years eats approximately 28% of your lifetime returns. Spending more on investment management does not mean better returns—it means worse returns after fees.
- **Crypto and get-rich-quick:** If you must speculate, limit it to 1–5% of a well-diversified portfolio. Data from the 2022 crypto crash shows roughly 70–80% of retail crypto investors lost money, with a significant share experiencing losses exceeding 50% of their portfolio.
- **Thinking you need to be rich to start:** You build wealth by investing $50–$200 per month automatically over decades, not by waiting until you have "enough" or a six-figure net worth.

### Step 8: Talk About Money with Your Partner Proactively

Bring up money during low-stress conversations such as after a shared meal or during a weekend walk, rather than only during crises. Ask specific questions like "How were you raised with money by your parents?" and "What does money mean to you in terms of freedom and security?" Share your complete financial situation—including income, debt, savings rate, and credit score—within the first three months of the relationship or before signing a lease together, so your partner is never uninformed. If discussing a prenup, frame it as protecting both people's pre-marital assets, not a lack of trust. If your partner refuses to discuss finances at all after you have raised it twice, that reveals fundamental differences in risk tolerance, financial priorities, or long-term goals.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Personal Trainer Doubling Revenue

A personal trainer earning $60,000/year with clients paying $100/hour and staying for three months can roughly double income by: (1) asking current clients for referrals to fill all available 20 weekly slots, (2) offering meal planning and macro coaching for an extra $200/month per client, and (3) giving a 10% discount for six-month commitments. The trainer does not need more hours—they need better packaging per hour.

### Example 2: The Graphic Designer's Market Jump

A skilled graphic designer in Manchester was earning $50–$100 per nightclub flyer. After moving to Dubai and targeting luxury brands with the same design software and skillset, the same hour of work yielded $10,000–$50,000 per branding project. The lesson: your income is less about how hard you work and more about how scarce your skills are in the market you choose.

### Example 3: Renting vs. Owning in New York

Ramit calculated that owning a comparable apartment next to his rental would cost 2.2 times more per month. By renting and investing the $3,400 monthly difference (accounting for phantom costs, maintenance, interest, and taxes), he made more money than he would have by owning over a 10-year holding period. The pressure to buy was social, not financial.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Automate everything: checking-to-savings transfers, investment contributions, and credit card payoff
- ✅ Spend 5–10 times the market rate on the three categories you value most, and cut costs to the minimum on categories that do not move the needle on your rich life
- ✅ Start investing at age 18 or as soon as you have earned income, even if you can only afford $50–$100 per month initially
- ✅ Keep expense ratios below 0.10% annually and avoid frequent trading that triggers short-term capital gains taxes
- ✅ Be specific about your rich life down to the airline seat class, hotel brand, and travel companions
- ✅ Discuss finances with your partner at least once per month and before any major purchase, investment, or life change
- ✅ Target industries or geographies where your specific skillset has the highest demand-to-supply ratio, such as technology skills in emerging markets or healthcare expertise in underserved regions

## Keep In Mind

- Less than 1% of people have a specific, detailed vision of their rich life written down—that specificity is your unfair advantage over every competitor for your money and attention
- Money directly determines three life outcomes: your geographic location, your choice of life partner, and how many hours per week you spend on work versus leisure
- The human brain is not wired for compounding—use a calculator or spreadsheet instead of intuition when projecting investment growth
- The biggest purchase of your life deserves more justification than "grandma bought a house in 1980 and made money"
- A rich life is built on the experiences, relationships, and health that money enables—not on tracking every penny—so automate your finances to free up mental bandwidth for what matters

## Security & Safety Notes

- Keep your emergency fund in a FDIC-insured or FSCS-insured liquid savings account, not in stocks, bonds, or under your mattress
- Never withdraw from your investment account for non-emergency spending—it is wealth accumulation, not a checking account
- If using investment apps, prefer desktop over mobile to avoid the temptation of daily checking and reactive selling
- Limit speculative assets like crypto to no more than 5% of your total portfolio if you choose to include them at all
- Protect pre-marital assets with a prenup drafted by a qualified attorney if you have business holdings, real estate, or investments exceeding $100,000 before marriage

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a house because "it's the American dream" without running the numbers
  **Solution:** Calculate the true monthly cost including mortgage, property taxes, homeowner's insurance, maintenance reserves, and opportunity cost of your down payment, then compare to renting the same property. Plan to stay at least 10 years if you buy to justify transaction costs typically totaling 5–10% of the purchase price.

- **Problem:** Treating investments like a checking account and withdrawing for bills or lifestyle purchases
  **Solution:** Use separate financial institutions for checking, savings, and investments so you must make a deliberate wire transfer or wait 2–3 business days to move money between wealth-building and spending accounts.

- **Problem:** Trying to beat the market by picking individual stocks or trading frequently
  **Solution:** Use a low-cost target date fund with an expense ratio below 0.10%, set up automatic contributions, and check your balance only every 3–6 months. Keep costs low and time in the market high over a 10–15 year horizon.

- **Problem:** Only talking to your partner about money when something goes wrong
  **Solution:** Schedule proactive money conversations once per month on a fixed calendar date. Start with two compliments about your partner's financial strengths before discussing budgets, debts, or concerns.

- **Problem:** Believing you need a high income to start investing
  **Solution:** Use Ramit's Conscious Spending Plan: 5–10% of take-home pay automatically invested is enough to start. Audit your current spending using the last three months of bank statements—there is almost always 10–20% of monthly spending that does not align with your stated financial goals and can be redirected to investments.
