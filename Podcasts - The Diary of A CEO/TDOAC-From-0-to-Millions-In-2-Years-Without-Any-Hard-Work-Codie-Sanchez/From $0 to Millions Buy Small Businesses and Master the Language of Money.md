# From $0 to Millions: Buy Small Businesses and Master the Language of Money

## Overview

Codie Sanchez reveals that financial freedom comes from understanding finance as a technical language and acquiring equity through small-business acquisitions rather than traditional saving. After working in finance and building Contessa Holdings into a $70 million holding company, she explains that deal-making is accessible to people who learn financial-statement analysis and develop operator networks, not only to wealthy investors.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you hold a high-paying job with restricted stock or deferred compensation that makes leaving financially painful, but you want business ownership instead of W-2 income
- When you want to leave full-time employment while maintaining professional relationships and avoiding severing industry connections
- When you want to build equity through business ownership rather than relying solely on 401(k) contributions and public-market index funds
- When you want to learn deal-making and seller financing

## Steps

### Step 1: Learn the Language of Money (Finance)

Study the three core financial statements: the profit-and-loss statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement. Financial terminology functions as a barrier that excludes non-specialists and justifies advisory fees. Like any technical language, finance becomes readable through repeated exposure, and financial literacy directly enables deal sourcing and underwriting.

### Step 2: Find Your Unfair Advantage

Audit your personal and business expenses to identify vendors and services you already pay. Target acquisition of a supplier or service provider in your existing industry so your prior operational knowledge reduces due-diligence risk. For example, if you work in media, acquire a podcast-production studio you have already hired. Use your existing professional knowledge as your entry point rather than pursuing random businesses in unfamiliar industries.

### Step 3: Learn Deal Structure and Seller Financing

According to 2023-2024 small-business transaction data, approximately 60% of businesses with under $10 million in annual revenue close with seller-financing terms. Valuations for small businesses typically fall between 2x and 5x seller's discretionary earnings (SDE) or EBITDA. Structure earn-outs or installment payments so sellers receive compensation tied to future performance. With seller financing and earned equity, buyers can acquire a business with zero cash down, because the purchase price is determined by contract terms rather than a fixed sticker price.

### Step 4: Find Motivated Sellers

Target low-profile, cash-flow-positive businesses such as laundromats, HVAC companies, or dental practices where the owner is personally ready to exit. Prioritize owners who have operated for 20-30 years, are approaching retirement age, or have no family member succeeding them. Initiate conversations with genuine curiosity about their business rather than a sales pitch. Monitor owner age, recent life events, and plateauing revenue to identify trigger moments that signal selling readiness.

### Step 5: Use Content as Your Net

Publishing industry content on LinkedIn, Twitter, or YouTube replaces cold outreach and in-person networking. By creating educational posts, podcasts, or videos about your target sector, you generate inbound deal flow and build credibility with sellers and operators. A consistent personal brand grants admission to investment groups and off-market deal lists that gatekeepers otherwise restrict. This inbound strategy eliminates the need for unsolicited pitches.

### Step 6: Break Your Frame

Compress your timeline: if your goal is to acquire one business this year, design a plan that closes a deal within six months rather than three years. Prioritize finding an experienced operator or mentor over studying deal-structuring tactics in isolation. In practice, the person who introduces you to a motivated seller or who manages day-to-day operations matters more than any single acquisition framework.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Laundromat Deal

While working 60-70 hours per week in a finance role, Sanchez acquired her first business, a laundromat, for $100,000. She had no background in the coin-laundry industry, so she recruited an operator with local experience to manage daily operations. That first acquisition became her proof-of-concept for seller financing and showed that full-time employees can buy cash-flowing businesses without industry expertise.

### Example 2: The Video Production Company

Sanchez acquired a podcast and video production company with zero cash down, providing $10,000 as a deposit. The company generated roughly $100,000 in annual revenue at the time of purchase. She introduced six new clients from her personal network, doubling the company's monthly recurring revenue, and negotiated away her $3,000-per-month service contract in exchange for a 25% ownership stake. Within two years, the company produced $300,000 to $400,000 in annual profit.

### Example 3: Seller Financing Structure

Consider a property-management company with $100,000 in annual SDE. The owner wants a sale price of 2x to 5x SDE. Instead of paying cash, you structure seller financing at an agreed-upon interest rate and use an installment sale so the seller defers capital-gains tax liability. You pay the $300,000 purchase price over five years through a defined payment schedule, retaining a negotiated share of quarterly profits while the remainder goes to the seller until the note is fully satisfied. Both parties benefit: the seller receives steady income and reduced immediate tax burden, and you acquire cash-flowing assets with minimal upfront capital.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Learn to read a balance sheet and understand the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement
- ✅ Find an operator who knows the business if you don't have industry expertise
- ✅ Look for motivated sellers, not just profitable businesses
- ✅ Use seller financing to reduce upfront capital requirements
- ✅ Create educational content in your target sector to build credibility and attract deal flow
- ✅ Think in terms of ownership, not just income
- ✅ Choose high-performing team members; top performers drive better results, while underperforming teammates reduce collective output
- ❌ Don't rely on saving alone to build wealth, because investment returns alone rarely generate significant capital for acquisitions
- ❌ Don't buy businesses you don't understand just for the sake of buying
- ❌ Don't try to change people's minds - find owners already predisposed to sell
- ❌ Don't be an underperformer yourself - maintain high standards in your own work

## Keep In Mind

- According to 2024 small-business market data, millions of privately held companies are listable for sale but remain off-market because owners do not realize their businesses have transferable value
- Businesses are like real estate - you can buy, hold appreciation, and sell them
- Deal-making is not zero-sum; structure outcomes where both buyer and seller gain
- Your first business does not have to be your permanent holding - treat it as a learning vehicle
- Most people reach the end of their working lives with no equity in income-producing assets because traditional education does not teach deal-making or seller financing
- Content is your backdoor to access - don't rely on cold outreach at events, build in public
- The transaction costs and opportunity costs of acquiring a business exist but are worth paying for cash-flowing assets
- Having your own deal flow and operator network - "the power of place" - is crucial during transitions

## Security & Safety Notes

- Verify a business's tax returns, bank statements, and P&L independently before submitting an offer
- Avoid allocating emergency savings to a down payment; maintain at least six months of living expenses in reserve
- Seller financing requires written agreements reviewed by a business attorney
- Ensure you have an operator who can run the business while you remain employed full-time

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting until you feel fully confident or until market conditions feel ideal
  **Solution:** Begin with a small SBA-loan-backed acquisition or a seller-financed deal under $50,000, then scale as you complete transactions.

- **Problem:** Delaying outreach because you lack direct industry experience
  **Solution:** Partner with an operator who has run the target business type, or acquire a company in a sector adjacent to your current career.

- **Problem:** Pitching owners who show no signs of selling readiness
  **Solution:** Monitor owner age, recent life events, and plateauing revenue to identify owners in transition.

- **Problem:** Chasing visible status symbols seen on social media instead of cash-flowing assets
  **Solution:** Prioritize businesses with recurring revenue and transferable operations over luxury goods or high-risk speculation.

- **Problem:** Retaining underperforming team members because they are familiar or low-conflict
  **Solution:** Remove low performers before they normalize lower standards and reduce team output.

- **Problem:** Believing that only founders or CEOs can hold meaningful business equity
  **Solution:** Acquire minority stakes, earn-out positions, or seller-financed notes that give you ownership without operational responsibility.
