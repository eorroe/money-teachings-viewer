# Billionaire's WARNING: I'm SELLING. The Crash Is Already Here!

## Overview

This money teaching is based on Jeremy Grantham's warning about the largest investment bubble in history—artificial intelligence—and his urgent advice for average investors, founders, and anyone with savings. Having managed $165 billion over 60 years and correctly predicted major market crashes including the 2000 dot-com bust and the 2008 financial crisis, Grantham explains why US stocks, crypto, and AI investments are dangerously overvalued, and what concrete steps people should take now to protect themselves before an inevitable collapse. U.S. equities are showing extreme overvaluation, with the cyclically adjusted price-to-earnings (CAPE) ratio at 40—above any level seen outside the peak of the 2000 internet bubble—and the market cap-to-GDP ratio at all-time highs. The teaching covers asset allocation, diversification, and long-term resilience in a deteriorating economic environment.

## Steps

### Step 1: Sell All US Technology Stocks and Avoid the S&P 500

Jeremy Grantham personally advises selling all US technology stocks immediately. He is not confident that US equities will be intact in 5 or 10 years due to extreme overvaluation measured by CAPE ratios, market cap-to-GDP, and price-to-sales multiples far above historical averages. The US market is in the biggest bubble in American history, and indicators of euphoria are everywhere—such as Palantir trading at over 100 times sales and Tesla at over 300 times earnings with earnings down 61% year over year. Avoid the S&P 500 entirely—do not own US stocks as part of your strategy.

### Step 2: Avoid Crypto Entirely

Grantham owns no crypto, has never owned any, and will never advise anyone to buy it. He calls it "an unnecessary piece of nonsense that facilitates nothing except criminals moving money." Bitcoin is not a store of value (it dropped from $69,000 to $35,000 because "it felt like it"), is too volatile, and is not used conveniently as a medium of exchange. He believes Bitcoin will certainly go to zero in the distant future.

### Step 3: Diversify Into Non-US Equities, Bonds, Cash, and Precious Metals

For the average person with $1,000 to $10,000 to invest, Grantham recommends:
- **60%** in broad-based non-US equities (emerging markets, Europe, Japan, Canada, Australia)
- **5–10%** in precious metals (gold and silver)
- **No more than 5%** in real estate, and only if you already own a primary residence for personal use—do not buy investment property
- Place the remaining funds in bonds

This is the opposite of conventional advice. Over the 12 months preceding June 2026, emerging markets gained 65%, while the S&P 500 returned 25%. Non-US stocks have handsomely outperformed US stocks.

### Step 4: Understand and Purchase Bonds Directly

A bond is a loan that carries a fixed interest rate (for example, 5%). You can buy US government bonds directly at treasurydirect.gov—open an account, link your bank, and purchase Treasury bills, notes, bonds, or savings bonds with no commissions or fees. You can also buy corporate bonds (such as Apple bonds yielding approximately 4.7% for a 10-year term) through major brokers like Fidelity or Vanguard by navigating to the fixed-income section of your account.

### Step 5: Be Cautious About Real Estate

Property is extremely overvalued by historical standards, with UK house prices rising from 3.4 times family income in 1994 to over 10 times today. Similar overvaluation exists in Canada, Australia, China, and most of Europe, where housing prices have similarly detached from historical norms. Even a 30% decline would still leave prices at 6–7 times income—twice what they used to be. Young couples are effectively locked out. Population decline and falling family formations will further pressure prices. If you must own property, ensure it is affordable and not a speculative bet. Allocate no more than 5% of your portfolio to real estate, and only if you already own a primary residence for personal use.

### Step 6: Brace Yourself for Tough Times and Build Practical Skills

For people without huge savings, Grantham advises planning your life as if times will not be easy. Build a cash reserve equal to 6–12 months of living expenses. Pursue a career in engineering, agriculture, or other practical, human-dependent fields that cannot be automated. Grantham specifically recommends learning skills such as raising crops, chickens, pigs, or mushrooms—practical abilities that will come in handy if things start to unravel. Learn skills that require humans.

### Step 7: Founders Should Lock Up Capital Now and Build Conservatism

If you are a founder or entrepreneur dependent on investment capital, lock up money now while you can. Build conservatism into your business. Brace yourself for impending problems. Grantham explicitly declined to forecast an exact timeline, stating the peak could occur in "weeks, months, or years," but emphasized that investors must act now as if a storm is coming while the sun is shining.

### Step 8: Recognize That Investment Advisers Have Conflicts of Interest

You will not receive honest warnings from investment advisers because it is bad for their business. From 1929 onward, firms like Goldman Sachs have never told clients to get out of an overpriced market. The engine room analysts know the data, but the marketers on stage say "we'll muddle through." You are on your own. Look at the data yourself. A bubble is not hard to see—there is a steady trend line and then a Himalayan peak that eventually returns to earth.

### Step 9: Reduce Exposure to Toxic Chemicals to Protect Fertility and Health

The environment is filled with endocrine-disrupting chemicals that are crashing sperm counts and fertility. Practical steps:
- Avoid cosmetics for at least 9 months during pregnancy (save money and reduce toxin exposure)
- Buy organic berries, apples, peaches, and spinach (the "dirty dozen" with highest pesticide residues)
- Avoid bananas, oranges, and melons (they have natural protection and lower pesticide loads)
- Use apps like Yuka, EWG's Healthy Living, Think Dirty, or Clear Ya to scan products for harmful chemicals
- Avoid plastics, Teflon pans, and gas stoves
- Be aware that PFAs in tap water (45% of US tap water is contaminated) are linked to lower sperm volume and testicular cancer

### Step 10: Consider Your Country of Residence Based on Social Contract

Grantham would not recommend living in the United States for average people because the social contract is dissolving. Neighbors are less likely to help you, and the safety net is ineffective. He recommends Denmark, Japan, France, or Germany as places with stronger social contracts, better life expectancy, and more reliable safety nets. The US has the worst maternal mortality rate in the rich world—22.3 deaths per 100,000 live births in 2022, roughly double the rate of most other high-income countries and triple that of Norway, Japan, and Germany.
