# Passive Income Expert: How To Make $10k Per Month In 90 Days!

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills actionable business frameworks from serial entrepreneur Chris Kerner on launching profitable side hustles with minimal capital. It covers how to validate ideas within 1-2 weeks, copy proven models, choose a business structure that fits your capital level (LLC, sole proprietorship, or partnership), and build toward $10k/month in 90 days using solo-operated ventures that require under $500 in startup costs.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to start a side hustle or business with $500, $1,000, or $5,000
- When validating a business idea before quitting your job
- When deciding between a solo founder path versus taking on a business partner
- When the user asks about passive income, AI-powered services, directory websites, or local service businesses

## Steps

### Step 1: Copy Proven Business Models First

Find a business that already works. Use the Wayback Machine (web archive) and SimilarWeb to reverse engineer their website evolution, pricing, and traffic patterns. Copy the competitor's pricing tiers, homepage layout, niche positioning, and business structure identically. Add your own twists only after you understand why the original model works. Avoid reinventing the wheel for "old problems" like hiring, cash flow, legal structure, or pricing — these have well-established solutions. Experiment only on new problems where no blueprint exists.

### Step 2: Validate Ideas With Low Friction Tests

Use Facebook Marketplace as your primary validation tool. Post two variants of a product concept (e.g., gummies vs. powder) with the same headline, description, and within a 25-mile radius. Track views, clicks, and outreach messages in a Google Sheet. Boost the best performer with $10 to see if results differ. Concurrently, join relevant Facebook groups and search for competitor mentions, price points, and customer complaints. Collect all data before spending on samples or inventory. True validation is when demand chases you — customers can't stop reaching out, orders pile up, and you are fulfilling requests faster than you can respond.

### Step 3: Pick a Business Model Based on Your Budget

**$500 — AI Implementation Agency:** Learn vibe coding tools like Replit or Lovable. Build a simple app or automation for a local small business. Target small businesses with 1-10 employees in your metro area via Facebook ads using plain language ("I will make your business more money with AI"). Charge $500–$5,000 upfront plus 20% monthly for maintenance. Every solution you build can be resold to every other business in that category.

**$500 — Drop Servicing:** Create a lead-generation website for a home service (e.g., car repair, gutter cleaning) that includes a professional logo, dedicated phone number, local address, and service pages for each offering. Run Facebook or Google ads to generate leads. Subcontract the work to a local business. You keep the margin between what the customer pays and what you pay the contractor. Focus on industries with bad websites and poor customer experience.

**$500 — Directory Website:** Build a niche directory site (e.g., dog parks in Seattle, ice suppliers in Wisconsin) using Replit or Lovable. Monetize with display ads via an ad network or by charging listed businesses for premium placement once traffic arrives. Copy the template across dozens of markets. Most will earn little, but a few can produce meaningful passive income.

**$500 — Vending Machines:** Buy a used snack or drink vending machine on Facebook Marketplace for $300–$400. Spend $100 on initial inventory from Costco or Sam's Club. Convince one local business owner to let you place the machine in their lobby for $100/month or 30% of revenue. Once you have one location and a track record, you can expand. Outsource restocking to a cash management company to make it more passive.

**$1,000 — Wedding and Funeral Rentals:** Build or buy one rental item with proven demand in your market (at least 10 bookings per month). Partner with wedding planners rather than brides — each planner coordinates 20-40 weddings annually. Rent each item for $500–$1,000 per event. Use The Knot, Zola, or WeddingWire to find planner contacts. The same model applies to funeral homes.

**$$1,000 — Local Email Newsletter:** Acquire subscribers in a specific city using Facebook or Instagram ads (~$0.50–$1 per subscriber). Write the newsletter yourself in a personal, local voice — do not use AI. Sell sponsorships to local business owners inside the list at roughly $0.50 per subscriber per month. A 1,000-subscriber list can generate $500/month in ad revenue and pays back acquisition costs within two months. Copy the model into additional cities.

**$5,000 — RV Parks or Mobile Home Parks:** Search Google Maps for RV parks with 10 or fewer pad sites within a three-hour drive. Call owners and look for a seller-financing opportunity. Use $2,000 for due diligence (inspections, title policy). Use the remainder for operating capital and first improvements. Buy undervalued, improve occupancy with free Facebook Marketplace ads and a simple vibe-coded website, then refinance or hold for cash flow. Parks with 3–10 pad sites can be 3–10x more profitable than a single-family rental.

### Step 4: Learn Facebook Ads as a Foundational Skill

Treat Facebook ads like email — a skill everyone should know. You can become proficient in 3-5 days by launching a real campaign, not by watching hours of unstructured tutorial content. Facebook ads are the highest-ROI channel for local businesses because you can target specific geographic areas, track conversions directly, and reach audiences already in buying mode. Master targeting, creative testing, and budget scaling.

### Step 5: Choose the Right Business Partner Structure

Most people do not need a business partner. Companies with co-founders have significantly higher failure rates than solo-founded companies. If you do partner, solo-found first so you understand your own strengths, weaknesses, and work style. Never agree to 50/50 equity at the outset. Instead, set a clear metric (e.g., $10,000 in revenue, product-market fit, or 90 days of traction). Once the milestone is hit, formalize equity based on actual contribution, experience, capital, and time invested. Avoid partnering with friends without structure, and never define the relationship too early or too late.

### Step 6: Decide When to Burn the Boats

Do not quit your job until you have proven the business works at a level where you can cover your living expenses for 6 consecutive months. Test whether scaling ads or operations is profitable and whether you can handle the resulting customer service and infrastructure load. Burn the boats only when you have a clear path to replacing your income and a safety net (e.g., your boss would take you back). Plan Bs reduce effort on Plan A. Removing a Plan B creates urgency and focus that can turn a working side hustle into a full-time income.

### Step 7: Follow Profit Before Passion

Ignore passion in the beginning. The statistical likelihood that what you love and what makes money overlap is less than 5% early on. Fall in love with commerce — turning $1 into $2 — and build your passion around that. Test many things to find what gives you energy. Use the Ikigai framework (what you love, what you are good at, what the world needs, what you can charge for) as a long-term compass, not a day-one filter. Once you have passive income, pivot to industries you genuinely care about.

### Step 8: Treat Entrepreneurship as a Test, Not an Identity

Most ideas will die. That is expected and healthy. Reduce the time between having an idea and testing it to under 7 days. Every test — whether it succeeds or fails — builds your "bias for action" muscle and expands your surface area for future opportunities. If you are distracted from a project, that signal may mean you should not be focusing on it. Lack of focus often points to a better opportunity, not a personal flaw.

## Examples

### Example 1: AI Implementation Agency ($500)

A resident of Omaha, Nebraska learns Replit and Lovable over a weekend. They run Facebook ads targeting small businesses with 1-10 employees in their metro area with the message "I will make your business more money or save it money with AI." They book discovery calls, identify common pain points (missed calls, scheduling, invoicing), and build simple AI tools for free to demonstrate expertise. They close a $3,000 deal to install an AI voice agent for a gutter cleaning business, then charge $500/month for maintenance. They replicate the same solution for every other gutter cleaner in the region.

### Example 2: Local Email Newsletter ($1,000)

Someone in Boise, Idaho spends $1,000 on Facebook ads to acquire 1,000 email subscribers with a promise of one weekly email about local deals, news, and events. They write the newsletter personally rather than using AI. After one month, they pitch 100 local business owners inside the list with a $500-per-send sponsorship. The list pays back acquisition cost in two months and scales by replicating the model in larger cities.

### Example 3: Wedding Arch Rental ($1,000)

An entrepreneur spends $300 on lumber to build a wedding arch, $100 on scraping wedding planner contacts from The Knot and WeddingWire, and $100 on gas and a truck rental. They pitch 50 planners, land one booking at $1,000 per wedding, and reinvest profits into photo walls and shakshuka carts. After six months, they are renting to four weddings per week and hire a part-time assistant for delivery and setup.

### Example 4: RV Park Acquisition ($5,000)

An entrepreneur finds 100 RV parks with 10 or fewer pad sites on Google Maps within a three-hour drive. They call each owner, identify three candidates with motivated sellers, and negotiate seller financing. They spend $2,000 on inspections and title work. They use the remaining $3,000 to trim trees, pay utilities, and run free Facebook Marketplace ads. Occupancy climbs from 50% to 80%, profit rises from $20,000 to $30,000 annually, and the park appreciates to a $375,000 value at an 8% cap rate. They hold for cash flow or sell.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Copy a proven business model's pricing tiers, page layout, niche positioning, and structure before adding your own twists
- ✅ Validate ideas with real human reactions, not just friends and family
- ✅ Use Facebook ads, Facebook Marketplace, and Facebook groups as primary testing tools
- ✅ Learn Facebook ads as a direct, trackable skill for acquiring customers
- ✅ Start solo to understand your own strengths and weaknesses before partnering
- ✅ Focus on old problems with established solutions; experiment only on new problems
- ✅ Set defined metrics before formalizing a partnership or quitting your job
- ✅ Reduce the time between having an idea and testing it to under 7 days
- ✅ Treat every business as a test, not a reflection of your identity
- ✅ Use seller financing and creative deals to acquire assets with little or no cash down

## Keep In Mind

- 90% of side hustle ideas can be launched with $500 or less
- The best time to start is today — tools, AI, and internet access remove most barriers to entry
- Business is not a zero-sum game; the market is large enough for multiple winners
- Entrepreneurship in the first 12-24 months is often harder and less free than a 9-to-5
- The financial pain of your current situation must outweigh the fear of social judgment from peers and family
- A 50/50 partnership is statistically likely to fail unless both partners have known each other for years and are in a mature stage of life
- "Passive income" is mostly a buzzword early on — expect to create active, sweaty, ugly income first
- Lack of focus is often a signal you are working on the wrong thing, not a personal flaw

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use personal funds or quit your job until the business has proven traction and a clear scaling path
- Avoid investing large sums in untested ideas; keep experiments cheap and repeatable
- Do not sign 50/50 partnership agreements without a defined milestone for renegotiation
- When using seller financing, ensure proper title searches and inspections are completed before taking over a business or property
- Avoid premature scaling — do not burn the boats until you have validated profitability at the next level of time commitment

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Reinventing the wheel for old problems like pricing, hiring, or legal structure.
  **Solution:** Copy an existing competitor's pricing, layout, and niche positioning identically. You will learn faster and avoid costly mistakes.
- **Problem:** Adding twists to a copied model before you understand why it works.
  **Solution:** Execute the proven model identically for at least the first phase. Innovate only after you have data.
- **Problem:** Using friends and family for validation instead of strangers.
  **Solution:** Sell to strangers and read their body language. Real validation is when demand chases you.
- **Problem:** Quitting a stable job too early based on an untested idea.
  **Solution:** Keep your job until you have proven profitability and a clear path to replacing your income.
- **Problem:** Partnering with someone before you know your own strengths and weaknesses.
  **Solution:** Solo-found first. Understand whether you are a starter, maintainer, or finisher before choosing a partner.
- **Problem:** Chasing "sexy" or "innovative" ideas that are already overcrowded.
  **Solution:** Go as unsexy as possible. Avoid mirage opportunities — markets where everyone is trying to solve the same problem and nobody is succeeding.
- **Problem:** Assuming lack of focus is a personal failure.
  **Solution:** Distraction is often data. If you cannot stop thinking about a new idea, it may deserve a test.
- **Problem:** Expecting entrepreneurship to deliver total freedom from day one.
  **Solution:** In the early years, you will answer to more people, not fewer. Trade stability for optionality, not freedom.
