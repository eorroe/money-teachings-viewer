# How to Get on the Right Side of Change: Invest in Disruptive Innovation

## Overview

Cathie Wood, who manages nearly $30 billion at ARK Invest, reveals how to identify and invest in the biggest technological disruptions of our time. She predicts that companies built on five core innovation platforms—robotics, energy storage, artificial intelligence, blockchain technology, and multiomic sequencing—will generate returns of at least 10x on invested capital over the next five to ten years. The key insight is to avoid seemingly safe investments that may be disrupted by AI, and instead focus on technologically enabled companies positioned to transform the world.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When deciding how to allocate capital for long-term growth
- When evaluating whether a company is truly innovative or vulnerable to disruption
- When building a portfolio around artificial intelligence and breakthrough technologies
- When the user asks about Bitcoin, autonomous vehicles, gene editing, or AI investments
- When trying to understand which sectors will be disrupted and which will do the disrupting

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify True Innovation Platforms Using Wright's Law

Focus on technologies that follow Wright's Law, meaning their costs drop by a predictable percentage each time cumulative production doubles, allowing them to proliferate across multiple economic sectors. The three characteristics of a genuine innovation platform are: (1) it follows a steep learning curve with falling costs, (2) it cuts across sectors and applies to at least two distinct customer segments, and (3) it serves as a launching pad for entirely new technologies. Avoid companies that merely benefit temporarily from a trend; invest in platforms that enable entirely new industries.

### Step 2: Focus on Disruptive Innovation, Not "Safe" Blue Chips

Do not assume that large, cash-rich companies with dominant market share are safe investments. Many incumbents, including major smartphone and retail players, will be disrupted by artificial intelligence. Cathie Wood notes that Apple, despite its profitability and loyalty, could face stagnant revenue growth because it is not positioned correctly for the AI era. Instead of chasing familiar names, seek companies that are building the physical and digital infrastructure enabling autonomous vehicles, gene editing, and blockchain-based financial services.

### Step 3: Build a Concentrated Portfolio Around High-Conviction Themes

Cathie Wood recommends averaging into a focused strategy over time rather than diversifying indiscriminately. For investors with less than $10,000 to invest, she suggests buying an innovation-focused ETF like ARKK, which holds 35 to 36 high-conviction stocks across robotics, AI, energy storage, blockchain, and genomics. If you prefer individual stocks, her top public picks include Tesla, Coinbase, Robinhood, Roku, CRISPR Therapeutics, Palantir, Archer, Shopify, and Roblox. The goal is exposure to the convergence of multiple innovation platforms in a single company.

### Step 4: Invest in Bitcoin as a New Asset Class and Global Monetary System

Bitcoin represents both a new asset class and a rules-based global monetary system. Cathie Wood forecasts a Bitcoin price of $1.5 million by 2030, driven by three tailwinds: institutional adoption, millennials and Gen Z investors treating Bitcoin as digital gold, and its role as a hedge against currency devaluation in economies with inflation rates above 10% annually. She personally bought Bitcoin at roughly $250 in 2015 and never sold. To invest, average in over time through trusted, regulatory-compliant platforms. Only allocate capital you can afford to lose, and always do your own research before investing.

### Step 5: Use Dollar-Cost Averaging to Build Positions Over Time

Whether investing in individual stocks, ETFs, or Bitcoin, buy a fixed dollar amount each month, equal to 5-10% of your paycheck. This approach removes the pressure of timing the market and smooths out volatility. Cathie Wood emphasizes that these are long-term investments based on years of original research. If the underlying thesis is correct, the compounding effect of disruptive innovation over five to ten years should generate returns that far exceed traditional benchmarks. Ignore daily political headlines and social media reactions, and stay the course.

## Examples

### Example 1: Building a $1,000 AI and Innovation Portfolio

If you have $1,000 to invest, split it across three to four individual stocks or ETFs rather than spreading it thin. You might allocate $300 to an innovation ETF like ARKK for broad sector exposure, $300 to Tesla for the convergence of robotics, energy storage, and AI, $200 to Coinbase for digital asset infrastructure, and $200 to Bitcoin directly. Each month, add another $100 to $200 using the same allocation. Over five to ten years, this strategy targets the explosive growth predicted by ARK Invest's research.

### Example 2: Evaluating a "Safe" Stock for Disruption Risk

Before buying a seemingly stable company with consistent quarterly earnings and a long history of dividend payments, ask whether AI or a breakthrough technology could disintermediate its core business. For example, Apple's dominance in smartphones does not protect it from autonomous vehicles becoming the ultimate mobile device, or from AI-driven shopping assistants replacing traditional retail discovery. If a company is not positioned to participate in the innovation platforms you have identified, it may be a mature company generating steady profits but with no meaningful growth pipeline rather than a growth investment. Move your capital to companies actively building the future.

- ✅ Dollar-cost average into positions over time instead of trying to time the market
- ✅ Focus on platforms that enable entirely new industries, not temporary beneficiaries of trends
- ✅ Do your own research and never invest money you cannot afford to lose
- ❌ Don't assume large, profitable companies like Apple are safe from AI disruption
- ❌ Don't put all your capital into a single stock, even if it is your highest conviction

## Keep In Mind

- Technology is deflationary: costs fall over time and are passed through as lower prices or better performance, which creates enormous opportunities for investors on the right side of change.
- Real GDP growth could accelerate to 7.3% over the next five years if the five innovation platforms deliver on their promise, more than twice the historical average annual growth rate of approximately 3%.
- Humanoid robots, autonomous taxis, and AI-driven healthcare will disrupt entire sectors, including transportation, retail, and life sciences.
- Passive investing in benchmarks like the S&P 500 will underperform if you cannot identify the companies transforming the world before they are reflected in indices.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never invest based solely on Cathie Wood's predictions or a single source of research. Cathie Wood explicitly states these are not investment recommendations and you can lose all of your money.
- Use regulated, compliant platforms when buying Bitcoin or digital assets. Coinbase is cited as a regulatory-compliant exchange, while Binance has faced regulatory challenges.
- Store digital assets in hardware wallets such as Ledger or Trezor, or use regulated custodial services with insurance, and enable two-factor authentication on all investment accounts.
- Be wary of scams and fraudulent investment schemes that use the names of ARK Invest, Cathie Wood, or popular innovation themes to lure investors.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Investing in a company because it is a well-known brand with consistent earnings and dividends, only to watch it get disrupted by AI.
  **Solution:** Apply Wright's Law and ask whether the company is on the right side of change. If it is not positioned for the innovation platforms you believe in, redirect capital to true disruptors.
- **Problem:** Selling too early because of short-term volatility or daily political headlines and social media reactions.
  **Solution:** Remember that these are long-term theses based on five- to ten-year cost curves. Stay invested through volatility if your research has not changed.
- **Problem:** Failing to adapt to new technologies because they feel unfamiliar or uncomfortable.
  **Solution:** Initiative and a willingness to learn are the greatest protections against displacement. Read the research, experiment with new tools such as AI-assisted development platforms that generate code from natural language prompts, and understand how AI intersects with your career.
- **Problem:** Putting all your capital into one stock because it has the highest predicted upside.
  **Solution:** Diversify within your high-conviction thesis. Even Tesla, the largest position, is part of a broader portfolio that balances risk across autonomous mobility, AI software, genomics, and digital assets.
