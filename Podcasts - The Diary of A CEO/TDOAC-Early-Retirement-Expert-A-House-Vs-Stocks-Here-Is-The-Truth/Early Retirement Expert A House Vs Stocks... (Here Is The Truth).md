# Early Retirement Expert: A House Vs Stocks... (Here Is The Truth)

## Overview

David Bach draws from 33 years in the financial services industry—including 9 years as a financial advisor at Morgan Stanley—to share his system for becoming an automatic millionaire at incomes ranging from $30,000 to $300,000 annually. He argues that wealth is built through two primary escalators: homeownership and stock market investing, and that the key to both is automation, not discipline or creating and adhering to detailed monthly budgets. The teachings cover how to pay yourself first, why owning a home creates generational wealth, how to get out of debt using the Debt Snowball method, and how to invest in boring, low-cost index funds for long-term growth.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When deciding whether to rent or buy a home
- When you want to build long-term wealth on an ordinary income
- When you are struggling with credit card debt and need the Debt Snowball payoff system, which prioritizes smallest-balance cards first
- When setting up an automatic financial plan that doesn't rely on willpower
- When you want to understand how much to save and where to invest it
- When preparing for retirement and want a realistic path to becoming a millionaire
- When in a relationship and need to align financially with your partner

## Steps

### Step 1: Pay Yourself First Automatically

Save and invest before paying any bills. The formula is simple: 12.5% of your gross income goes to retirement, 5% to an emergency account, and 5% to a dream account. Set up automatic transfers the day your paycheck hits so the money moves without you having to think about it. Start at 1% if you are not saving anything today—just automate the first transfer.

### Step 2: Get Out of Debt Using the Debt Snowball (DOLP)

List all your credit cards from smallest balance to largest. Make minimum payments on every card automatically, then put all extra money toward the smallest balance. Once that card is paid off, roll that payment to the next smallest card. After eliminating the cards, negotiate lower interest rates by calling your issuer and requesting a hardship or loyalty rate reduction, or transfer balances to 0% intro APR balance-transfer cards. Do not close paid-off accounts because it lowers your credit score.

### Step 3: Buy a Home to Build Forced Savings and Generational Wealth

Homeownership is the #1 wealth-building tool for ordinary Americans. Homeowners are worth 40 times more than renters on average. A mortgage payment acts as forced savings because part of each payment builds equity. Use a bi-weekly mortgage payment plan to pay off a 30-year mortgage 5 to 7 years early and save $50,000 to $100,000 in interest. When selling, you keep up to $250,000 in gains tax-free (single) or $500,000 (married).

### Step 4: Invest in Boring, Low-Cost Index Funds

Do not trade individual stocks, meme coins, NFTs, or options. Buy broad-market index funds and hold them automatically. Recommended funds include Vanguard Total Stock Market ETF (VTI), Vanguard Global ex-US ETF (VEA), and Nasdaq 100 ETF (QQQ). For a one-stop solution, use a target-date mutual fund in your 401k that automatically rebalances from stocks to bonds as you age. Keep investing through market pullbacks.

### Step 5: Track Your Spending and Eliminate Waste

Carry a notepad for 7 days and write down every single expense. Most people spend unconsciously through one-tap digital payments. The magic number is $27.40 per day—spend that much more than you need to and you blow $10,000 per year. If you instead invest $27.40 per day for 40 years at 10% annually, you accumulate over $4,424,000. Cancel unused subscriptions, brown-bag your lunch, and find small daily savings.

### Step 6: Increase Your Income Through Value and Skills

Making more money alone does not make you rich if you spend it all. Grow income by becoming excellent at what you do, showing up early, working late, and doing what you say you will. Learn skills with national average wages above $60,000 per year according to Bureau of Labor Statistics data, such as AI tool implementation, HVAC certification, commercial truck driving, or cloud computing. Charge rates 20–50% above local averages for your skill level. Mindset matters: decide your future can be better and act like an optimist.

### Step 7: Align Financially with Your Partner

Marry your financial opposite and you will fight about money—money fights are the #1 cause of divorce. Both partners must know every account, password, and financial obligation. Have an annual account review together. Both people need a will, and families with children need $1 million to $2 million in term life insurance. Run a financial fire drill so your partner could manage every account, bill, and investment if you died or became permanently disabled today.

## Examples

### Example 1: The McIntyres—Ordinary Income, Extraordinary Wealth

Jim and Sue McIntyre earned an average of $40,000 per year and were 52 years old when they walked into David Bach's office with a net worth of $1.8 million. They never used a budget. Instead, they automated their savings, bought a home in San Leandro, California, paid off the mortgage, bought a second home on the same street, rented it out, and saved consistently in their 401k plan. Their wealth came from home equity and retirement accounts—not a high income.

### Example 2: The $27.40-a-Day Rule

If you waste $27.40 per day on unnecessary spending—coffee, cocktails, lunch out, subscriptions—you burn through $10,000 per year. Invested at 10% annually with reinvested dividends, that same $27.40 per day grows to $4,424,000 over 40 years. Even half that amount creates life-changing wealth. For context, approximately 40 to 50 million American families can realistically save this amount.

### Example 3: Grandmother Rose—From 50 Cents a Week to Self-Made Millionaire

At age 30, David Bach's grandmother decided she would no longer be poor. She started saving 50 cents per week out of her paycheck while working retail, and her husband worked in a plant. She taught herself about investing over her lifetime and became a self-made millionaire. At age 7, she took David to McDonald's and taught him the three types of people: consumers, employees, and owners—then bought him his first share of McDonald's stock.

- ✅ Keep investments boring: index funds, target-date funds, and broad-market ETFs

## Security & Safety Notes

- The primary financial manager in a household must share all account information, passwords, and document locations with their partner. David Bach's own mother was left vulnerable when his father died because she had never been involved in the finances
- Both partners should know where the will, life insurance policies, 401k plans, IRA accounts, and bank accounts are located
- Run a financial fire drill: ask yourself if your partner could manage everything if you died today. If the answer is no, fix it now
- Have a will at any age if you are in a relationship or have children—six out of 10 people do not have one
- Get a term life insurance policy of at least $1 million to $2 million if you have children; it is inexpensive and protects your family
- Prenuptial agreements require separate attorneys for both parties and must be signed well before the wedding to avoid being thrown out as undue influence

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that earning more money will make you rich
  **Solution:** Lifestyle creep is real—you can go from $50,000 to $300,000 per year and still be broke. Focus on saving and investing automatically at every income level.

- **Problem:** Thinking renting is smarter because you can invest the difference in stocks
  **Solution:** In practice, people do not invest the difference—they spend it on a nicer apartment. Rent rises over time while a fixed mortgage stays the same. Homeownership builds equity and generational wealth.

- **Problem:** Using credit cards to celebrate paying them off
  **Solution:** Getting out of debt is a huge victory, but going back into debt to celebrate it is a common trap. David Bach himself fell into this after college and did not carry a credit card for 30 years.

- **Problem:** Waiting too long to pay off the mortgage
  **Solution:** Make one extra payment per year or switch to bi-weekly payments. A 30-year mortgage can be paid off 5 to 7 years early, saving $50,000 to $100,000 in interest. Ensure extra payments are applied to principal.

- **Problem:** Not automating finances
  **Solution:** If you have to think about it, you will not do it consistently. Set up automatic transfers on payday for retirement, emergency, and dream accounts. Everyone else—Netflix, your gym, the government—already takes money from you automatically.

- **Problem:** Taking excessive investment risk when young
  **Solution:** The myth is that young people should gamble on meme stocks, crypto, and options. If you put $200 per month into a bad investment for 10 years and lose it, you will stop investing forever. Stick to broad-market index funds.

- **Problem:** One partner managing all money without the other's knowledge
  **Solution:** In 48% of couples, one spouse cannot accurately state their household's total debt and asset values within a 10% margin. This creates catastrophic risk if the managing partner dies. Share everything and hold annual account reviews together.
