1: # 7 AI Businesses You Can Start This Weekend
2: 
3: ## Overview
4: 
5: This money teaching documents seven no-code AI business ideas extracted from the Just Claude video "7 AI Businesses You Can Start This Weekend." It exists to help problem solvers — not coders — identify repetitive work in businesses, remove it with free AI tools like Claude and Zapier, and monetize that value through services, retainers, and micro-products. The core thesis is that AI is replacing repetitive work, not jobs, and the opportunity belongs to the AI generalist who can spot business problems and quietly eliminate them.
6: 
7: ## When to Follow These Money Teachings
8: 
9: - When you need to identify a business problem worth solving with AI
10: - When working with a client who is drowning in repetitive admin, sales, or knowledge work
11: - When the user asks how to start an AI business with zero clients and zero budget
12: 
13: ## Steps
14: 
15: ### Step 1: Become an AI Workflow Consultant
16: 
17: Target local small businesses — plumbers, dental practices, agencies — drowning in admin. You do not write code or build software. Instead, find one task eating up their week and rebuild it with free tools like Claude and Zapier so it runs on autopilot. Use the three-question discovery framework: (1) What task do you do most and hate most? (2) Does it follow the same steps every time? (3) What does an hour of your time cost? If the answer to (2) is yes, that task is a candidate and the answer to (3) is the hourly rate you use to set your invoice. Charge around £1,000 for the audit and £300–£500 per month to keep the workflow running.
18: 
19: ### Step 2: Build a Private AI Assistant for an Expert
20: 
21: Target consultants, solicitors, financial advisors, and other experts whose value is locked inside their heads. Open Claude, start a project, and drag in their last 20 proposals, FAQs, and any existing IP. Then use this exact prompt: "Answer every single question in their voice using only the documents within this project. If the answer is not in the documents, say so and ask one qualifying question." The result is an assistant that drafts proposals in their tone, answers client questions, and trains new staff from work they already have. You are not replacing the expert — you are multiplying them. Charge around £1,500 to build it and £300 per month as a retainer as their workflow grows.
22: 
23: ### Step 3: AI-Powered Prospecting and Outreach
24: 
24: Every business needs more conversations without hiring more people. Feed Claude one prospect and one trigger — a funding round, a new hire, a prospecting list, or a relevant post. Then write a one-line email to the prospect referencing that actual trigger with no pitch and one question designed to get a real reply. This turns cold spam into genuine warm conversations. You handle the research, personalization, and CRM hygiene so the sales team walks into only warm prospects. Charge around £1,500 to set it up and £750 per month to keep it running.
25: 
26: ### Step 4: Content Repurposing Agency (Start Here If Zero Clients and Zero Budget)
27: 
28: Find one business already making content — a podcast, webinar, or long-form video — and let it die after a single use. One podcast can become five LinkedIn posts, a newsletter, three short-form videos, and a blog post article, all pulled from the actual transcripts. Run the transcript through Claude, keep the owner's actual voice, and hand back a full month's worth of content across every channel they already have set up. You are not asking them to create more; you are squeezing more from what already exists. One local business set up this way went from posting once a fortnight to daily multiple posts and tripled their inbound inquiries. Charge around £800 per month as a retainer. This is the zero-budget start: land one, use the result to land the next, and build from there.
29: 
30: ### Step 5: Internal Knowledge Base Builder
31: 
32: Every company has knowledge, processes, scripts, policies, and meeting notes scattered everywhere. Every week someone asks where a document is, who knows how something works, or what was decided in a meeting. Load all of those documents, processes, scripts, policies, and meeting notes into a cloud project so the whole team can access answers in under 30 seconds instead of interrupting each other. Onboarding gets faster, support gets consistent, and managers stop answering the same questions 20 times. You are not selling AI; you are selling clarity. Charge at least £2,000 to build the system and £400 per month to keep it fully updated.
33: 
34: ### Step 6: AI-Powered Landing Page Builder
35: 
36: Restaurants, gyms, dentists, estate agents — all sitting on slow, confusing sites untouched in years. Open Framer, describe the business in plain language, and AI generates a full landing page in under 5 minutes. Then do the part AI cannot: fix the message, sharpen the call to action, and make it actually convert. AI removes approximately 80% of the work; you make every decision that actually matters. Every business needs an online presence, but almost none have one that turns visitors into customers consistently. Charge around £1,500 per page, with optional ongoing support.
38: 
39: ### Step 7: Build a Tiny AI Product
40: 
41: Forget building the next billion-pound app. Solve one annoying problem for one type of person. Examples: recruiters reformatting CVs, accountants answering the same client questions weekly, agencies rebuilding the same report every Friday. AI has dropped the barrier to entry — it writes the code, explains it, and helps you prototype before you need to learn a programming language. The value is not in the code; it is in spotting the problem worth £50 per month to make disappear and charging a subscription around it. Even £30 per month per user across multiple customers stacks into real income and runs while you sleep.
43: 
44: ### Step 8: The 90-Day Plan to Land Your First Paying Client
45: 
46: **Week 1:** Rebuild your own worst recurring admin task on Claude. Automate the specific task you dread every single week for yourself first.
47: **Week 2:** Turn that into a reusable Claude project so you can run it again with one click. That is your first product, and it is the first service you sell to your first paying client.
48: **Week 3:** Do the same thing for one friend's business and watch their face when the boring job suddenly vanishes off their plate.
49: **Week 4:** Ship one landing page free for a local business and keep the portfolio piece for yourself. Now you have proof.
50: **Month 2:** Package it as one offer at one price and message one qualifying local business — one that already has a website, social media presence, or active customer base — not 50. Pitch 10 businesses with personalized outreach and land your first paying client.
51: **Month 3:** Land your first paying client or launch your first tiny product. Ship a working solution rather than waiting for a flawless one. Every AI business starts the same way: a problem into a solution into a customer.

68: ## Best Practices
69: 
70: - ✅ Reframe AI as removing repetitive work, not replacing people
71: - ✅ Always ask what business problem you can solve, not what the tool can do
72: - ✅ Start with your own worst admin task to build proof before selling
73: - ✅ Use the three-question discovery framework before designing any solution
74: - ✅ Package every offering as one problem, one solution, one price
75: - ❌ Do not pitch AI technology — pitch the outcome and the time saved
76: - ❌ Do not try to serve 50 businesses at once; serve 10 businesses with full, personalised outreach
77: - ❌ Do not build software or write code — use free no-code tools like Claude, Zapier, and Framer
78: - ❌ Do not wait for perfection — ship the first version and improve with the client
79: 
80: ## Keep In Mind
81: 
82: - The future belongs to the AI generalist, not the specialist. Specialist knowledge still matters, but information alone is no longer as valuable. The money goes to the person who can look at a whole business and use AI to create leverage in every avenue.
83: - Businesses buy outcomes, not tools. They do not care about ChatGPT, Claude, or prompts. They care whether Monday morning just got easier for them.
84: - Idea number four — content repurposing — is the recommended starting point if you have zero clients and zero budget. It requires no upfront cash, no extra tools, and no extra clients beyond one business already making content.
85: 
86: ## Security & Safety Notes
87: 
88: - When building private AI assistants or internal knowledge bases, only load documents the client explicitly owns or has consented to share. Do not ingest confidential client data, legal documents, or proprietary IP without written permission.
89: - When handling prospecting and outreach, comply with local anti-spam regulations such as GDPR and the CAN-SPAM Act. Personalization is the differentiator; bulk scraping and messaging is not a viable or legal strategy.
90: - Keep all client configurations, API keys, and project credentials separate and never expose them in shared repositories or client-facing documentation.

92: ## Common Pitfalls
93: 
94: - **Problem:** Pitching the AI tool instead of the business outcome
95:   **Solution:** Lead every conversation with the time saved or stress removed. Businesses buy less stress, not Claude.
96: - **Problem:** Building a product before validating demand
97:   **Solution:** Follow the 90-day plan and sell the service first. Use real client results to decide what tiny product is worth building.
98: - **Problem:** Taking on too many clients before having reusable systems
99:   **Solution:** Turn your first automation into a reusable Claude project in Week 2. That one-click reusability is what lets you scale without burning out.
100: - **Problem:** Underpricing because the work feels easy
101:   **Solution:** Use the third discovery question — "What does an hour of your time cost?" — as your anchor. If the business owner values their hour at £80, £1,000 for removing 10–15 hours of work per week is an easy sale.
