# Challenge Traditional Education and Reframe Cooperation as Business Preparation

## Overview

Robert Kiyosaki's Money Teaching challenges the conventional belief that formal education is the primary path to wealth. Kiyosaki argues that traditional schooling emphasizes individual competition — what he calls "survival of the fittest" thinking, where schools rank students by individual test scores and grades — which actually diminishes one's ability to get rich. He reframes behaviors that schools label as "cheating" — such as asking questions of smarter classmates rather than competing alone — as cooperative skills that prepare students for business success. He encourages learning financial intelligence independently, particularly from mentors who understand money, rather than relying solely on academic credentials.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When evaluating whether traditional education aligns with your wealth-building goals
- When working with teams and need to distinguish between harmful cheating and productive cooperation
- When the user asks about alternative paths to financial success outside of formal schooling
- When mentoring young people who feel pressured by academic performance standards
- When preparing to enter business environments where collaboration drives results

## Steps

### Step 1: Question the Value of Formal Education for Wealth Building

Examine the assumption that attending school, obtaining a degree, securing employment, and working diligently for wages will make you rich. Recognize that this path worked in the past but does not work today. Acknowledge that Kiyosaki acquired essential financial knowledge through real-world experience and mentorship from his rich dad rather than formal classroom instruction.

### Step 2: Identify Your "Rich Dad" Mentor

Find a mentor, like Kiyosaki's rich dad, who demonstrates financial success and who is willing to teach practical money skills. This person should operate with a different philosophy from traditional educators or parents who emphasize job security over wealth creation. Learn directly from their experiences, mistakes, and documented strategies.

### Step 3: Reframe "Cheating" as Cooperation

When you observe someone in school asking a smarter classmate for help rather than struggling alone, recognize this as cooperation rather than cheating. Kiyosaki notes that in business, this same behavior is called teamwork, networking, and leveraging other people's knowledge and strengths. In his experience, school punished asking others for help while business rewards it.

### Step 4: Adopt the "Survival of the Fittest" Mindset Carefully

Understand that schools often teach an individual competition mindset focused on rankings and test scores — which Kiyosaki calls "survival of the fittest" thinking. Kiyosaki found that business success comes from cooperating, forming strategic alliances, and creating mutually beneficial outcomes rather than competing individually. Shift your mindset from defeating classmates to building collaborative networks.

### Step 5: Read and Apply Knowledge from Relevant Books

Read books like "Big Potential" by Shawn Achor — which Kiyosaki is having people in his programs read — to understand how cooperation and positive environments produce better outcomes than individual competition. Apply these principles to your financial education and business practices.

### Step 6: Recognize the Link Between School Pressure and Mental Health

Be aware that, according to research cited by Harvard educator Shawn Achor in "Big Potential," excessive academic competition correlates with declining mental health among young people. According to Achor's research, depression onset age dropped from age 28 to age 14 and suicide rates are increasing at younger ages. Kiyosaki notes that this pressure not only damages mental health but also cultivates a competitive mindset that makes it harder to get rich. Prioritize collaborative learning environments and mental well-being over purely individual achievement.

### Step 7: Practice Collaborative Learning

Actively seek out smart, capable people and learn from them, as Kiyosaki did by sitting next to the smartest girl in his class and asking her questions. In school or business settings, sit next to knowledgeable individuals, ask questions, and observe their approaches. This is not cheating — it is how you accelerate your own learning and prepare yourself to do well in business.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Student Who Collaborates Instead of Competes

A college student who struggles with math sits next to a classmate who excels at it. Instead of viewing this as a competitive disadvantage, the student asks for help understanding concepts. This cooperation helps the student gain understanding. In business, this translates to seeking mentors and forming mastermind groups.

### Example 2: The Entrepreneur Who Leverages Networks

Kiyosaki advises entrepreneurs starting a business not to try to do everything alone. Instead, they should partner with people who have complementary skills — someone good at marketing, someone good at accounting, someone with industry connections. This cooperation accelerates business growth. The entrepreneur who views this as "cheating" fails to see how cooperation prepares one for business success.

### Example 3: The C Student Who Built Wealth

Robert Kiyosaki was a C student who sat next to the smartest girl in class and asked her for help. Teachers called it cheating; his rich dad called it cooperation. By learning to work with talented people and leverage their strengths, he developed the collaborative skills necessary for business success. His formal grades did not predict his financial outcomes.

## Best Practices

- Seek mentors, like Kiyosaki's rich dad, who have achieved the financial results you want
- Reframe collaboration as a strength, not a weakness or cheat
- Read books on cooperation, teamwork, and positive psychology
- Surround yourself with people who are smarter or more skilled than you
- Question assumptions about what makes someone "successful" in school versus life
- Focus on learning practical financial skills rather than chasing grades
- Build networks and alliances rather than competing individually

## Keep In Mind

- Traditional education worked in the past but does not work in today's financial environment
- Kiyosaki found that school taught him little to nothing about money, focusing instead on individual competition and test scores rather than financial intelligence, risk assessment, asset evaluation, or business decision-making skills required to build wealth.
- Kiyosaki's poor dad (traditional mindset) and rich dad (entrepreneurial mindset) had conflicting definitions of success
- In Kiyosaki's experience, asking others for help in school was punished but cooperation in business is rewarded — understand this distinction
- Mental health suffers under extreme academic competition. Achor's research in "Big Potential" shows that collaboration reduces stress and improves outcomes.
- Being a C student, like Kiyosaki, does not limit your potential for wealth if you develop practical skills and relationships

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never share actual exam answers or confidential academic materials during collaboration
- Distinguish between learning from others and violating academic integrity policies that could harm your record
- Be transparent about how you collaborate to avoid misunderstandings with educators
- Protect your financial information when seeking mentors or business partners
- Verify the credentials and track record of anyone you consider a "rich dad" mentor before following their advice
- Understand that some forms of cooperation in school may still violate rules — use judgment about when and how to collaborate

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Confusing cooperation with actual cheating (stealing answers, plagiarism, or violating clear rules).
  **Solution:** Focus on learning concepts and principles together rather than copying specific answers or work. The goal is understanding, not just grades.

- **Problem:** Dismissing all formal education as useless and failing to develop basic literacy, numeracy, or critical thinking skills.
  **Solution:** Recognize that while traditional schooling may not teach money directly, foundational skills are still valuable. Supplement formal education with practical financial learning.

- **Problem:** Waiting for a "rich dad" mentor to appear instead of taking initiative to learn independently.
  **Solution:** Seek out mentors actively through networking, books, podcasts, and communities. Take responsibility for your own financial education.

- **Problem:** Using the "cooperation not cheating" mindset to justify laziness or refusal to work independently.
  **Solution:** Kiyosaki says collaboration enhances your own effort — it does not replace it. Develop your own skills while leveraging others' strengths.

- **Problem:** Assuming that because school failed to teach money, no structured learning is valuable.
  **Solution:** Distinguish between institutional schooling and targeted financial education. Seek out specific programs, books, and courses designed to teach wealth-building skills.

## Glossary

**Cash Flow Quadrant** — A framework from Kiyosaki's book *Cashflow Quadrant* that categorizes people into four groups based on how they earn income: E (Employee), S (Self-Employed/Specialist), B (Big Business owner), and I (Professional Investor).

**Cooperation** — Working with others to learn and achieve results, which Kiyosaki frames as the same behavior called teamwork or networking in business — not the same as copying answers during a test.

**Shiny Object** — Any trendy investment that you do not fully understand and that distracts you from your chosen asset classes, causing you to stay in the E or S quadrants instead of building lasting wealth.

**Survival of the Fittest (in school context)** — The mindset schools foster by ranking students individually by grades and test scores, which Kiyosaki argues cultivates competition rather than the collaboration needed for business success.
