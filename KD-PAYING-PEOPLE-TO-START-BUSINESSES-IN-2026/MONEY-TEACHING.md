# Leveraging U.S. Government Programs to Fund and Grow Your Business in 2026

## Overview

This Money Teaching breaks down the various U.S. government programs, incentives, and financing options that entrepreneurs can use to access capital, reduce taxes, improve cash flow, and build wealth when starting or growing a business. Most entrepreneurs are unaware of these resources, yet billions of dollars in grants, tax credits, and low-interest loans are available annually. Understanding how to leverage these programs reduces personal risk and accelerates business growth.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to access capital without giving up equity to investors
- When working with a new or growing business and want to reduce taxable income
- When the user asks about grants, tax credits, SBA loans, or government-backed financing
- When buying equipment, real estate, or another business and want to maximize deductions
- When investing in a startup and want to protect capital gains

## Steps

### Step 1: Research and Claim State Economic Development Incentives

Nearly every U.S. state offers economic development incentives designed to attract and support businesses that create jobs. These incentives include tax credits, wage reimbursements, workforce training funds, and direct grants.

- Search for "[your state] economic development incentives" on Google and visit your state's economic development agency website.
- Explore specific programs such as the Texas Enterprise Fund (grants for relocating/expanding companies), North Carolina employee training grants, Georgia's Job Tax Credit and Mega Project Tax Credit, Ohio's R&D Investment Tax Credit and Jobs Ohio workforce training grants, and Louisiana's Enterprise Zone Tax Credit and LED Fast Start Training Program.
- Contact your state's economic development agency to inquire about qualifying for workforce training credits, job creation tax credits, and capital investment reimbursements.
- Understand that these programs are designed to reward legitimate job creation and business growth — they are not handouts.

### Step 2: Secure SBA-Backed Business Financing

The Small Business Administration (SBA) offers loan programs that allow founders to borrow money with lower interest rates, longer repayment terms, and reduced personal risk — all without giving up equity.

- Explore SBA 7(a) loans for general business purposes and SBA 504 loans for real estate and equipment purchases.
- Prepare a business plan, financial statements, tax returns, and a clear explanation of how the funds will be used.
- Apply through a bank or credit union, which underwrites the loan before seeking SBA approval.
- Use the financing to launch, expand, or acquire a business while preserving ownership and control.

### Step 3: Implement Tax Strategies for Founders and Early Investors

Structure your business and investments to take advantage of tax code provisions designed to reward entrepreneurship and early-stage risk-taking.

- Hold qualifying shares for at least 5 years to exclude up to 100% of capital gains under the Qualified Small Business Stock (QSBS) rule.
- Target the maximum exclusion of $15 million or 10x your initial investment, whichever is greater.
- Use Section 179 and bonus depreciation deductions to write off the full cost of qualifying equipment, machinery, technology, and even vehicles in the same year they are purchased.
- With 100% bonus depreciation restored in 2025, you can deduct over $1,000,000 in qualifying equipment purchases in a single year, significantly improving cash flow and lowering your taxable burden.
- Deduct costs for training programs, consulting services, and professional development as ordinary and necessary business expenses.

### Step 4: Explore Additional Tax Credits for Specialized Activities

Beyond general deductions, specific tax credits exist for activities that align with government policy goals.

- Claim R&D tax credits if your business creates new products, software, or technology improvements — these are not exclusive to large corporations.
- Investigate historic rehabilitation tax credits if you are restoring a qualified historic building; these credits allow businesses to receive a percentage of the renovation costs back, helping preserve historic architecture while stimulating economic activity in older neighborhoods.
- Research New Markets Tax Credits if you are investing in low-income areas that need economic development; companies that participate may receive substantial tax credits over several years, making these projects financially attractive while benefiting local communities.

### Step 5: Consider Acquiring an Existing Business with Government-Backed Financing

Buying an existing business with established revenue and employees can be a lower-risk path to entrepreneurship, especially when paired with government-backed financing.

- Use SBA-backed loans to acquire an established company with smaller down payments than conventional financing would require.
- Supplement with seller financing or outside investors as needed.
- Investigate whether your state offers additional incentives for purchasing and operating businesses in local communities.

## Examples

### Example 1: A Startup Founder in Georgia Hiring 10 Employees

A founder launching a tech company in Georgia creates 10 net new jobs in year one. Through Georgia's Job Tax Credit program, the founder earns an income tax credit per net new job created. If the company later makes a large capital investment, the Mega Project Tax Credit may also apply, further reducing tax liability and freeing up capital to reinvest in the business.

### Example 2: An Entrepreneur Buying Equipment for a Consulting Business

An entrepreneur purchases $200,000 in computers, office equipment, and a vehicle for a new consulting business. Under Section 179 and bonus depreciation, the full $200,000 can be deducted in the same tax year, significantly reducing taxable income and preserving cash flow during the critical early months of the business.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Research your state's economic development programs before launching or expanding your business
- ✅ Keep detailed records of job creation, capital investments, and eligible expenses to support incentive and credit applications
- ✅ Consult with a tax professional to ensure proper structuring for QSBS, depreciation, and R&D credits
- ✅ Apply early and follow up with state economic development agencies to stay informed about available programs
- ❌ Don't assume government programs are only for large corporations — small businesses and startups often qualify
- ❌ Don't overlook the 5-year holding period required for QSBS capital gains exclusion
- ❌ Avoid treating incentive programs as guaranteed income; they require legitimate business activity and compliance

## Keep In Mind

- Most government programs require legitimate business activity and job creation — they are designed to support real economic growth, not to provide handouts.
- Billions of dollars in incentives, credits, and funding go unclaimed each year simply because entrepreneurs don't know where to look.
- State programs vary widely; what is available in one state may not exist in another.
- SBA loans are underwritten by banks and credit unions, not issued directly by the government — building a strong relationship with a lender is important.
- Early investors and founders benefit most from QSBS when shares are held for the full required period.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never provide false or misleading information on loan applications, tax forms, or incentive program applications — fraud carries severe civil and criminal penalties.
- Protect sensitive financial and business documents used in SBA or state applications with appropriate access controls.
- Consult a qualified attorney and tax advisor before making major business or investment decisions involving government programs.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Missing the 5-year holding period for QSBS and losing the capital gains exclusion
  **Solution:** Plan exits and share transfers carefully with a tax advisor to ensure QSBS requirements are met
- **Problem:** Failing to document job creation or capital investments, making it impossible to claim state incentives
  **Solution:** Maintain organized, contemporaneous records of hires, payroll, and capital expenditures from day one
- **Problem:** Assuming eligibility for R&D or other credits without understanding qualifying criteria
  **Solution:** Review IRS guidelines and consult a tax professional to confirm which activities and expenses qualify before claiming credits
- **Problem:** Overlooking bonus depreciation limits or phase-out thresholds in future years
  **Solution:** Stay current on tax law changes and plan large equipment purchases strategically with your accountant
