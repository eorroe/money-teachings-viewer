# How to PROFIT from Market Crashes (Survive & Thrive)

## Overview

This money teaching distills how SMB Capital, a proprietary trading education firm, trains traders to identify, prepare for, and execute profitable trades during US equity market crashes and high-volatility events characterized by a 2% or more intraday S&P 500 sell-off within 3% of index highs. The core philosophy centers on disciplined risk management, rapid news interpretation, and using the right instruments (S&P 500 E-mini futures, VIX index, zero-days-to-expiration / 0 DTE options) to take a directional position on the market. The overriding rule is capital preservation first: preserve capital, honor stop-loss orders, and avoid over-leverage to remain active in markets.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When breaking news (a tariff announcement, geopolitical escalation, or macroeconomic data surprise) triggers a 2%+ intraday sell-off in the S&P 500 while the index is within 3% of its 52-week high
- When a negative headline hits on a Friday afternoon and you must decide whether to close equity or options positions before the weekend
- When you feel fear of missing out (FOMO) watching sector-specific stocks or Bitcoin and alternative cryptocurrencies with market capitalizations in the top 5 by market cap (such as Ethereum) rise at index highs and are tempted to add leverage to existing long positions
- When every stock on your watchlist is moving and you need a disciplined process to avoid chasing breakouts that lack a tradeable thesis

## Steps

### Step 1: Secure Risk Before Seeking Reward

Within 30 seconds of a negative headline, the first action is to close all open long equity positions in your trading account and reduce gross equity exposure to 50% or less of account equity, not to enter new trades. SMB's team leader frames it as: preserving your trading capital is the priority over any single trade. Stop-loss orders and a maximum-per-trade risk limit of 1% of account equity are non-negotiable, especially in a market that has been advancing with low realized volatility and complacency.

### Step 2: Interpret the News in Real Time

News interpretation must happen within 5 minutes. Read the headline, classify the catalyst as geopolitical, macroeconomic, or corporate, and assess whether the S&P 500 has room to decline to its 20-day moving average before the close. When a US President announces 100% tariffs on a major trading partner while the S&P 500 is within 1% of its 52-week high, traders recognize there is 5% to 10% of additional technical downside (use 7.5% as the midpoint for risk planning) before the close.

### Step 3: Choose the Right Instrument for the Play

If the thesis is a broad S&P 500 decline of 2% or more, do not express it through a single high-volatility (beta above 1.5) individual stock. Trade the S&P 500 E-mini futures (/ES), Nasdaq-100 E-mini futures (/NQ), or the VIX index instead. On a day with a 2%+ intraday decline and immediate downside momentum (price declining at least 0.5% per 5 minutes), zero-days-to-expiration (0 DTE) put options capture the first 30 to 60 minutes of the initial downside price move (enter at 30 minutes unless price is declining at least 1% per 5 minutes, then enter at 60 minutes). If you believe the selling will continue across sessions (e.g., into Monday after a Friday sell-off), use options expiring in 3 to 5 calendar days so you can hold overnight without assignment risk.

### Step 4: Exploit the Friday Weakness Dynamic

When a market sells off on a Friday and cannot recover at least 50% of its opening decline by the final 30 minutes of the trading session, treat that failure to recover as a reason to hold or add to short positions into the close and over the weekend. Linda Raschke's adage applies: never sell a weak market on a Friday. Traders who sold S&P 500 E-mini futures into the close or held zero-days-to-expiration (0 DTE) put options into the final 60 minutes of trading benefited from the continued price decline into the close.

### Step 5: Watch Volume and Higher-Timeframe Context

A 1-minute chart can create the illusion that you are entering a trade too late. Zoom out to the 15-minute or 60-minute timeframe. Confirm the sell-off is occurring on volume at least 1.5 times the 20-day average volume and that the S&P 500 is within 3% of its 52-week high, meaning a 5% to 10% decline to the next major support level (support level = a price level where buying pressure has previously prevented further decline, confirmed by at least two prior tests) is within the historical range for such conditions. This context turns a large-appearing single-period decline into a quantifiable opportunity with a risk-reward profile where potential upside exceeds defined risk.

### Step 6: Let Price Action Confirm, Not Opinion

Form a thesis from the news and fundamentals, but let support and resistance levels on the chart confirm entry and exit timing. If a stock or index is not declining in line with the S&P 500, do not force the trade. The price action on the chart tells you when a reversal or continuation is confirmed. Trading against the price chart because you "know" what should happen is a path to losing your entire trading account.

### Step 7: Narrow Focus and Eliminate FOMO

In a market where stocks are breaking out, monitoring more than 20 individual stocks simultaneously leads to delayed decisions and missed entries on setups that do not meet trade criteria. Maintain a tight watchlist of 10 to 15 stocks (use 12 as the default watchlist size), set price alerts at support and resistance levels, and ignore stock tips from neighbors, Twitter traders, or Discord chat rooms. Comparing your profit and loss (P&L) to other traders' results leads to poorly timed trades at the worst possible time.

### Step 8: Recognize Topping Patterns in Parabolic Moves

Sector-specific stocks such as quantum computing, robotics, rare earth minerals, battery technology, drones, nuclear energy, and artificial intelligence (AI) can top via a parabolic advance: a vertical price run of 50% or more in 10 or fewer trading days that fails to consolidate, followed by an opening gap down of 5% or more and a continued decline. That pattern is the setup to watch for short exposure, not to join the longs. Super Micro Computer (SMCI) and MicroStrategy (MSTR) both exhibited this pattern in 2024 and 2025; quantum computing stocks can exhibit the same behavior.

## Examples

### Example 1: Trading a Friday Tariff Crash in the S&P 500

On a Friday morning, the US President announces 100% tariffs on a major trading partner while the S&P 500 is within 1% of its 52-week high. Traders close all open long equity positions within minutes, classify the catalyst as geopolitical within 5 minutes, and see 5% to 10% of additional downside to the 20-day moving average before the close. They sell S&P 500 E-mini (/ES) and Nasdaq-100 E-mini (/NQ) futures first, then buy zero-days-to-expiration (0 DTE) S&P 500 put options for the first 30 to 60 minutes of downside momentum. Because it is Friday and the index cannot recover 50% of its opening decline by the final 30 minutes of the session, they hold into the close and also buy S&P 500 put options expiring in 3 to 5 calendar days to carry the position over the weekend. The S&P 500 declines 3% to 5% into the close, and the President later walks back the comments, but the options produce a substantial gain for those positioned correctly.

### Example 2: Avoiding the Crypto Leverage Trap

A trader watches acquaintances turn $10,000 into $100,000 in Bitcoin and alternative cryptocurrencies at index highs. Envy and greed push them to open 5x or greater leveraged positions on Bitcoin and altcoins at the peak. When a geopolitical tariff announcement hits, Bitcoin experiences $19 billion in leveraged long positions being force-liquidated within approximately 15 minutes. The over-leveraged trader's entire account is liquidated. The disciplined trader, who kept stop-loss orders, avoided the 5x leveraged Bitcoin ETF, and stayed in cash, preserved their capital and remains active in the markets.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always honor stop-loss orders and reduce long equity exposure by at least 50% before entering short positions in a crash
- ✅ Trade the S&P 500 E-mini futures, Nasdaq-100 E-mini futures, or the VIX index for broad market crashes, not individual high-volatility (beta above 1.5) stocks
- ✅ Use zero-days-to-expiration (0 DTE) options for the first 30 to 60 minutes of immediate momentum; use weekly options (expiring within 7 calendar days) for multi-day trend days
- ✅ Maintain a tight watchlist of 10 to 15 stocks with price alerts at support and resistance levels; ignore tips from Twitter, Reddit, and Discord trading chat rooms
- ✅ Let price action on the chart confirm your thesis before sizing up any position
- ✅ Keep your written trade plan and rules visible during volatile sessions

## Keep In Mind

- A 3% single-day decline in the S&P 500 produces a 15% to 30% loss on a 5x leveraged position (use 22.5% as the midpoint for scenario planning), which represents a severe loss when your gross equity exposure exceeds 100% of account equity
- $19 billion in leveraged Bitcoin and alternative cryptocurrency positions were force-liquidated within 15 minutes during the tariff announcement event due to widespread over-leverage
- Individual traders using their own capital (retail traders) are not automatically "dumb money"; a significant number are well-informed and tactically savvy
- Sector-specific stocks (quantum computing, robotics, rare earth minerals, battery technology, drones, nuclear energy, artificial intelligence) can change leadership over a 1 to 4 week period (use 2 weeks as the typical rotation window); a top-performing sector ETF one week can underperform the S&P 500 the following week

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never size a position based on a neighbor's reported returns or a screenshot of a social media trading account
- Avoid 5x leveraged single-stock exchange-traded funds (ETFs); a single-day decline of 20% in the underlying stock can send a 5x leveraged ETF to zero
- Do not hold leveraged positions of at least 3x over a weekend after a major negative geopolitical or macroeconomic catalyst

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Forcing a trade on a high-volatility (beta above 1.5) individual stock because the broad market view is correct but the stock is declining less than the S&P 500
  **Solution:** If it is a market-level thesis, sell S&P 500 E-mini futures or buy VIX index calls first; add individual stock positions only if they confirm by declining more than the index
- **Problem:** Buying zero-days-to-expiration (0 DTE) options after the first 30 to 60 minutes of downside momentum have passed and the trade has become opportunistic
  **Solution:** Size 0 DTE options for the initial downside price move only; if you want to hold a position overnight or over the weekend, use weekly options (expiring within 7 calendar days)
- **Problem:** Comparing your profit and loss (P&L) to other traders' results and changing your trade plan after the market has moved against your position by a meaningful amount
  **Solution:** Write your trade plan, stop-loss rules, and maximum position size down before the market opens; stick to them regardless of others' reported gains or losses
- **Problem:** Selling a weak market on a Friday because you believe it is oversold according to the RSI indicator below 30
  **Solution:** Never sell a weak market on a Friday when it has not recovered at least 50% of its opening decline by the final 30 minutes of the session; late-day weakness going into the weekend is likely to persist
- **Problem:** Holding into a crash without stop-loss orders because you believe the stock "cannot go down"
  **Solution:** Stop-loss orders are mandatory on every position; using leverage above 3x without stop-loss orders is what turns a 3% single-day decline into a complete loss of account equity
