# Beginner's Guide: The 4-Step Swing Trading Process

## Money Teaching Title

Beginner's Guide: The 4-Step Swing Trading Process

## Overview

This Money Teaching outlines the four-step framework that SMB Capital uses to help active day traders transition into swing trading. It teaches you how to find swing candidates among day trade setups you already trade, build confidence holding overnight with small position sizes, master specific swing trading setups, and apply a rule-based trade management system. The goal is to help traders who already know how to enter setups scale their results by capturing multi-day moves without learning an entirely new playbook.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are an active day trader looking to expand into swing trading without starting from scratch
- When you want to review past winning trades to identify setups that could have been held longer
- When you need to keep your overnight risk at a level you have explicitly decided is acceptable before entering any swing trade
- When you need a systematic approach to managing multi-day swing trade exits and stops

## Steps

### Step 1: Review Your Biggest Winners

Look back at your 10 largest dollar winners across both day trades and swing trades. Ask what specific conditions made them successful, and whether those conditions could have supported a multi-day hold. Pay attention to trades that had a catalyst that produced a price move of at least 5% on the announcement day with above-average volume (such as an earnings beat, product announcement, or sector rotation), a weekly chart breakout as the higher-timeframe confirmation, or a fundamental event that could have driven continued momentum after day one. This helps you find your strongest swing candidates because you already know how to enter these setups; you only need to learn how to manage them across days.

### Step 2: Get Reps With Small Size

Do not jump to full position sizes immediately. Hold a small core of your existing day trades overnight — 5 to 10 shares to start — so you can experience overnight price gaps and intraday volatility firsthand before increasing exposure. A recommended approach is to increase your overnight core by 10 shares per week, or after you have logged 5 consecutive successful overnight holds, whichever comes first. Determine your Momentum to Core (MCR) ratio before the market close on day one: decide what percentage of the position you will sell into strength on day one versus what portion you will hold as a swing core. Writing this predetermined split down before the trading day ends removes emotional decision-making and impulsive selling driven by fear of missing out.

### Step 3: Learn Your Setups

Swing trading is a style, not a single strategy. Start with one setup, back test it, and master it before expanding. Key setups include:

- **Mean Reversion / Backside Short**: Identify a stock whose price has moved at least 5% above its 5-day simple moving average (SMA) while the daily close is also above the 10-day, 20-day, and 50-day SMAs. A move of at least 5% above the 5-day SMA signals a short-term overextended condition and potential mean reversion. Confirm with two or three consecutive days of volume at least 20% above the stock's 20-day average daily volume, showing that institutional participation drove the move.
- **Consolidation Breakout**: Focus on stocks in one of the top 3 performing sectors (measured by the relative strength rank of Sector exchange-traded funds (ETFs) over the past 1 month) with a clear fundamental catalyst such as an earnings beat, product launch, or regulatory approval. Look for a price range that is 3% or less of the stock's price over the prior 2 to 4 weeks, with the 10-day and 20-day moving averages converging within 1% of each other, forming a base where the linear regression slope of price over the consolidation period is between -0.5% and +0.5% per week. When the stock breaks above the high of that range on volume at least 50% above its 20-day average daily volume, a breakout from this tight range can produce at least a 10% multi-week move. Confirm the stock is not already more than 50% above its 200-day moving average before entering; beyond that level, the risk of a sharp pullback rises.
- **Continuation Trade**: After a stock has broken out of a consolidation and is trading above its rising 10-day or 20-day moving average, enter on a pullback that taps or slightly penetrates that moving average. Confirm a pattern of consecutively higher swing lows on the daily chart, confirmed before entry, by requiring the stock to reclaim the prior day's high (for a 1-day continuation) or the prior week's high (for a multi-week continuation) within 1 to 2 trading days of entry. Hold the position across days to weeks depending on whether the stock remains above its 10-day SMA on a closing basis.

### Step 4: Have a System to Manage the Trades

Define your exit rules before entering the trade. For mid-cap stocks (market capitalization between $2 billion and $10 billion) and large-cap stocks (above $10 billion), trail your stop loss against the prior day's closing low or the 20-day SMA, whichever is furthest from your entry price — choose whichever of those three levels provides the widest stop that is still closest to your entry price as your initial stop. For small-cap stocks (below $2 billion), trail against the 3-day volume-weighted average price (VWAP) or the most recent swing low that is at least 2% below the current price. If no such swing low exists within the prior 20 trading days, use the 3-day VWAP as the stop level. As the S&P 500 or your benchmark index extends more than 10% above its 200-day moving average and individual stocks have extended more than 50% above their 200-day moving average, tighten your stop to 1-day's prior low or the 5-day SMA, whichever is tighter. Cut losing positions on breakouts in an extended market even if the price is still above the original consolidation low. Assess the market environment before placing any swing trade by evaluating: (1) market breadth, measured as the number of advancing stocks minus declining stocks on the New York Stock Exchange (NYSE) over the prior 5 days; (2) risk appetite, confirmed by offensive sectors (technology, consumer discretionary) outperforming defensive sectors (utilities, consumer staples) over the past month; and (3) breakout success rate, measured as the percentage of stocks breaking out of a 3-week consolidation that gain at least 5% within the following 10 trading days.

## Examples

### Example 1: ARM Consolidation Breakout

ARM broke out of a two-year weekly base in May 2024 after a ceasefire-driven catalyst in the semiconductor sector. It then consolidated for three weeks in a price range of 2% or less, with the 10-day, 20-day, and 100-day moving averages converging within 1% of each other. On the breakout day, the stock gapped 2% above the prior range high and closed above it on volume 60% above its 20-day average daily volume. A trader following the consolidation breakout setup could enter with a starter size equal to 20% of the planned full position mid-range, add another 40% on the confirmed breakout above the range high, and trail the stop against the prior day's low or the 10-day exponential moving average (EMA) for a multi-week swing.

### Example 2: Nvidia Continuation Trade

After Nvidia reported Q1 2024 earnings that beat consensus estimates by more than 10% on both revenue and earnings per share (EPS), a trader identified it as the strongest name in the market on day two of the reaction. It reclaimed its all-time high and closed above it. The trader watched for a morning pullback to $170 — near the prior day's low and the 10-day moving average. When the stock reclaimed that level within 90 minutes of the open, it served as a tactical entry for a multi-day momentum continuation trade, with a stop placed 1% below the prior day's low. This setup requires confidence that the broader market is supporting the stock's move and recognition that the stock is acting with relative strength compared to the QQQ.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Review your own past trades over the prior 6 months to find swing candidates from setups you already know how to enter, before learning entirely new strategies
- ✅ Hold a small overnight core (5 to 10 shares to start) to bridge the gap between day trading and swing trading
- ✅ Write down your predetermined MCR ratio before the market close on day one to prevent emotional decision-making under pressure
- ✅ Master one swing setup, backtest it across at least 40 historical examples, and only add a new strategy once you have consistent results over at least 10 live trades
- ✅ Assess the market environment before every swing trade using the three metrics: 5-day NYSE advancing-minus-declining stocks, 1-month sector relative strength rank, and 10-day breakout success rate
- ✅ Use an initial stop that is the prior day's closing low or the 20-day SMA, whichever is furthest from your entry price and tighten it only when the benchmark index is more than 10% above its 200-day moving average

## Keep In Mind

- Swing trading is a distinct trading style, not a specific strategy; you still need a clearly defined entry, stop, and exit for every trade
- Mean reversion, consolidation breakout, and continuation trades each have different expected win rates and average profit-per-win profiles — do not apply the same position size or stop distance to all three
- The market environment directly controls how wide or tight your entry and exit levels should be: in a bull market with strong breadth is confirmed when the 5-day NYSE A/D total is above +500, use wider stops and target 3% to 5% gains; in an extended market where the S&P 500 is more than 10% above its 200-day moving average, use tighter stops and take profits at 1% to 2%
- Being early in a strong trend (S&P 500 within 5% of its 52-week high and above its 200-day SMA) favors wider initial stops and patience; being late in an extended market (S&P 500 more than 10% above its 200-day SMA with stocks extended more than 50%) favors tighter stops and faster profit-taking

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use borrowed capital or margin accounts during your first 20 swing trades; the combination of overnight price gaps and leverage can produce losses that exceed your intended risk on a single trade
- Before entering any swing trade, calculate your maximum dollar loss per share (entry price minus stop-loss price, multiplied by share count) and write it down. Never move your stop-loss price further away from your entry because you are emotionally hoping the trade will recover
- If a stock gaps more than 3% against your position at the market open, do not automatically hold. First evaluate whether the original thesis — the catalyst, sector trend, or technical pattern — is still intact. A catalyst is broken if the news is reversed or contradicted within 24 hours; a sector trend is broken if the sector ETF closes below its 10-day SMA; a technical pattern is broken if price closes below the pattern low on above-average volume. Exit immediately if the thesis is broken, regardless of the unrealized loss

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Selling your entire position on day one of a winning trade because you are uncomfortable holding the position overnight, leaving potential multi-day gains unrealized
  **Solution:** Write down your MCR ratio before the market close on day one so you know in advance exactly what portion of the position you are permitted to hold overnight, removing the need to make an emotional decision at the close
- **Problem:** Attempting to trade all three swing setups (mean reversion, consolidation breakout, continuation trade) simultaneously before mastering any of them
  **Solution:** Select one setup, backtest it across at least 40 historical examples, trade it live for a minimum of 10 positions, and only add a second setup once your win rate and average profit-per-win exceed those of your first setup over at least 10 additional live trades
- **Problem:** Exiting a swing trade at a small loss or small profit because the stock pulled back 1% to 2% intraday — a pullback of 1%–3% that holds above the 10-day SMA in a trend where the stock remains above its 20-day SMA on a closing basis — instead of waiting for the stop level to be hit
  **Solution:** Set your initial stop at the prior day's closing low or the 20-day SMA, whichever is furthest from your entry price. Only tighten the stop if the benchmark index moves more than 10% above its 200-day SMA or if the stock fails to reclaim its 10-day SMA within 2 consecutive days
- **Problem:** Entering a breakout trade in a stock that is the weakest performer in its sector, or in a sector that has ranked in the bottom 3 of all sectors over the past month
  **Solution:** Before entering any breakout, confirm that the stock's sector ranks in the top 5 sectors by 1-month relative strength, the stock is outperforming its sector ETF, and there is a specific catalyst or fundamental narrative (such as an earnings beat, product announcement, or regulatory approval) supporting the price move
