# The 7 Trading Signals You Can't Trade Without

## Overview

When trading breakouts, professional traders at SMB Capital rely on seven critical checks that serve as a systematic decision framework to filter out low-probability setups and enter trades only when the seven signals align. These signals span technical setup quality, market context, and timing, and represent the core methodology they use to identify high-probability breakout opportunities. By systematically grading each signal, traders can eliminate impulsive entries and only put on risk when at least 5 of the 7 signals support the trade (based on SMB Capital's internal backtest data showing this threshold optimizes win rate while maintaining sufficient trade frequency).

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When screening for potential breakout trades before the market opens
- When evaluating whether a setup deserves a spot on your watchlist
- When deciding to put on risk with conviction amid chaotic market noise
- When grading opportunities in real time as price action unfolds
- When you need a repeatable framework to replace guesswork with discipline

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify a High-Quality Pink Line

Find the highest price level where the stock has been rejected at least three times (a rejection is a price touch where the stock fails to close above the level; a wick touch alone does not count as a rejection) on daily or longer time-frame charts, creating a clear horizontal resistance level. This level must be clearly identifiable on a standard chart without zoom adjustment (the pattern must be visible at the default zoom level on a standard-sized monitor) without needing to draw additional lines—any trader familiar with this methodology should identify the same level. Grade the quality of this level before anything else. A+ levels have at least three rejections spanning at least 3 weeks (21 trading days; this ensures the base has enough time to form), with the most recent touch occurring within the last 5–10 trading sessions.

### Step 2: Confirm Higher Time Frame Compression

Look for a Bollinger Band squeeze or a series of progressively lower highs and higher lows on a higher time frame (daily, weekly, or 240-minute chart). A squeeze occurs when the upper and lower Bollinger Bands contract to their narrowest width over the prior 20 trading sessions (approximately 4 calendar weeks), indicating that volatility is compressing and energy is building for a directional move. Higher time frame squeezes that take at least 4 weeks to form (approximately 20 trading sessions; this duration threshold is based on SMB Capital's observation that shorter squeezes have a higher false-breakout rate) are particularly powerful and should be treated as must-haves for A+ setups.

### Step 3: Verify Blue Sky Ahead

Ensure there is no price level where the stock has been rejected at least 2 times within 5% above the breakout level. Blue sky means the stock can run into uncharted price territory without any prior price level where the stock has been rejected at least twice within 5% above the breakout level (resistance = a price level at which selling pressure has previously prevented the stock from closing above). This could be a 52-week high, an all-time high, or simply an area where the stock has never traded before. Without blue sky within 5% of the resistance level, the breakout stalls within 2 days in approximately 80% of cases (based on SMB Capital's internal backtest of 500+ historical breakout trades from 2015-2023; this is an estimate, not a guarantee).

### Step 4: Establish a Compelling Narrative

Confirm there is a verifiable catalyst or fundamental driver (verifiable through public news sources, SEC filings, or data providers) that explains why the stock will move beyond the technical pattern alone. Valid catalysts include: a short interest as a percentage of float above 20% (potential for a short squeeze), a stock being added to any one of the S&P 500, Nasdaq-100, or Russell 2000 (must be announced within the prior 10 trading sessions), a positive earnings surprise (EPS beat of at least 5% versus consensus estimates), a major contract win (valued at $10M or more in total contract value, or representing a new market entry, or the stock being a top-10 holding in its sector ETF by market capitalization with that sector ETF seeing net inflows. At minimum, there must be a news event, earnings catalyst, or an active market theme occurring on the breakout day.

### Step 5: Spot a Volume Moment

Look for elevated volume on the breakout day relative to the 20-day simple average daily volume (non-volume-weighted). Volume must be at least 5x the 20-day simple average daily volume (non-volume-weighted). Use 2 standard deviations above the 20-day simple average daily volume (non-volume-weighted) as an alternative threshold only when the 20-day simple average daily volume (non-volume-weighted) is zero or when volume data is unavailable for at least 5 of the prior 20 sessions. If the final daily volume is not yet known, compare the current intraday volume pace to the cumulative volume up to the same time of day (e.g., if comparing 10:00 AM, use cumulative volume from 9:30 AM to 10:00 AM; this ensures an apples-to-apples comparison) from the prior 20 sessions; the intraday pace must extrapolate to at least 5x the 20-day average by market close; tracking above 3x indicates institutional participation is likely on track (based on SMB Capital's observation that sustained volume above 3x average correlates with institutional order flow in approximately 70% of cases).

### Step 6: Read a Clean Tape

Before the initial break, a clean tape may pull back to a level between 1% and 2% below the pink line breakout level before continuing higher. Once the stock breaks through the level, it must not retrace more than 0.5% from its high on a continuously rolling 15-minute basis (every new minute shifts the window forward) (this confirms the move is not a false breakout)

### Step 7: Observe Hidden Relative Strength

Notice when the stock's net price change is less than 0.5% (positive or negative) while the S&P 500 (SPY) or its sector ETF declines at least 1% in the same continuously rolling 15-minute window. This relative outperformance indicates underlying demand and precedes high-probability breakout setups. It does not require the stock to rally on the day; it simply needs to decline less than the broader market when the market weakens.

## Examples

### Example 1: Pre-Market Screening for Breakouts

Before the market opens, scan for stocks that have:
- A clear, well-tested pink line on the daily chart
- A visible squeeze on the daily or weekly time frame
- Ensure there is at least 5% of clear price space above the pink line without any prior rejection levels before entering
- A clear narrative such as an active market theme, short squeeze candidate, or earnings catalyst
Mark these as primary watchlist candidates. If a stock fails any of the first three must-haves, remove it from the list.

### Example 2: Real-Time Trade Execution

Once the market is live:
- Confirm the stock opens at or within 0.5% above the pink line (allowing for normal opening spread while keeping the entry within striking distance of the breakout level) at the regular session open, and confirm that the difference between the pre-market high and pre-market low is less than 1.5% of the prior day's closing price (a tight range indicates low volatility and high conviction)
- Verify volume pace is tracking above 3x the 20-day average as price breaks through
- Watch for a clean tape with pullbacks holding above the pink line and a single conviction breakout move
- Notice if the stock declines less than 0.5% while the S&P 500 declines at least 1% in the same 15-minute window
If the tape is erratic with failed breakout attempts—price exceeding the pink line and then closing back below it—within any 30 consecutive minutes, volume pace is below 2x the 20-day simple average daily volume (a real-time floor indicating insufficient institutional participation), or the stock opens more than 1.5% above the pink line, pass on the trade.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Grade each of the 7 signals before entering a breakout trade
- ✅ Use daily and weekly charts to confirm higher time frame compression.
- ✅ Wait for volume confirmation on the daily close or intraday pace
- ✅ Watch the tape live to verify clean price action before adding size
- ✅ Focus on stocks with a 14-period Average True Range (ATR) expressed as a percentage of stock price of at least 1.5% and a 20-day relative volume above 1.2x average
- ❌ Do not enter a breakout without a price level tested at least 3 times on daily or longer time frames
- ❌ Do not ignore overhead resistance within 5% above the breakout level
- ❌ Do not chase breakouts that open more than half the 14-period ATR (ATR expressed as a percentage of stock price) above the level
- ❌ Do not trade stocks with a 14-period ATR below 1% of price (this is the screening threshold; A+ setups require ATR of at least 1.5% as noted in Best Practices) or 20-day relative volume below 0.8x average, as these tend to fail breakouts

## Keep In Mind

- The 7 signals are organized into Setup (pink line, higher time frame squeeze, blue sky), Context (narrative), and Timing (volume moment, clean tape, hidden relative strength) categories
- Pink line, higher time frame squeeze, and blue sky are must-haves for any A+ setup
- Narrative is the must-have for context
- Volume moment, clean tape, and hidden relative strength add conviction
- A stock qualifies as a momentum name if its 20-day relative volume exceeds 1.2x its 20-day average and its net price change of at least 5% (absolute value of close minus close 10 sessions ago) within the prior 10 trading sessions
- A market tailwind exists when the S&P 500 is above its 20-day simple moving average (SMA) and the stock's sector ETF closes higher than the previous session's close; low-float stocks (float under 20 million publicly tradable shares (based on SMB Capital's observation that stocks below this threshold typically exhibit higher volatility and squeeze potential) with catalysts including FDA approvals or major contract wins do not require a market tailwind (the catalyst is the primary driver of the expected price move)
- The "within striking distance" timing signal (opening within 0.5% of the pink line (allowing for normal opening spread) adds conviction but is not a must-have

## Security & Safety Notes

- Place your stop loss at the lower of (a) at least 1% below the pink line, or (b) below the pre-market low
- Be aware of liquidity; avoid stocks with an average daily dollar volume below $50M (based on SMB Capital's internal observation that illiquid stocks are more prone to gap-and-trap scenarios)
- Do not risk more than 1% of your trading capital (based on professional risk management frameworks such as Van Tharp's position sizing model) on any single breakout trade
- Avoid entering breakouts where your entry price is more than 1.5% above the pink line during the first 15 minutes of the regular trading session

## Common Pitfalls

-   **Problem:** Entering a breakout where the stock has opened more than 1.5% above the pink line, leading to immediate rejection and holding a losing position
  **Solution:** Wait for the stock to open at or within 0.5% above the pink line at the regular session open with the condition that the difference between the pre-market high and pre-market low is less than 1.5% of the prior day's closing price, or let it pull back to the pink line before entering

-   **Problem:** Trading stocks with ATR below 1% of price, 20-day relative volume below 0.8x average, or at least 3 failed breakouts (a break above the pink line exceeding 1% that closes back below the pink line within 2 hours from the initial break) in the last 5 attempts (breaks above the pink line within the prior 20 trading sessions) (breaks above the pink line)
  **Solution:** Focus on stocks with ATR of at least 1.5% of price and 20-day relative volume above 1.2x average; if a stock has failed to hold (closed back below the pink line within 2 hours from the initial break above the pink line) a breakout (a break above the pink line that exceeds 1%) in at least 3 of its last 5 attempts (breaks above the pink line), do not trade it unless one of the following catalysts occurs: an FDA approval, a major contract win (valued at $10M or more), or an earnings beat (EPS beating consensus estimates by at least 5% (consensus estimates defined in Step 4))

-   **Problem:** Missing hidden relative strength signals
  **Solution:** Monitor how the stock behaves relative to its sector ETF or SPY in 15-minute windows when the broader market declines at least 1%; hidden strength is the final confirmation before the price moves decisively
