# The 7 Trading Signals You Can't Trade Without

## Overview

When trading breakouts, professional traders at SMB Capital rely on seven critical checks that serve as a systematic decision framework to filter out low-probability setups and enter trades only when the seven signals align. These signals span technical setup quality, market context, and timing, and represent the core methodology they use to identify high-probability breakout opportunities. By systematically grading each signal, traders can eliminate impulsive entries and only put on risk when the evidence supports it.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When screening for potential breakout trades before the market opens
- When evaluating whether a setup deserves a spot on your watchlist
- When deciding to put on risk with conviction amid chaotic market noise
- When grading opportunities in real time as price action unfolds
- When you need a repeatable framework to replace guesswork with discipline

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify a High-Quality Pink Line

Find the highest price level where the stock has been rejected at least three times on daily or longer time-frame charts, creating a clear horizontal resistance level. This level must be obvious on the chart without needing to draw additional lines—any trader looking at the chart should identify the same level. Grade the quality of this level before anything else. A+ levels have at least three touch points spanning weeks or months, with the most recent touch occurring within the last 5–10 trading sessions.

### Step 2: Confirm Higher Time Frame Compression

Look for a Bollinger Band squeeze or a series of progressively lower highs and higher lows on a higher time frame (daily, weekly, or 240-minute chart). A squeeze occurs when the upper and lower Bollinger Bands contract to their narrowest width in at least 20 periods, indicating that volatility is compressing and energy is building for a directional move. Higher time frame squeezes that take at least 4 weeks to form are particularly powerful and should be treated as must-haves for A+ setups.

### Step 3: Verify Blue Sky Ahead

Ensure there is no price level where the stock has been rejected at least 2 times within 5% above the breakout level. Blue sky means the stock can run into uncharted price territory without immediately hitting a known supply zone. This could be a 52-week high, an all-time high, or simply an area where the stock has never traded before. Without blue sky within 5%, the breakout is likely to stall quickly as sellers emerge at the familiar resistance zone.

### Step 4: Establish a Compelling Narrative

Confirm there is a verifiable catalyst or fundamental driver that explains why the stock will move beyond the technical pattern alone. Valid catalysts include: a short interest ratio above 20% (potential squeeze), a stock being added to a widely-tracked index, a positive earnings surprise with raised guidance, an FDA approval or major contract win, or the stock being a primary holding in a sector ETF seeing inflows. At minimum, there must be a news event, earnings catalyst, or thematic tailwind that occurred within the last 2 weeks. Technical patterns without catalysts fail.

### Step 5: Spot a Volume Moment

Look for elevated volume on the breakout day relative to the 20-day average daily volume. Volume must be at least 5x the 20-day average daily volume, or at least 2 standard deviations above the 20-day average. If the final daily volume is not yet known, compare the current intraday volume pace to the same time-of-day volume from the prior 20 sessions; the pace must be on track to exceed 3x the 20-day average by market close to confirm institutional participation.

### Step 6: Read a Clean Tape

Watch the intraday price action for orderliness. A clean tape pulls back to the prior resistance level (now support) within 1–2% before continuing higher, holds pullbacks without breaking the prior day's low, and breaks through the level in a single sustained move. A messy tape reverses below the level, shows erratic 2–3% wicks in both directions within a 10-minute window, and never holds support. Clean tape is easier to read and increases confidence in the move.

### Step 7: Observe Hidden Relative Strength

Notice when the stock declines less than 0.5% while the S&P 500 (SPY) or its sector ETF declines 1% or more in the same 15-minute window. This relative outperformance of at least 1.5 percentage points indicates underlying demand and precedes breakouts. It does not require the stock to rally on the day; it simply needs to decline less than the broader market when the market weakens.

## Examples

### Example 1: Pre-Market Screening for Breakouts

Before the market opens, scan for stocks that have:
- A clear, well-tested pink line on the daily chart
- A visible squeeze on the daily or weekly time frame
- No resistance within 5% above the breakout level
- A clear narrative such as an active market theme, short squeeze candidate, or earnings catalyst
Mark these as primary watchlist candidates. If a stock fails any of the first three must-haves, remove it from the list.

### Example 2: Real-Time Trade Execution

Once the market is live:
- Confirm the stock is opening at or within 0.5% above the pink line with a pre-market high/low range that spans less than 1.5% of the prior day's close
- Verify volume pace is tracking above 3x the 20-day average as price breaks through
- Watch for a clean tape with pullbacks holding above the pink line and a single conviction breakout move
- Notice if the stock declines less than 0.5% while the S&P 500 declines 1% or more in the same 15-minute window
If the tape is erratic with failed breakout attempts within a 30-minute window, volume pace is below 2x the 20-day average, or the stock opens more than 1.5% above the pink line, pass on the trade.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Grade each of the 7 signals before entering a breakout trade
- ✅ Use daily and weekly charts to confirm higher time frame compression
- ✅ Wait for volume confirmation on the daily close or intraday pace
- ✅ Watch the tape live to verify clean price action before adding size
- ✅ Focus on stocks with an Average True Range (ATR) of at least 1.5% of the stock price and a 20-day relative volume above 1.2x average
- ❌ Do not enter a breakout without a price level tested at least 3 times on daily or longer time frames
- ❌ Do not ignore overhead resistance within 5% above the breakout level
- ❌ Do not chase breakouts that open more than half the 14-day Average True Range (ATR) above the level
- ❌ Do not trade stocks with an ATR below 1% of price or 20-day relative volume below 0.8x average, as these tend to fail breakouts

## Keep In Mind

- The 7 signals are organized into Setup (pink line, higher time frame squeeze, blue sky), Context (narrative), and Timing (volume moment, clean tape, hidden relative strength) categories
- Pink line, higher time frame squeeze, and blue sky are must-haves for any A+ setup
- Narrative is the must-have for context
- Volume moment, clean tape, and hidden relative strength add conviction
- A stock qualifies as a momentum name if its 20-day relative volume exceeds 1.2x its 60-day average and its price has moved at least 5% in either direction within the prior 10 sessions
- A market tailwind exists when the S&P 500 is above its 20-day moving average and the stock's sector ETF is up on the day; low-float stocks (under 20M shares) with strong standalone catalysts may not require a market tailwind
- The "within striking distance" timing signal (opening within 0.5% of the pink line) adds conviction but is not a must-have

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always place a stop loss at least 1% below the pink line or below the pre-market low, whichever is lower
- Be aware of liquidity; avoid stocks with an average daily dollar volume below $50M that can gap and trap traders
- Do not risk more than 1% of your trading capital on any single breakout trade
- Be cautious of breakouts that occur on the opening drive more than 1.5% above the pink line, as they fail back down within the first hour

## Common Pitfalls

-   **Problem:** Entering a breakout where the stock has opened more than 1.5% above the pink line, leading to immediate rejection and holding a losing position
  **Solution:** Wait for the stock to open at or within 0.5% above the pink line with a pre-market high/low range spanning less than 1.5% of the prior day's close, or let it pull back to the pink line before entering

-   **Problem:** Trading stocks with ATR below 1% of price, 20-day relative volume below 0.8x average, or at least 3 failed breakouts in the last 5 attempts
  **Solution:** Focus on stocks with ATR of at least 1.5% of price and 20-day relative volume above 1.2x average; if a stock has failed to hold a breakout in at least 3 of its last 5 attempts, do not trade it unless a catalyst such as an FDA approval, major contract, or earnings beat occurs

-   **Problem:** Missing hidden relative strength signals
  **Solution:** Monitor how the stock behaves relative to its sector ETF or SPY in 15-minute windows when the broader market declines 1% or more; hidden strength is the final confirmation before the price moves decisively
