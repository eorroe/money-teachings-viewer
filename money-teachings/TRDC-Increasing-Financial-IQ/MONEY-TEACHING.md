# Increasing Your Financial IQ

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills the core financial education principles discussed by Donald Trump and Robert Kiyosaki on increasing your Financial IQ. It explains why traditional financial advice like "get out of debt" and "live below your means" can be harmful without proper context, and how financial intelligence determines whether an asset truly creates wealth. The goal is to shift your mindset from trading time for money to understanding how money works so you can make informed investment decisions.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to evaluate whether an investment is truly an asset or a liability
- When working with debt, real estate, or any investment vehicle
- When the user asks how to build wealth beyond traditional savings and conventional advice
- When you want to understand why smart people still lose money in "safe" investments

## Steps

### Step 1: Redefine What an Asset Is

Understand that your primary residence is not an asset—it is a liability. When you cannot make your house payments, it becomes your biggest financial burden. An asset puts money in your pocket whether you work or not; a liability takes money out. Apply this definition consistently to everything you own or are considering buying.

### Step 2: Use Debt Strategically, Not Blindly

Debt is a two-edged sword. It can make you richer or wipe you out. The key difference is financial education. Do not follow blanket advice to "get out of debt"—instead, learn to use good debt to acquire cash-flowing assets like real estate. The more debt you use, the smarter you must become about evaluating deals, interest rates, and cash flow.

### Step 3: Expand Your Means Instead of Living Below Them

"Live below your means" kills your spirit and limits your growth. Rather than restricting your lifestyle, invest in financial education so you can expand your means without taking on excessive debt. Feed your brain—your mind is your greatest asset. Use inspiring goals and role models to build confidence and keep score in ways that motivate you to improve.

### Step 4: Achieve Goals First, Then Decide If You Still Want Them

Build the capability to acquire the things you want—luxury homes, investments, experiences—then decide if you still want them. The pain is not in having; the pain is in wanting something you cannot afford. Achieve it, and you can take it or leave it from a position of strength.

### Step 5: Develop Your Financial IQ to Evaluate Investments

Financial intelligence determines whether something is valuable or not. You can lose money in gold, real estate, stocks, and oil—and you can make money in all of them too. Without a high Financial IQ, you cannot tell a good investment from a bad one, or good advice from bad advice. Study cash flow statements, learn the language of money, and practice evaluating deals before committing capital.

## Examples

### Example 1: Evaluating a Home Purchase

Before buying a house, calculate the monthly cash flow. If the property does not generate positive cash flow after mortgage, taxes, insurance, and maintenance, treat it as a liability. Consider using bank debt (good debt) to acquire a rental property that pays you monthly, rather than draining your savings for a personal residence that costs you money.

### Example 2: Responding to Market Downturns

When conservative stocks drop 50% in value, do not assume the asset class itself is bad. A person with high Financial IQ sees the opportunity; a person with low Financial IQ sees only loss. Use downturns to practice evaluating whether the underlying business still has strong cash flow, and whether the price now reflects a margin of safety.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study financial statements (income statement, balance sheet, cash flow) as your report card
- ✅ Feed your brain daily with financial education through books, mentors, and real deal analysis
- ✅ Use good debt to acquire cash-flowing assets like income-producing real estate
- ✅ Treat money as a language—learn to speak it fluently through practice and experience
- ❌ Do not accept "live below your means" as a permanent strategy without building financial intelligence first
- ❌ Do not assume your house is automatically a good investment just because home prices have risen historically
- ❌ Do not take investment advice from people who lack direct experience in the asset class they are recommending

## Keep In Mind

- Financial IQ is measured by your ability to solve financial problems in dollars, not by how much money you currently have
- The rich use debt to get richer; without financial education, debt makes the poor and middle class poorer
- Money is a language. Learning to be rich is like learning a foreign language—it takes time, practice, and dedication
- Your greatest asset is your mind—choose teachers wisely and continue learning throughout your life
- Achieve it first, then decide if you still want it. The spirit is killed by restriction, not by responsible ambition

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never invest based on hot tips from unqualified sources, especially those within the employee quadrant who lack real investing experience
- Verify the track record of anyone giving financial advice—look for someone who has successfully done what they are teaching
- Be aware that many financial advisors operate on a "sell" model rather than a "teach" model; prioritize education over product recommendations
- Understand the tax implications and legal structure of any investment before committing significant capital

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a house and calling it an asset without understanding cash flow or opportunity cost
  **Solution:** Calculate true cash flow after all expenses. If the property does not pay you monthly, classify it correctly as a liability and factor that into your wealth-building plan.
- **Problem:** Following the "live below your means" mantra without expanding financial knowledge, leading to a scarcity mindset
  **Solution:** Replace restriction with education. Invest time and money into learning how to generate more income through assets rather than simply cutting expenses.
- **Problem:** Losing money in conservative stocks and blaming the asset class instead of the lack of financial intelligence
  **Solution:** Recognize that any investment can lose money. Focus on building Financial IQ so you can distinguish between a temporary price drop and a permanently impaired asset.
- **Problem:** Taking investment advice from people who have never successfully invested in the asset class they are recommending
  **Solution:** Seek mentors and teachers who have verifiable results in the specific investment type you are studying. Prioritize experience over credentials.
