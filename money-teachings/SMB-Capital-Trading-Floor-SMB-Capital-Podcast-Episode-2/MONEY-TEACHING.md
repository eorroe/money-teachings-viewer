# How to Trade a Day-One Biotech Catalyst Breakout Like SMB Capital

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills SMB Capital's approach to trading day-one biotech catalyst stocks. The core behavior is to identify biotech names in the pre-market that are trading at at least 5x of their average daily volume (ADV), confirm the catalyst by comparing the company's projected revenue to its current market cap using comparable public biotech valuations, then enter on a breakout above a consolidation range that is no more than 1% of the stock price, with a risk of no more than 0.5% of trading capital per trade. The intent is to let setups offering at least a 2.5:1 risk-reward ratio present themselves rather than forcing trades, using volume, price action, and valuation context to determine when to deploy capital.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When a biotech stock gaps up at least 100% on day-one news
- When the stock shows at least 5x its average daily volume in the pre-market
- When you want to trade catalyst-driven momentum with a stop placed 0.5% to 1% below the entry level
- When you need to determine if the catalyst is supported by comparing the company's potential revenue to its current market cap using comparable public biotech valuations, rather than relying on news headlines alone
- When deciding whether to hold an intraday breakout position overnight as a swing trade

## Steps

### Step 1: Scan for Pre-Market Volume Surges

Use a pre-market volume scanner that compares current pre-market volume to average daily volume (ADV). Look for stocks doing at least 20% of their ADV, with at least 5x being exceptional. High pre-market volume signals the stock is "in play" and will likely be liquid when the market opens.

### Step 2: Verify the Catalyst Through Valuation Analysis

Read the full press release or FDA document, not just the headline. For biotechs, determine if the therapy treats a condition with no existing approved treatments (an unmet medical need) and if the target patient population is at least 100,000 people to justify the valuation. Use the 10-K filings or clinical trial data from at least one comparable biotech company to estimate revenue potential per patient. If the calculated potential revenue divided by the share count shows the stock price is below the revenue-based valuation even after a 200% gap, the catalyst has real conviction behind it.

### Step 3: Let the Trade Come to You

Observe the stock in the pre-market and the first 30 minutes of the regular session without entering. Let it establish a range where the high and low are within 1% of each other for at least 10 minutes. You want to see the stock price stay above its opening price (hold the gap), reclaim the Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) after dipping below it, and show that sellers cannot push the price down for more than 2 consecutive 1-minute candles. If the stock opens and drops more than 2% below the opening price within the first 5 minutes, it is not your setup. Wait for it to demonstrate a tradable setup.

### Step 4: Identify the Battle and the Breakout

Look for the "battle" on the tape—buyers and sellers competing at a specific price level, visible as alternating green and red 1-minute candles with wicks that reject the opposite direction. This competition happens after one attempted breakout above the consolidation range fails and price returns to the range. When a 1-minute candle closes above the range high with volume at least 1.5x the average volume of the prior 10 minutes, that is your entry signal. This breakout point maximizes information and gives you a risk-reward ratio of at least 2.5:1.

### Step 5: Enter with a Structured Order (Optional TWAP)

If the breakout is happening within a 2-minute window, consider using a TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) order to split your intended position size into 4 equal parts executed over 60 seconds to 2 minutes, rather than all at once. Structure the TWAP to only buy while price stays above the midpoint of the consolidation range. This improves your average fill without chasing price higher.

### Step 6: Manage Risk with a Tight Stop

Place your stop 0.5% to 1% below the breakout level or below the most recent swing low within the prior 30 minutes. In a volatile catalyst play like this, you should be risking no more than 0.5% of your total trading capital per trade, targeting a reward of at least 2.5 points for every 1 point risked (a 2.5:1 risk-reward ratio). The tight range breakout is where you control risk with a predefined stop at 0.5% to 1% below the breakout level.

### Step 7: Take Partial Profits and Trail the Rest

When price reaches 75% of your target or shows exhaustion (volume drops below 50% of the prior 5-minute average for two consecutive minutes while price stalls within 0.5% of the high), sell 50% of your position into strength. Trail the remaining 50% using a 5-minute 20-period exponential moving average (EMA) or a trend line drawn from the last two swing lows. Do not give back more than 30% of your open profits—if the stock is bouncing between a support and resistance level that is no more than 1% apart and the 5-minute Relative Strength Index (RSI) is below 50, flatten the entire position.

### Step 8: Decide on Swing vs. Intraday Before Entering

Before you take the trade, decide if you will hold overnight. To make this decision, confirm: (1) you have read the complete SEC Form 8-K or press release and can explain the mechanism of action and target patient population, and (2) you have checked the company's SEC filings for any shelf registration or at-the-money (ATM) equity offerings within the prior 6 months. Low-float small caps (fewer than 50 million shares outstanding) can drop offerings after hours. If you have not completed both checks, plan to exit into the close and take your intraday profit.

## Examples

### Example 1: QURE Day-One Breakout (September 24th)

SMB Capital traded QURE, a biotech gene therapy company, on the day it announced positive top-line results for AMT-130, showing a 75% slowing of Huntington's disease progression. The stock gapped up 188% to approximately $40, did at least 7M in pre-market volume (5x its ADV of 1.3M), and held its gap above VWAP. The team entered on the breakout above the morning consolidation range at 11:30 AM with a stop placed 0.5% to 1% below the entry level, targeting $50. The trade worked cleanly and was closed.

### Example 2: ABBVX Analog

Before trading QURE, the team reviewed ABBVX, a similar biotech that gapped up approximately 500% on medical conference news in July. ABBVX held its gap, trended higher throughout the day, and continued rising for approximately 22 trading days. This analog gave the team conviction that a similar setup could unfold for QURE, reinforcing their decision to watch closely and wait for the right entry.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always verify the catalyst is real by reading the primary source (press release or SEC filing) and compare the company's projected revenue to its market cap using at least one comparable public biotech
- ✅ Use comparable company pricing to check if the gap is justified by calculating enterprise value per patient or price-to-revenue multiple
- ✅ Wait for the range to tighten to no more than 1% of the stock price and the breakout to close above the range high on 1.5x average volume before entering
- ✅ Risk no more than 0.5% of your total trading capital on any single trade
- ✅ Enter with a pre-defined stop at 0.5% to 1% below the breakout level and a target at least 2.5x the risk distance before you press the button
- ✅ Use TWAP orders split into 4 parts over 60 seconds to 2 minutes to improve fills on momentum breakouts
- ✅ Take 50% profits into strength when price reaches 75% of your target; never let a winning trade turn into a loser
- ✅ Decide swing vs. intraday time horizon before entering, and verify the catalyst and SEC filings for offering risk before holding overnight

## Keep In Mind

- A 200% gap can still leave the stock priced below its revenue potential if the catalyst is first-in-class and targets a patient pool of at least 100,000 people in the United States
- Not every day requires a trade. If your account is down at least 3% in a week, slow down and wait for a setup where the stock is at least 5x ADV, has a catalyst verified from a primary source document (press release, FDA letter, or clinical trial results), and offers at least a 2.5:1 risk-reward ratio
- Sellers losing the battle (a failed breakdown attempt where price drops more than 1% below the consolidation low but recovers above the consolidation high within 3 minutes) is a signal that buyers are in control
- You do not need to be right every time when your risk-reward ratio is at least 2.5:1
- Catalyst analysis is not about predicting the future outcome of a clinical trial—it is about predicting whether other market participants will buy or sell based on the information presented

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never trade a biotech stock without reading the primary source document (press release, FDA letter, or clinical trial results) that is causing the price move
- Understand the risk of secondary offerings on low-float small caps (fewer than 50 million shares outstanding) before holding overnight, and check the company's recent SEC Form 8-K filings for any announced offerings
- Biotech day-one runners (stocks that gap up on day-one catalyst news) can gap up at least 100% in the pre-market and then gap down at least 50% on offering news released after hours

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying the gap immediately at the open without waiting for a 1-minute candle to close above the consolidation range high on at least 1.5x average volume
  **Solution:** Let the stock demonstrate a tradable setup (a tradable setup means the stock holds the gap, reclaims VWAP, and tightens into a range where the high-low spread is no more than 1%). Wait for it to hold the gap (price stays above the opening price for at least 10 minutes), reclaim VWAP (the Volume-Weighted Average Price), and tighten into a range where the high-low spread is no more than 1%
- **Problem:** Ignoring the catalyst and trading purely on the technical pattern without checking if the projected revenue justifies the current market capitalization
  **Solution:** Use valuation context to filter setups. Calculate the enterprise value per patient or price-to-revenue multiple using comparable biotech companies. A breakout pattern with at least a 2.5:1 risk-reward ratio on a catalyst that does not address an unmet medical need affecting at least 100,000 people is not worth the risk
- **Problem:** Holding a winning intraday trade into the close without a defined exit plan
  **Solution:** Trail your stop at the 5-minute 20 EMA, take 50% partials at 75% of your target, and know your exit criteria before you enter
- **Problem:** Swing trading a low-float small cap (fewer than 50 million shares outstanding) without checking for recent or pending equity offerings
  **Solution:** Search the company's SEC filings for any shelf registration or at-the-money (ATM) offering programs within the prior 6 months. If you have not verified that no offering is imminent, stick to the intraday trade
