# SMB Capital Trading Floor: The Ethereum 4K Breakout Trade

## Overview

This Money Teaching covers how SMB Capital traders Mike, Tommy, and Steven identified, executed, and managed a high-conviction swing trade on the Ethereum 4K breakout using the ETHA ETF. The SMB Capital podcast hosts walk through their full trade process, from building a fundamental and technical tailwind checklist to position sizing, desk debate, and exit strategy. The goal is to teach traders how to combine catalysts with chart structure, decide between momentum and swing plays before entering, and hold a position through uncertainty without selling too early.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are setting up a high-timeframe breakout trade with a clear fundamental catalyst such as a new ETF approval or major protocol upgrade
- When you need to decide between taking a partial profit after a quick 1-3% intraday move versus swinging a position for multiple weeks
- When you want to learn how to use desk debate to improve trade management decisions after a large gap in your favor
- When you need to build a checklist of tailwinds (sentiment shift, short positioning, technical base, compression, sector trend, macro conditions) to confirm a trade idea before entering

## Steps

### Step 1: Build a Tailwind Checklist Before Entering

Identify every factor supporting the trade, including: (1) long-term bearish sentiment shifting bullish, (2) elevated short positioning that could trigger a squeeze, (3) clean technical bases on the monthly and daily charts, (4) tight multi-week or multi-month compression on the daily chart, (5) emerging sector trends (e.g., new ETFs, institutional inflows), and (6) macro conditions such as stablecoin adoption growth or a pro-crypto regulatory administration. Use this checklist to confirm the trade has multiple independent reasons to work beyond just price action.

### Step 2: Define the Trade Type and Time Frame Up Front

Decide whether the trade is a single-day momentum play (holding for hours to one day), an overnight hold (1-2 days), or a multi-week swing trade (holding 2-8 weeks). In this trade, SMB Capital traders treated the Ethereum 4K breakout as a 4-6 week swing trade because of the strong fundamental backdrop (new ETF product, Bitcoin leading sector rotation), even though many traders on the desk played it as a one-day momentum move.

### Step 3: Use Anticipatory Positioning on the Breakout Day

Begin building a position on the day before the confirmed close above resistance using an execution algorithm such as TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) to accumulate size passively over 2-3 hours as long as price holds above the breakout level. Do not lever up the full position until there is a confirmed daily close above resistance.

### Step 4: Wait for the Close Above Resistance to Take Full Risk

Only add the remaining position and take it overnight after the daily close above the key level. In this trade, the close above $4,000 on the ETHA ETF was the trigger to finalize full size and hold through the weekend.

### Step 5: Manage the Trade With Desk Debate and a Clear Plan

When a gap of more than 5% appears in your favor, hold a structured 30-minute discussion with your trading desk about whether to take 30-50% partial profits or hold the full swing position. Use the fundamental backdrop to decide. SMB Capital held because the ETF catalyst justified a larger multi-week move, and they divided their playbook into two separate sub-playbooks: one for momentum trades (target: 1-3% in one day, kick out quickly) and one for swing trades (target: 5-15% over 4-8 weeks, hold through volatility).

### Step 6: Trail the Stop and Avoid Selling Into Every Rip

Do not sell 20-50% of your position simply because the price is making new highs every day. Wait for the market to show confirmed weakness, such as a daily close below the prior 1-day bar low or a failed reclaim of the prior day's high on above-average volume. In this trade, the exit trigger came when ETHA closed below the prior daily bar low after four consecutive days of grinding higher on 2x average volume.

### Step 7: Scale Out With Options to Generate Cash Flow While Holding Stock

Sell covered call options (1-2 strikes out-of-the-money, 30-45 day expiration) against your stock or ETF position as the trade moves in your favor. This generates premium income (typically 1-3% of position value) without reducing your core share position, keeping you in the trade longer while collecting cash.

### Step 8: Review the Trade as a Desk to Build Better Criteria

After the trade is closed, spend 45-60 minutes reviewing what worked, what was debated, and how the decision played out. Document the outcome and use it to create clearer rules for the next time a similar situation arises, such as explicit percentage thresholds for when to trim size (e.g., 20% at 2x risk-reward, 30% at 3x) versus when to hold the full position.

## Examples

### Example 1: Ethereum 4K Breakout (Swing Trade)

SMB Capital traders Mike, Tommy, and Steven saw Ethereum build a four-year multi-month base on the monthly chart with tight 4-week compression on the daily chart. After Bitcoin broke out in early July 2024 and Ethereum spot ETF products emerged, they waited for ETHA (the Ethereum futures ETF) to close above $4,000. They used a TWAP algorithm on Thursday to build an anticipatory 40% position size over 2 hours, added the remaining 60% at the daily close, held through a 7% gap over the weekend, and exited when price closed below the prior daily bar low after four consecutive days of grinding higher on 2x average daily volume.

### Example 2: Tesla Stock Breakout (Momentum Trade)

SMB Capital also traded a Tesla (TSLA) stock breakout that had no clear catalyst. Because there was no fundamental tailwind such as an earnings beat or product announcement, they treated it as a pure technical momentum play and sold 80% of the position within 24 hours rather than swinging it, illustrating the difference between trades with and without a catalyst.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Build a 6-factor tailwind checklist (sentiment shift, short positioning, technical base, compression, sector trend, macro conditions) before entering a high-conviction trade
- ✅ Define whether the trade is single-day momentum, overnight hold, or 4-8 week swing before you are in the heat of the moment
- ✅ Use execution algorithms like TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) to accumulate 30-60% of position passively over 2-3 hours when there is no intraday see-it moment
- ✅ Wait for the daily close above resistance (e.g., close above $4,000) before adding the remaining position and taking it overnight
- ✅ Use a 30-minute structured desk debate with documented arguments for and against holding before managing a large gap in your favor
- ✅ Trail your stop based on market structure (e.g., daily close below prior bar low) rather than selling into strength out of fear
- ✅ Sell 1-2 strikes out-of-the-money covered calls with 30-45 day expiration to extract 1-3% premium while maintaining your core share position
- ✅ Review every trade for 45-60 minutes with your desk to refine your playbook and set explicit trim thresholds for the next similar opportunity

## Keep In Mind

- No trade will give you a perfect entry and exit; the goal is to best express your directional view at the correct position size, not to nail the exact high and low price.
- The same conviction required to hold a winning multi-week swing trade is the same conviction that got you into it, making it psychologically difficult to switch from accumulation mode to distribution mode after a large gain.
- You will almost never sell the exact high or buy the exact low. Accept that selling 1-2 days early or 1-2 days late is normal, and focus on following your predefined process.
- Different traders on the same desk can validly play the same breakout setup in different ways (e.g., momentum vs. swing vs. overnight hold) because their time horizons and risk parameters differ.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always set stop-loss levels based on the prior daily bar low or a meaningful multi-day technical structure (e.g., 2-3 day low), not arbitrary percentage distances like 1% or 2%.
- Do not add 50-100% of your intended position size into a steep opening drive (a move of 3%+ in the first 30 minutes) without confirmation; chasing after a fast gap up often leads to buying the high and suffering a 5-10% pullback.
- Verify that volume is at least 1.5x the 20-day average before increasing position size, especially when entering on a gap up day.
- When selling covered calls, ensure the strike price is at least 1-2 strikes out-of-the-money (5-10% above current price) to avoid having your shares called away at a loss if the price spikes.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying 50-100% of your intended position into a steep gap up (3%+ in first 30 minutes) without waiting for confirmation or pullback to the breakout level.
  **Solution:** Draw a clear horizontal line at the resistance level and wait for price to hold above it for at least 1-2 hours on 1.5x average volume before adding size. If price fails and closes back below resistance, do not chase the move.
- **Problem:** Selling 50-100% of a strong trade too early simply because it is making new highs every day for 3-4 consecutive days.
  **Solution:** Trail your stop-loss using market structure (e.g., daily close below the prior 1-day bar low or a 2-day moving average) and let the market tell you when the trend is over. Do not move your stop higher just because the price is rising.
- **Problem:** Failing to decide on trade type (momentum vs. swing) before entry and then improvising under pressure after a large gap.
  **Solution:** Classify every trade as single-day momentum, overnight hold, or 4-8 week swing before entering, and write down the exact rules for adding size (e.g., 40% anticipatory, 60% at close) and taking profits (e.g., 30% at 2x risk, trail rest).
- **Problem:** Letting a single loud voice on the desk override structured debate without documenting the arguments for and against holding.
  **Solution:** Encourage a 30-minute structured debate where each trader presents 1-2 data points supporting their view, document the outcome, and use the result to refine your trading playbook checklist for the next similar setup.
