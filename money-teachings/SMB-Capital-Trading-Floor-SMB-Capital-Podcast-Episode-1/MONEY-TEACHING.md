# SMB Capital Trading Floor: The Ethereum 4K Breakout Trade

## Overview

This Money Teaching covers how SMB Capital traders Garrett and Tim identified, executed, and managed a swing trade on the Ethereum 4K breakout using the Ethereum futures ETF (ETHA). Garrett and Tim walk through the traders' full trade process, from building a fundamental and technical tailwind checklist to position sizing, desk debate, and exit strategy. This Money Teaching aims to teach traders how to combine catalysts with chart structure, decide between momentum and swing plays before entering, and hold a position through uncertainty.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are setting up a multi-week swing breakout trade with a fundamental catalyst such as a new ETF approval or protocol upgrade
- When you need to decide between taking a partial profit after a quick 1-3% intraday move versus swinging a position for 4–8 weeks
- When you want to learn how to use desk debate to improve trade management decisions after a gap of more than 5% in your favor
- When you need to build a checklist of tailwinds (sentiment shift, short positioning, technical base, compression, sector trend, macroeconomic factors such as stablecoin adoption growth or a pro-crypto regulatory administration) to confirm a trade idea before entering

## Steps

### Step 1: Build a Tailwind Checklist Before Entering

Identify every factor supporting the trade, including: (1) bearish sentiment shifting bullish, (2) short positioning, (3) technical bases on the monthly and daily charts, (4) tight one-month compression on the daily chart, (5) sector trends (e.g., new ETFs, institutional inflows), and (6) macroeconomic factors such as stablecoin adoption growth or a pro-crypto regulatory administration. Use this checklist to confirm the trade has multiple independent reasons to work beyond just price action.

### Step 2: Define the Trade Type and Time Frame Up Front

Decide whether the trade is a single-day momentum play (one day), an overnight hold (1-2 days), or a multi-week swing trade (holding 2-8 weeks). In this trade, SMB Capital traders treated the Ethereum 4K breakout as a 4-6 week swing trade because of the fundamental backdrop (new ETF product, Bitcoin breaking out to all-time highs), even though traders on the desk played it as a one-day momentum move.

### Step 3: Use Anticipatory Positioning on the Breakout Day

Begin building a position on the day before the daily close above $4,000 using an execution algorithm such as TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) to accumulate size passively over 2-3 hours as long as price holds above the 4K level. Do not put on full size until there is a confirmed daily close above the $4,000 resistance level.

### Step 4: Wait for the Close Above Resistance to Take Full Risk

Only add the remaining 60% of the position and take it overnight after the daily close above $4,000. In this trade, the close above $4,000 on the ETHA ETF was the trigger to finalize full size and hold through the weekend.

### Step 5: Manage the Trade With Desk Debate and a Clear Plan

When a gap of more than 5% appears in your favor, hold a structured 30-minute discussion with your trading desk about whether to take 30-50% partial profits or hold the swing position. Use the checklist of tailwinds to decide. SMB Capital held because the ETF catalyst justified holding the multi-week swing position, dividing its playbook into two separate sub-playbooks: one for momentum trades (target: 1-3% in one day, kick out quickly) and one for swing trades (target: 5-15% over 4-8 weeks, hold through volatility).

### Step 6: Trail the Stop and Avoid Selling Into Every Rip

Do not sell 20-50% of your position simply because the price is making new highs for consecutive days. Wait for the market to show a daily close below the prior bar low or a failed reclaim of the prior day's high on volume at least 1.5x the 20-day average. In this trade, the exit trigger came when ETHA closed below the prior daily bar low after four consecutive days of grinding higher on 2x the average daily volume.

### Step 7: Scale Out With Options to Generate Cash Flow While Holding Stock

Sell covered call options (1-2 strikes out-of-the-money, 30-45 day expiration) against your stock or ETF position as the trade moves in your favor. This generates premium income (typically 1-3% of position value) without reducing your core share position, keeping you in the swing trade through volatility while collecting cash.

### Step 8: Review the Trade as a Desk to Build Better Criteria

After the trade is closed, spend 45-60 minutes reviewing what worked, what was debated, and how the decision played out. Document the outcome and use it to create clearer rules for the next time a similar swing trade setup arises, such as exact percentage thresholds for when to trim size (e.g., 20% when the potential reward is twice the amount risked, 30% when the potential reward is three times the amount risked) versus when to hold the full position.

## Examples

### Example 1: Ethereum 4K Breakout (Swing Trade)

Garrett and Tim saw Ethereum build a four-year base on the monthly chart with tight 4-week compression on the daily chart. After Bitcoin broke out in early July 2024 and Ethereum spot ETF products emerged, Garrett and Tim waited for ETHA (the Ethereum futures ETF) to close above $4,000. Garrett and Tim used a TWAP algorithm on Thursday to build an anticipatory 40% position size over 2 hours, added the remaining 60% at the daily close, held through a 7% gap over the weekend, and exited when price closed below the prior daily bar low after four consecutive days of grinding higher on 2x the average daily volume.

### Example 2: Tesla Stock Breakout (Momentum Trade)

SMB Capital also traded a Tesla (TSLA) stock breakout that had no fundamental catalyst such as an earnings beat or product announcement. Because there was no tailwind such as an earnings beat or product announcement, Garrett and Tim treated it as a momentum play based only on chart structure and sold 80% of the position within 24 hours rather than swinging it, illustrating the difference between trades with and without a catalyst.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Build a 6-factor tailwind checklist (sentiment shift, short positioning, technical base, compression, sector trend, macroeconomic factors) before entering a swing trade
- ✅ Define whether the trade is single-day momentum, overnight hold, or 4-8 week swing before you are in the heat of the moment
- ✅ Use execution algorithms like TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) to accumulate 30-60% of position passively over 2-3 hours when there is no intraday clear confirmation moment
- ✅ Wait for the daily close above the $4,000 resistance level before adding the remaining 60% of the position and taking it overnight
- ✅ Use a 30-minute structured desk debate with documented arguments for and against holding before managing a gap of more than 5% in your favor
- ✅ Trail your stop-loss level based on market structure (e.g., daily close below prior bar low) rather than selling into strength impulsively
- ✅ Sell 1-2 strikes out-of-the-money covered calls with 30-45 day expiration to extract 1-3% premium while maintaining your core share position
- ✅ Review every trade for 45-60 minutes with your trading desk to refine your playbook and set exact percentage trim thresholds for the next similar swing trade setup

## Keep In Mind

- No trade will give you a perfect entry and exit; the goal is to execute your directional trade with appropriate position size that matches your risk tolerance, not to nail the absolute high and low price points.
- The same conviction required to hold a winning multi-week swing trade is the conviction that got you into it, making it psychologically difficult to switch from adding to a position to reducing a position after a gain.
- You will almost never sell the absolute high or buy the absolute low. Accept that selling 1-2 days early or 1-2 days late is expected, and focus on following your predefined exit rules.
- Different traders on the same desk can legitimately trade the same breakout setup in different time horizons (e.g., momentum vs. swing vs. overnight hold) because their time horizons and risk tolerances differ.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always set stop-loss levels based on the prior daily bar low or a multi-day technical structure (e.g., 2-3 day low), not fixed percentage distances like 1% or 2%.
- Do not add 50-100% of your full position size into a steep opening drive (a move of 3%+ in the first 30 minutes) without a confirmed daily close above resistance; chasing such a move often leads to buying at a local high before a pullback and suffering a 5-10% pullback.
- Verify that volume is at least 1.5x the 20-day average before increasing position size, especially when entering on a gap up day.
- When selling covered calls, ensure the strike price is at least 1-2 strikes out-of-the-money (5-10% above current price) to avoid having your shares called away at a realized loss if the price spikes.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying 50-100% of your full position into a steep gap up (3%+ in first 30 minutes) without waiting for a confirmed daily close above resistance or pullback to the 4K breakout level.
  **Solution:** Draw a horizontal line at the resistance level and wait for price to hold above it for at least 1-2 hours on 1.5x average volume before adding size. If price fails and closes back below resistance, do not chase the move.
- **Problem:** Selling 50-100% of a trade with a multi-week swing target too early simply because it is making new highs for 3-4 consecutive days.
  **Solution:** Trail your stop-loss level using market structure (e.g., daily close below the prior 1-day bar low or a 2-day moving average) and let the market tell you when the trend reverses. Do not move your stop higher just because the price is rising.
- **Problem:** Failing to decide on trade type (momentum vs. swing) before entry and then making decisions without a predefined plan after a gap of more than 5%.
  **Solution:** Classify every trade as single-day momentum, overnight hold, or 4-8 week swing before entering, and write down specific rules for adding size (e.g., 40% anticipatory, 60% at close) and taking profits (e.g., 30% when the potential reward is twice the amount risked, trail rest).
- **Problem:** Letting a single loud voice on the desk override structured debate without documenting the arguments for and against holding.
  **Solution:** Encourage a 30-minute structured debate where each trader presents 1-2 data points supporting their view, document the outcome, and use the result to refine your trading playbook checklist for the next similar setup.
