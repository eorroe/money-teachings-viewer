# SMB Capital Secrets Behind 7-Figure Short Trade: Build A+ Playbooks, Spot Overextended Setups Using Scanner Criteria, And Execute Mean Reversion Shorts With Two-Step Entries

## Overview

This money teaching breaks down how to build a systematic trading playbook using daily chart and intraday technical analysis to identify overextended mean reversion short opportunities. An A+ trade is a daily chart breakout on high volume with a news catalyst that fits your playbook criteria. You will learn how to scan for setups using criteria such as consecutive up days, rising volume, and an ATR bigger than the ranges prior, manage risk with two-step entries and tight stops, and exit using trailing stops and tape-reading capitulation signals. The content is derived from a live SMB Capital internship review of a seven-figure short trade in gold on October 21st.

- When managing high-conviction short trades intraday with disciplined entries and exits
- When reviewing trade performance to extract repeatable edge

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Your Daily Playbook Exercise

At the end of every trading day, deconstruct one trade that worked well—either one you executed or one you spotted on the desk or in the market. Record the setup, catalyst, entry, exit, and why it fit your criteria. Over time this builds a database of A+ trades that you can reference in real time so you do not hesitate when the same pattern appears.

### Step 2: Use Multi-Timeframe Analysis To Find A+ Setups

First review longer-term charts to spot overextended assets. An asset is overextended when it is further above a key EMA, has a significant percentage gain in a short period, has an ATR bigger than the ranges prior, has rising volume over consecutive days, has consecutive higher closes, or has gaps at the end of the trend. Once you identify a candidate, zoom to the intraday chart and wait for the longer-term thesis to be confirmed by price action before entering.

### Step 3: Scan For Overextended Candidates Automatically

Build a scanner that flags stocks meeting criteria such as: price closed higher than the prior day for multiple consecutive sessions, increasing volume over the last several days, and an ATR on the prior day that is bigger than the ranges prior. Toggle between pre-market and regular hours without rebuilding the scan so you can act quickly when conditions appear.

### Step 4: Confirm With Intraday Structure And Pre-Market Levels

On the day of the trade, watch pre-market support and resistance. A break of a pre-market low, a failed reclaim of a key level such as VWAP, and weak tape action in the first minutes all serve as intraday confirmation. An A+ trade is a daily chart setup on high volume with a news catalyst that fits your playbook criteria. Gold is especially messy to trade intraday because hedging flows unrelated to directional view distort the tape, so only trade it when the setup meets your A+ criteria and the price action supports your thesis.

### Step 5: Enter In Two Steps With A Tight Stop

Take an initial partial position on the first rejection or breakdown candle, then add on a second confirmation such as a two-bar break below the pre-market low on a one-minute chart. A two-bar break occurs when price closes below the pre-market low for two consecutive one-minute candles. Place the stop for the full position above the high of the day or above the VWAP rejection high. This two-step entry gives you information while in the trade and keeps risk defined.

### Step 6: Manage The Trade With Systematic Exits

Do not take profits on an A+ trade too early just because the first scalp worked. Trail the position using a short-term indicator such as the 9 EMA or the 90 MA. Watch for tape shifts: a candle with the highest volume of the day signals capitulation and the end of the move. Exit the full position when the tape shows exhaustion and the stock cannot push lower.

### Step 7: Review And Refine After Every Trade

After the session, open your trade journal and rate each component of the trade—big picture thesis, intraday fundamentals, entries, exits, and risk management. Identify at least one specific thing to improve next time. Use that feedback to sharpen your scanner rules, stop placement, and exit discipline.

## Examples

### Example 1: GLD Short On October 21st

After a 27% rally with three measured moves of the flag or wedge from roughly 3,150 to 4,400, gold gapped up on lower volume after the prior session's sell-off. A measured move equals the distance from the beginning of the move to the consolidation or the height of the flag or wedge. Trump remarks de-escalating the China tariff war provided the catalyst. The short thesis was: overextended daily chart with price further above the 9 EMA, weakening dollar, equities at all-time highs shifting risk appetite away from gold, and pre-market weakness with a break below 390. Entries were taken at VWAP rejection and again on the two-bar break below the pre-market low on the one-minute chart, with a stop above the high of the day. The position was trailed and the bulk of profits were captured at the capitulation candle near 377. A gap up on lower volume after a sharp unwind signals short covering rather than real buying when the volume on the gap-up day is the same as or lower than the day prior to the unwind.

### Example 2: Scanner Catching An Overextended Move

A scanner configured for consecutive up days, rising volume, and an ATR bigger than the ranges prior flagged a stock meeting those criteria with low volume. While low volume made it untradeable, the scan proved that the rules can surface setups to review each morning. Adding filters for price above a key EMA or distance from the 9 EMA can further narrow the list to only A+ candidates.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Do a playbook exercise at the end of every session
- ✅ Use longer-term charts first, then confirm with intraday structure
- ✅ Enter in two stages so you gather information while in the trade
- ✅ Trail A+ trades with the 9 EMA or 90 MA instead of taking quick profits
- ✅ Exit on definitive capitulation: the biggest volume candle of the day
- ✅ Review every trade and rate each component for continuous improvement

## Keep In Mind

- Gold is almost always frustrating to trade intraday because hedging flows unrelated to directional bias distort the tape. Treat it as a special-day instrument, not a daily vehicle.
- When equities and gold both rally to all-time highs, the correlation can break down; rely on tape confirmation rather than assumptions.
- A gap up on lower volume after a sharp unwind signals short covering rather than real buying when the volume on the gap-up day is the same as or lower than the day prior to the unwind, and most probably precedes another move down.
- Having the conviction to let an A+ trade run is as important as the discipline to enter with the right size.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking profits too early on an A+ trade because the first scalp worked.
  **Solution:** Trail the position with a moving average and wait for the biggest volume candle of the day before exiting the majority of the position.
- **Problem:** Using a wide stop on gold, which kills risk-adjusted returns because hedging flows distort the tape intraday.
  **Solution:** Only trade gold on A+ days with a tight stop above the high of the day or above the VWAP rejection high.
- **Problem:** Entering the full position at once without gathering information from being in the trade.
  **Solution:** Use a two-step entry—partial on the first signal, add on confirmation—to improve your edge and reduce second-guessing.
- **Problem:** Reviewing trades without specific, actionable feedback.
  **Solution:** Quantify what overextended means for your playbook, use Bollinger Bands or percentage-from-EMA rules, and add each reviewed trade to your playbook database with explicit lessons.
