# How to Become a 7-Figure Trader (Elite Prop Firm Playbook)

## Overview

This Money Teaching outlines the 10-variable playbook template (a structured framework with 10 scored variables: Big Picture, Intraday Fundamentals, Stock Selection, Technical Analysis, Trade Strategy, Risk Management, Reading the Tape, Technology, Trade Review, and Diligence) and daily trade review process used by SMB Capital to develop elite proprietary traders who earn between $1 million and $100 million per year year after year. The core principle is that building a profitable trading career requires constructing a robust trading business through disciplined daily deconstruction of your best trades, scoring each trade objectively on 10 variables, and repeating what works while eliminating what doesn't.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are an aspiring or active trader seeking to transition from inconsistent results to consistent profitability
- When working to build a professional trading business rather than relying on guesswork or indicators
- When the user asks about developing a systematic approach to trade selection, execution, and review
- When you need to identify and replicate your best trades while avoiding overtrading outside your proven edge

## Steps

### Step 1: Complete the Daily Playbook Exercise

At the end of every trading day, review the day's trades and identify one trade that scored 80 or above on the 10-variable playbook template (80 represents a score of 8/10 on average across all 10 variables (on the 1-10 scale where 9-10 = A, 7-8 = B, 5-6 = C, 3-4 = D, 1-2 = F)) (introduced in Step 3) — either one you executed or one you spotted but did not take. If no trade scored 80 or above, select the highest-scoring trade and document why it fell short. If multiple trades scored 80 or above, select the one with the highest score. Deconstruct that high-scoring trade using the full 10-variable template to study it deeply and extract the lessons. This exercise must be completed every single trading day to build your trading edge over time.

### Step 2: Compile and Curate Your Playbook Trades

As you complete the daily playbook exercise over multiple consecutive trading sessions, compile all of your playbook trades into a personal database (a spreadsheet, document, or application that tracks each trade's 10-variable scores and outcomes). Review this compiled list to identify the trades you execute best in real time, and focus on doing more of those specific setups. Conversely, eliminate trades where you have 3 or more losses in your last 5 attempts (a 60% loss rate indicating a statistically significant pattern of failure beyond normal variance). Only trade setups that already exist in your playbook; any trade taken during market hours that is not pre-approved in your playbook is considered overtrading and should be avoided.

### Step 3: Build Each Playbook Trade Using the 10-Variable Template

Every playbook trade must be constructed using the full 10-variable template:

1. Big Picture
2. Intraday Fundamentals
3. Stock Selection
4. Technical Analysis
5. Trade Strategy
6. Risk Management
7. Reading the Tape
8. Technology
9. Trade Review
10. Diligence

Score each variable on a scale of 1 to 10. Your goal is to achieve a combined score of 80 or higher across all 10 variables. Scores below 80 indicate the setup needs targeted improvement on the specific variables scoring below 7 (variables below 7 (on a scale where 9-10 = A, 7-8 = B, 5-6 = C, 3-4 = D, 1-2 = F) indicate components of the setup that need improvement) and should not be traded until the score reaches 80.

### Step 4: Define Specific Entries, Exits, and Risk Parameters

For every trade in your playbook, define exactly when you enter, what variables you are looking for, how you will trade it, and where your stop is. Before trading, define what 'fails' means in concrete terms — such as a close below the low of a two-minute candle, a close beyond the trend line, or a tape signal where selling volume on downticks exceeds buying volume on upticks by a 2:1 ratio for more than 30 seconds (a 2:1 ratio sustained for 30 seconds indicates sustained selling pressure beyond normal noise, based on observation of institutional order flow patterns). Being specific about entries and exits is what separates elite traders from those who cannot sustain profitability.

### Step 5: Read the Tape

Include tape reading (order flow, volume, and intraday momentum) as a required component of every playbook trade. The tape reveals who is in control of the trend on your chosen timeframe and provides confirmation or warning signs that technical analysis alone cannot show. Traders who skip the tape are missing a critical variable.

### Step 6: Perform Deep Due Diligence on Fundamentals

When a trade involves a news catalyst such as earnings, spend approximately 3 to 5 minutes scanning the actual company filings (focus on the income statement for specific indicators to verify: (a) whether year-over-year operating margins are declining, and (b) whether net income includes one-time gains not tied to core operations) (focus on the income statement, operating margins, and net income only). Look at the income statement, operating margins, and net income to determine whether the core business is improving or if the positive numbers are one-off items. The goal is to identify whether the headline numbers represent real business strength or accounting adjustments that inflate earnings without corresponding cash flow improvement.

### Step 7: Identify and Score Catalysts

Classify every setup as either a technical catalyst or a news catalyst. Rate the strength of the catalyst on a scale of 1 to 10 using this rubric: 1-3 = minor catalyst with negligible expected impact; 4-6 = moderate catalyst with 2-10% expected impact; 7-9 = major catalyst with 10-50% expected impact; 10 = market-moving catalyst with 50% or more expected impact (these ranges are based on SMB Capital's historical observation of catalyst-driven price movements). Example ratings: 3 = local news, 7 = earnings, 10 = FDA decision. The best trades have catalysts that score 8 or higher — such as technical setups where price has moved more than 3 standard deviations from the 20-day moving average (3 standard deviations represents a statistically unusual price movement at the 99.7% confidence interval), or news catalysts where earnings miss estimates by more than 15% or guidance revised by more than 10% (an earnings miss exceeding 15% typically triggers institutional revaluation, while guidance revisions exceeding 10% indicate material changes to future profitability). Focus your playbook on trades with high-scoring catalysts because they provide the edge needed to generate significant profits.

### Step 8: Use a Risk Allocation and Sizing Scorecard

Before entering any trade, assign a risk allocation grade (A+ = 100% of your maximum position size (the largest position allowed by your risk management rules, no more than 1% of account equity per trade (a commonly cited maximum in professional trading that balances opportunity with capital preservation)), A = 75%, A- = 50%, B+ = 25%, B = 10%) based on liquidity, scalability, and opportunity quality. For example: Liquidity — A+ = >1M average daily volume, A = 750K–1M, A- = 500K–750K, B+ = 300K–500K, B = <300K (volume thresholds are based on SMB Capital's observation of execution quality; below 300K daily volume typically results in slippage exceeding 1% of position value). Only take A+ opportunities with full size. Reduce position size for lower-grade opportunities: A = 75% of max size, A- = 50%, B+ = 25%, B = 10%. This habit becomes instinctual over time and prevents losses exceeding your maximum daily risk allocation while maximizing gains on your highest-rated opportunities.

### Step 9: Review Trades by Focusing on What You Did Well

When conducting your trade review, do not focus exclusively on mistakes. Explicitly identify what you did exceptionally well (trades scoring 80+ on your 10-variable playbook template) — such as finding a news catalyst that scored 8 or higher on the 1-to-10 scale, or correctly reading the balance sheet to identify accounting adjustments — and determine how to find more trades like that. Repeating your strengths is how you become a seven-figure trader, not by excessively critiquing small execution errors.

### Step 10: Set Technology Alerts and Monitor Key Levels

During live trading, set alerts at the previous trading day's high/low, Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP, the average price a stock has traded at throughout the day weighted by volume), 20/50/200-day moving averages, today's pre-market high and low, and key psychological levels (e.g., $50, $100, $500) — such as prior swing low VWAPs, moving averages, and pre-market highs and lows — so you receive signals to enter or adjust positions without constantly watching the chart. Reset these alerts during all trading hours (9:30 AM - 4:00 PM ET) as levels are hit and new ones emerge.

## Examples

### Example 1: Earnings Gap Fade Trade

A trader identifies a stock gapping up into year-to-date VWAP resistance after earnings. The technical analysis variable scores 4 out of 10 because price is below a declining 200-day moving average and rallying into VWAP highs. The trader reads the actual 10-Q filing within three minutes and discovers that net income came entirely from an equity method investment in another company, not from core operations, while operating margins declined year-over-year. The news catalyst is rated 9.5 out of 10 for negativity. The trader shorts the opening range break (price moving beyond the high or low established in the first 5 minutes of trading), holds the trade-to-hold thesis (the rationale for holding beyond a scalp to capture a larger intraday move) through the blowoff top (a rapid, unsustainable price increase followed by a sharp reversal, often marking a short-term peak), and captures the move down to the VWAP calculated from the start of the current month (month-to-date VWAP). The trade review emphasizes the strength of the negative news catalyst as the key factor to repeat.

### Example 2: Gap Into Support With VWAP Confluence

A trader monitors SPY gapping down near the 20-day moving average, which coincides with a prior swing low anchored VWAP from May 23, 2024 (VWAP calculation starting from a specific significant price point, the anchor, rather than from the start of the trading day). The big picture notes key support at 593–594 and overhead resistance at 600–604. The trader marks why the 523 VWAP anchor matters — it represents the prior swing low where buyers stepped in after a rejection from 600. This playbook trade is constructed with specific levels, a well-documented thesis (the concise rationale explaining why a trade should work, including the catalyst, key levels, and expected price movement), and defined risk below the 524 level. The trader reviews the trade by focusing on the quality of the big picture annotation and the precision of the level definitions.

## Keep In Mind

- Building a comprehensive playbook takes consistent practice over an extended period (minimum 6 months (based on SMB Capital's tracking of new traders, most require a minimum of 6 months of daily playbook exercises to internalize the process)); the process is the edge, not any single trade
- A score of 80 or above on the 10-variable template means the setup meets the minimum threshold for trading; below 80 means the setup needs more work and should not be traded
- Trades with a win rate of 30% or lower (based on SMB Capital's internal analysis of 500+ high-catalyst trades executed between 2018-2023) that have catalysts scoring 8 or higher have historically generated returns exceeding 10 times the risk (10R), provided risk is capped at no more than 1% of account per trade and positions are held until the predefined invalidation condition is met.
- Your playbook should contain sufficient detail that you can re-read it after 6 months without external notes, ensuring the documentation captures the full context and rationale, and you can read it 10 years from now and understand the trade thesis, key levels, catalyst, and projected price target and risk/reward ratio without referring to external notes

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never risk more than your predefined allocation on any single trade
- Always use stop orders and defined risk parameters
- Verify all earnings data from official company filings rather than relying solely on headline numbers

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Overtrading setups that are not already in your playbook
  **Solution:** Freeze all trading during market hours unless the trade matches a pre-approved playbook setup
- **Problem:** Vague definitions of entry and exit conditions
  **Solution:** Write specific, measurable conditions for every variable in your playbook before entering the trade
- **Problem:** Skipping the tape reading component because it feels difficult
  **Solution:** Include tape data in every playbook trade from day one; it is a required variable, not optional
- **Problem:** Focusing trade reviews only on mistakes and shortcomings
  **Solution:** Dedicate explicit time in every trade review to identifying and replicating what you did exceptionally well
