# Donald Trump and Robert Kiyosaki: Financial Education Fundamentals

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills the core financial principles shared by Donald Trump and Robert Kiyosaki in their discussion on financial education and investment fundamentals. It challenges conventional wisdom about debt, homeownership, and wealth building while emphasizing the critical role of financial literacy in achieving true financial independence. The teachings focus on mindset shifts and practical strategies for building wealth through real estate, intelligent use of debt, and continuous financial education.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to evaluate whether something functions as an asset or a liability
- When working with debt as a tool for wealth building rather than a burden
- When evaluating real estate investing principles
- When building or refining your financial intelligence and investment strategy
- When questioning conventional advice like "live below your means" or "get out of debt"

## Steps

### Step 1: Redefine Assets and Liabilities

Challenge the common belief that your primary residence is an asset. Robert Kiyosaki asserts that your house is your biggest liability, especially when you cannot make payments. Evaluate every holding by asking: "Does this put money in my pocket or take money out?" Apply this test to real estate, stocks, and other investments.

### Step 2: Master Real Estate as Your Primary Investment Vehicle

Treat real estate as a vehicle for wealth building when used with financial knowledge. Study market fundamentals, understand cash flow (money received minus money spent), and learn to leverage debt strategically. Recognize that real estate can build wealth or cause financial harm depending on your level of financial knowledge and execution. Commit to ongoing education about property types, markets, and financing structures.

### Step 3: Use Debt Intelligently, Not Avoid It

Reject the blanket advice to "get out of all debt." Understand that debt can build wealth or cause financial ruin, depending on how you use it. The key is financial education. Learn to use "good debt" (debt that acquires cash-flowing assets) while avoiding "bad debt" (debt for consumption). The more debt you use, the greater your financial knowledge must be.

### Step 4: Expand Your Means Instead of Living Below Them

Dismiss the concept of "living below your means"; some view the concept as problematic, according to Trump and Kiyosaki. Instead, focus on expanding your means through financial education and increased earning capacity. Prioritize learning and continuous education as your most valuable asset. Use specific goals and mental framing to build the confidence needed to increase your income.

### Step 5: Develop Your Financial IQ (financial intelligence) Above All Else

Prioritize financial education as your most important investment. Your financial intelligence determines whether an investment is valuable or worthless. Commit to learning how to evaluate investments and advice through financial literacy. Study successful investors, read extensively, and continuously upgrade your financial knowledge.

### Step 6: Evaluate Risk and Reward (comparison of potential gain to potential loss) in Every Investment

Never invest without understanding the risk-reward ratio. Remember that you can lose money in gold, real estate, stocks, or oil - but you can also make money in all of them. The difference is your financial intelligence. Before investing, assess whether the potential reward justifies the risk, and whether you have the knowledge to execute the strategy successfully.

## Examples

### Example 1: Evaluating a Primary Residence

You are considering buying a home to live in. Instead of viewing it as an automatic asset, apply Kiyosaki's test: Will this property generate positive cash flow each month? If not, it is a liability. Consider renting instead, or buying a multi-unit property where you can live in one unit and rent the others to offset costs. Track the true cost of ownership including maintenance, property taxes, and opportunity cost of the down payment.

### Example 2: Using Debt to Acquire Real Estate

You want to purchase a rental property but are nervous about taking on a mortgage. Research the cash-on-cash return (annual cash flow divided by cash invested), calculate the monthly cash flow after expenses, and compare it to the cost of the debt. If the property generates more than the mortgage payment and expenses, the debt is working for you. Start with a property, learn the numbers, and scale up as your financial intelligence grows.

### Example 3: Shifting from "Live Below Your Means" to "Expand Your Means"

Instead of cutting expenses and feeling deprived, invest in courses, books, and mentorship to increase your earning potential. If a first-class flight or a professional wardrobe makes you feel more confident and productive, factor that into your business development budget. The goal is not overspending, but spending on your own growth and income-generating capabilities.

## Best Practices

- View every purchase through the lens of "does this generate income or consume it?"
- Treat real estate as your primary wealth-building vehicle
- Use "good debt" to acquire assets that generate cash flow
- Invest continuously in financial education as a core activity
- Surround yourself with successful investors and learn from their mistakes
- Expand your means through skill development and income growth
- Evaluate risk and reward before committing to any investment
- Distinguish between assets that put money in your pocket versus liabilities that take it out

## Keep In Mind

- Your house is a liability, not an asset
- Debt is not inherently bad - it is a tool that requires skill to use properly
- "Living below your means" can undermine motivation and limit growth potential, according to Trump and Kiyosaki
- Financial education is the foundation of all successful investing
- The same investment vehicle can be good or bad depending on your knowledge and execution
- Financial literacy is a key factor in investment outcomes

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never invest in something you do not understand, regardless of its appeal
- Always verify the credibility of financial advisors and investment advice sources
- Be cautious of "get rich quick" schemes - real wealth building requires education and patience
- Protect your financial information and never share sensitive data with unverified sources
- Use legal structures and professional guidance when using debt for real estate

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a house assuming it is an automatic asset
  **Solution:** Calculate the true cost of ownership and cash flow. If it does not generate income, treat it as a liability and consider alternatives.
- **Problem:** Avoiding all debt out of fear
  **Solution:** Learn the difference between good debt (for assets) and bad debt (for consumption). Start small with leveraged real estate investments.
- **Problem:** Following conventional "live below your means" advice and feeling deprived
  **Solution:** Shift your focus to expanding your means through education, skill-building, and strategic investments in yourself.
- **Problem:** Investing in assets without understanding them
  **Solution:** Never invest based on others' recommendations alone. Build your financial IQ first by studying the asset class, market, and strategy thoroughly.
- **Problem:** Letting emotions drive investment decisions
  **Solution:** Always evaluate investments based on numbers, risk-reward ratios, and your financial knowledge - not hype or fear.
