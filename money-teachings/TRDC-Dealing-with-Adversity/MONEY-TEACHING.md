# Dealing with Adversity: Trump and Kiyosaki on Financial Resilience

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills the core financial resilience principles shared by Donald Trump and Robert Kiyosaki in their discussion on "Dealing with Adversity." It focuses on how to reframe setbacks, leverage financial intelligence, and use adversity as a catalyst for wealth building rather than a reason to retreat. The teaching emphasizes that financial education—not frugality or traditional saving—is the primary tool for navigating economic hardship and emerging stronger.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When facing a career setback, business failure, or significant financial loss
- When working through a recession, market downturn, or economic uncertainty
- When the user asks how to build resilience against financial shocks
- When learning how to use debt strategically rather than fearing it
- When seeking to develop an entrepreneurial mindset during tough times

## Steps

### Step 1: Redefine Adversity as Opportunity

Reframe setbacks as temporary "blips" rather than permanent catastrophes. Donald Trump emphasizes asking yourself, "Is this a blip or is it a catastrophe?" to maintain perspective during bad news. This mental shift prevents panic-driven decisions and opens you to recognizing opportunities that others miss during downturns.

### Step 2: Commit to Continuous Financial Education

Make reading and learning your greatest asset. Kiyosaki stresses feeding your brain to expand your financial means rather than shrinking your lifestyle. Study financial statements, understand the difference between assets and liabilities, and learn how money works. Financial literacy is the foundation that allows you to make smart decisions when others are making emotional ones.

### Step 3: Distinguish Between Good Debt and Bad Debt

Understand that debt is a two-edged sword. Use debt to acquire income-producing assets—this is "good debt" that pays you. Avoid consumer debt that drains your cash flow—this is "bad debt." Kiyosaki notes that the rich use debt to get richer, while the poor and middle class get wiped out by it due to lack of financial education. Real estate is highlighted as the best vehicle for leveraging good debt.

### Step 4: Shift Focus from Income to Assets

Stop focusing solely on earned income from a job. Instead, shift your mindset toward acquiring assets that generate passive income. Kiyosaki explains that the rich don't work for money—they have assets that work for them. Focus on building or buying assets (real estate, businesses, intellectual property) that produce cash flow regardless of your daily labor.

### Step 5: Embrace Real Estate as a Primary Wealth Vehicle

Real estate is repeatedly emphasized as the best wealth-building tool because it allows you to use other people's money (bank financing) while controlling a valuable asset. Trump and Kiyosaki both advocate for real estate investing, noting that it provides tax advantages, cash flow, and appreciation potential that stocks and mutual funds cannot match.

### Step 6: Reject "Live Below Your Means" Mentality

Challenge the conventional wisdom that tells you to live below your means. Instead, focus on expanding your means through financial education and smart investing. This doesn't mean reckless spending—it means building the skills and assets to afford the life you want without guilt or financial strain.

## Examples

### Example 1: Real Estate Investment During a Market Downturn

During the 2008 financial crisis, while many investors panicked and sold properties at a loss, Trump and Kiyosaki's approach would be to look for opportunities. Use seller financing to acquire undervalued properties, knowing that banks aren't lending. The key is having the financial education to evaluate deals accurately and the resilience to hold through the cycle. As Trump notes, "This is a great time for a person to go out, make a deal, get a house bigger and better than the one you're living in now, except the bank will give you virtually 100 percent financing."

### Example 2: Using Debt to Build Wealth

Kiyosaki shares the principle of using debt to purchase assets that generate cash flow. For example, borrowing money to buy a rental property where the tenant's rent covers the mortgage payment. The property pays for itself while you build equity. This is the opposite of using credit cards to buy liabilities that drain your bank account. The wealthy use debt as a tool; the financially uneducated are destroyed by it.

### Example 3: Overcoming Personal Adversity

When Kiyosaki and his wife Kim were homeless and sleeping in a car, they chose not to take "safe" jobs. Instead, they kept their purpose front and center—financial freedom—and built their business from scratch. They celebrated small wins (affording a motel room and KFC for dinner) and used optimism to stay focused. Their resilience came from knowing their "why" and refusing to let temporary circumstances define their future.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Read financial books and educate yourself daily—make this your greatest asset
- ✅ Use debt to buy income-producing assets, not consumer goods
- ✅ Focus on acquiring assets rather than chasing higher salaries
- ✅ View every setback as a learning opportunity, not a permanent defeat
- ✅ Invest in real estate to leverage other people's money and gain tax advantages
- ✅ Maintain optimism and celebrate small wins during difficult periods
- ✅ Build multiple streams of passive income to diversify risk
- ❌ Don't live below your means—expand your means through education and smart investing
- ❌ Don't rely on a single income source (job, pension, or social security)
- ❌ Don't let fear of loss prevent you from taking calculated risks
- ❌ Don't follow conventional advice like "get out of debt" without understanding the difference between good and bad debt
- ❌ Don't invest in assets you don't understand just because someone recommends them

## Keep In Mind

- Your house is not an asset if it takes money out of your pocket each month—it is a liability
- The middle class is disappearing because they follow outdated financial formulas (save money, get a good job, invest in mutual funds)
- There are three types of income: earned income (taxed heavily), portfolio income (from trading), and passive income (cash flow that bypasses taxes)
- Financial IQ determines whether an investment is valuable or a mistake—not the asset class itself
- Adversity reveals character; how you respond to tough times determines long-term success

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never invest in assets you don't understand, even if someone successful recommends them
- Always verify investment advice against your own financial education—don't blindly follow "experts"
- Good debt requires cash flow coverage—ensure your assets can service the debt before borrowing
- Beware of conventional financial advisors who promote mutual funds and 401(k)s as the only path to retirement
- Understand that Wall Street collects fees whether your account grows or collapses

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Following traditional advice to "save money and get out of debt" without understanding the difference between good and bad debt
  **Solution:** Learn to classify debt by whether it puts money in your pocket (good) or takes it out (bad). Use debt strategically to acquire assets.

- **Problem:** Focusing only on earned income from a job and neglecting asset building
  **Solution:** Shift your primary focus from income columns to the asset column on your financial statement. Let assets generate the income you need.

- **Problem:** Letting fear of loss prevent you from taking calculated risks during market downturns
  **Solution:** Reframe fear as a signal to educate yourself further. As Kiyosaki notes, the first way you understand risk is by looking in the mirror—you are the risk, not the investment.

- **Problem:** Investing in assets without understanding the underlying financial mechanics
  **Solution:** Study financial statements, learn to read cash flow statements, and understand the tax implications of any investment before committing capital.

- **Problem:** Living paycheck to paycheck despite working hard and following conventional wisdom
  **Solution:** Recognize that the school system teaches you to be an employee, not an investor. Take responsibility for your financial education and stop expecting employers or the government to secure your future.
