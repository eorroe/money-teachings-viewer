# Money Teaching Title

## Overview

This money teaching explains how to maximize profit potential on a trade by 10x or more by shifting from being personally focused on P&L to being market-focused on the stock's tape, momentum, and volume. Both traders in the example saw the same setup at the same price, but one made $200 by exiting too early and mentally checking out, while the other made $2,000 by listening to the stock, adding to the position when momentum confirmed, and riding the trade until the 9 EMA broke.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to stop leaving money on the table after entering a good trade
- When you find yourself mentally checking your P&L instead of watching the tape
- When the stock continues moving in your favor after you already exited
- When you want to learn how to let winners run without increasing your initial risk

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup Before Entering

Watch for three potential trade types: a hitchhiker (quick sideways consolidation), a 9 EMA continuation trade (buyers step in as price approaches the 9 EMA), or a VWAP continuation trade (bounce off VWAP with buying pressure). Study the setup beforehand so you know when the opportunity should be presented.

### Step 2: Watch the Tape Patiently

Do not act until you see a green candle closing above its open with volume higher than the prior few candles, and the bid stack (the list of buy orders on the order book) is stepping up. Wait for buyers to show consistent strength, not just a single wick or a random spike. Calculate where your stop will go (below the pullback low or the 9 EMA if it is rising) and where your entry price target is before the opportunity fully develops.

### Step 3: Start with Your Stop

Before entering any trade, determine your stop level. Use the lowest low of the most recent pullback (the last down-candle before the current green candle) as your stop reference, or a clearly visible support level on the chart where prior price reversals occurred. Do not use an arbitrary number. Decide how much risk you want to take on relative to your daily stop (typically 1-2% of your account). This gives you an exact exit price before you ever enter the trade.

### Step 4: Get In When the Tape Confirms

Enter the trade only when you see a green candle closing above its open with volume higher than the prior two to three candles, and the bid stack is actively stepping up. Do not enter on a single long wick, a red candle, or low volume. Do not guess; react to what the tape is showing you.

### Step 5: Do Not Make It All About Your P&L

Once you are in the trade, stop watching your P&L and start listening to the stock. Do not mentally check out after hitting a self-proclaimed target. If you focus on your P&L, you will miss signals that large buyers are still involved and momentum is building.

### Step 6: Recognize When the Trade Is Evolving

A setup can transition into a better trade. If the stock continues to show strength after your initial entry, it may shift from a simple VWAP continuation scalp into a hitchhiker or even a larger momentum trade. Watch for volume increases, persistent buying, and the 9 EMA curling back up.

### Step 7: Add to the Position When the Story Changes

If the tape confirms that the trade is real, add to your position by buying more shares within 1-2% of your original entry price — for example, if your original entry was $50.00, your add-on buys must fall between $49.00 and $51.00. Do not add above that range, as it means you are chasing. Keep your original stop in place or move it up to the lows of the latest pullback. This lets you increase your profit potential without increasing your total risk beyond what you already committed.

### Step 8: Use the Mantra "Is It Real?"

Repeat the question "Is it real?" to yourself while in the trade. If it is real, the stock will continue to grow and momentum will build. If it is not, the opportunity will start to decompose and break down. This mantra keeps you calm, prevents you from forcing trades that are not there, and helps you stay in trades that are genuine.

### Step 9: Exit When the 9 EMA Breaks

For a momentum trade, your exit signal is a full candle (i.e., a candle that has closed) whose low is below the 9 EMA line. Wait for that candle to fully close before exiting — do not exit intra-candle on a wick that merely touches the 9 EMA. Do not try to top tick the move or sell into every spike. Let the stock tell you when it is done. If the momentum is truly on your side, the trade will often get better after you enter, and your risk will shrink as the price moves in your favor.

## Examples

### Example 1: Steve's $200 Trade (Exiting Too Early)

Steve identified the same setup as Paula and entered at the same price. He caught a solid VWAP continuation move and made about $200. But as soon as he hit his target, he mentally checked out. He stopped listening to the tape and missed the shift where buyers kept stepping in, the volume accelerated, and the stock set up a hitchhiker and then a larger momentum trade. He was focused on his P&L instead of the stock.

### Example 2: Paula's $2,000 Trade (Listening to the Stock)

Paula entered at the same price with the same initial risk. But she stayed focused on the tape. She saw the bid stepping up, volume accelerating, and the 9 EMA curling back up. She recognized the trade was evolving and added to her position. She used the mantra "Is it real?" to stay calm and ride the momentum. She held until the 9 EMA broke and made roughly $2,000 without ever increasing her initial risk.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study your setups beforehand so you recognize the opportunity when it appears
- ✅ Start with your stop and calculate risk before entering
- ✅ Watch the tape patiently and wait for a distinct change before acting
- ✅ Focus on the stock, not your P&L, while in a trade
- ✅ Add to the position when momentum confirms the trade is real
- ✅ Use a simple mantra like "Is it real?" to stay grounded and avoid premature exits
- ✅ Exit on a close below the 9 EMA for momentum trades instead of trying to pick tops

## Keep In Mind

- The difference between a $200 trade and a $2,000 trade is not size or luck; it is focus and listening.
- Good momentum stocks are often the most forgiving. They tend to pull back cleanly, set up multiple scalps, and allow you to add with confidence.
- When a stock is building momentum, it is actually riskier to take profits too early than to stay with it and listen.
- You are not missing trades; sometimes you are just missing the best part of the trades you are already taking.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never risk more than 1-2% of your trading capital on a single trade, regardless of how confident you feel about the setup.
- Adding to a position must be done at the same price zone as your original entry, not at a worse price. Do not chase.
- The "Is it real?" mantra is a discipline tool, not a guarantee. A trade can look real and still fail. Always respect your stop.
- This teaching is for educational purposes only and does not constitute financial advice. Always trade with money you can afford to lose.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Exiting a trade immediately after a quick profit because you feel satisfied.
  **Solution:** Do not let instant gratification override the tape. If the stock is still showing strength, the trade may not be over.
- **Problem:** Obsessing over P&L while in a trade.
  **Solution:** Shift your attention entirely to the stock. Watch volume, bid/offer changes, and momentum shifts. The market does not care about your daily target.
- **Problem:** Trying to top tick every move and selling into every spike.
  **Solution:** Use a clear exit rule: exit only after a full candle closes below the 9 EMA line, not on an intra-candle wick that briefly touches it. Let the stock fully close a candle below the 9 EMA before you exit.
- **Problem:** Thinking a setup and a trade are the same thing.
  **Solution:** A setup is the chart pattern; a trade is the live tape confirming that the pattern is real. Wait for the tape to confirm before you commit fully.
