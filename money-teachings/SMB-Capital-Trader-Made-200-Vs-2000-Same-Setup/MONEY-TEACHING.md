# SMB Capital: Two Traders, One Setup — $200 vs $2,000, Same Risk

## Overview

This money teaching explains how to maximize profit potential on a trade by 10x or more by shifting from being personally focused on P&L (profit and loss) to being market-focused on the stock's tape (price and volume data), momentum, and volume. Both traders in the example saw the same VWAP continuation setup at the same entry price, but one made about $200 by exiting too early and stopping his tape analysis, while the other made roughly $2,000 by listening to the stock, adding to the position when momentum confirmed, and riding the trade until a full candle closed below the 9 EMA (9-period Exponential Moving Average).

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to avoid ending a profitable trade before capturing its full potential
- When you find yourself mentally checking your P&L (profit and loss) instead of watching the tape (price and volume data)
- When the stock continues moving in your favor after you already exited
- When you want to learn how to hold a profitable position until the exit signal triggers, without increasing your initial risk

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup Before Entering

Watch for three potential trade types: a hitchhiker (quick sideways consolidation), a 9 EMA (9-period Exponential Moving Average) continuation trade (buyers step in as price approaches the 9 EMA), or a VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) continuation trade (bounce off VWAP with buying pressure). Study these three setups beforehand so you can recognize the entry signal — a green candle (a candlestick whose close is above its open) closing above its open with volume above the prior two or three candles and the bid stack stepping up — when the conditions appear.

### Step 2: Watch the Tape Patiently

Do not act until you see a green candle closing above its open with volume higher than the prior two to three candles, and the bid stack (the list of buy orders on the order book) is stepping up. Wait for buyers to show sustained buying pressure across multiple consecutive candles, not just a single long upper wick or an isolated volume spike. Calculate where your stop will go — below the lowest low of the most recent pullback, or below the rising 9 EMA if it sits above that pullback low — and plan to enter at or slightly above the high of the confirmation candle before the opportunity fully develops.

### Step 3: Start with Your Stop

Before entering any trade, determine your stop level. Use the lowest low of the most recent pullback (the last down-candle before the current green candle) as your stop reference, or a support level on the chart confirmed by at least two prior price reversals. Base your stop on the pullback low or a confirmed support level; do not choose a stop based on a fixed dollar amount unrelated to the chart. Decide how much risk you want to take on relative to your maximum daily loss limit (typically 1–2% of your trading capital). This gives you an exact exit price before you ever enter the trade.

### Step 4: Get In When the Tape Confirms

Enter the trade only when you see a green candle closing above its open with volume higher than the prior two or three candles, and the bid stack is actively stepping up (the visible buy orders on the order book are increasing in size and price). Do not enter on a wick extending more than 50% of the candle's total range, a red candle, or volume below the average of the prior 20 candles. Do not guess; react to what the tape (price and volume data) is showing you.

### Step 5: Do Not Make It All About Your P&L

Once you are in the trade, stop watching your P&L (profit and loss) and start listening to the stock. Do not stop your tape analysis after hitting a target you set for yourself before entering. If you focus on your P&L, you are likely to miss signals that large buyers are still involved and momentum is building.

### Step 6: Recognize When the Trade Is Evolving

A setup can transition into a better trade. If the stock continues making higher highs with volume rising above the prior 20-candle average after your initial entry, it may shift from a simple VWAP continuation scalp into a hitchhiker (quick sideways consolidation) or even a larger momentum trade. Watch for volume rising above the prior 20-candle average, sustained bid pressure for at least 3 consecutive candles, and the 9 EMA slope turning positive (rising).

### Step 7: Add to the Position When the Story Changes

If the tape confirms the trade is valid — volume is above the prior 20-candle average, the bid stack is stepping up, and the 9 EMA slope is turning positive (rising) — add to your position by buying more shares within 1-2% of your original entry price — for example, if your original entry was $50.00, your add-on buys must fall between $49.00 and $51.00. Do not add above that range, as it means you are chasing. Keep your original stop in place or move it up to the lows of the latest pullback. This can increase your profit potential if you keep your total risk at or below your original risk level.

### Step 8: Use the Mantra "Is It Real?"

Repeat the question "Is it real?" to yourself while in the trade. If the trade is valid, the stock will continue making higher highs with above-average volume and the 9 EMA will keep rising. If the trade is not valid, the opportunity will lose momentum, volume will drop, and the 9 EMA will flatten or turn down. This mantra keeps you calm, prevents you from forcing trades that lack tape confirmation, and helps you stay in trades that are supported by sustained volume and a rising 9 EMA.

### Step 9: Exit When the 9 EMA Breaks

For a momentum trade, your exit signal is a full candle (i.e., a candle that has closed) closing below the 9 EMA. Wait for that candle to fully close before exiting — do not exit intra-candle on a wick that merely touches the 9 EMA. Do not try to sell at the exact peak of the move or sell into every sudden sharp price move. Let the 9 EMA break signal tell you when to exit. If the momentum is truly on your side, the trade will often get better after you enter, and your risk will shrink as the price moves in your favor.

## Examples

### Example 1: Steve's $200 Trade (Exiting Too Early)

Steve identified the same VWAP continuation setup as Paula and entered at the same price. He caught a solid VWAP continuation move and made about $200. But as soon as he hit his target, he stopped his tape analysis. He stopped watching the tape and missed the shift where buy orders kept stepping up on the order book, volume rose above the prior 20-candle average, and the stock formed a hitchhiker (quick sideways consolidation) and then a larger momentum trade. He was focused on his P&L (profit and loss) instead of the tape.

### Example 2: Paula's $2,000 Trade (Listening to the Stock)

Paula entered at the same entry price as Steve with the same initial risk. But she stayed focused on the tape (price and volume data). She saw the bid orders stepping up on the order book, volume rising above the prior 20-candle average, and the 9 EMA slope turning positive (rising). She recognized the trade conditions had shifted and added to her position. She used the mantra "Is it real?" to stay calm and ride the momentum. She held until a full candle closed below the 9 EMA and made roughly $2,000 without ever increasing her initial risk.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Study the three setups described in Step 1 so you recognize the entry signal — a green candle closing above its open with volume above the prior two or three candles and the bid stack stepping up — when the conditions appear
- ✅ Start with your stop and calculate risk before entering
- ✅ Watch the tape patiently and wait for a green candle closing above its open with volume above the prior two or three candles and the bid stack stepping up before acting
- ✅ Watch the tape (price and volume data), not your P&L (profit and loss), while in a trade
- ✅ Add to the position when volume is above the prior 20-candle average, the bid stack is stepping up, and the 9 EMA slope is turning positive (rising)
- ✅ Use a simple mantra like "Is it real?" to stay calm and focused on the tape and avoid premature exits
- ✅ Exit on a close below the 9 EMA for momentum trades instead of trying to sell at the exact peak

## Keep In Mind

- In this example, the difference between the $200 outcome and the $2,000 outcome was not position size or luck; it was focus on the tape and listening to the stock's signals.
- Good momentum stocks often give traders multiple chances to enter and add positions. They tend to pull back to key levels without erratic price action, set up two or more sequential scalp opportunities, and give clear signals that a second or third entry is warranted.
- When a stock is building momentum, exiting too early can leave profit on the table — but always respect your predefined stop level.
- You are not missing trades; sometimes you are just ending a trade before the stock has completed its move.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never risk more than 1-2% of your trading capital on a single trade, regardless of how confident you feel about the setup.
- Adding to a position must be done within 1–2% of your original entry price, not above that range. Do not chase.
- The "Is it real?" mantra is a discipline tool, not a guarantee. A trade can show all the valid entry signals — volume above the prior 20-candle average, the bid stack stepping up, and the 9 EMA slope turning positive (rising) — and still move against you. Always respect your stop.
- This teaching is for educational purposes only and does not constitute financial advice. Always trade with money you can afford to lose.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Exiting a trade immediately after a quick profit because the initial profit meets your personal target.
  **Solution:** Do not let a personal profit target override the tape. If the stock is still making higher highs with above-average volume, the trade may not be over.
- **Problem:** Focusing on your profit numbers instead of the tape while in a trade.
  **Solution:** Shift your attention entirely to the tape. Watch volume, bid/offer changes, and momentum shifts. Your profit target does not change the market's direction.
- **Problem:** Trying to sell at the exact peak of every move and selling into every sudden sharp price move.
  **Solution:** Use a clear exit rule: exit only after a full candle closes below the 9 EMA, not on an intra-candle wick that briefly touches it. Wait for the full candle to close below the 9 EMA before you exit.
- **Problem:** Thinking the chart setup and the live trade are the same thing.
  **Solution:** A setup is the chart pattern; a trade is the live price and volume data confirming that the pattern is valid. Wait for the tape to confirm before you commit fully.
