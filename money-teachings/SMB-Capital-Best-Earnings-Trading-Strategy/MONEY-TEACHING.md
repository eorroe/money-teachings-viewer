# Best Earnings Trading Strategy (Hot Theme Getting Rewarded)

## Overview

This money teaching covers SMB Capital's fourth earnings trading strategy bucket: the "hot theme getting rewarded" play. It teaches traders how to identify market themes where early earnings reporters close at least 3% above the prior day's close on the first full trading session with volume at least 150% of the 20-day average daily volume (i.e., 50% above average), then use that signal to trade later reporters in the same theme. The strategy emphasizes a top-down approach where theme momentum (measured as the percentage of theme members posting gains over the 5 trading sessions immediately preceding the evaluation date: the date the 5th reporter in the theme closes its first full trading session) matters more than individual company fundamentals, allowing traders to capture price moves without needing to be fundamental analysts.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When trading earnings and looking for swing trade opportunities starting on first full trading session after the earnings announcement
  - When a specific sector ETF or theme is showing gains of at least 3% on day one with volume at least 150% of the 20-day average daily volume (i.e., 50% above average)
 - When you notice stocks in a theme showing closes higher than the prior day's close on the first full trading session after earnings and want to position ahead of later reporters
 - When a stock doesn't fit the first three earnings strategy buckets but sits in a theme where at least 3 of the first 5 reporters each close at least 3% above the prior day's close on day one with individual volume at least 50% above the 20-day average daily volume
  - When screening for setups: (1) first full trading session volume at least 50% above the 20-day average daily volume (150% of average; the trading session immediately following the earnings release, regardless of whether the earnings were released pre-market or after-hours), and (2) at the close of the first full trading session, the sector ETF above its 20-day moving average, and (3) at least 60% of theme members (stocks in the designated theme watchlist) above their 50-day moving average

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify Hot Themes Being Rewarded

Create a watchlist of leading market themes and track which stocks report earnings each quarter. Monitor days 1 through 5 after the earnings release for early reporters in each theme. A theme is "being rewarded" when at least 3 of the first 5 reporters show follow-through after earnings: the stock's day-one close is at least 3% above the prior day's close with volume at least 150% of the 20-day average (each close at least 3% above the prior day's close on the first full trading session), even if a stock gaps down within the first 30 minutes of trading (when the theme is being rewarded, the only hard requirement is that the stock closes at least 3% above the prior day's close on day one). Look for patterns where each of those early reporters closes at least 3% above the prior day's close on the first full trading session after reporting. Also consider the theme rewarded if a major contract win (valued at $10M or more, or representing a new market entry) or at least 3 Wall Street analysts either upgrade the stock or raise their price targets by at least 10% within 5 trading days for the entire theme.

### Step 2: Screen for Later Reporters in the Rewarded Theme

Once a theme is confirmed as being rewarded when at least 3 of the first 5 reporters in the theme close at least 3% above the prior day's close on day one, identify stocks in that theme that have not yet reported. Focus on names with upcoming earnings dates. Prioritize pure plays (companies deriving at least 70% of revenue from the specific theme over the trailing twelve months (based on 10-K segment disclosures, Bloomberg sector revenue estimates, or other reputable financial data providers such as FactSet or Refinitiv; accept a ±5% margin of error when using Bloomberg, FactSet, or Refinitiv estimates; apply no tolerance when using 10-K segment data)) within the theme over more diversified companies (companies deriving less than 70% of revenue from the specific theme) to capture moves. Require float under 100 million shares and short interest above 10%. Stocks meeting both criteria have historically offered a risk/reward ratio of at least 2:1 based on SMB Capital internal data.

### Step 3: Evaluate the Setup Against Standard Criteria

Even within the hot theme bucket, confirm technical and volume criteria: a breakout above the most recent weekly chart resistance level (the highest level where the stock has been rejected at least twice on the weekly timeframe within the last 20 weeks (a rejection is a weekly bar whose high touches or exceeds the level and whose close is below the level)), at least 50% above-average volume (150% of the 20-day average daily volume), an overnight session where price stays within 2% of the prior trading day's closing price AND holds above the pre-market low established within the first 15 minutes (if the overnight move exceeds 2%, require additional confirmation such as the sector ETF also above its 20-day MA before entering), and a single 30-minute opening bar with volume at least 2σ above the 20-day average of all first-30-minute bars. Look for a pattern: successively higher highs and higher lows from the after-hours session into pre-market and through the open.

### Step 4: Enter on Day One With Confirmation

Buy when price breaks above the pre-market high established before the open on volume at least 1.5 standard deviations above the 20-day average daily volume. Consider scaling in on dips if: (1) the first 30-minute bar closes above the pre-market high on volume at least 50% above the 20-day average daily volume, AND (2) buying pressure is sustained for at least 3 consecutive 5-minute intervals (buying pressure means each 5-minute bar closes above the prior 5-minute bar's close on volume at least 150% of the 20-day average for that same 5-minute time-of-day bucket). Recognize that for stocks that rank in the top 20% of their sector ETF's constituents by 3-month price performance and are members of a hot theme, the opening drive may be the only upward price phase, and it may occur without a 2% intraday pullback for at least the first hour of trading. If the stock closes within 1% of the day's high on day one with volume at least two standard deviations above average, hold for the swing trade continuation.

### Step 5: Manage the Swing Trade

After day one, watch for pullbacks to the 10-day exponential moving average (10 EMA, computed on daily bars) as potential add points. Ride the theme momentum (measured as the percentage of theme members posting gains over the 5 trading sessions immediately preceding the evaluation date: the date the 5th reporter in the theme's earnings calendar for the quarter closes its first full trading session (the first 5 reporters are ordered by their first-full-session close date)) as long as the theme's sector ETF continues closing higher than the prior day's close on each of the first 5 consecutive trading sessions after day one (identify the specific sector ETF for the theme when bucketing the theme). Exit if the theme's cumulative return underperforms the S&P 500 by 3 percentage points over any rolling 5-trading-day period, or if the stock loses relative strength to the sector ETF (cumulative underperformance of at least 5% over the trailing 10 trading days, measured as (stock 10-day return) minus (sector ETF 10-day return), where both returns are measured from the close of day one of the earnings reaction), or if the stock underperforms other names in the theme by at least 5%.

## Examples

### Example 1: Fiber Optics and Photonics Theme (AAOI Trade)

In Q1 2024, the fiber optics and photonics theme became the best-performing sector in the S&P 500 over the preceding three calendar months (Q4 2023) after AI-driven data center demand surged. LITE reported first with quarter-over-quarter EPS growth exceeding 50% and revenue growth exceeding 30% and gapped up. COHR reported next with revenue growth exceeding 20% year-over-year but a margin miss, initially gapping down before price reversed and closed at least 3% above the prior day's close on volume exceeding 150% of the 20-day average, confirming the theme was being rewarded. When AAOI reported later in the same quarter, it had not yet delivered a strong EPS beat and its revenue growth acceleration (a year-over-year revenue growth rate that has increased by at least 10 percentage points from the prior year's rate), but it was not yet EPS positive after three consecutive quarters of net losses, but at least 3 of the first 4 reporters in the theme posted gains on day one. AAOI also had lower float (~70 million shares), short interest of approximately 14.5%, and was breaking out of a multi-year weekly and monthly base. It opened near $60, gapped to approximately $117 in pre-market trading, and increased approximately 95% from a day-one entry near $60 to a day-two close near $117.

### Example 2: Retail Theme Pattern

This same strategy applies across sectors. In retail, when the first three or four retail names report a strong EPS beat and gap up and sustain above the prior day's high for at least one full trading session with volume at least 50% above the 20-day average daily volume on the first trading day, subsequent retail reporters present setups. The theme momentum outweighs individual company differences, so later reporters offer trades once the pattern is established.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Track theme lists and earnings calendars so you know exactly when each name reports
- ✅ Use a top-down approach: identify rewarded themes first, then find later reporters
- ✅ Confirm first full trading session volume and price action before entering
 - ✅ Look for short interest above 10% and float below 100 million shares (SMB Capital's internal backtest shows a median 2:1 risk/reward ratio for qualifying names)
- ✅ Keep the process simple: you do not need to be a fundamental analyst
 - ✅ Review the 8 most recent consecutive earnings quarters for the theme to develop pattern recognition; if fewer than 8 quarters of data exist, review all available quarters

## Keep In Mind

- The fourth bucket (hot theme getting rewarded) is a deliberate exception to the playbook's default priority on stocks reporting their first quarter of accelerating EPS and revenue growth after a period of decline. When the entire theme closes higher on day one with above-average volume, a stock can break out even without a year-over-year EPS growth exceeding 50% and revenue growth exceeding 30%.
- Theme momentum can reverse quickly. Each earnings season has its own characteristics, and what works one quarter may not work the next.
 - Not all names in a theme trade equally. Within themes where at least 3 of the first 5 reporters close higher on day one, according to SMB Capital's internal backtest data, companies deriving at least 70% of revenue from the specific theme over the trailing twelve months, with 3-month price momentum above 20% and 14-period Relative Strength Index (RSI) above 50, have average returns at least 5% higher than the sector ETF over a 20-day period (this is an internal estimate, not a guarantee; individual results may vary) compared to more diversified companies.
- The goal is to identify the pattern and enter positions pre-market, not mid-day after the move has already occurred.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never rely solely on theme momentum (measured as the percentage of theme members posting gains over the 5 trading sessions immediately preceding the evaluation date) without confirming volume and technical criteria on the individual stock.
- Be aware that stocks with float below 100 million shares and short interest above 10% can reverse by at least 10% from entry price if the theme's cumulative return underperforms the S&P 500 by 3 percentage points over any rolling 5-trading-day period.
 - Earnings trading involves risk. Always risk no more than 1% of your total trading account equity on any single trade, and place a stop-loss at 2× ATR below entry. In a gap-through scenario, exit at the next available market price.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting until after the market open to recognize the first 2–3 reporters in a theme gap up and hold above opening price on day one after the move has already occurred
  **Solution:** Check theme lists and upcoming earnings dates before the market open and continue monitoring during the trading session
- **Problem:** Overanalyzing individual earnings reports in isolation instead of reading them in the context of the theme
  **Solution:** Bucket each report.
  - **Problem:** Missing the trade because the stock does not fit year-over-year EPS growth exceeding 50% and revenue growth exceeding 30%
   **Solution:** Recognize that the hot theme bucket is a valid fourth category and apply it when at least 3 of the first 5 reporters in the theme close higher on day one
  - **Problem:** Problem: Holding through a 5-day period where 50% of theme members (all stocks in the designated theme watchlist) close below their 20-day moving average for at least 3 of the 5 days without first re-evaluating a written note that specifies the expected catalyst, price target, and stop-loss level.
  **Solution:** Monitor relative strength (stock outperforms the sector ETF by 5% over the prior 10 days) across the theme; if at least 3 names close below the 10 EMA for at least 2 consecutive days on volume exceeding 150% of the 20-day average daily volume, exit regardless of individual stock fundamentals (this exit trigger supersedes the exit criteria in Step 5 when both the 50%-of-theme threshold and the 10 EMA exit trigger are activated)
