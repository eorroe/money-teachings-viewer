# Grading Trading Setups to Grow Your Account

## Overview

Traders who treat every trade setup as equal find that assigning a grade to each setup is a distinct advantage that separates winning traders from losing ones. This teaching explains how to categorize trades by grade (A+, A, A-, B, C), assign specific percentage allocations of your daily risk limit to each grade, and adopt a distinct risk management approach for each grade. This approach helps high-conviction opportunities generate returns while you filter out lower-grade setups.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to grow account size without changing your execution strategy
- When reviewing trade journals and need to separate A+ trades from B/C trades
- When setting daily, weekly, and monthly risk limits

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Your Risk Limit and Grade Percentages

Set a fixed maximum daily risk limit (for example, $1,000) as your starting point. Assign each grade a specific percentage of that limit:
- A+: up to 80% (for example, $800)
- A: 40%
- A-: 20%
- B: 10%
- C: 5%

### Step 2: Grade Every Setup Before Entering

Evaluate each potential trade using the criteria in your trading plan and assign a grade based on the following guidelines:
- A+: The best trade you see in a quarter. Meets every criterion in your plan with strong performance. Has a measurable trading advantage with confirming factors.
- A: A high-quality trade that meets the majority of A+ criteria but has one weakness. Represents stronger opportunities you see in a month.
- A-: A reliable trade that meets all required criteria with a measurable advantage. A regular weekly opportunity.
- B: A passable trade that meets entry criteria but does not meet A- or higher standards. Fills a gap when higher-grade trades are not available.
- C: An experimental trade where you are applying a new strategy or setup to see how it performs. Use only for experimentation, not for capital allocation.

### Step 3: Adopt the Matching Trader Personality

Adopt the following risk management approach based on each grade:
- A+ approach: Take two or more positions or adjust your entry using varied strategies. Be willing to accept losses (stop-outs) on individual legs if the overall market view remains valid. Express the trade through entry and exit methods.
- A/A- approach: Execute with precision using your typical position size. Stick strictly to your predefined trading rules and risk management plan.
- B/C approach: Reduce your position size to the minimum percentage for the grade. Be selective and only enter if the setup meets all required criteria. Prioritize protecting capital over generating returns. Do not take more trades than your monthly trade limit allows.

### Step 4: Track Stats Per Grade

Track ongoing performance for each grade: the percentage of profitable trades (win rate), the number of trades that were assigned the wrong grade (misgrades), and cumulative profit and loss (P&L). Review these statistics monthly to verify that B and C grades show a win rate below 50% or cumulative losses over a tracking period.

### Step 5: Avoid Overtrading Lower Grades

Set a monthly limit on the number of B-grade trades. If your B-grade statistics show a win rate below 40% or cumulative losses, reduce the B-grade risk allocation to the C-grade level (5%). Focus exclusively on A+, A, and A- trades.

### Step 6: Scale Risk by Raising the Max Limit

When your performance consistently meets your performance targets and you want to increase position sizes, do not change your risk percentages or trading approach. Maintain the same percentage allocation for each grade and simply increase your fixed daily risk limit.

## Examples

### Example 1: Gold Parabolic Blowoff Short (A+ Trade)

Gold opened with an upward gap into a rapid price surge. All criteria in the trading plan were satisfied: all four timeframes showed alignment above the Kelner indicator, price gaps had not been filled, price showed extension beyond its normal range, the Average True Range was elevated relative to the five-day low, and trading volume at the open was higher than average. This trade was graded A+ before the market opened. Using the A+ approach, the trader purchased put options at the market open as a hedge, added to the position when price broke below the opening range, and used entry strategies to maximize the opportunity.

### Example 2: FSLY Earnings (A-Grade Trade)

The underlying catalyst for FSLY was initially graded A+ due to an inflection in quarterly results, growth acceleration, alignment with a market theme, and a strong earnings call. However, a broader market decline reduced the overall market risk appetite, downgrading the trade to A grade. Using the A approach, the trader added to the position on pullbacks to the Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP), used Time Weighted Average Price (TWAP) execution to build the position gradually, and held for a multi-day swing trade. The trader did not treat it as a B trade by scalping in and out for quick profits.

### Example 3: B Trade Warning (Tesla and Ethereum In-Range)

Both the Tesla and Ethereum trades were B-grade setups. The assessment that "this could be something bigger" was actually a warning that the setup did not meet all criteria. The solution is to wait until all criteria in your trading plan are confirmed before entering, rather than projecting gains that the setup does not support.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Grade every trade setup before entering a position
- ✅ Maintain your daily, weekly, and monthly risk limits
- ✅ Track win rate and cumulative profit/loss for each grade
- ✅ Increase position size by raising your daily risk limit, not by changing your percentage allocations
- ✅ Be selective: prioritize A+, A, and A- trades and avoid B and C trades
- ✅ Review past trades monthly to improve grading accuracy

## Keep In Mind

- Choosing not to enter a trade is itself a decision that carries zero risk.
- Risking the same percentage of a larger daily risk limit should feel similar to risking the same percentage of a smaller limit. For example, risking 2% of $100,000 should feel the same as risking 2% of $50,000.
- A+ trades do not occur daily. B and C trades appear daily. Be selective with B and C setups and avoid them whenever possible.
- When trading B or C grades, reduce position size and increase selectivity rather than trying to maximize returns.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Risk limits act as protective boundaries to prevent losses beyond your allocated risk during periods when your strategy underperforms.
- Never increase position size on B or C trades simply because your account balance has grown.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking more B-grade trades than your monthly limit allows because they are readily available.
  **Solution:** Set a monthly limit on the number of B-grade trades and reduce B-grade risk allocation to C-grade level if statistics are poor.
- **Problem:** Waiting for the preferred entry point on an A+ trade and missing the opportunity.
  **Solution:** Trade the A+ situation using entry and exit strategies; let the market provide the entry.
- **Problem:** Increasing position size by changing your risk percentages or trading approach instead of raising your daily risk limit.
  **Solution:** Maintain the same percentage allocation for each grade and simply increase your fixed daily risk limit.
- **Problem:** Allowing observed patterns or attachment to an expected outcome to override your analysis of the actual setup.
  **Solution:** Stay flexible, follow price action, and use entry and exit strategies for the A+ trade.
