# Grading Trading Setups to Scale Your Account

## Overview

Most traders treat every trade setup as equal, but top-performing traders know that assigning a grade to each setup is the primary advantage that separates profitable traders from unprofitable ones. This teaching explains how to categorize trades by grade (A+, A, A-, B, C), assign specific percentage allocations of your daily risk limit to each grade, and adopt a distinct risk management approach for each grade. This ensures that rare, high-probability opportunities generate the majority of returns while lower-quality setups are filtered out.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to scale account size without changing your execution strategy
- When reviewing trade journals and need to separate A+ trades from B/C noise
- When setting daily, weekly, and monthly risk limits

## Steps

### Step 1: Define Your Risk Limit and Grade Percentages

Set a fixed maximum daily risk limit (for example, $1,000) as your starting point. Assign each grade a specific percentage of that limit:
- A+: up to 80% (for example, $800)
- A: 40%
- A-: 20%
- B: 10%
- C: 5%

### Step 2: Grade Every Setup Before Entering

Evaluate each potential trade using the criteria in your trading playbook and assign a grade based on the following guidelines:
- A+: The best trade you see all quarter. Meets every criterion in your playbook with exceptional strength. Has a clear, well-defined trading advantage with multiple confirming factors.
- A: A strong trade that meets most A+ criteria but has one minor weakness. Represents one of the better opportunities you see in a month.
- A-: A standard trade that meets all minimum criteria with a clear, confirmed advantage. A reliable weekly opportunity.
- B: An acceptable trade that meets basic criteria but lacks the strength of an A- or higher grade. Fills a gap when higher-grade trades are not available.
- C: A test trade where you are applying a new strategy or setup to see how it performs. Use only for experimentation, not for significant capital allocation.

### Step 3: Adopt the Matching Trader Personality

Adopt the following risk management approach based on each grade:
- A+ approach: Take multiple positions or adjust your entry using different strategies. Be willing to accept small losses (stop-outs) on individual legs if the overall thesis remains valid. Express the trade through various entry and exit methods.
- A/A- approach: Execute with precision using your standard position size. Stick strictly to your established trading rules and risk management plan.
- B/C approach: Reduce your position size to the minimum allowed for the grade. Be highly selective and only enter if the setup meets all minimum criteria. Prioritize protecting capital over generating returns. Do not take more trades than your monthly limit allows.

### Step 4: Track Stats Per Grade

Track ongoing performance for each grade: the percentage of profitable trades (win rate), the number of trades that were assigned the wrong grade (misgrades), and cumulative profit and loss (P&L). Review these statistics monthly to verify that B and C grades show a win rate below 50% or cumulative losses over time.

### Step 5: Avoid Overtrading Lower Grades

Set a monthly limit on the number of B-grade trades. If your B-grade statistics show a win rate below 40% or cumulative losses, reduce the B-grade risk allocation to the C-grade level (5%). Focus exclusively on A+, A, and A- trades.

### Step 6: Scale Risk by Raising the Max Limit

When your performance consistently meets your targets and you want to increase position sizes, do not change your risk percentages or trading approach. Maintain the same percentage allocation for each grade and simply increase your fixed daily risk limit.

## Examples

### Example 1: Gold Parabolic Blowoff Short (A+ Trade)

Gold opened with a large upward gap into a parabolic price surge. All criteria in the trading playbook were satisfied: all four timeframes showed alignment above the Kelner indicator, multiple price gaps had not been filled, price showed significant extension beyond its normal range, the Average True Range was large relative to the five-day low, and trading volume at the open was significantly higher than average. This trade was graded A+ before the market opened. Using the A+ approach, the trader purchased put options at the market open as a hedge, added to the position when price broke below the opening range, and used multiple entry strategies to maximize the opportunity.

### Example 2: FSLY Earnings (A-Grade Trade)

The underlying catalyst for FSLY was initially graded A+ due to a major inflection in quarterly results, accelerating growth, alignment with a strong market theme, and an excellent earnings call. However, a broader market crash reduced the overall market risk appetite, downgrading the trade to A grade. Using the A approach, the trader added to the position on pullbacks to the Volume Weighted Average Price (VWAP), used Time Weighted Average Price (TWAP) execution to build the position gradually, and held for a multi-day swing trade. The trader did not treat it as a B trade by scalping in and out for quick profits.

### Example 3: B Trade Warning (Tesla and Ethereum In-Range)

Both the Tesla and Ethereum trades were B-grade setups that felt promising. The subjective impression that "this could be something bigger" was actually a warning that the setup was incomplete and did not meet all criteria. The solution is to wait until all criteria in your playbook are confirmed before entering, rather than projecting potential gains that the setup does not support.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Grade every trade setup before entering a position
- ✅ Maintain your daily, weekly, and monthly risk limits
- ✅ Track win rate and cumulative profit/loss for each grade
- ✅ Increase position size by raising your daily risk limit, not by changing your percentage allocations
- ✅ Be selective: prioritize A+, A, and A- trades and avoid B and C trades
- ✅ Review past trades monthly to improve grading accuracy

## Keep In Mind

- Choosing not to enter a trade is itself a decision that carries zero risk.
- Risking the same percentage of a larger daily risk limit should feel identical to risking the same percentage of a smaller limit. For example, risking 2% of $100,000 should feel the same as risking 2% of $50,000.
- A+ trades occur a few times per quarter at most. B and C trades appear daily. Be highly selective with B and C setups and avoid them whenever possible.
- When trading B or C grades, reduce position size and increase selectivity rather than trying to generate large returns.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Risk limits act as protective boundaries to prevent large losses during periods when your strategy underperforms.
- Never increase position size on B or C trades simply because your account balance has grown.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking too many B-grade trades because they are readily available.
  **Solution:** Set a monthly limit on the number of B-grade trades and reduce B-grade risk allocation to C-grade level if statistics are poor.
- **Problem:** Waiting for the ideal entry point on an A+ trade and missing the opportunity.
  **Solution:** Trade the A+ situation using multiple entry and exit strategies; let the market provide the entry.
- **Problem:** Increasing position size by changing your risk percentages or trading approach instead of raising your daily risk limit.
  **Solution:** Maintain the same percentage allocation for each grade and simply increase your fixed daily risk limit.
- **Problem:** Allowing recently observed patterns or attachment to a particular outcome to override your analysis of the actual setup.
  **Solution:** Stay flexible, follow price action, and use multiple entry and exit strategies for the A+ trade.
