# Money Teaching: The Science of Persuasion and Modern Sales Mastery

## Overview

This money teaching distills the psychology, technique, and career strategy behind high-performance selling, based on Jeremy Miner's 23-year journey from struggling door-to-door salesperson to founder of one of the largest sales training organizations in the world. The core insight is that sales is not a natural talent — it is a learnable skill rooted in behavioral science. By mastering question design, tonality, emotional pacing, and the ability to get prospects to sell themselves, you can close higher-ticket deals, command premium income, and build a career that scales from individual contributor to business owner.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are entering sales for the first time and want to avoid the common mistakes that derail beginners
- When you are an experienced salesperson who wants to move from six figures to seven or eight figures
- When you run a sales organization and need to hire, train, and scale top performers
- When you want to understand why prospects resist buying and how to eliminate objections before they arise
- When you are building a personal brand as a sales expert and want to ensure your reputation matches your results

## Steps

### Step 1: Learn the Science of How People Make Decisions

Sales is applied behavioral science. Study how the human brain processes persuasion, including the difference between the emotional survival brain and the logical decision-making brain. Understand that most resistance comes from the prospect's protective mechanisms, not from a genuine lack of need. When you grasp the psychology behind why people say no, you can design conversations that bypass resistance and lead prospects to discover the problem and the solution on their own.

### Step 2: Start in High-Pressure, Low-Structure Sales Environments

The fastest way to develop sales skills is to start in a role where you must create opportunities rather than receive inbound leads. Door-to-door sales, cold calling, or SDR positions force you to react quickly, handle rejection, and learn what works under pressure. Inbound sales conversations are slower and more forgiving, which means skills develop more slowly. If you can master the ability to engage a stranger, build rapport in seconds, and recover from a slammed door, you will excel in any sales environment — including high-stakes, slow-moving enterprise deals.

### Step 3: Disarm Prospects by Breaking the Salesperson Pattern

Prospects are constantly bombarded with sales messages and have built up defensive mechanisms that trigger the moment they sense a pitch. Your first job in any conversation is to interrupt that pattern by sounding and acting nothing like the salespeople they expect. Use playful, curious, or familiar tonality instead of a generic professional script. Ask unexpected questions. Make them laugh. Lower their guard before you ever mention a product or price. The more you can make the conversation feel like a dialogue between equals, the faster trust builds.

### Step 4: Master Five Core Tonalities to Control the Conversation

Your tone communicates your intention more clearly than your words. Master these five tonalities and use them deliberately:

- **Curious:** Use when gathering information. It signals that you genuinely want to understand their situation.
- **Confused:** Use when a prospect says something emotional or vague. It prompts them to explain their pain in greater detail.
- **Challenging:** Use later in the conversation to prompt the prospect to defend why they need to change. It creates productive tension that moves them toward a decision.
- **Playful:** Use throughout to keep the interaction light and human. It prevents the conversation from feeling like an interrogation.
- **Concerned:** Use when a prospect is stuck or hesitant. A soft, empathetic tone signals that you care about their outcome, which encourages them to share their real objection.

### Step 5: Ask Consequence Questions to Create Emotional Urgency

People buy emotionally and justify logically. If your questions stay at a surface level, the prospect will never feel the pain of staying where they are. Ask consequence questions that force them to imagine what happens if they do nothing. For example: "If you lose Dan's income years earlier than you expected, how would you pay for the house and the kids' school?" Pause. Let the question land. Slow down. If you rush through the question, you will get a knee-jerk dismissal instead of a real emotional response. The more they feel the future pain, the more motivated they become to solve it.

### Step 6: Prevent Objections Instead of Handling Them

Most objections are not genuine barriers — they are signals that the prospect is still uncertain. Uncertainty is created by the salesperson's failure to get the prospect emotionally invested in the outcome. Focus on objection prevention rather than objection handling. Build enough certainty during the conversation that the prospect does not need to stall, delay, or deflect. When you do encounter resistance, trace it back to a gap in your questioning or tonality earlier in the conversation, not to a stubborn prospect.

### Step 7: Listen Actively and Interject With Purpose

Listening is one of the most underrated sales skills, but passive listening is dangerous. Prospects will ramble if you let them, and their brains will become disorganized. Ask a question, listen deeply, and then interject with a clarifying or reframing statement that pulls them back to the core issue. You are not ignoring their story — you are guiding it toward the decision they came to make. The best salespeople speak for a small percentage of the conversation but control the direction of the entire exchange.

### Step 8: Build Skill Level to Build Confidence

Confidence is not something you pump yourself up to feel before a call — it is a byproduct of skill. The more techniques, frameworks, and tonal variations you have mastered, the more naturally confident you will become in any sales situation. If your skill level is at a five but your confidence needs to be at a ten, you will crash the moment a prospect pushes back. Invest in deliberate practice: study top performers, record your own calls, hire coaches who have actually achieved the results you want, and rehearse until your skills become automatic.

### Step 9: Scale From Individual Contributor to Business Owner by Hiring Complementary Strengths

The skills that make you a top salesperson are not the same skills required to run a company. When you transition from selling to scaling a business, surround yourself with people who have strengths in the areas where you are weakest. Hire a COO for operations, a CMO for marketing, and a revenue leader for sales. Choose a business partner whose skills and vision align with yours, and who can make decisions when you cannot. The fastest way to stall growth is to keep doing everything yourself because you do not trust anyone else to do it well.

### Step 10: Build a Personal Brand Backed by Real Results

Personal branding is powerful, but only if it is anchored in verified skill and results. Do not rush to build a public profile before you have the track record to support it. Focus first on mastering your craft, generating consistent results, and developing a unique methodology. Once you have credibility, share your framework publicly through content, speaking, and writing. Protect your brand by staying consistent in your message and avoiding trend-chasing. If you pivot from sales training to crypto to AI within eighteen months, people will not know what you stand for — and trust erodes.

## Examples

### Example 1: Disarming a Prospect With Playful Tonality

A salesperson on a Zoom call with a prospect begins with the typical "How are you doing today?" and gets a polite but guarded response. Instead of launching into a script, the salesperson uses a playful, familiar tone and says, "Just hanging out, being the boring guy. What about you?" The unexpected tone and low-pressure framing cause the prospect to relax and respond naturally. The conversation shifts from a formal pitch to a genuine dialogue within the first thirty seconds, setting the stage for deeper questioning later.

### Example 2: Using a Consequence Question in a Life Insurance Conversation

A life insurance salesperson is speaking with a prospect who is interested but hesitant. Instead of pushing for the close, the salesperson slows down and asks: "If something happened to you years before you expected, and your spouse had to keep the house and pay for the kids' school without your income — how would that feel?" The prospect pauses, the emotional weight of the question registers, and the logical objections about price fade. The prospect is no longer weighing a policy against a cost; they are weighing security against uncertainty.

### Example 3: Transitioning From Top Salesperson to Company Owner

A salesperson earning multiple seven figures realizes that the skills that made him great at selling — persuasion, relationship building, and deal closing — are not the same skills required to run a training company. He hires a CEO to handle operations, a CMO to lead marketing, and a sales leader to build the team. Instead of micromanaging, he focuses on refining the training methodology and protecting the brand. Revenue grows faster because the right people are in the right roles, and the founder is no longer the bottleneck.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Treat sales as a learnable craft, not a innate talent. Invest in deliberate practice and coaching.
- ✅ Start in high-pressure, unstructured sales roles to accelerate skill development.
- ✅ Use playful, curious, or familiar tonality to break the prospect's defensive pattern.
- ✅ Ask consequence questions that make the prospect feel the emotional cost of inaction.
- ✅ Focus on preventing objections rather than handling them after they appear.
- ✅ Listen actively but interject to keep the conversation on track.
- ✅ Build skill first, brand second. Results create credibility; content amplifies it.
- ✅ Hire for complementary strengths when scaling. You need a team, not a clone.

## Keep In Mind

- No one is born with advanced questioning, tonality, or closing skills. Every top performer learned through repetition and feedback.
- People do not buy from people they merely like — they buy from people they trust to deliver the best result. Like is a bonus; trust is the requirement.
- Confidence always follows skill level. If you are not confident, invest in more training rather than positive affirmations.
- AI will handle lower-ticket, transactional sales, but high-ticket, high-trust sales will always require a human who can navigate emotion, ambiguity, and relationship.
- The best salespeople do not feel like they are being sold — they feel like they made a smart decision.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not claim results or credentials that you have not verified. The market will expose exaggeration quickly.
- When hiring sales coaches, verify their track record. Ask for proof of the income levels they claim to have achieved.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that sales ability is innate and either you have it or you do not.
  **Solution:** Sales is a set of techniques — tonality, questioning, pacing — that anyone can learn. Study the mechanics, practice deliberately, and measure your results.
- **Problem:** Sounding like every other salesperson and triggering the prospect's defensive instinct.
  **Solution:** Interrupt the pattern before you pitch. Use unexpected tone, humor, or framing to make the conversation feel different from the sales calls they are used to.
- **Problem:** Rushing through questions and getting only surface-level answers.
  **Solution:** Slow down. Ask one question at a time, pause, and let the prospect think. Verbal pace the question so it feels like a conversation, not an interrogation.
- **Problem:** Trying to build a personal brand before you have the results to back it up.
  **Solution:** Focus on mastering your craft and producing consistent, measurable results. Once you have credibility, content and branding will amplify it. Before that, they will expose the gap.
