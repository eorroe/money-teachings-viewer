# They're Lying To You About Buying a House... Here's How To Get Rich ｜ Ken McElroy

## Overview

This teaching distills the core real estate wealth principles shared by Ken McElroy, who manages over $2 billion in real estate and built his empire starting as a property manager, into actionable steps. McElroy's central insight is that the path to wealth in real estate is not through flipping houses or chasing appreciation — it is through owning cash-flowing assets, using tax advantages to shield income, and leveraging other people's money to scale without taking on personal risk. He demonstrates that you can rebuild a billion-dollar portfolio from scratch in six months if you understand three things: how to find underperforming assets, how to force equity through better management, and how to use depreciation and cost segregation to eliminate current-year tax bills. Cash flow is the goal. Appreciation is a bonus.

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Property Management Is the Best Education

McElroy did not start as an investor. He started as a property manager. For seven or eight years he managed hundreds of properties of every type in every market condition. That experience taught him what broken buildings looked like, how tenants behaved, what expenses were inflated, and where owners were losing money. Before you buy your first property, work in property management, underwrite deals for an experienced investor, or find a mentor who will let you learn the operational side. You cannot buy what you cannot evaluate. The operational edge is where all the profit lives.

### Step 2: Force Equity by Buying What Is Broken

McElroy's best returns came from buying properties that were underperforming and fixing the operations, not the building. His first large purchase was a 182-unit building where the upstairs units would not rent because seniors did not want to walk upstairs. He raised the first-floor rent, did not discount the upstairs, and within months the whole property was full. The building was worth more not because he renovated it, but because he understood how to manage it. Always buy for how the property is running today. Manage it to how it could run. Buy wholesale. Manage to retail. That is where the forced equity lives.

### Step 3: Use Other People's Money Through Syndication

McElroy does not buy buildings with his own cash. He raises money from high-net-worth investors who want the returns real estate provides. The key is to make the math so simple that a potential investor can understand the risk and reward in 60 seconds. Show them the forced equity, the cash flow after reserves, and the exit strategy. If you cannot explain the deal simply, you do not understand it well enough to raise money for it. Use patient private capital from high-net-worth individuals who understand the business and will not force a sale during a market downturn. Avoid institutional money with mandatory return timelines.

### Step 4: Use Depreciation and Cost Segregation to Eliminate Taxes

Real estate provides two powerful tax advantages: bonus depreciation and cost segregation. These allow you to write off large portions of the property value in the current year, dramatically reducing or eliminating your tax bill on income-producing real estate. McElroy loves real estate partly because he can buy it with other people's money, cash flow it, and shield that cash flow from taxes. Refinance the property later to pull out tax-free cash, then redeploy that capital into more assets. The combination of OPM, cash flow, and tax shields is what makes real estate a wealth accelerant at scale.

### Step 5: Find a Mentor Who Has Done What You Want to Do

McElroy met Charlie Dunlap at a YPO meeting and asked if he could meet with him once a month. He met with Charlie for ten years — for free. The condition was that he had to show up prepared with real questions. The best mentors are not the ones with the biggest social media followings. They are the ones who have actually built what you are trying to build and are willing to share the blueprint with someone who shows up ready to work. When you find a mentor, show up prepared, take the time seriously, and treat every meeting as if it is a gift — because it is.

### Step 6: Build Systems That Run Without You

At the peak of his operation, McElroy runs his entire $2 billion real estate business in 90 minutes a week. He does this by hiring the right people, building strong systems, and refusing to become the bottleneck. If you have to make every decision, you have built a job, not a business. Design your acquisitions, property management, and investor reporting so that a competent team can execute while you focus on strategy, relationships, and new opportunities. Money is for buying time back. The sooner you build systems that run without you, the sooner you have real freedom.

### Step 7: Always Focus on Cash Flow, Not Appreciation

Cash flow is the goal. Appreciation is a bonus. If a property does not cash flow from day one, it is a liability dressed up as an investment. McElroy does not sell properties to realize gains — he refinances them to pull out tax-free cash while keeping the income stream. Selling triggers taxes. Refinancing does not. Focus on assets that pay you every month regardless of what the market is doing. The best investors are not the ones who are right the most — they are the ones who can afford to be wrong the longest. Control your downside through reserves and conservative underwriting.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Property Manager to Billion-Dollar Owner

McElroy took a job as an apartment manager while finishing school. One day the owner sat down with him and thanked him for turning the property around. McElroy calculated what the building was worth after his improvements and realized he was on the wrong side of the desk. He made a decision that day: he would be the owner, not the manager. It took years of saving, learning syndication, and buying undervalued assets, but that single moment of clarity defined the rest of his career. The lesson: a single moment of seeing the gap between where you are and where you want to be can change everything — if you act on it.

### Example 2: The Billboard Side Business

McElroy bought a two-acre parcel with a billboard on it for $250,000. The billboard was making only $5,000 a year. He moved the billboard, put an easement around it, rented it through a management company, and started cash-flowing $2,000–$3,000 per month. He later sold the land for $250,000 — meaning the billboard was free. He repeated the play with a second billboard, converted it to digital, and sold it to Clear Channel for a couple million. The side business eventually became a full media company that he used to offset taxes across his real estate portfolio. The lesson: look for assets that generate income while you hold them and can be sold separately from the land.

### Example 3: The 10-Year Mentor Relationship

McElroy met Charlie Dunlap at a YPO meeting and asked to meet once a month. Charlie said yes — but only if McElroy showed up prepared with meaningful questions. There were times McElroy took the meetings casually, and Charlie "slapped him upside the head" and reminded him to take the time seriously. Ten years of monthly meetings with someone who had already built what McElroy wanted to build compressed a decade of learning into a single mentorship. The cost was zero. The value was immeasurable. The lesson: access to a real mentor is one of the highest-ROI investments you can make — if you show up ready to work.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is educational content based on a publicly available podcast episode.
- Real estate syndications and private placements are only available to accredited investors in many jurisdictions. Consult a securities attorney before raising money from others.
- Cost segregation studies should be performed by qualified professionals. Improper application can result in IRS penalties.
- Always perform due diligence — environmental reports, title searches, and property inspections — before acquiring any real estate asset.
- When raising money from investors, always disclose the risks clearly and ensure they understand the nature of the investment before contributing capital.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying properties at retail prices from the MLS with no forced equity.
  **Solution:** Only buy properties that are underperforming. If it is already optimized, there is no deal.

- **Problem:** Relying on appreciation rather than cash flow to build wealth.
  **Solution:** If the property does not cash flow from day one, do not buy it. Appreciation is unpredictable. Cash flow is within your control.

- **Problem:** Taking on debt with mandatory repayment timelines from institutions.
  **Solution:** Use private capital from high-net-worth individuals who understand the business and will not force a sale during a market downturn.

- **Problem:** Trying to do everything yourself instead of building a team.
  **Solution:** The fastest way to scale is to hire the best operator you can find and give them equity in the outcome. Your time is better spent on deal flow and investor relations.

- **Problem:** Skipping the property management learning phase.
  **Solution:** Spend at least a few years in property management before you start buying. The operational knowledge you gain will save you millions in bad purchases.
