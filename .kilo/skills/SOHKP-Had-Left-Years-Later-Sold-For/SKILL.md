# He Had $15 Left… 10 Years Later He Sold For $1.5 Billion | Mike Wystrach

## Overview

Actionable insights from Mike Wystrach's journey from $15 in his bank account and a negative $1 million judgment to selling his company Freshly for $1.5 billion. The skill distills his lessons on mindset, entrepreneurship, business growth, and life design.

## When to Use This Skill

- Use when starting or scaling a business and need mindset and operational guidance
- Use when feeling stuck or overwhelmed as an entrepreneur
- Use when building a team and defining company culture
- Use when making decisions about business partnerships, investors, or co-founders
- Use when designing your life and business around personal strengths

## How It Works

### Step 1: Adopt the Right Mindset

- Recognize there are two types of people: those who think the world happens to them, and those who think they happen to the world
- Stop wishing and decide to take action today
- Understand that life is not done accidentally - spend time defining what you want in 10 years
- Remove yourself from envy and comparison; everyone is on their own timeline
- Practice gratitude and focus on building the life you want, not just monetary success

### Step 2: Take Action Despite Imperfect Conditions

- The only way to learn is by doing - podcasts and advice can only get you so far
- Embrace the messiness of starting a business
- If you're at your lowest point, you have nothing to lose - use that as fuel
- Manifest your goals through daily practice and written goal setting

### Step 3: Build on Your Strengths

- Understand who you are and build on your natural strengths
- Don't just fix weaknesses - lean into where you have energy and passion
- Create a business that plays to your strengths and eliminates what you dislike
- Example: Mike loved backend restaurant operations but hated front-of-house service, so he created Freshly as a back-of-house-only meal delivery service

### Step 4: Secure Your Financial Foundations

- Never sign a personal guarantee if you can avoid it
- Don't rely on student loans unless it's for a STEM degree; ROI is often not worth it
- Set up 529 education accounts for family members to provide optionality
- Real estate is a strong investment - your primary residence should be your first major asset purchase
- Liquid wealth over $100M is generally needed before flying private becomes justifiable

### Step 5: Get the Right People Around You

- Get a co-founder - the entrepreneurial journey is very lonely
- Recruit people who signed up for crazy challenges and want to be pushed
- Build a team of people with complementary skills and mindsets
- Choose your city/ecosystem based on your industry:
  - Influencer/crypto: Miami
  - CPG/services: Austin, LA, New York
  - Tech: San Francisco (primary), New York (secondary)
- Proximity is king - surround yourself with highly motivated, talented people

### Step 6: Execute with Relentless Focus

- Treat marketing like day trading with daily goals and benchmarks
- Set NPS (Net Promoter Score) targets and measure them religiously - aim for 80+ (Apple is ~73)
- Get obsessed with your metrics and refresh them constantly
- Make your mistakes only once - don't repeat errors
- Be prepared to work 7 days a week, especially in the early stages

### Step 7: Leverage Timing and Distribution

- Be at the right place at the right time with the right idea
- Amazon Prime forced UPS/FedEx to extend ground networks, enabling 2-day refrigerated shipping
- Facebook was a new, cheap advertising channel - get on new platforms early before they get expensive
- Warm up ad algorithms by testing in cheaper markets before scaling to the US

### Step 8: Raise Capital and Choose Partners Wisely

- Recruit a co-founder first, then seek investors
- Pitch relentlessly - expect 100 nos for every yes
- Choose investors who become true partners, not just money
- Look for investors who will be the first call when things go bad
- Own around 10% of a billion-dollar company and you still win if the team does well

### Step 9: Define Your Culture Early

- Define who you are and what you stand for before you need it
- Create a culture code that is simple and actionable (example: BEST = Best medicine, Excellence, Strong as a team, Transparency)
- Let people self-select out if they don't fit the culture
- Be explicit about what your culture means - avoid vague words like "accepting"

### Step 10: Design Your Life Beyond the Exit

- Money is a byproduct, not the goal
- Decide early if you want to build a 30-year company or build to sell
- Invest in experiences and assets that improve your daily life (primary residence, family experiences)
- Set up structures that help your family succeed (529s, education funding)
- Give back and help others start businesses - upward mobility is real

## Examples

### Example 1: From $15 to $1.5B

At 35, Mike had $15 in his bank account, no home, a bankrupt business, and a $1 million negative judgment. He used a 30-day Tony Robbins seminar to reset his mindset, practiced daily manifestation, and built Freshly into the third fastest growing food company to $500M in revenue. He sold to Nestle in October 2020 for $1.5B.

### Example 2: Freshly's Growth Strategy

Freshly grew by exploiting Amazon Prime's 2-day shipping mandate, which forced carriers to extend their ground networks. They also capitalized on Facebook advertising when it was still new and cheap, warming up ads in the Philippines before scaling to the US. 90% of their growth came from Facebook.

### Example 3: Building Petfolk with Lessons Learned

After Freshly, Mike applied lessons about customer obsession and culture to Petfolk. They set an NPS target of 80 (above Apple's 73) and measure it religiously. They defined their culture as BEST: Best medicine, Excellence, Strong as a team, Transparency.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Never sign a personal guarantee
- ✅ Get a co-founder for the entrepreneurial journey
- ✅ Define your 10-year vision before taking action
- ✅ Build on your strengths, not just fix weaknesses
- ✅ Measure customer satisfaction (NPS) religiously
- ✅ Define your culture early and live by it
- ✅ Get on new marketing channels before they get expensive
- ✅ Treat business metrics like day trading with daily goals
- ✅ Make each mistake only once
- ✅ Invest in your primary residence
- ✅ Set up 529 education accounts for family
- ✅ Surround yourself with people better than you

## Limitations

- These lessons are based on one entrepreneur's experience in food/CPG and may not apply to all industries
- The specific tactics (Facebook ads, Amazon Prime timing) were time-specific and may not be replicable today
- Mike acknowledges luck and perfect timing played a role in Freshly's exit
- Individual results will vary based on execution, market conditions, and personal circumstances

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Signing personal guarantees that put personal assets at risk
  **Solution:** Structure business financing to avoid personal liability whenever possible
- **Problem:** Trying to fix all weaknesses instead of leveraging strengths
  **Solution:** Double down on what you're naturally good at and enjoy
- **Problem:** Neglecting customer experience during rapid growth
  **Solution:** Set NPS targets and measure them daily; make customer obsession a core value
- **Problem:** Vague culture statements that mean nothing
  **Solution:** Define culture with specific, actionable principles and hold everyone accountable
- **Problem:** Waiting for the "right" time to start
  **Solution:** Today is the day - stop wishing and start doing
