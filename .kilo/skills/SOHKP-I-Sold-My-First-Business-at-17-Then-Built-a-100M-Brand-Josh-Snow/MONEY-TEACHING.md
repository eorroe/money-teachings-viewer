# I Sold My First Business at 17… Then Built a $100M Brand ｜ Josh Snow

## Overview

This money teaching breaks down Josh Snow's path from building websites as a teenager in a public library to founding the Snow teeth whitening brand, which grew into a nine-figure business. The core lessons cover how to use early constraints as fuel, map your skills to revenue, recruit elite talent, build multiple sales channels, and operate a high-performance brand through disciplined hiring and delegation.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are starting with limited capital or technical resources and need to build skills quickly
- When scaling a brand from seven to eight to nine figures in revenue
- When recruiting top talent from companies larger than your own
- When managing a team of high-performing individuals and deciding where to focus your own time
- When building a consumer product brand across digital and retail channels
- When the user asks about influencer marketing, brand building, or early entrepreneurship

## Steps

### Step 1: Get Paid to Learn Whatever Moves Your Revenue

Snow did not have money for formal education in marketing, so he learned SEO, web design, ad buying, and video editing by doing client work. Every skill he acquired was directly tied to making websites rank and convert. Identify the skills that most directly affect your ability to generate revenue, then learn them by building real projects rather than consuming courses. The gap between knowing and earning closes fastest when you practice under real commercial pressure.

### Step 2: Find Your Disproportionate Skill and Lead With It

At seven figures, an entrepreneur's unfair advantage is usually a self-taught skill that most competitors cannot match. For Snow, it was the full stack: design, media buying, SEO, and video. Map your own capabilities and find the one where you are demonstrably stronger than the people around you. That skill becomes your moat in the early and middle stages of growth.

### Step 3: Build Your Team by Recruiting Better Than You

Eight figures requires people who are better at specific functions than you are. Snow used LinkedIn to identify top performers at winning brands, reached out with genuine respect for their work, and offered paid consultations to extract insight before making a hire. When you approach talented people with curiosity and compensate them for their time, you build trust and get shortcuts that save months of trial and error.

### Step 4: Move Your People to Higher-Value Work

As your team matures, stop micromanaging and start elevating. Triple-train employees so they can handle multiple roles during contractions or recessions. Then move them into work that drives brand equity or profitable sales. If a task does not move either of those metrics, outsource it or eliminate it. Your job as the leader is to spend your own time on the highest-value decisions and trust your team to execute.

### Step 5: Add Sales Channels Methodically to Reach Nine Figures

Nine figures requires multiple distribution channels, not just one winning channel. Snow started with direct-to-consumer digital, then added retail partners like CVS, Target, and Nordstrom. Each channel should be tested with a budget, evaluated on profitability and scalability, and only scaled if it passes both tests. Do not assume a channel is done when it works; diversify before platform algorithms or ad costs shift against you.

## Examples

### Example 1: Learning by Building Client Work

A college student who wants to learn paid advertising offers to run campaigns for local businesses for free or at a steep discount in exchange for references. Within a year they have case studies, tested tactics, and enough confidence to launch their own brand or consulting practice.

### Example 2: Recruiting a Top Performer

An e-commerce brand needs a head of TikTok organic growth. The founder identifies five brands that are winning on TikTok, finds their social media directors on LinkedIn, and sends a short message acknowledging their results and offering a 30-minute paid consultation to ask how they structure their team. The conversation reveals a candidate who is under-recognized and ready for a bigger role.

### Example 3: Expanding Distribution Channels

A skincare brand that sells primarily on Amazon tests DTC on its own website with a small ad budget, then adds Target wholesale after proving unit economics. Each channel adds a new revenue stream and reduces dependence on any single platform.

## Best Practices

- Learn skills by doing paid client work, not passive consumption
- Identify and exploit your disproportionate skill before hiring someone to replace it
- Recruit talent from brands that are already winning in the areas you need
- Triple-train employees and move them to higher-value work as quickly as possible
- Test new sales channels with disciplined budgets before committing

## Keep In Mind

- Getting paid to learn is a strategy, not a fallback
- At seven figures you are the product; at nine figures your team and distribution are the product
- Recruiting is a continuous activity, not a one-time event

## Security & Safety Notes

- All advice is drawn from a publicly available podcast interview
- Do your own due diligence before making any business or investment decisions
- Consult qualified legal and financial advisors when structuring deals or hiring

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting until you are an expert before starting
  **Solution:** Start building and learning under real commercial pressure immediately

- **Problem:** Hiring to fix weaknesses instead of recruiting around strengths
  **Solution:** Hire for the specific gaps that are blocking your next growth stage

- **Problem:** Scaling a channel that is not profitable
  **Solution:** Set profitability and scalability thresholds before increasing spend

- **Problem:** Micromanaging high performers
  **Solution:** Give autonomy, set clear outcomes, and measure results
