# They're Lying To You About Debt... Here's How To Get Rich ｜ Robert Kiyosaki

## Overview

This teaching distills the core financial principles shared by Robert Kiyosaki, author of Rich Dad Poor Dad, into actionable steps. Kiyosaki's central argument is that the traditional advice about debt — "get out of debt and save money" — is dangerous in a fiat currency system. Since 1971, when Nixon took the US dollar off the gold standard, money became debt. In this system, savers are losers, debt is a tool, and the people who understand how to use it become wealthy. The key is to choose your teachers carefully, because most of the people telling you to avoid debt are employees who will never build wealth themselves.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are trying to understand the difference between good debt and bad debt
- When you want to learn how to use real estate to generate cash flow instead of relying on a salary
- When you are questioning traditional financial advice such as "save money" and "get out of debt"
- When you need to choose mentors, teachers, or financial advisors who actually have the results you want
- When you want to understand why your primary residence is not an asset
- When the user asks about financial literacy, debt strategy, real estate investing, or alternative financial education

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose Your Teachers Wisely

Most people learn about money from their parents, their school teachers, or their financial planner — all of whom are employees with a 401(k) and a need for job security. Their financial advice reflects their reality: get a job, work hard, save money, get out of debt, invest in a diversified portfolio of stocks. That advice will not make you rich. It will make you comfortable. If you want to build wealth, find teachers who have already built wealth in the environment you are entering. Choose mentors by their results, not their credentials.

### Step 2: Understand That Your House Is Not an Asset

An asset puts money in your pocket. A liability takes money out. Your primary residence takes money out every month in the form of mortgage payments, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance. It is a liability. Kiyosaki calls this out because it contradicts the most fundamental belief in the American middle class. If you want a home that is also an asset, turn it into a rental. Collect rent that exceeds the mortgage payment. Then it becomes an asset.

### Step 3: Learn to Use Debt as a Tool

After 1971, the US dollar became fiat — it has no intrinsic value and is created by debt. In this system, saving money is a losing strategy because inflation erodes the purchasing power of cash every year. The wealthy do not avoid debt — they use it strategically. They borrow at a fixed rate (often 4-5%) and invest in assets that generate 12-15% returns. The spread between what they pay and what they earn is their profit. This is how real estate investors build generational wealth without ever saving a large pile of cash.

### Step 4: Build Three Core Skills: Sales, Capital Raising, and Negotiation

Kiyosaki identifies the three skills that separate the wealthy from everyone else: the ability to sell, the ability to raise capital, and the ability to negotiate. If you cannot sell, you cannot get customers. If you cannot raise capital, you cannot scale. If you cannot negotiate, you will overpay for every deal. These skills are not taught in schools. You have to learn them by doing — preferably under the supervision of someone who has already mastered them.

### Step 5: Focus on Cash Flow, Not Salary

Salary income is the most taxed form of income and the hardest to scale. Cash flow — from rental properties, businesses, or royalties — is taxed at a lower rate and can grow without a corresponding increase in your time. Kiyosaki owns 15,000 rental properties that generate $200 per month each. That is $3 million per month in passive cash flow that continues while he sleeps. Build assets that generate cash flow first. Everything else is secondary.

### Step 6: Assemble a Team of Specialists

Kiyosaki does not try to do everything himself. He has the best attorneys, the best CPAs, and the best dealmakers in his network. He calls this "cheating" — and argues that it is the smartest thing you can do. A great CPA will save you more in taxes than they cost. A great attorney will protect you from deals that would otherwise destroy you. A great partner will bring skills you do not have. Find people who are better than you in their domain and build a team.

### Step 7: Study What the Rich Actually Do, Not What They Say

Kiyosaki points out that the wealthiest people on Earth — the ones who built billion-dollar empires — use debt constantly. They have mortgages on their homes, loans on their yachts and planes, and leverage on every real estate portfolio. The public narrative is that they earned cash and bought things outright. The reality is that they borrowed strategically and deployed capital at a higher rate of return. Study what wealthy people actually do with their balance sheets, not what they say in interviews.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Silver Coin Lesson

At 18, Kiyosaki held up a half-dollar and realized it was no longer silver — it was copper. He had been saving silver coins, only for his mother to spend his collection because she thought it was "dirty old money." The lesson was twofold: first, poor people do not understand the difference between real money and fiat currency. Second, you cannot trust anyone else to protect your financial future. If you do not understand what money is, you will lose it regardless of how much you earn.

### Example 2: The Marine Corps Lesson

Kiyosaki joined the Marines after graduating from Kings Point, the United States Merchant Marine Academy, where starting pay was $120,000 a year in 1969. His military instructor told him to go find out what it was like to get shot down in a B-17, captured, and forced to escape. Kiyosaki flew in Vietnam, went down three times, and broke his back on the third crash. The lesson: hard knocks build character. If you never fall down, you never learn how to get back up. Most people do not build wealth because they are too afraid to fail publicly.

### Example 3: The Rich Dad Formula

Kiyosaki's rich dad taught him the formula for wealth using Monopoly: four green houses, then a red hotel. But the real lesson was the 1031 tax-deferred exchange — you can swap one property for another and defer taxes indefinitely. This is how families stay rich for generations: they never sell, they always exchange. Every time you sell an asset and pay capital gains tax, you reset your compounding clock. Every time you do a 1031 exchange, you keep your capital working for you.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Choose teachers by their financial results, not their credentials or job titles
- ✅ Treat your primary residence as a liability until it generates positive cash flow
- ✅ Use fixed-rate debt to buy income-producing assets that generate returns higher than the interest rate
- ✅ Build the three core wealth skills: sales, capital raising, and negotiation
- ✅ Focus on cash flow before salary, appreciation, or job promotions
- ✅ Assemble a team of specialists who are better than you in their domain
- ✅ Study what wealthy people actually do with their balance sheets, not what they say
- ❌ Do not listen to financial advice from people who have never built significant wealth
- ❌ Do not treat your home as an investment unless it is producing positive monthly cash flow
- ❌ Do not save large amounts of cash in a fiat currency system without understanding inflation risk
- ❌ Do not try to do everything yourself — "cheat" by hiring specialists

## Keep In Mind

- "Poor people save money. Rich people invest. Wealthy people use other people's money." These are three different strategies with three different outcomes.
- The system was designed to produce employees, not owners. School teaches you to follow instructions, not to build businesses.
- Hard knocks are not optional — they are the mechanism through which most wealth is built. The people who never fall down never learn anything.
- Debt is not inherently good or bad. It is a tool. The outcome depends on what you deploy it into.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is educational content based on a publicly available podcast episode.
- Using leverage in real estate carries significant risk. If the property cash flow does not cover the debt service, you can lose the asset.
- 1031 exchanges have strict timelines and requirements. Consult a qualified intermediary and tax attorney before attempting one.
- Do not take on debt for consumables or liabilities. Only use leverage to acquire income-producing assets.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Listening to financial advice from people who have never built significant wealth.
  **Solution:** Audit your teachers. If their financial results do not match the results you want, stop listening.

- **Problem:** Treating your primary residence as your main investment.
  **Solution:** A house you live in is a lifestyle choice, not a wealth-building vehicle. Buy rental assets that pay you monthly cash flow.

- **Problem:** Avoiding all debt because of fear.
  **Solution:** Learn the difference between bad debt (consumer debt that takes money from you) and good debt (borrowed money that buys assets that pay you more than the interest cost).

- **Problem:** Trying to go it alone instead of building a team.
  **Solution:** Your attorney, CPA, and deal partners will make or break you. Hire the best people you can afford and structure deals so they are incentivized to make you money.
