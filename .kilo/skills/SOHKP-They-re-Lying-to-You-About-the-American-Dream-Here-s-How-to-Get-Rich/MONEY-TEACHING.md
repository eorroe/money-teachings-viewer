# They're Lying To You About the American Dream... Here's How to Get Rich

## Overview

This teaching distills the core principles shared by Abdullah "AK" Cudra — an emergency room doctor, entrepreneur, and owner of 13+ businesses — into actionable steps for building wealth while living a fulfilling life. AK's central insight is that the American Dream is not a home, a job, or a title. It is the freedom to design your own life, serve on your own terms, and build systems that continue to create value even when you are not in the room. True wealth is not measured by your bank account — it is measured by your ability to say yes to the opportunities that genuinely excite you and to say no to the ones that do not.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to build multiple streams of income without burning out
- When you are trying to figure out how to scale beyond a single profession or business
- When you want to understand the relationship between health, wealth, and time freedom
- When you need to hire and trust operators to run businesses on your behalf
- When you want to break out of routine and expand your thinking through travel and new experiences
- When the user asks about entrepreneurship, lifestyle design, multiple income streams, or building systems that outlast you

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Institutions, Not Just a Career

AK was an emergency room doctor when he went on a missions trip to East Africa and saw 250 people a day with a team of nine. He realized that as one person, he could only do so much. If he built an institution — a system, a company, an organization — he could serve thousands even when he was not there. The shift from practitioner to builder is the most important transition an entrepreneur can make. Stop asking "How do I do more?" and start asking "How do I build something that does it without me?"

### Step 2: Prioritize Health Before Wealth

AK treats wealthy patients who have given everything to build their businesses — and then been diagnosed with terminal illnesses who would trade every dollar to get their health back. His first rule for any entrepreneur is simple: health comes first. If you had to choose between $10 million and perfect health, you would choose health every time. Most people live as if they are guaranteed tomorrow. You are not. Protect your sleep, your nutrition, your movement, and your mental health with the same discipline you apply to your business.

### Step 3: Diversify Strategically, Not Randomly

The default answer to "should I diversify?" is no. Stay in your lane, master your craft, and scale it as far as it can go. Diversify only when you have already mastered your field and have the capital, the knowledge, and the team to enter a new one. AK diversifies not for the money but for the learning. Every new business teaches him a principle he can apply to the others. The best diversifications are adjacent to your existing strengths — they leverage what you already know.

### Step 4: Hire Operators With Track Records, Not Promises

When you hire someone to run a business unit, the first question is not "Do they seem nice?" or "Did they interview well?" It is "Have they done this exact thing before?" If they have never run the type of business you are hiring them for, the answer is no. They will tell you what you want to hear, but they do not have the pattern recognition to execute. Always background check, always verify results, and always set clear metrics so you can see early whether the hire is working.

### Step 5: Take Total Responsibility — No Blame, No excuses

AK's father taught him never to blame anyone for anything. If an employee fails, it is your fault for hiring them. If a business is underperforming, it is your fault for not building the right systems. Blaming others gives you an excuse to stay stuck. Taking responsibility gives you the power to fix it. "The blame game is fueled by ego," AK says. Drop the ego. Admit the mistake, find the root cause, and fix the system so it never happens again. The people who take responsibility for everything tend to be far happier than the people who blame everything on others.

### Step 6: Break Your Routine to Expand Your Thinking

AK travels constantly — not to relax, but to break his routine and force new perspectives. A trip to the Amazon jungle made him question everything he thought he knew about biology, nature, and his own career path. A trip to East Africa made him realize he wanted to build institutions, not just treat patients. You do not need to travel across the world. You need to do something that challenges your assumptions. Take a different route to work. Read a book by someone you disagree with. Talk to someone whose life looks nothing like yours. New input produces new output.

### Step 7: Lead by Example — Culture Is Not What You Say, It Is What You Do

AK's employees watch everything he does. If he picks up a piece of trash on the floor, they notice. If he stays late to solve a problem, they notice. If he treats a vendor or a customer with respect, they notice. Culture is not a document. It is the accumulated behavior of the leader over time. If you want your team to show up early, you show up early. If you want them to take ownership, you take ownership of your mistakes publicly. Culture flows downhill.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Africa Pivot

AK went to East Africa on a church missions trip as an ER doctor. He expected to treat patients. Instead, he found himself seeing 250 people a day with a team of nine, building systems that allowed non-medical volunteers to triage and distribute care. He realized that medicine was a one-person-at-a-time game, but systems could serve thousands. That trip changed his entire career arc. He came back to the US and started asking not "How do I become a better doctor?" but "How do I build systems that scale impact?"

### Example 2: The Amazon Self-Discovery Trip

AK was a computer science major in his second year of college when he randomly ended up on a research trip to the Amazon jungle. The trip cost $1,700 — a stretch for his family at the time. But three weeks in the jungle, disconnected from routine and surrounded by nature he had never cared about, made him question everything. He started reading about biology, asking why the world worked the way it did, and eventually decided to become a doctor. The trip cost $1,700. The career it produced was worth tens of millions.

### Example 3: The RV Park Purchase

AK bought an RV park because a friend owned it with a partner who passed away. The deal made sense on paper, and he liked the idea of building tiny homes around a lake. What did he know about RV parks? Nothing. So he developed a skill: how to analyze any business from 30,000 feet, identify where he lacked information, and hire a specialist to run the operation. He applied the same pattern he had used for every other business — learn the principles, find the gaps, hire the operator — and the RV park became another cash-flowing asset without consuming his time.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Build institutions and systems that outlast your personal involvement
- ✅ Put health first — your body and mind are the only assets that cannot be replaced
- ✅ Diversify only after you have mastered your primary field
- ✅ Hire operators with proven track records in the exact role you are filling
- ✅ Take full responsibility for every outcome — blame is a trap
- ✅ Break your routine regularly to force new perspectives
- ✅ Lead by example — culture is what the leader does, not what the leader says
- ❌ Do not diversify for the sake of diversification — it dilutes focus and capital
- ❌ Do not hire for potential when you can hire for proven results
- ❌ Do not sacrifice your health to build wealth — the math does not work
- ❌ Do not blame your team, your market, or your luck — own the outcome and fix the system

## Keep In Mind

- "If someone offered you $10 million but you could not wake up tomorrow, would you take it?" Every entrepreneur should ask this question regularly. It is a forcing function for clarity about what actually matters.
- You can only grow to what you are exposed to. If everyone around you is comfortable, you will stay comfortable. If you surround yourself with people building at the level you aspire to, your brain will start to map the path.
- The skills you learn in one domain are almost always transferable to another. AK's skill in the ER — triage, rapid decision-making under pressure, systems thinking — made him a better business owner.
- Time is the one resource you can never get back. Every business decision should be evaluated through the lens of: does this buy me more freedom or less?

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Diversifying into businesses you do not understand because you have extra cash.
  **Solution:** Stay in your lane until you have mastered it. Diversify only when you have the capital, the knowledge, and the team to do it well.

- **Problem:** Hiring based on charisma or interview performance rather than track record.
  **Solution:** Ask for specific results from previous roles. Verify them. A great interview is not the same as a great operator.

- **Problem:** Sacrificing health to build the business, then having the health to enjoy it.
  **Solution:** Schedule your sleep, your exercise, and your downtime as non-negotiable appointments. Your business will survive a slower pace. Your body might not survive an unsustainable one.

- **Problem:** Staying in routine and expecting different results.
  **Solution:** Routine is the enemy of growth. Force yourself into new environments at least once a quarter. Travel, read outside your field, and talk to people who disagree with you.
