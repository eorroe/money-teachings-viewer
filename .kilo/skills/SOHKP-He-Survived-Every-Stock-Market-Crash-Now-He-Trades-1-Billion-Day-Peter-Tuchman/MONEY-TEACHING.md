# He Survived Every Stock Market Crash… Now He Trades $1 Billion/Day | Peter Tuchman

## Overview

This money teaching distills the wisdom of Peter Tuchman, the longest-standing trader on the floor of the New York Stock Exchange, who has survived every major market crash since 1987. The central insight is that discipline, detachment from personal portfolio risk, and a service-first mindset allow you to thrive while others panic. You should use these teachings when trading, managing client capital, or navigating extreme market volatility.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When trading stocks, options, or other volatile assets
- When managing other people's money and need to avoid emotional decisions
- When you want to understand how to maintain composure during market crashes
- When deciding whether to day trade or invest for the long term
- When the user asks about trading psychology, market crashes, or Wall Street discipline

## Steps

### Step 1: Separate Your P&L From Your Client's P&L

If your profit and loss is tied to the same portfolio you are trading for clients, you will make emotional decisions. Structure your finances so that your personal wealth is not directly affected by daily market moves. Your only job is to execute correctly for the client; the market's direction is not your responsibility.

### Step 2: Trade With a Strategy That Survives Crashes

Build a rules-based system that works in both bull and bear markets. Peter Tuchman trades the S&P 500 with a disciplined strategy that has held through Black Monday, the internet bubble, 2008, COVID, and tariff selloffs. A system with rules removes the need to predict the future.

### Step 3: Recognize That Crashes Always Happen at Record Highs

History shows that major crashes do not occur after long declines; they occur when the market is at or near all-time highs. When everyone is euphoric and media is celebrating record levels, that is when you must tighten risk management and prepare for a violent selloff.

### Step 4: Rise to Adversity Instead of Running From It

Develop the habit of running toward chaos rather than away from it. The traders who survive are the ones who keep executing when everyone else is paralyzed. Think of it like emergency triage: when the room is on fire, the person who stays calm and follows protocol is the one who saves lives.

### Step 5: Avoid the Get-Rich-Quick Trap

Trading is not a path to rapid wealth if you are doing it to pay bills or feed your family. It requires disposable income, years of practice, and realistic expectations. The people who blow up accounts are usually the ones who need the money too badly to think clearly.

### Step 6: Mentor the Next Generation

Once you have survived multiple cycles, share your knowledge with younger traders. The best mentors teach risk management, emotional control, and process mastery. New traders coming from gaming or video-game backgrounds often have the focus and reflexes to succeed if they are taught the right habits.

## Examples

### Example 1: Surviving Black Monday

A 25-year-old clerk on the New York Stock Exchange floor worked through the chaos of Black Monday in 1987, when the Dow fell 608 points—the biggest percentage selloff in history. Orders were flying, machines were spitting out sell orders, and panic was everywhere. He kept executing correctly, did not crumble, and earned a reputation for performing under pressure.

### Example 2: The Jordan Belford Lesson

A famous stockbroker tried to recruit floor traders to front-run customer orders—buying stock for himself before executing a client's large buy order. The traders who took the deal were arrested by the FBI and sent to prison. The lesson: never compromise your integrity for short-term gains; reputations and freedom are worth more than any single trade.

### Example 3: Trading Without Owning Stock

A legendary trader deliberately avoided owning any stock to eliminate conflicts of interest. By not having a personal portfolio, he could focus entirely on serving his customers without being influenced by hope, fear, or greed. That discipline allowed him to trade calmly through crashes that ruined others.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Build a rules-based trading system that removes emotion
- ✅ Separate personal wealth from client portfolios
- ✅ Prepare for crashes when the market is at record highs
- ✅ Run toward chaos and volatility instead of hiding from it
- ✅ Treat trading as a skill that takes decades to master
- ✅ Mentor younger traders and pass on hard-won lessons
- ❌ Do not trade with money you cannot afford to lose
- ❌ Do not let your personal portfolio bias your client decisions
- ❌ Do not chase get-rich-quick schemes or meme stocks

## Keep In Mind

- Every major crash in history happened when the market was at a record high
- Greed and fear destroy more traders than bad strategies do
- The best traders are not the ones who pick the most winners; they are the ones who manage risk best
- Resilience is a skill you can build by repeatedly choosing to stay calm under pressure

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never front-run or use client information for personal gain; these are criminal offenses
- Use regulated platforms and brokers; avoid unregulated offshore firms
- Keep detailed records of all trades for compliance and review
- Do not trade with retirement funds or emergency savings

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Letting personal portfolio losses cloud judgment on client trades
  **Solution:** Structure finances so your wealth is not tied to daily P&L; focus only on correct execution

- **Problem:** Believing you can get rich quickly through day trading
  **Solution:** Start with disposable income, treat trading as a long-term craft, and expect years of modest gains before mastery

- **Problem:** Panic selling during crashes
  **Solution:** Remember that crashes happen at record highs and are part of the cycle; trust your system

- **Problem:** Letting ego drive trades instead of data
  **Solution:** Follow your written rules; if you are not writing down your strategy, you do not have one
