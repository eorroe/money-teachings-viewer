# I Had $78 in My Bank Account… Now I Own a Private Jet ｜ Vick Tipnes

## Overview

This money teaching distills actionable principles from Vick Tipnes' journey from $78 in his bank account to building Blackstone, the largest sleep medical device testing company in the United States. The content covers burning your boats, eliminating safety nets, serving customers with radical care, and building through relentless action.

**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=W4_kxlRzdMc

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When starting a business with no safety net or backup plan
- When operating in a niche industry with little competition
- When building a service business where customer experience is the differentiator
- When you need motivation to keep going after hitting rock bottom
- When the user asks about bootstrapping, healthcare businesses, or resilience

## Steps

### Step 1: Burn the Boats and Eliminate Plan B

Vick had $78.80 in his account in November 2014 and no lifeline. Both parents had passed away. He had no college degree, no trade, and no one to call. That absence of a safety net became his greatest asset because he had to succeed. Destroy every escape route so failure is not an option.

### Step 2: Welcome Suffering as a Teacher

Vick states that success teaches you nothing. Suffering is your best teacher. When every variable is against you, you are forced to adapt, innovate, and grow. Do not shy away from hardship. Step into it willingly because it forges the character required to hold wealth.

### Step 3: Build Through Customer Service Obsession

Vick built an empire in healthcare by caring about every phone call, every concern, and every follow-through. Service is a lost art. If the CEO does not obsess over service, no one else will. Answer the phone properly, do not put people on hold unnecessarily, and make every customer feel heard.

### Step 4: Lead From the Top Down

Do not hide behind policies. Get on the floor, listen to calls, critique mistakes in front of managers, and show your team exactly what great service looksounds like. Being a great owner means doing the unscalable and the unsexy work every single day.

### Step 5: Choose Partners Based on Values, Not Just Results

Vick learned from an earlier radiology business sale that wrong partners and excessive personal guarantees can wipe you out. Pick partners the way you would pick a spouse. Check morals, work ethic, philosophy, and energy. Success requires the right people alongside you.

## Examples

### Example 1: The $78 Bank Screenshot

Vick took a screenshot of his $78.80 bank balance in November 2014 as a reminder that no matter where he went from there, it could not get worse. That mindset turned fear into fuel.

### Example 2: The Radiology Sale Lesson

Vick built and sold a radiology company for $9 million but did not make money because of bad partners and too many personal guarantees. He learned to vet partners carefully and avoid over-leveraging personally.

### Example 3: The Service Standard

Vick criticized a receptionist who answered the phone with "Please hold for a brief moment." He taught his team to say "Hey, what can I help you with?" first, then offer to hold only if necessary. That level of nuance separates surviving businesses from thriving ones.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Eliminate all safety nets so failure is not an option
- ✅ Welcome hardship as a catalyst for growth
- ✅ Obsess over customer service from the CEO level down
- ✅ Vet partners using the same rigor you would use to choose a spouse
- ✅ Lead by example on the floor, not from behind a desk
- ❌ Don't keep a plan B that lets you quit when things get hard
- ❌ Don't ignore warning signs in partnerships because you need the money
- ❌ Don't delegate service standards to a policy manual

## Keep In Mind

- Real entrepreneurs thrive when everything is against them
- Money does not change the mindset that built it; you must grow into the person who can hold it
- Suffering is inevitable; welcoming it is optional but powerful
- Service excellence is a daily discipline, not a one-time training

## Security & Safety Notes

- Avoid personal guarantees on business debt whenever possible
- Keep personal and business finances strictly separated
- Never sign partnership agreements without legal review
- Always have documentation for business expenses in case of audit

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Keeping a safety net that makes quitting easy
  **Solution:** Burn the boats. Make success the only option.

- **Problem:** Ignoring bad partner behavior because capital is needed
  **Solution:** Slow down and vet values, morals, and work ethic before signing

- **Problem:** Letting service standards slip as the company grows
  **Solution:** The CEO must remain intimately involved in customer experience

- **Problem:** Thinking success will solve underlying character flaws
  **Solution:** Suffering teaches what success cannot. Keep growing through hardship.
