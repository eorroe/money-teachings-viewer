# Build a Business in 24 Hours Using AI Tools

## Overview

This Money Teaching shows you how to go from zero to a real paying customer in 24 hours using AI tools, a demand-first validation framework, and a repeatable process for identifying, building, branding, and launching a business online. The core idea is that AI is a force multiplier on good thinking—not a replacement for strategy—so every tool is only as good as the framework and research behind it.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to launch a new business quickly without a team of more than 2 people or more than $500 in upfront spending
- When you need to validate whether a product idea has real demand before building anything
- When you are building a landing page, brand identity, ad copy, or customer service system for an online store
- When you want to use AI tools to move faster without sacrificing brand quality or conversion rates

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify a Demand-Backed Product Idea Using Exploding Topics and the RRT Test

Start with demand, not a cool idea. Open Exploding Topics (an AI trend spotter) and scan search traffic, social media, and online retail data to find products people are actively searching for and buying. Once you have a list of candidates, apply the RRT test to each one in 3–5 minutes:

1. Does the business resist a recession? (Examples: pet care, garbage collection, health care—categories people keep spending on during hard times.)
2. Can you raise prices without losing customers? This is how you increase revenue without acquiring more customers.
3. Can technology improve margins or output by at least 15% relative to a manual alternative? AI tools, automation platforms, or software integrations must deliver a quantifiable efficiency gain.

If the answer is yes to all three, you have a business worth building. Choose the product that clears all three filters and has rising search volume and sales data to back it up.

### Step 2: Define a Specific Avatar Using Perplexity, ChatGPT, and Claude

Before building anything, deeply understand your buyer. A vague audience like "dog owner" is useless. You need a single specific person who sees your product and thinks "that was made for me." Build your avatar in three phases:

1. Open Perplexity and ask it to research your target demographic and the problem space. Perplexity returns answers with cited sources, so you get real-world evidence without AI hallucinations. Example prompt: research the pain points, spending habits, and online behavior of active women who walk their dogs daily.
2. Take Perplexity's research output and prompt ChatGPT to structure it into a full avatar profile. ChatGPT is best for fast, structured drafts. Give it a prompt like: "Based on this research, create a detailed buyer avatar for a hands-free dog leash product including demographics, daily routines, frustrations, purchasing habits, and who they follow online."
3. Paste both outputs into Claude and ask it to reconcile the two into a complete, data-backed avatar. Claude is best for deep, nuanced reasoning. Ask: "Compare the research from Perplexity and the avatar from ChatGPT. Synthesize them into one complete picture of our ideal buyer."

Name your avatar (e.g., "Jordan") and use her language, frustrations, and lifestyle in every piece of copy and design that follows.

### Step 3: Use WhisperFlow to Dictate Ideas and Prompts Instead of Typing

Because thinking is faster than typing, install and run WhisperFlow as a background voice-to-text tool on your computer. Dictate ideas, prompts, and copy directly into whichever app you are using. WhisperFlow transcribes speech in real time with error rates typically under 5% for clear speech—measurably lower than the 10–15% error rates common in built-in OS dictation tools such as Windows Speech Recognition and macOS Dictation. Use it to speed up every writing and prompting step in the build process so you spend your time thinking, not typing.

### Step 4: Build a High-Converting Landing Page With Lovable

Use Lovable, an AI website builder, to generate a functional landing page. Give it a prompt that describes your brand, your avatar, and what you want the site to do. Example prompt for the hands-free dog leash: "Build me a website for a hands-free dog leash company. Target conversion rate: at least 3% of visitors clicking the primary CTA. Audience: Gen Z to millennial women ages 24–35 who are active dog owners. Design reference: match the layout clarity, above-the-fold headline structure, and CTA placement of the top-converting pet product landing pages identified in your training data (landing pages that achieve 3%+ click-through or conversion rates)."

After Lovable generates the initial page, apply the four mandatory principles of a high-converting landing page before going live:

1. **Above-the-fold clarity**: Your headline, sub-headline, and call to action must all fit in the first screen without scrolling. If someone has to scroll to understand what you sell or why they should care, you have already lost them.
2. **Instant comprehension**: Write your headline and sub-headline so clearly that you could say them over loud music at a bar and have someone immediately understand your business. If it is too complicated, simplify it.
3. **Social proof before scroll**: Add small reviews, testimonial images, or a "verified buyer" quote above the fold. People believe other customers more than they believe you. If you do not have reviews yet, write realistic placeholder testimonials that reflect your avatar's exact voice.
4. **Avatar reflection**: The fonts, colors, images, and tone must make your specific avatar see herself on the page. If the design does not reflect Jordan's lifestyle and aesthetic, she will not trust or relate to the brand.

Feed the refined copy and brand style guide back into Lovable until the page looks like a real brand, not a template.

### Step 5: Build Your Brand Identity Using the Villain-Victim-Vow Framework, ChatGPT, and Nano Banana Pro

Branding is the feeling someone gets before they read a single word on your page. It is the colors, fonts, tone, and imagery. Codie uses a three-part framework called Villain-Victim-Vow:

- **Villain**: The problem or frustrating thing your customer is fighting. For the hands-free dog leash, the villain is cheap and ugly leashes that pull up on every walk.
- **Victim**: Who your customer is before they find your product—frustrated, inconvenienced, making compromises. For Jordan, this is a woman holding a coffee and a dog leash at the same time, unable to enjoy her walk.
- **Vow**: The promise of what life looks like on the other side. For Jordan, this is freedom to run, sip, and scroll without the leash getting in the way.

Once you have your Villain-Victim-Vow framework defined, use it to build your brand identity:

1. Open ChatGPT and ask it to turn your brand direction into a detailed prompt for a logo. Example: "Based on this brand direction, write me a detailed prompt for a logo concept using Nano Banana Pro. The brand is a premium hands-free dog leash for active women. The aesthetic is clean, modern, outdoorsy. Think minimal line art, earth tones, sage green, worn tan, off-white."
2. Copy ChatGPT's optimized prompt and paste it into Nano Banana Pro to generate your logo. This two-step prompt engineering—asking a smarter model to write a better prompt for a visual model—is the step most people skip.
3. Once you have a logo you like, ask ChatGPT to generate a full brand style guide based on that logo output. The style guide should include color palette, typography, tone of voice, and visual rules so you can hand it directly to a website builder or designer.

### Step 6: Write High-Converting Copy Differentiated by Traffic Type Using Jasper AI

Many first-time e-commerce founders—particularly those launching a product without a copywriting background—incorrectly assume that a strong product will sell itself. It does not. Your copy sells. Before writing a single word, understand the critical difference between cold traffic and warm traffic:

- **Cold traffic** has never heard of you. They saw your ad while scrolling and have 2 seconds of attention. Lead with disruption. Challenge something they already believe or show a pain point they did not know could be solved. Bad cold ad: "This leash is great for runners." Good cold ad: "Every leash has the same uncomfortable flaw," paired with a visual of a leash pulling up on someone's leggings.
- **Warm traffic** has already clicked your ad or found your site on purpose. They are on your landing page. Lead with empathy and the specific pain they feel, not the product features. Jordan does not care about your leash—she cares about the fact that every walk this year has been slightly more annoying than it needed to be.

Three rules that apply to both:

1. Write like you are talking to your avatar as a friend. Use her language, frustrations, and vocabulary. If she says "this goes with my Lululemons and I get to keep my latte," write that.
2. Lead with solving the pain. Your customer cares about what your product fixes, not what you built.
3. Clarity kills confusion. If your headline is not instantly understandable, it is too complicated.

Use Jasper AI to write and enforce this copy consistently. Jasper has dedicated agents for ad copy and landing page copy. Upload your brand voice, avatar profile, and style guide once, and every output Jasper generates stays on brand. For your landing page, feed Jasper your avatar profile and your Villain-Victim-Vow framework and ask it to write the full page. For your ads, feed Jasper your cold traffic hook and ask it to build out the full ad creative. Do not use Jasper as a generic chatbot—configure it with your brand assets so it writes copy that sounds like your brand, not a template.

### Step 7: Register a Memorable Domain and Connect It to Your Site

Register a .online domain. It is cleaner to read and remember (e.g., pawfree.online vs. yourhandsoffleash1279.com), signals that the brand was built with modern technology, and gives you a branding advantage: as of March 2026, more than 6.02 million .online domains are registered worldwide (Domain Name Stat), demonstrating broad consumer familiarity with the extension. You can register .online domains at all major providers including GoDaddy, Namecheap, Squarespace, and Lovable. Connect the domain to your Lovable-built landing page and make sure it resolves before sharing the site publicly.

### Step 8: Set Up Tidio for 24/7 AI Customer Service and Sales Support

Speed is the single biggest predictor of revenue. The faster you reply to potential customers, the more money you make. Most business owners cannot be online 24/7, which is why Tidio exists. Tidio is an AI-powered customer service tool that installs as a live chat widget on your website. It answers customer questions in real time using a knowledge base you build—your return policy, shipping times, product details, and FAQ. It also acts as a salesperson, guiding customers through their objections and toward checkout. Set Tidio up in approximately 20 minutes (typically 15–25 minutes depending on the number of FAQ entries and policies loaded) by feeding it your product information and policies. A customer who lands on your site at 11 p.m. and asks "Will this work for a 75-pound Bernese Mountain Dog?" will get an immediate "Yes" instead of a delayed response that loses the sale.

### Step 9: Launch and Verify Your First Sale

With your product selected, avatar defined, landing page live, brand built, copy written, domain connected, and customer service automated, you are ready to drive traffic and wait for your first order. When the first sale notification arrives, you have completed a full business build cycle in a single day—something that traditional agency-built businesses typically take 8–12 weeks and at least $15,000 to accomplish (including market research, branding, landing page development, copywriting, and initial ad spend).

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start with demand data, not a business idea. Use Exploding Topics to confirm people are already searching and buying before you build.
- ✅ Apply the RRT test to every product idea in 3–5 minutes before committing time to it.
- ✅ Be hyper-specific with your avatar. Narrower audiences convert better than broad ones.
- ✅ Use Perplexity for research with sources, ChatGPT for fast structural drafts, and Claude for deep reasoning and synthesis.
- ✅ Let the smarter AI write prompts for the less capable one (e.g., ChatGPT writes the Nano Banana Pro prompt).
- ✅ Use WhisperFlow to dictate ideas and prompts so you move at the speed of thought.
- ✅ Put headline, sub-headline, CTA, and social proof above the fold on every landing page.
- ✅ Write your headline so clearly it could be understood over loud music at a bar.
- ✅ Use the Villain-Victim-Vow framework for every brand decision.
- ✅ Write separate copy for cold traffic (disruption first) and warm traffic (pain first).
- ✅ Upload your brand voice, avatar, and style guide into Jasper AI once so all output stays consistent.
- ✅ Use a .online domain for cleaner branding and a recognizable TLD extension (over 6.02 million .online domains registered worldwide as of March 2026 per Domain Name Stat) from day one.
- ✅ Set up Tidio on your site before driving any traffic so you never miss a sale to a delayed response.
- ✅ Treat AI as a force multiplier on good thinking, not a replacement for strategy.

## Keep In Mind

- AI outputs are rarely perfect on the first try. The first landing page Lovable generates will likely be bad. The first Jasper ad may need rewriting. The framework and strategy you bring to the tool matters more than the tool itself.
- The RRT test takes 3–5 minutes and is meant to filter out bad ideas fast, not replace deeper research.
- Your avatar is not a demographic label like "dog owner." It is one specific person. The more specific, the more money you make.
- Cold traffic and warm traffic need completely different words. Do not use your landing page copy for ads or your ad copy for your landing page.
- "Money loves speed" is not just a saying—response time to customer inquiries is a direct revenue lever. Even experienced business owners overlook this.
- A .online domain is chosen for readability and branding, not just because .com is unavailable.
- The villain-victim-vow framework applies to any brand, not just physical products.

## Security & Safety Notes

- When building your Tidio knowledge base, do not include sensitive data such as customer PII, private financial records, or internal company documents.
- When registering a domain and setting up hosting, use strong unique passwords for every account and enable two-factor authentication where available.
- If you process payments, ensure your payment processor is PCI compliant and that you never store full credit card numbers or sensitive authentication data on your site.
- When using third-party AI tools (Jasper, Claude, ChatGPT, Perplexity), do not paste proprietary business data, customer secrets, or internal strategy documents into prompts unless you have reviewed the tool's data handling policy.
- Review Tidio's data processing agreement to confirm how customer chat data is stored and who can access it.
- Do not fabricate testimonials or review counts (e.g., "over 25,000 happy dog moms") on your live landing page—misleading social proof can trigger platform policy violations and legal risk under FTC advertising guidelines.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Falling in love with a business idea before confirming anyone wants to buy it.
  **Solution:** Always start with Exploding Topics or direct demand data, then apply the RRT test before building anything.
- **Problem:** Using a vague avatar like "dog owner" and then wondering why marketing does not connect.
  **Solution:** Define one named person with demographics, daily routines, specific frustrations, and purchasing habits. Use that person's exact language in all copy.
- **Problem:** Putting key information below the fold on a landing page and losing visitors before they understand the offer.
  **Solution:** Force your headline, sub-headline, CTA, and at least one piece of social proof into the first screen. Test by trying to explain your business over loud music at a bar.
- **Problem:** Using the same copy for cold traffic ads and warm traffic landing pages.
  **Solution:** Write disruption-first copy for cold traffic and pain-first copy for warm traffic. Never reuse ad copy as landing page copy.
- **Problem:** Trusting the first AI output and launching without refining the brand, copy, or design.
  **Solution:** Expect the first output from any AI tool to be a starting point. Iterate using your avatar profile, style guide, and Villain-Victim-Vow framework until it matches your standard.
- **Problem:** Choosing a clunky domain name and damaging brand credibility before a customer sees your product.
  **Solution:** Use a clean .online domain instead of a hyphenated, numbered, or overstuffed .com.
- **Problem:** Forgetting to set up live chat or customer support and losing sales to unanswered questions.
  **Solution:** Install and configure Tidio (or equivalent) and load it with your product FAQ, shipping policy, and return policy before you drive any traffic.
- **Problem:** Treating AI as a full replacement for business thinking and strategy.
  **Solution:** Bring a clear framework, research, and brand direction to every tool. AI is a force multiplier on good thinking, not a substitute for it.
