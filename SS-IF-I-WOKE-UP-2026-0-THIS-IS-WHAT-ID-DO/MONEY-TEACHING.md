# If I Woke Up In 2026 With $0… This Is What I'd Do

## Overview

This money teaching outlines the exact step-by-step approach entrepreneur Brian Scudamore—founder of 1-800-GOT-JUNK?, now valued at over $700 million—would follow to rebuild wealth starting from zero at age 20 in 2026. It emphasizes adopting a beginner's mindset, defining your core "why," generating your first $1,000 through small no-money ventures, and building a business aligned with your unique strengths. The teaching exists to help young and aspiring entrepreneurs overcome the paralysis of perfectionism and fear, showing that the journey of starting—and failing—is where real wealth and self-discovery are built.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you have no money, no connections, and no "perfect" business idea but want to start building wealth
- When you feel stuck in a middle-class trap with a stable job but an entrepreneurial idea you're scared to pursue
- When you've started something before and failed, and need a framework for turning that failure into your next opportunity

## Steps

### Step 1: Adopt the Beginner's Mindset

If you are the smartest person in the room, you are in a room that doesn't challenge you. Put yourself in environments—entrepreneur meetups, networking events, mastermind groups—where you are the least experienced person present so you can absorb knowledge from those ahead of you. This positions you as a sponge for knowledge and opens doors that closed minds never find.

### Step 2: Start Something Immediately—Even If It Fails

Do not wait for the perfect idea or the perfect timing. Start any business now and assume your first venture will likely fail. The act of starting puts you in the entrepreneurial mindset and reveals what you actually love to do. Most young entrepreneurs get tripped up because they think they need to feel confident before they start; instead, start first and the confidence follows.

### Step 3: Define Your "Why" and Write Down Your Biggest Dreams

Write down your biggest, most far-fetched dream on paper. Use your "why" as the fuel that keeps you going when the journey gets hard. Scudamore's favorite saying: "It's kind of fun to do the impossible."

### Step 4: Generate Your First $1,000 Through Any Means Necessary

Find money however you can. Clean cars on weekends, start a small neighborhood service, or do work no one else wants to do. $1,000 is enough to start meaningful businesses—Apple started with roughly $1,000 out of a garage, and 1-800-GOT-JUNK? started with under $1,000. This initial capital teaches you the value of earning and builds momentum.

### Step 5: Use AI to Brainstorm Zero-Cost Business Ideas

If you genuinely cannot find a business idea, open any AI tool with voice or text input (such as ChatGPT) and spend 5 minutes telling it your name, the hobbies and activities you have always loved doing, and any skills you already have. Ask it to suggest five specific business ideas you can start today with zero upfront cost. Read the ideas, pick the one that excites you most, and take one tangible action toward it within the next 24 hours.

### Step 6: Master Marketing and Branding to Make Any Business Exciting

You do not need a sexy business to build wealth. Learn marketing and branding, because these are the forces that turn an unsexy business into a compelling brand. Park your vehicle at a high-profile intersection, paint your phone number on the side, and make sure people see you. Enthusiasm about something no one else is enthusiastic about is a competitive advantage.

### Step 7: Choose Unsexy Niches With High Demand

Look for boring, underserved businesses that most people avoid—cleaning, junk removal, laundry, pressure washing, or other unglamorous services. These niches often have the least competition and the most consistent, recession-resistant demand. Take whichever of these niches you enter and inject personality, viral marketing, and brand energy into it to make it stand out from every other provider in the space.

### Step 8: Identify Your Strengths and Hire for Your Weaknesses

Take a sheet of paper and write down everything you love to do and are good at on one side. On the other side, write every role the business requires—bookkeeping, sales, operations, customer service—that you either cannot do, are not good at, or simply do not want to do. Then find a partner or hire someone whose strengths and interests perfectly fill every gap on that second list. The right partner should genuinely enjoy the tasks on your weaknesses list and never want to take on the tasks on your strengths list.

### Step 9: Reframe Risk and Embrace Failure as Preparation

Risk is not a negative word—it is a chance to learn. Do not take stupid financial risks that could harm people, but consistently put yourself outside your comfort zone. When you fail, take out a sheet of paper and ask: "What can I learn from this failure?" Failure is preparation. Most people stop after one or two failures, but you do not know when the wave will hit—you have to keep surfing.

## Examples

### Example 1: Starting a Car Wash at Age 12

At 12 years old, Brian started a car wash in his neighborhood at a busy intersection. A neighbor kid started a competing car wash on the other corner, sparking a price war. They eventually merged and washed cars all weekend, making a ton of money and having fun. This early venture taught him that his passion was people—finding people, building meaning, and making money. He discovered this at 12, not 30, simply by starting something with no money.

### Example 2: The Macka Twins' Cleaning Business

Two young identical twins dreamed of starting a cleaning business and embraced the unsexy nature of it by leaning into the message: "We clean places no one else wants to clean." They found a bin in Camden, London, that had not been cleaned in 10 years and made a viral video of themselves cleaning it. This single video drove massive bookings for their cleaning business, proving that you can take an unsexy business and make it exciting and profitable through marketing, branding, and viral storytelling.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Start before you feel ready—action creates confidence, not the other way around
- ✅ Write down your biggest, most impossible dream and use it as your north star
- ❌ Do not wait for the perfect idea, the perfect time, or enough money to begin
- ❌ Do not stay in a role where you are the bottleneck—delegate or hire for your weaknesses

## Keep In Mind

- Most learning comes from failure, not success—you cannot write your story without failure in it
- The business that sounds unsexy to you may be the most profitable niche if you bring energy and marketing to it
- At 55, you do not want to be tired from spending your whole life doing things that drain you—focus on what gives you energy
- Enjoy the journey every day, not just the pot of gold at the end

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not take stupid financial risks or do anything that could harm people while pursuing your business
- Protect your personal finances by starting ventures with minimal or no upfront capital
- Build a business that supports your "why" and your desired lifestyle—not one that consumes it

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Being the bottleneck in your own business as it scales, causing stagnation or collapse
  **Solution:** Honestly document everything you are bad at or do not want to do, then find a complementary partner or COO who thrives on those exact tasks
- **Problem:** Getting discouraged after one or two failures and giving up before the opportunity arrives
  **Solution:** After every failure, write down exactly what you learned and why it may have been protecting you from a worse outcome; then immediately take the next risk
- **Problem:** Hiring someone who looks good on paper but does not align with your energy and working style
  **Solution:** Before hiring a key leader, share your written list of strengths and weaknesses with them and confirm they genuinely love everything on your weaknesses side—if they hesitate, they are not the right fit
