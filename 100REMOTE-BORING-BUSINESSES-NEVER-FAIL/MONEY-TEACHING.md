# 100REMOTE-Boring-Remote-Businesses-That-Almost-Never-Fail

## Overview

This money teaching outlines a low-overhead, remote-first business model for local home services. You build a branded website with upfront pricing and online booking, subcontract the work to local professionals, and keep the margin. It requires no trucks, buildings, equipment loans, or payroll—just a laptop—and has an extremely low failure rate, making it ideal for beginners.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to start a remote business with almost no overhead or payroll
- When working on a side income that can eventually replace a full-time job
- When the user asks about beginner-friendly business models with recurring revenue
- When looking for businesses that can run from anywhere in the world

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose a Low-Competition Local Service Niche

Pick a home service with high search volume and low keyword difficulty. Strong options include gutter cleaning, dog poop removal, water heater replacement, large-item moving (pianos, pool tables, sheds), window cleaning, carpet cleaning, or house cleaning. Avoid generic, saturated markets by targeting specific, low-competition keywords like "piano moving in [city]" or "water heater replacement in [city]."

### Step 2: Build a Branded Website with Upfront Pricing and Online Booking

Create a local brand, secure a Google Business listing, and build a website that shows clear, upfront prices for each service. Let customers select their exact needs—such as house size, number of stories, or number of dogs—and book and pay online, just like an e-commerce store. Upfront pricing is your biggest differentiator against competitors who gouge customers on quotes.

### Step 3: Find and Onboard Local Subcontractors

Recruit small local operators or solo professionals who already perform the service but lack marketing and booking systems. They handle the physical work; you handle the customer acquisition, booking, and payment processing. Pay them a cut and keep the margin for yourself.

### Step 4: Drive Recurring Revenue Through Subscriptions

Structure services as recurring contracts whenever possible—weekly, bi-weekly, annually, or seasonally. For example, offer gutter cleaning twice a year at a discounted subscription rate, or dog poop pickup on a weekly route. Recurring customers dramatically reduce the need for constant new lead generation.

### Step 5: Expand with Upsells and New Cities

Once profitable, add high-ticket upsells relevant to your niche—such as roof washing, window washing, or pressure washing for a gutter cleaning business. Replicate the model in additional cities by building location-specific pages for each service.

## Examples

### Example 1: MaidThis (House Cleaning)

A founder runs a house cleaning company in Los Angeles entirely from Bali. Customers visit the website, select their home size and add-ons, see an upfront price, and book online. The job is automatically sent to a local subcontractor who performs the cleaning. The business reportedly generates $150,000 per month with no physical presence in LA.

### Example 2: College Kids (Window Cleaning)

Two college students started a commercial window cleaning business between classes. They focused on office buildings and commercial properties that require regular cleaning, built routes for subcontractors, and reached $60,000 per month while reportedly working only 5 hours per month on the business.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Target specific, low-competition keywords with dedicated city pages
- ✅ Offer upfront, transparent pricing to build trust and differentiate from gouging competitors
- ✅ Prioritize recurring services and subscription models to stabilize revenue
- ✅ Use personalized, offline outreach like flyers with your face and a handwritten price for exterior home services
- ✅ Build a simple brand and Google Business listing before investing in expensive marketing
- ❌ Don't compete directly with established brands on generic, high-competition keywords
- ❌ Don't hire employees—start with subcontractors to keep overhead at zero
- ❌ Don't skip upfront pricing just because competitors don't offer it; it's your biggest advantage

## Keep In Mind

- This is not a passive business; it requires active sales, marketing, and subcontractor management, especially early on.
- Success depends on local market research—verify search volume and competition in your target cities before launching.
- Recurring revenue is the key to low ongoing work; one-time jobs require constant customer acquisition.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never share subcontractor personal information or customer data without consent.
- Use secure payment processing and keep financial records separate from personal accounts.
- Ensure subcontractors are properly licensed and insured for the services they perform.
- Do not misrepresent the brand or make guarantees about pricing or outcomes that you cannot control.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Choosing a saturated niche with too many competitors already offering online booking.
  **Solution:** Drill down into specific, low-competition keywords within a broader category (e.g., "hot tub removal" instead of generic "junk removal").
- **Problem:** Underpricing services and leaving no margin for unexpected costs or subcontractor pay.
  **Solution:** Research local rates, build in a healthy buffer, and round up prices slightly for the convenience of online booking.
- **Problem:** Relying on one-time jobs instead of building recurring routes.
  **Solution:** Always offer a discounted subscription or recurring contract, and prioritize neighborhoods where you can build dense routes for subcontractors.
