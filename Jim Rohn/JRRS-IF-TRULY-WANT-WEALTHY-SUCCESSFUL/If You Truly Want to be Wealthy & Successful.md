# If You Truly Want to be Wealthy & Successful...

## Overview

This teaching explains how personal development is the foundation for wealth and success. Jim Rohn shares his journey from broke to rich and explains how changing yourself—not external circumstances—is the key to attracting success. He uses the seasons metaphor to illustrate how to handle difficult times, seize opportunities, and take responsibility for results.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to build long-term wealth instead of relying on external factors
- When facing financial setbacks or "winters" in your business or career
- When you need a framework for personal growth and self-improvement

## Steps

### Step 1: Accept Full Responsibility for Your Situation

Stop blaming external factors like the economy, your boss, or your pay. Mr. Shoaff taught Jim Rohn that if you say "this is all they pay," the answer is "that's all you're worth." If you say "it costs too much," the answer is "you can't afford it." Recognize that blaming others is like going through withdrawals—it's tough to give up, but necessary for growth. The turning point is looking inward because that's where the problem is.

### Step 2: Understand the Core Principle: Success is Attracted by Who You Become

The major value in life is not what you get, but what you become. The major question to ask is not "What am I getting here?" but "What am I becoming here?" Success is not something you pursue like a butterfly; it's something you attract by becoming an attractive person. To attract powerful people, you must be powerful. To attract dedicated people, you must be dedicated. The whole key is to work hard on yourself.

### Step 3: Internalize the Promise: If You Will Change, Everything Will Change

Mr. Shoaff gave Jim Rohn this promise: "If you will change, everything will change for you." If you change, your income will change, your health will change, your future will change, and the equities you hoped for will start to grow. Don't wait for the government, people around you, or society to change. Take a personal interest in your own personal development and you'll start to attract success.

### Step 4: Learn to Handle Winters (Difficult Times)

Winters are inevitable—they come every year with regularity. There are financial winters, political winters, social winters, and personal winters. You cannot escape them any more than you can escape being on the planet. When winter comes, you must get wiser, stronger, and better. You can read more books, attend more classes, get more testimonials, and make more notes. Don't settle for get-along or just-get-by skills—strive for excellence. Anyone can get better with practice.

### Step 5: Seize the Spring (Opportunities) with Urgency

Spring follows winter every year for 6,000 years of recorded history. Spring represents opportunity, and opportunity always follows difficulty. When spring arrives, don't just hope it sweeps you along—articulate it, pass along the story, make the calls, do the meetings, tell the story, translate it. Seize the moment because spring doesn't last all year. Work while it's day, before night comes and shuts everything down.

### Step 6: Make the Best of Each Opportunity

Life is very brief. You have only a handful of springs handed to you. Think of them as "times" rather than years. Make the most of each person you meet, each meeting, each occasion, each phone call, each contact, each opportunity, and each class. Don't be lazy or drift—concentrate and show up with urgency.

### Step 7: In Summer, Nourish Your Values and Fight Your Enemies

Summer is the season after you've planted the garden. Busy bugs and noxious weeds will try to take it unless you defend it. You must nourish your children, ideas, and yourself, but also defend them. Be vigilant against internal enemies like complaining, doubt, worry, over-caution, and pessimism. Drive these tendencies into a small corner. Don't let worry conquer you, but let it serve you. Be hostile toward laziness, drifting, and settling.

### Step 8: Reap the Harvest with Full Responsibility and No Complaint

At harvest, take full responsibility for your results. Your paycheck is your responsibility, not the marketplace's. If you're not doing the best you can, that's where the problem is. Complaining is devastating—like the children of Israel who griped their way out of the promised land. Complain long enough and you get your future canceled. Learn to take full responsibility and complain not.

## Examples

### Example 1: Jim Rohn's Personal Transformation

Jim Rohn went from broke to rich within 12 years (two 6-year cycles) by applying these principles. He stopped blaming his employer, the economy, and his relatives, and instead focused on personal development. He learned to handle winters by getting stronger and wiser, seized spring opportunities by building his business, and took full responsibility for his financial results without complaint.

### Example 2: The Children of Israel

The children of Israel were slaves whom God delivered through dazzling miracles. They were heading for the promised land, but they never arrived because they complained about the food, the water, the leadership, and the journey. This illustrates how complaining can cancel your future and prevent you from reaching the wealth and success you're destined for.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Accept 100% responsibility for your economic situation
- ✅ Invest in personal development daily through books and classes
- ✅ Get wiser, stronger, and better during difficult times
- ✅ Seize opportunities immediately with urgency
- ✅ Nourish your values and defend against internal enemies
- ✅ Drive doubt, worry, and complaining into a small corner
- ❌ Don't blame external circumstances for your lack of success
- ❌ Don't confuse pursuing success with attracting success
- ❌ Don't complain about your results
- ❌ Don't settle for get-by skills—strive for excellence

## Keep In Mind

- You cannot change the seasons (external circumstances), but you can change yourself
- Success is attracted by the person you become, not pursued like a butterfly
- Each "spring" (opportunity) is limited—make the most of every one
- Internal enemies (complaining, doubt, pessimism) are often more dangerous than external ones

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is a mindset and personal development teaching; no financial transactions or sensitive data are involved
- Apply these principles to your own life and business decisions at your own discretion

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Blaming the economy, boss, or pay for your financial situation
  **Solution:** Accept 100% responsibility; your paycheck is your responsibility, not the marketplace's
- **Problem:** Waiting for the "right time" or hoping opportunities will sweep you along
  **Solution:** Seize spring with urgency—make calls, tell stories, plant seeds immediately
- **Problem:** Letting worry, doubt, and complaining consume you
  **Solution:** Drive these internal enemies into a small corner; let worry serve you but not conquer you
- **Problem:** Going through life with get-by skills instead of striving for excellence
  **Solution:** Commit to continuous improvement—get better at parenting, communication, and your craft every year
