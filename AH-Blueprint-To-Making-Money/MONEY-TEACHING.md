# Alex Hormozi's Blueprint To Making Money Blew My Mind

## Overview

This video presents Alex Hormozi's nine-step blueprint for building a million-dollar business, covering identity formation, target market selection, product/service offering, sales systems, repeat purchases, team building, competitive advantage, and persistence. The framework is designed to be actionable for entrepreneurs at any level, breaking down complex business concepts into executable behaviors and strategies that can be applied immediately.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When starting a new business or side hustle and need a proven framework
- When trying to scale an existing business to seven figures
- When struggling with sales, customer acquisition, or closing deals
- When building a team and need to recruit and retain top talent
- When feeling stuck, unmotivated, or unsure how to get better as an entrepreneur
- When trying to figure out what business to start or who to sell to
- When you want to increase revenue from existing customers without acquiring new ones

## Steps

### Step 1: Figure Out Who You Are

Traits like charisma, patience, and leadership are "bundled terms" made up of specific micro-behaviors. Identify the traits you want, break each one down into observable behaviors, and practice those behaviors daily. Stop trying to "be" something and start "doing" the actions that create that identity. As Alex says, "Nike had it right" - just do it.

### Step 2: Decide Who to Sell To

Choose a target market where you have "surface area" - ideally someone like you who has the problem you already solved. Look for markets with four characteristics: they have money, they're easy to find, the market is growing, and they're in searing pain. Avoid markets where people are broke, have too many choices, or where you have to educate them on why they need your product.

### Step 3: Determine What to Sell

Start with one of three paths: Pain (a problem you overcame), Profession (a skill you already have), or Passion (something you love). Services are the easiest business to start - you're just doing something other people don't want to do for money. You can sell Products, Services, SaaS, Access, Risk, or Money. The key is to match what you sell to who you're selling to.

### Step 4: Get Them to Buy (The CLOSE Framework)

Use this sales conversation structure:

- **C - Clarify**: Ask why they're there ("Why did you respond to my post?") to understand their initial interest
- **L - Label**: Summarize their problem back to them ("It sounds like you're here and you want to get there and this has been in your way")
- **O - Overview past experiences**: Ask what they've tried, how it worked, what they liked and hated (the pain cycle)
- **S - Sell with a three-pillar pitch**: Present 3 key benefit pillars, each paired with a concrete metaphor or short story that makes the benefit tangible. Focus on the end result (the destination), not the process of delivering it (the journey).
- **E - Explain away concerns**: Handle timing, preference, ROI, stalls, and past bad experiences with logical arguments
- **Reinforce the decision**: Bridge them to onboarding so they feel great about their choice

### Step 5: Get Them to Buy More Times

There are eight ways to increase revenue from a customer:

1. Increase the price
2. Decrease your cost
3. Get them to buy more quantity (buy two instead of one)
4. Get continuity (subscription model - buy repeatedly over time)
5. Increase quality (upsell to a better version)
6. Down-sell (turn a no into a yes with a smaller offer)
7. Cross-sell (sell something different like fries with a burger)
8. Identify hyper-buying cycles - when someone enters a new identity (marathon runner, new pet owner, bride), they buy lots of related products in a short time. Be there with the next offer.

### Step 6: Get Help From Others (The Core 4)

You have only four ways to let people know about your stuff:

1. **Warm 1:1**: Text friends, DMs, calls, emails to people you know
2. **Warm 1:many**: Post content to your social media/audience
3. **Cold 1:1**: Cold calling, cold emailing, cold outreach to strangers
4. **Cold 1:many**: Paid ads to people who don't know you

Apply the same pipeline for recruiting as you do for sales: Lead Gen → Nurture → Sales (interview) → Onboarding → Retention/Ascension. Hire A players by reducing friction in your hiring process and selling candidates on why they should work for you. Onboard aggressively with multiple touchpoints in the first two weeks.

### Step 7: Keep Your Advantage

Your brand is your moat. Build a virtuous cycle where advertising creates friends, friends create personal experience, and personal experience perpetuates advertising. Use the RICE framework to prioritize innovation: Reach (number of people impacted), Impact (severity of the problem), Confidence (likelihood the solution works), Expense (implementation cost). Define strategy simply as "prioritization of resources" and apply the Value Equation: increase Dream Outcome and Perceived Likelihood of Achievement while decreasing Time Delay and Effort/Sacrifice.

### Step 8: Stick With It

Treat business as an infinite game. There is no permanent #1 in business - the richest person 30 years ago is forgotten today. The only way to fail is to stop. Motivation comes from deprivation, not inspiration. Your reference group (the 5 people you compare yourself to, not the 5 people you spend time with) predicts long-term financial success. Burn the boats - eliminate fallback options to remove the safe choice. Use what you have as fuel: shame, anger, or hunger can all drive you forward. Ask the North Star question: "Does having X in my life increase the chance I hit my goal?" Accept trade-offs - you can't have the six-pack and the cake.

### Step 9: Get Better

Define work as: Volume × Leverage = Output. It's not about how hard you try, it's about what you get done. First, do the volume (100 cold calls, 100 Instagram reels, 100 sales conversations). Then analyze using Common Factors Analysis: compare your top 10% performers to your bottom 10% and identify what they have in common that the bottom ones don't. Do more of what works, less of what doesn't. Repeat this cycle for years. The biggest gains come at the very end from deep, narrow expertise - the difference between #2 and #1 is everything, but it's created by tiny improvements compounded over thousands of repetitions.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Old Car Story

A father gives his son an old car and suggests selling it. The pawn shop offers $2,500. The dealership offers $5,000. The collectibles auction house offers $100,000. The lesson: it's the exact same thing, but who you sell to changes everything. If the agency owner had tried to sell his creative agency services to small local lawn care businesses, they wouldn't understand the value of branding. But sell to a larger company that understands brand halo effect and return on advertising spend, and the same service is worth exponentially more.

### Example 2: The CRO Company

A conversion rate optimization expert worked with $1M/year e-commerce stores. A 10% lift in revenue added $100,000 to the business. The same work for a $100M e-commerce store with a 10% lift added $10M to the business. Same effort, same skill, but dramatically different value because of who he sold to. Lesson: Do the same work for better people in better markets.

### Example 3: The Burger Store - Eight Ways to Make More Money

Alex uses a burger store to illustrate the eight levers: increase price ($10 → $15 burger), decrease cost, get them to buy two burgers (quantity), create a burger subscription (continuity), upgrade to sirloin (quality), offer a junior burger (down-sell), cross-sell fries and drinks, and solve the dessert problem created by the burger (hyper-buying cycle). Every solution creates a new problem to solve.

### Example 4: The Creative Agency Pivot

Alex's creative agency was selling logo design and branding as commodities. Then he realized they should sell ideas and concepts, not execution. The creative work was almost secondary - clients would pay millions for the right concept. Once they had the concept, anyone could execute it. By repositioning what they sold, they went from charging design fees to landing seven-figure accounts like CNN.

- ✅ Sell to people like you who have the problem you already solved
- ✅ Use the three-pillar pitch with metaphors and stories instead of listing features
- ✅ Ask for permission before making the sale ("Can I tell you about it?")
- ✅ Present offers during hyper-buying cycles when people are entering new identities
- ✅ Use all four Core 4 outreach methods - don't rely on just one
- ✅ Apply the sales pipeline to recruiting - you're selling top talent on why they should join
- ✅ Reduce friction in your hiring process to attract A players
- ✅ Onboard new employees aggressively with frequent check-ins in the first two weeks
- ✅ Build a personal brand as your long-term competitive advantage
- ✅ Use Common Factors Analysis to improve any skill by comparing top and bottom performers
- ✅ Treat business as an infinite game where the only failure is quitting
- ✅ Ask the North Star question before committing time or resources to anything
- ❌ Don't model where successful people are today; model what they did at the beginning
- ❌ Don't sell the plane flight; sell the vacation - focus on the destination, not the process
- ❌ Don't put 100 steps of friction in your hiring process or you'll only get B players and below
- ❌ Don't quit when progress feels slow - compounding happens at the very end after years of repetition
- ❌ Don't wait for passion or perfect motivation to start - use shame, anger, or hunger as fuel
- ❌ Don't try to have your cake and eat it too - accept trade-offs and cut anything that doesn't pass the North Star test
- ❌ Don't focus only on promise and proof while ignoring time delay and sacrifice in your value proposition

## Security & Safety Notes

- When doing cold outreach, respect anti-spam laws (CAN-SPAM, GDPR) and always honor opt-out requests
- When collecting payments, use secure payment processors and consider money-back guarantees to reduce perceived risk
- When hiring, verify credentials, conduct proper interviews, and ensure decision makers are involved for major commitments
- When handling customer data, comply with data protection regulations and be transparent about data usage
- Don't over-promise in sales to close the deal - delivering on promises is what builds lasting brand value
- When using offshore labor or automation for cost reduction, implement quality control to maintain your brand promise
- Burn the boats carefully - ensure you have basic safety nets (healthcare, emergency fund) before eliminating all fallback options
- When offering delayed payment terms, have clear contracts and processes to protect yourself if payment is refused

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to "be" charismatic, patient, or successful instead of doing the specific behaviors that create those traits
  **Solution:** Break the trait down into 5-10 specific micro-behaviors and practice them daily until they become automatic. You don't need to feel charismatic - you just need to nod, smile, make eye contact, and speak with volume.

- **Problem:** Selling to the wrong market and blaming yourself when sales are hard
  **Solution:** Ask "who am I selling to?" before "what am I selling?" The exact same service can be worth $2,500 or $100,000 depending on the buyer's ability to pay and understand the value.

- **Problem:** Getting stuck in the early grind and quitting before compounding kicks in
  **Solution:** Treat business as an infinite game. The only failure is stopping. Income growth is flat for years and then explodes at the end - the difference between #2 and #1 is everything, but it comes from years of tiny improvements.

- **Problem:** Waiting for passion or perfect motivation to start a business
  **Solution:** Use what you have as fuel. Shame, anger, hunger, and deprivation are all valid motivators. As Alex says, "Use what you got" - wishing you had different fuel is what gets people stuck.

- **Problem:** Trying to have it all without making trade-offs
  **Solution:** Ask the North Star question before any commitment: "Does having X in my life increase the chance I hit my goal?" Cut anything that doesn't pass. You can't have the six-pack and the cake.

- **Problem:** Focusing only on promise and proof in marketing while ignoring time delay and sacrifice
  **Solution:** Use the full Value Equation. Amazon and the world's best companies compete on making things faster, easier, and with less effort - not just bigger promises.

- **Problem:** Not asking for help because you think people won't want to assist you
  **Solution:** People love giving help - you just have to ask. Use the Core 4 outreach methods systematically rather than hoping people will magically discover your business.

- **Problem:** Hiring based on resume qualifications instead of selling top talent on why they should join
  **Solution:** The interview is a sales conversation. A players have options - you need to sell them on your vision, growth path, and why working with you is the best opportunity available. Tell them where they can be in 5 years and what they need to do to get there.

- **Problem:** Slow, friction-heavy hiring processes that filter out A players
  **Solution:** B players will tolerate 100 steps of friction because they have fewer options. A players will move on. Reduce friction in your application process to attract top talent who expect efficiency.
