# SMB - Why Did One Trader Make $200 and Another $2000 on the Same Setup

## Overview

This money teaching explains why two traders given the exact same chart, the same setup, and the same entry can walk away with vastly different results. Steve made approximately $200 because he mentally checked out, focused on his own P&L, and sold into strength at the top. Paula made approximately $2,000 because she stayed engaged with the stock, listened to what the tape was telling her, and held her position to let the momentum play out. The difference comes down to one core shift: stop making the trade about yourself and start letting the stock lead.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you enter a trade and immediately start calculating your P&L instead of watching the tape
- When you take a good scalp and wonder why you missed the bigger move afterward
- When you recognize a strong momentum setup and want to maximize it instead of taking the first reward
- When you catch yourself wanting to top-tick your exit or sell into strength too early
- When you want to understand the psychology gap between a developing trader and a consistently profitable one

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup and Know What You Are Looking For

Study the chart before the open and understand what setup you are watching. In this example, the traders were looking for a day two reversal off a strong prior close and a big pullback. The setup can play out in one of three ways: a quick sideways consolidation (hitchhiker), a 9 EMA continuation trade, or a VWAP continuation trade. Know your setup so well that you understand when the opportunity should be presented to you. Steve had Hood on his watchlist after a strong close with a big pullback the day before. He knew the setup. He just did not maximize it.

### Step 2: Enter the Trade Only After a Distinct and Noticeable Change in the Tape

Do not enter until you see a real, verifiable shift in price action. Watch the tape develop. Wait for buyers to step in, volume to accelerate, and the character of the price action to change. Calculate your stop placement before entry. Place your stop below the low of the pullback candle. Enter at or near the highs of that candle. This is a calculated decision, not an impulsive one. Both Steve and Paula entered at roughly the same price with the same initial risk. The difference came after entry.

### Step 3: Immediately After Entry, Stop Talking to Yourself About P&L

The moment you enter, do not start running P&L calculations in your head. That voice saying "I just want to make $200" is pulling your attention away from the stock. Nobody on the other side of the trade cares about your target. JP Morgan is not calling to help you hit your daily goal. When you make the trade about yourself, you miss the information the tape is giving you: increasing volume, persistent buyers, accelerating momentum. Steve exited right into the top because he was focused on his P&L. Paula stayed in because she was focused on the stock.

### Step 4: Keep Watching for a Change in Character

After you are in the trade, continue watching the tape. Look for a distinct and noticeable change in the character of the price action. If the stock reclaims VWAP and the buyers keep stepping in with accelerating volume, the trade is shifting. It is no longer just a scalp. It may be becoming a larger continuation. Do not assume your initial read is the final read. Paula watched the bid step up, saw the volume accelerate, and noticed the 9 EMA start to curl back up. That told her the entire stock was shifting.

### Step 5: Add to Your Position When the Tape Tells You the Opportunity Is Real

When the trade shifts and the momentum is confirmed, it is appropriate to add to your position. Paula entered the initial trade, then as the tape confirmed buyers were in control, she added another 100 shares. She then moved her stop up to the lows of the new pullback candle. This lets you lock in risk while staying in the trade. Your open P&L from the original position covers the new risk. You are not increasing your total exposure in a meaningful way. The trade can get better and better once momentum is truly on your side.

### Step 6: Set Your Exit Rule Before the Trade and Stick to It

Know your exit before you enter. For a momentum trade like this, Paula used a close below the 9 EMA as her exit signal. Do not top-tick the exit. Do not sell into strength because it feels good to take a quick win. Let the stock lead. When the bar closes below the 9 EMA, that is your reason to sell. Trust the process. Good stocks with momentum are surprisingly forgiving. They will pull back cleanly and give you another chance to exit. The paradox is that taking profits too early is often riskier than letting the stock run until your rule triggers.

### Step 7: Use the "Is It Real?" Mantra to Stay Present

Paula's secret was a simple question she repeated to herself throughout the trade: "Is it real?" If the momentum is real, the stock will continue to grow and the setup will keep presenting new opportunities. If it is not real, the trade will start to decompose and break down. Asking this question keeps you calm. It keeps you from forcing the trade. It lets you stay in when the tape is confirming and stay out when it is not. Practice this mantra in your journaling and then bring it into execution.

## Examples

### Example 1: Steve's $200 Trade - Focusing on Self Instead of the Stock

Steve studied the setup. He had Hood on his watchlist. He waited for the pullback, saw the buyers step in, and entered at the right time with the right stop. He sold right into the top of the move. Textbook execution on the entry. But he immediately started checking his P&L. The voice in his head said "I just made $200, that is enough." He mentally checked out and stopped watching the tape. He missed the volume acceleration, the persistent buyers, the 9 EMA curling back up, and the continuation. He walked away with a couple hundred dollars on a trade that had the potential for much more. There is nothing wrong with the $200 trade, but if you keep doing this every day, you are leaving thousands on the table annually.

### Example 2: Paula's $2,000 Trade - Staying Market Focused

Paula saw the exact same setup. She entered at the same price with the same initial risk. The difference is what she did next. She did not focus on her P&L. She focused on the tape. She watched the buyers step in after the VWAP reclaim. She saw volume accelerate. She watched the 9 EMA curl up. She recognized that this was no longer just a VWAP continuation scalp. The entire stock was shifting. She added another 100 shares and moved her stop up. She watched for the next setup. She stayed in until the 9 EMA broke. She walked away with approximately $2,000 on the same trade Steve took $200 from. Same setup. Same chart. Different outcome.

### Example 3: The "Fast Cars Have the Best Brakes" Principle

Good stocks with momentum are the most forgiving, but this is counterintuitive. Your natural instinct is to protect profits quickly when the stock moves in your favor. But in a strong momentum move, selling into strength is often the wrong move. The stock will likely pull back cleanly and give you another chance to exit. Paula understood this. She trusted the momentum. She did not need to top-tick her exit. The 9 EMA break gave her a clear, emotion-free exit rule. Let the stock run until your rule says stop.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Watch the tape, not your P&L. Your P&L is a lagging indicator. The tape is real-time information.
- ✅ Know your setup cold before the market opens so you recognize the opportunity when it appears.
- ✅ Calculate your stop and your entry before the setup triggers. Do not wing it in the moment.
- ✅ Wait for a distinct and noticeable change in the tape before entering. Patience is part of the edge.
- ✅ When a strong momentum trade shifts in your favor, consider adding to the position and moving your stop up.
- ✅ Set a mechanical exit rule before the trade (for example, close below the 9 EMA) and honor it without emotion.
- ✅ Practice the "Is it real?" mantra in your journaling so it becomes automatic in live trading.
- ✅ Let the stock lead. Do not try to predict where it will go or where it will turn against you.
- ✅ Trust that momentum is on your side when the tape shows persistent buyers and accelerating volume.
- ✅ Stay present. If you are thinking about your P&L, you are not listening to the stock.

## Keep In Mind

- This technique is for traders who already know their setup and are actively watching the tape in real time. It is not advice for beginners who have not yet identified a repeatable edge.
- The $200 trade was not a bad trade. Steve executed the entry correctly. The issue was the exit and the mindset shift that happened after the first profit target was hit.
- Paula did not take on additional risk beyond what Steve took. Her initial stop was the same. The add was backed by the open P&L from her first position. This is a risk management technique, not a doubling down on a gamble.
- The "Is it real?" question is a filter for presence. It forces you to look at the tape, not your internal narrative.
- Good momentum stocks tend to be more forgiving than slow or choppy stocks. The tape gives you clean signals and clean exits.
- You are not missing trades. You are often missing the best part of trades you have already taken because you checked out mentally.
- The setup is not the trade. The setup is the beginning. The trade is what happens when the stock starts moving in real time.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You enter a good trade and immediately start calculating how much profit you need or want from it.
  **Solution:** Recognize the P&L chatter as a distraction. Shift your focus back to the tape. Ask yourself "Is it real?" and let the stock answer.

- **Problem:** You take a quick scalp profit and later see the stock continue without you.
  **Solution:** Before exiting, scan the tape one more time. Check volume, buyer persistence, and the 9 EMA. If the tape is confirming momentum, do not exit just because you hit a psychological dollar target.

- **Problem:** You sell into strength because it feels good to take a win and you are afraid of giving it back.
  **Solution:** Understand that in strong momentum, selling into strength is often more risky than staying in. Let your mechanical exit rule (e.g., close below 9 EMA) trigger the exit, not your fear of missing out.

- **Problem:** You top-tick your exit by trying to sell at the exact high of the move.
  **Solution:** Stop trying to predict the top. Top-ticking requires luck, not skill. A mechanical exit rule gives you consistent, repeatable results.

- **Problem:** You mentally check out after a winning trade and miss the next setup that forms in the same stock.
  **Solution:** Treat each trade as part of a session, not an isolated event. After entering, stay engaged. Watch for adds and new entries. The best opportunities often appear right after your first trade is working.

- **Problem:** You confuse the setup with the trade and do not recognize when the setup has evolved into something better.
  **Solution:** Continuously reassess. A VWAP continuation can become a 9 EMA continuation can become a full scalp. Let the tape tell you what the trade is now, not what it was when you entered.

- **Problem:** You feel attached to your initial thesis and ignore tape evidence that contradicts it.
  **Solution:** The tape is always right. Your thesis is a starting point, not a conclusion. If the tape shows something different, adapt. Paula adapted. Steve did not.
