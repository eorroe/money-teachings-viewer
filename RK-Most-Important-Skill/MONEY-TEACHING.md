# The #1 Most Important Skill You Need To Be Successful

## Overview

Robert Kiyosaki teaches that the single most important skill for entrepreneurial success is communications, specifically the ability to sell. The video breaks down the BI Triangle framework, explains why businesses fail when entrepreneurs cannot communicate effectively, and provides actionable steps for mastering public relations, marketing, and sales. Kiyosaki demonstrates how these three disciplines directly determine whether someone remains a millionaire or becomes a billionaire, and why high-income professionals like accountants and attorneys often struggle despite being intelligent.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are starting or scaling a business and need to raise capital
- When you want to move from employee or self-employed status to big business owner or investor
- When you need to improve your ability to sell products, ideas, or yourself
- When your business income depends on customer acquisition and team leadership
- When you are building a personal or company brand that must remain consistent over time

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the BI Triangle Framework

Learn the five components of the BI Triangle: Employee (E), Self-employed/Small Business (S), Big Business (B), and Investor (I). A small business has fewer than 500 employees. A big business has more than 500 employees. To reach the B or I quadrant, you must build a business that is properly structured before investing money into it. Self-employed individuals and employees often cannot stop working because their income stops when they stop working. Use the BI Triangle to evaluate how well any business or investment is structured before committing capital.

### Step 2: Build Systems Into Your Business

Treat your business as a system of systems, similar to how a car is a system of systems or how the human body has a cardiovascular system, lung system, brain system, and digestive system. As a business owner, you do not need to personally operate the accounting systems, web systems, or marketing systems. Instead, hire and place someone in charge of each system. The goal is to do less work while earning more money by having systems run without your constant involvement.

### Step 3: Develop Military-Grade Leadership and Team Skills

Adopt the military school training model of mission, team, and leadership. You must be a team player before you can become a leader. Train yourself to follow orders when necessary and give orders when required. This discipline translates directly to civilian business leadership. If you are not a team player, you cannot effectively lead others, and your business will struggle.

### Step 4: Master Public Relations (PR)

PR is the foundation of how much media exposure, press coverage, and public positioning you receive. PR determines how much print space, advertising, television coverage, and news articles you generate. Strong PR creates priceless assets, such as maintaining a book on the New York Times bestseller list for multiple years or gaining access to high-level relationships. Never damage your PR relationships, because they provide long-term credibility and opportunity that money cannot buy.

### Step 5: Build Authentic Marketing With a Clear Brand

Marketing is the hype, awareness, and messaging that tells people what your company stands for. Develop a clear brand identity and stay true to it. Kiyosaki markets only to less than 10 percent of the population because he knows exactly who his target market is. Avoid flip-flopping on your brand message. If you were a mutual fund advisor in 1995 and then became a real estate advisor in 2005 without a clear brand, your market will not trust you. A strong brand means people know what you stand for before you ever speak to them.

### Step 6: Learn to Sell as Your Number One Skill

Sales is the most important skill for an entrepreneur because if you cannot sell, you have no income and you cannot raise capital. Kiyosaki raised approximately 150,000 dollars for the Rich Dad company by selling the vision to investors. Every entrepreneur must master three sales conversations: selling investors on why their money is safer or grows better with you, selling employees on why they should work for you for less money than they might earn elsewhere, and selling customers on why your product is more valuable than the money in their pockets.

### Step 7: Understand the Relationship Between PR, Marketing, and Sales

If your PR is bad and your marketing is bad, you must sell like a wild person because nobody knows who you are, what you stand for, or what your price positioning is. If your PR is strong but your marketing is bad, you still have to sell aggressively because people do not understand your product or brand. If your PR and marketing are both strong, selling becomes much easier because the prospect already knows who you are and what you represent. Study these three subjects for your entire career because mastery of communication separates millionaires from billionaires.

### Step 8: Protect Your Intellectual Property

When acquiring real estate or building a business, define and protect your intellectual property and property lines clearly. Property disputes over land ownership can destroy value. Use legal systems and attorneys to ensure your property rights and intellectual property are secure before investing time or money.

## Examples

### Example 1: Raising Capital for a Startup

An entrepreneur with a new product must first prove they can sell before anyone will give them money. Kiyosaki raised capital for the Rich Dad company by communicating the vision convincingly to investors. If you cannot articulate why an investor's money is better placed with you than anywhere else, you will not secure funding no matter how good your product is.

### Example 2: Selling Expensive Products in a Competitive Market

When Kiyosaki worked at Xerox, the company sold the most expensive copier on the market. They succeeded because they had strong brand awareness and knew their exact market position. Even though their price was high, customers already knew who they were, which made the sales process manageable. This demonstrates how PR and brand awareness reduce the burden on the sales process.

### Example 3: Accountants and Attorneys Who Cannot Sell

Many accountants and attorneys are extremely intelligent but cannot sell. They become angry when less-educated entrepreneurs make more money, not realizing the gap is caused by a lack of sales and communication skills. Steve Jobs and Donald Trump succeeded in large part because they could sell internally to their teams and externally to the public. Technical expertise alone does not create wealth if you cannot communicate value.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Master sales by learning to sell to investors, employees, and customers
- ✅ Build a brand that is clear and consistent, and never flip-flop on your message
- ✅ Create systems for every function in your business so you do not have to do the work yourself
- ✅ Study public relations, marketing, and sales continuously throughout your career
- ✅ Define and protect your intellectual property and property rights before investing
- ✅ Position yourself in the B or I quadrant of the BI Triangle rather than staying in E or S
- ✅ Use military-style discipline around mission, team, and leadership to build strong organizations
- ✅ Know your exact target market instead of trying to appeal to everyone

## Keep In Mind

- The number one reason businesses fail is that the entrepreneur is bad at communications and cannot sell
- Cash flow can be mismanaged by people who have a lot of money, so sales ability matters more than current wealth
- The difference between a millionaire and a billionaire is how good you are at communication, not what school you attended or which firm you work for
- Being a best-selling author is very different from being a best-writing author; results matter more than credentials
- Rich Dad as a brand only markets to people who want to learn what the rich teach their kids about money, and rejects saving, mutual funds, diversification, and working as an employee as core messages

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always protect your intellectual property with proper legal structures and attorneys
- Define property lines and ownership clearly before purchasing real estate or entering partnerships
- Do not rely on verbal agreements for property or business ownership disputes
- Secure your brand positioning and messaging so competitors cannot copy your market position

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Thinking that having a great product is enough to build a successful business.
  **Solution:** Master sales first. A great product without the ability to sell it will generate no revenue and no capital.
- **Problem:** Trying to appeal to the entire market with a generic brand message.
  **Solution:** Define a clear target market of less than 10 percent of the population and stay true to your brand identity.
- **Problem:** Failing to build systems and remaining trapped in self-employment.
  **Solution:** Build systems of systems and place qualified people in charge of each system so you can step back and the business continues running.
- **Problem:** Allowing PR or marketing to be weak and then expecting sales to compensate.
  **Solution:** Invest in all three: strong PR builds awareness, strong marketing builds understanding, and strong sales closes deals. Weakness in any area increases the workload exponentially.
- **Problem:** Avoiding sales because of fear of rejection.
  **Solution:** Accept that fear of rejection, cash flow concerns, and feeling lousy at communications are common barriers, and train specifically to overcome them through practice and study.
