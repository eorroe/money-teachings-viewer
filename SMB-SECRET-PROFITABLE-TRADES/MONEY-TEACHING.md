# SMB Approach to Finding Stocks to Trade

## Overview

This money teaching explains why trading stocks with a catalyst or theme context is a critical factor in trading profitably. Indicators and chart patterns are tools for executing trades, but without the context of a stock having a catalyst or being part of a theme, those patterns will not produce consistent outcomes. By learning to identify stocks in play, track market themes, and filter for setups with context, traders can improve their risk-reward profiles.

## When to Apply This Teaching

- When you experience repeated losses when trading chart patterns
- When you want to narrow your focus from watching all available stocks to only stocks with a catalyst or theme context
- When a catalyst emerges or a theme becomes active in the market and you need to identify which stocks have a catalyst or theme context
- When building or refining your active trading watchlist and stock screening process
- When you want to understand why trades using the same pattern succeed on some stocks and fail on others

## Steps

### Step 1: Accept That Stock Selection Drives Pattern Success

Understand that no indicator or chart pattern produces consistent results when applied without regard to stock context. A pattern traded on a stock with no catalyst or theme context is likely to fail. The stock's context is the primary variable. Treat identifying stocks with a catalyst or theme context as the first filter before evaluating any intraday setup.

### Step 2: Learn to Identify "Stocks in Play"

A stock is in play when it has an active catalyst or is part of an active market theme. Catalysts include new products, partnerships, earnings reports, government policy shifts, or short interest. Themes are groups of stocks moving together under the same narrative, such as artificial intelligence (AI), data centers, robotics, or quantum computing. Build the habit of asking: "Does the stock you are evaluating have a catalyst or theme context?"

### Step 3: Build and Maintain Theme-Based Watchlists

Create watchlists broken down by theme rather than by traditional sector or exchange-traded fund (ETF). For each theme, identify leaders, secondary beneficiaries, and low-float stocks. Know the price charts, liquidity profiles, and trading behavior of these stocks before a catalyst occurs. Review your watchlists each active trading day and update them as themes develop.

### Step 4: Use Volume as Your Volume Confirmation Signal

When a catalyst emerges, scan for stocks with higher-than-average volume in the relevant theme. Use tools like force-index scanners to identify the 10 strongest and 10 weakest stocks by volume. If at least two stocks from the same theme appear among the stocks with the highest volume, this suggests the theme is active and increases the probability of favorable trade outcomes.

### Step 5: Apply Intraday Patterns as Execution Tools

Once you have identified stocks with an active catalyst or theme context, use chart patterns and indicators to time your entries and exits. The pattern you are trading tells you where to place stop-loss orders and how to time entries and exits. But always remember: the pattern is the execution tool, not the stock selection criterion. The trading advantage comes from selecting a stock with a catalyst or theme context.

### Step 6: Project Potential Future Catalysts Within a Theme

Study how past themes developed by reviewing their catalyst timelines. When you identify a first catalyst, research what catalysts in similar themes have done in prior market cycles. Identify potential catalysts, such as U.S. government announcements, executive orders, or corporate partnership announcements. This research prepares you to take a position before the next phase of buying begins.

### Step 7: Review and Refine Your Process

After each active trading day, review which stocks you traded, which patterns you used, and whether each stock traded had an active catalyst or theme context. Identify instances where you traded a strong pattern on a stock without a catalyst or theme context, and note what catalyst or theme context you overlooked. Refine your watchlists and your ability to identify stocks with a catalyst or theme context.

## Examples

### Example 1: Trading the Robotics Theme

On a Wednesday in December 2024, a tweet from the White House announced the administration's intention to accelerate the robotics program in 2026. Traders who had been monitoring the robotics theme recognized the tweet as a catalyst. These traders checked their theme-based watchlists and identified key stocks: Tesla as the primary leader, iRobot (IRBT) as a low-float stock, Kit as a secondary runner, and Serve as a delayed mover. As these stocks gapped higher in the pre-market session and traded throughout the day, traders who focused on these stocks found long-position setups with a catalyst or theme context regardless of their preferred pattern types. The theme increased the likelihood of favorable trade outcomes; the intraday patterns provided the entry.

### Example 2: Data Center Sympathy Plays

On September 8, 2024, Enphase Energy announced a $19 billion deal with Microsoft. While this was a catalyst specific to Enphase Energy, traders who understood the data center theme also had Iron and Cypher on their theme-based watchlists. Both Iron and Cypher broke out on their daily price charts on the same trading day, even though these stocks had no direct involvement in the Enphase deal. This occurred because traders and investors were pricing in possible future contracts that Iron and Cypher could also secure with large cloud providers. Traders who recognized this sympathy price movement captured price gains before Iron and Cypher subsequently announced their own deals.

### Example 3: The Power of Context Over Patterns

A developing trader early in his trading career was mentored by Garrett Drinan at SMB Capital (a proprietary trading firm). Early in his trading career, he was trading more frequently than his strategy warranted, searching for patterns in every stock available regardless of context. Garrett's guidance changed everything: "The context would change. I read the context first to direct me to what I want to be looking at or looking to do, if anything. And then I use the intraday patterns as they reveal themselves for areas to watch the tape, gauge strength and weakness, time the trade, and control my risk. I want to be marrying the intraday patterns I like to trade with the context of the trade because the patterns are nothing without context." After adopting this approach, the trader's trading performance improved and the trader's stress decreased because he no longer felt compelled to scan every stock available for trading.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Read the context first before looking for patterns
- ✅ Build and maintain theme-based watchlists with categorized tickers and known catalysts
- ✅ Use volume scanners to confirm which names are moving when a catalyst emerges
- ✅ Project potential future catalysts by studying how similar themes developed in prior market cycles
- ✅ Narrow your universe to 1-3 stocks you have researched per day to preserve trading focus
- ✅ Treat intraday patterns as execution tools, not stock selection tools
- ✅ Review your trades daily to identify context gaps that led to losses

## Keep In Mind

- A trader's performance depends on the stocks they trade. This means an experienced day trader using a well-defined pattern can still lose money on a stock with no catalyst or theme context.
- The trading opportunity is in selecting the right stock, not in the pattern. The pattern is only the execution tool for trades.
- Market themes exhibit recurring patterns across different market environments. The robotics theme in 2026 followed a similar pattern as AI in 2025 and data centers earlier in 2025.
- Even when you cannot predict the specific catalyst, researching potential future catalysts prepares you to act when a theme begins to show price movement.
- Not all market themes will result in U.S. government action or executive orders. The practice of tracking themes is valuable even if the anticipated catalyst never materializes.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Verify the source of catalyst information before entering any trade. Unverified social media posts or unverified news headlines can cause price movement in a low-float stock but may not represent a sustained market theme.
- Use caution with low-float stocks with high short interest. While they can produce significant price moves, they are also susceptible to manipulation and high volatility.
- Understand that government policy announcements do not guarantee revenue for every stock in a given theme.
- Use a stop-loss order on every trade. Even in a stock with a catalyst or theme context, intraday patterns can fail.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trading a well-defined chart pattern on a stock with no catalyst or theme context
  **Solution:** Before entering, ask "Does the stock you are evaluating have a catalyst or theme context?" If the answer is no, wait for a trading setup with a catalyst or theme context.

- **Problem:** Excessive trading by scanning every stock available for the same pattern
  **Solution:** Narrow your watchlist to stocks that are in play. Prioritize quality setups over quantity to preserve trading focus and improve trading performance.

- **Problem:** Assuming every catalyst is always material
  **Solution:** Distinguish between a material catalyst (partnership, revenue, or acquisition) and a non-material catalyst (administrative mention, tweet, or meeting). Non-material catalysts can still cause stock price movement, but they require additional caution in position size and risk management.

- **Problem:** Ignoring the theme because a single stock in the theme group has weak financial metrics
  **Solution:** Focus on whether the theme itself is showing price movement, not on whether each stock in the theme is a long-term investment. Traders profit from the price movement driven by the theme, not from the underlying business performance of each participant.

- **Problem:** Holding a position after the theme has shown reduced volatility and trades in a narrower price range
  **Solution:** Themes become less volatile as they mature. Recognize when a theme is showing reduced volatility by watching for volatility decreasing, price ranges narrowing, and new competing themes appearing.
