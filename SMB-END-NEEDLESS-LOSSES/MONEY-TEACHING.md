# SMB: The Pink Line Rule - End Needless Losses & Catch More Winners

## Overview

The pink line rule is a trading methodology where traders only take breakout trades above a level where the stock has been rejected at least three times with failures that create conditions consistent with short covering pressure on subsequent breakouts, traders who got stopped out and want to get back in, and new buyers waiting to step in (the "pink line"). This reduces losses from entries before the level is confirmed and enforces patience and discipline. The rule simplifies trading decisions by creating a clear boundary: below the line, wait and do not chase; above the line, the opportunity begins. It addresses the psychological traps of fear of missing out (FOMO) and overtrading by giving traders a concrete visual and process-based filter that removes emotion from the decision.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are a new or experienced trader who chases breakouts and take losses from entries before the level is confirmed
- When you struggle with fear of missing out and overtrading around breakout levels
- When you want a rule-based framework with a single visual filter and two sets of rules for entering breakout trades
- When you are trading stocks with clear consolidation ranges and levels tested at least three times on elevated volume, not random intraday highs
- When you want to avoid the mistake of buying below the breakout level only to watch the stock reverse

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify and Mark Your Pink Line

Find the level on your chart where the stock has been rejected at least three times — a consolidation top or prior rejection level tested across multiple sessions, not a single-session pivot high. A test qualifies if either condition is met: a price excursion above the level that reverses such that the close is at least half the 14-period daily ATR below the level, or a wick extending at least 1% of the level price above the level that reverses to a close below the level. Make this line bold and bright (pink) on your chart so it is visually on top of all indicator and prior level lines in your chart's layer stack. Place it on a daily or weekly time frame, never a random intraday high. The level should be one where the stock shows volume confirmation and holds above it when it breaks. A strong pink line has been tested at least three times on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average with failures that create conditions consistent with short covering pressure on subsequent breakouts, traders who got stopped out and want back in, and new buyers waiting to step in.

### Step 2: Build Rules for When the Stock Is Below the Pink Line

When the stock is below the pink line, your default action is to wait, with exceptions described in Step 5. Do not enter within 1% below the pink line without confirmation. Do not place orders above the level expecting it to break (anticipate). Remember: if you follow a clear rule-based filter, you reduce the chance of missing high-probability setups. If you do not know what you are looking for, it is easy to overtrade. The pink line simplifies the decision by showing you that you are not missing any high-probability breakout opportunity below this point under the default rules. Breakout opportunity only occurs above the pink line under normal conditions; specific exceptions are documented in Step 5. Wait for confirmation that the stock holds above the level before entering.

### Step 3: Build Rules for When the Stock Is Above the Pink Line

When the stock breaks above the pink line, that is when you enter or place orders. Do not buy the exact second it crosses. Wait for confirmation that it holds above the level for at least 5 minutes (measured as one fully closed 5-minute candle above the level, or 300 continuous seconds without a single tick below the level; any tick below resets the timer to zero), or wait for Level 2 data showing buy orders resting at or within one tick of the pink line price exceeding sell orders by at least 2:1 without sell orders exceeding 100,000 shares pushing price back below. This confirmation improves your win rate. There is a difference between buying through the level and waiting for it to hold - the latter gives you better information.

### Step 4: Consider Time of Day and Energy

Be especially careful in the first 10 minutes of regular trading hours (09:30–09:40 ET for U.S. equities). This period is price discovery with frequent fakeouts and frequent mean reversion. If a stock opens a distance of half the 14-period daily average true range (ATR) below the pink line and rallies to it, it has already moved half the 14-period daily ATR and lost directional velocity. Ask yourself: what are you giving up by waiting? If you are looking for a 10-to-20-point move in a stock, giving up a small fraction of the expected move — for example, approximately 50 cents on a stock expected to move 10 to 20 points — to see if it actually holds the level is worth it. Do not buy above a level that has already run at least half the 14-period daily ATR — the risk/reward is reduced and it is likely to pull back.

### Step 5: Know When to Break the Rule (Nuances)

There are specific scenarios where buying below the pink line is allowed:
- **Catalysts**: A scheduled or unscheduled event with a historically observable price impact occurs while you are already watching the trade. The context has changed and you can anticipate the breakout because the volume will lead to it. Examples: Gold on a Fed pivot announcement; SMCI before an earnings pre-announcement.
- **Compressed Ranges**: If the stock has been consolidating in a range whose width is less than half the 14-period daily ATR for two consecutive regular trading sessions (09:30–16:00 ET, excluding weekends and holidays) — a two-session consolidation — and breaks out of that compressed range, you can anticipate the pink line breakout because the range has narrowed and the stock has not already run half the 14-period daily ATR to reach the level.
- **Strong Closes**: For swing trades, if a stock closes at or within the top 2% of the day's range (calculated as (close - low) / (high - low) ≥ 0.98) after outperforming its sector by at least 2 percentage points (stock return minus sector return ≥ 2%) or ranking in the top quartile of ranking in the top quartile of SSor ranking in the top quartile of the broader marketP 500P 500 performers by percentage gain and breaking a range spanning less than the 14-period daily ATR over three sessions, you may start getting in below the pink line because the close increases the probability of a breakout on the next trading session. - **Outlier Scenarios**: When a stock is actively trading on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average (where the 20-day average is the simple moving average of daily volume over the prior 20 trading sessions), confirmed alongside a catalyst event — a 1-hour or 4-hour time-frame level that meets the same test criteria (3+ rejections, 2σ volume) as daily/weekly levels can serve as a high-grade pink line for that session only, but only in a stock with volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average and catalyst-driven interest.

### Step 6: Grade Your Pink Lines

Not all pink lines are equal. Grade them based on:
- Time frame (daily and weekly are best because if the stock gets above a daily or weekly level, new buyers step in — the stock typically experiences follow-through buying from new participants; it should generally not be just a line on a five-minute or lower-time-frame chart, except under the catalyst and compressed-range exceptions described in Step 5)
- Number of tests (at least three tests on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average that failed produce a high-grade pink line)
- How narrow the price range is relative to the average true range near the level — measure how many 14-period daily ATRs the range spans; a tighter range relative to ATR means more compressed volatility is present
- Relative strength and market context
A line that has been tested five times on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average where prior sellers likely covered, inferred from the rejection and volume profile, creating pressure below the level is stronger than a simple pivot high. Each additional test builds more participants below the level: shorts who need to cover, traders who got stopped out and want back in, and new buyers waiting to step in — each failed test traps more traders below the level, creating a latent buying pool that executes when price finally breaks above, making the eventual breakout with a higher follow-through rate.

There was a breakout they were watching where the advance-decline line and up-volume exceeded down-volume — the Q's were rallying hard and many stocks were strong — and the stock had already moved one full average true range (ATR) to reach the pink line by midday. ### Example 3: Gold Breakout (Fed Pivot)

Gold was in the middle of a large weekly range — a huge weekly wedge — when a Fed pivot announcement hit. Even though it was below the pink line and within the bottom 5% of that range (i.e., (price - low) / (high - low) ≤ 0.05), the catalyst combined with price and volume data confirmation made it an allowed entry. The key was understanding the catalyst and seeing confirmation on the price and volume data.

### Example 4: SMCI Pre-Announcement

SMCI issued an early earnings release and was trading at 360 before its stock split. It was below the pink line in pre-market but the catalyst was so significant that buying the pre-market pullback made sense. The interpretation was that this catalyst was large enough to move the stock through the level — the pre-market price was less than 2% above the prior session close, but the catalyst's scale made the breakout likely. This shows that you are not ignoring the pink line - you are recognizing that the catalyst changes the conditions under which the rule applies.

### Example 5: The Q's Range Example

The Q's had been stuck in a range for at least three sessions with at least three days where it looked like it would break out, but it never closed above the level. ## Best Practices

- Build a full methodology around the line - rules for below and rules for above
- Wait for the stock to hold above the level for at least 5 minutes (measured as one fully closed 5-minute candle above the level; in the event of a conflict, the 5-minute candle takes precedence over continuous tick data and Level 2 order book data) before entering
- Ask yourself "what am I giving up by waiting?" when you feel urgency to enter
- Watch for the cumulative limit buy orders at the pink line to hold without sell orders exceeding 100,000 shares pushing price back below as confirmation
- Grade your pink lines based on time frame, number of tests, and volume
- Be patient off the open - the first 10 minutes of regular trading hours (09:30–09:40 ET for U.S. equities) are price discovery with frequent fakeouts and frequent mean reversion; avoid chasing strength in this window
- Trade within 5% of the boundary price (the edge), not in the middle of the range where price/volume patterns are less clear
- Monitor whether the stock has moved more than half the 14-period daily ATR to reach the level — if it has already run at least half the 14-period daily ATR, it has lost directional velocity and the risk/reward is no longer there; do not chase moves that have already lost directional velocity
- Look for closes at or within the top 2% of the day's range — where closing price is in the top 2% of the day's range with above-average volume — and sector outperformance as confirmation signals
- Do not buy above a level that has already run at least half the 14-period daily ATR to reach the pink line
- Do not buy the exact second the stock crosses the pink line out of fear of missing out
- Do not use untested intraday highs as pink lines — use daily or weekly levels tested at least three times on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average, where a test is a price excursion above the level that reverses such that the close is at least half the 14-period daily ATR below the level, or a wick extending at least 1% of the level price above the level that reverses to a close below the level
- Do not trade in the middle of ranges trying to guess direction
- Do not let fear of missing out cause you to buy above the level without waiting for confirmation
- When breaking the rule for a catalyst, apply the exception only after you can see the catalyst's impact on the price and volume data and confirm that the current session's volume is at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average; catalysts vary in magnitude, and only those meeting the volume threshold qualify as exceptions

## Keep In Mind

- The pink line rule works on multiple time frames, but daily and weekly levels are preferred because they capture the accumulation of participants over multiple sessions and produce higher follow-through rates than intraday time frames.
- Breakouts at the open happen but are the exception, not the rule
- A sharp move off the open — where a stock opens at least 0.5 times the 14-period daily ATR below the pink line and rallies to it — can reverse quickly when the stock has not yet broken the level; a close at or within the top 2% of the day's range carries more information about buying pressure than a rapid intraday rally that reverses
- If you follow a clear rule-based filter, you reduce the chance of missing high-probability setups - patience comes from clarity of rules
- The goal is not to catch every move - it is to avoid losses from entries before confirmation while catching confirmed breakouts
- Choosing not to take a trade is a correct decision when the stock never closes above the level
- At least three tests and failures at a level accumulate shorts who need to cover, traders who got stopped out and want back in, and new buyers waiting to step in — all building pressure below the level and making the eventual breakout with a higher follow-through rate
- Stocks in a range have regular volatility and mean reversion — a lot of price discovery, choppiness, and price reversals at support and resistance — look for three consecutive daily bars with higher highs and higher lows on the daily chart with volume above the 20-day average, indicating a shift from choppy to trending
- You can afford to wait because you are not missing any breakout opportunity below the pink line
- The open is where fear of missing out is most acute in the first 10 minutes of regular trading hours — stocks are making rapid intraday rallies that reverse, making it easy to chase strength off the open — recognize this and do not let it drive your decisions

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Chasing stocks off the open that look strong but have not broken the pink line
  **Solution:** Wait for the breakout above the pink line with confirmation that it holds for at least 5 minutes (measured as one fully closed 5-minute candle above the level, or 300 continuous seconds without a single tick below the level; any tick below resets the timer to zero). Strength off the open frequently fails when the stock has not broken the level — the open involves price discovery, fakeouts, and mean reversion, especially when the stock opens at least half the 14-period daily ATR below the level and has already lost directional velocity. If it opens a distance of half the 14-period daily ATR below, it has already moved too far before reaching the level.

- **Problem:** Buying the exact moment the stock crosses the pink line out of fear of missing out
  **Solution:** Let it get above the level and hold for at least 5 minutes (measured as one fully closed 5-minute candle above the level, or 300 continuous seconds without a single tick below the level; any tick below resets the timer to zero), or wait for Level 2 data showing buy orders resting at or within one tick of the pink line price exceeding sell orders by at least 2:1 without sell orders exceeding 100,000 shares pushing price back below. Make the stock prove it can hold above the level before you enter.

- **Problem:** Trading in the middle of a range and interpreting noise as signal
  **Solution:** Focus on the edges of the range where there is a clear price/volume pattern. Trades in the middle of the range have less clear price/volume patterns and lead to overtrading and guesswork.

- **Problem:** Using untested intraday highs as pink lines
  **Solution:** Grade your lines. Use daily or weekly levels tested at least three times on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average where a test is a price excursion above the level that reverses such that the close is at least half the 14-period daily ATR below the level, or a wick extending at least 1% of the level price above the level that reverses to a close below the level, and the stock shows volume confirmation when it breaks. An intraday high with no prior tests on volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average is not a pink line.

- **Problem:** Entering a stock that has moved more than half the 14-period daily ATR to reach the pink line without breaking out
  **Solution:** Monitor whether the stock has already moved more than half the 14-period daily ATR to reach the level. If it has moved that far without breaking out, the 5-period rate-of-change (ROC) of price is less than or equal to zero, where ROC = (current price - price 5 periods ago) / (price 5 periods ago), indicating loss of directional velocity and the risk/reward is no longer there — entering more than 1% above the prior close without confirmation is a chase to avoid.

- **Problem:** Using the catalyst exception as a reason to abandon process discipline
  **Solution:** When breaking the rule for a catalyst, confirm the catalyst's impact on the price and volume data with volume at least two standard deviations above the 20-day average. Not all catalysts are equal - you need visible price and volume confirmation on the chart that this catalyst meets the volume threshold.
