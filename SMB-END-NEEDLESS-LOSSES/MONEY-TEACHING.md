# SMB: The Pink Line Rule - End Needless Losses & Catch More Winners

## Overview

The pink line rule is a trading methodology where traders only take breakout trades above a level where the stock has been tested three or more times with failures that build up pressure (the "pink line"). This prevents losses from entries before the level is confirmed and enforces patience and discipline. The rule simplifies trading decisions by creating a clear boundary: below the line, wait and do not chase; above the line, the opportunity begins. It addresses the psychological traps of fear of missing out (FOMO) and overtrading by giving traders a concrete visual and process-based filter that removes emotion from the decision.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are a new or experienced trader who chase breakouts and take losses from entries before the level is confirmed
- When you struggle with fear of missing out and overtrading around breakout levels
- When you want a rule-based framework with a single visual filter and two sets of rules for entering breakout trades
- When you are trading stocks with clear consolidation ranges and levels tested multiple times on high volume
- When you want to avoid the mistake of buying below the breakout level only to watch the stock reverse

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify and Mark Your Pink Line

Find the level on your chart where the stock has been rejected multiple times - often a pivot high, consolidation top, or prior rejection level. Make this line bold and bright (pink) on your chart so it stands out above all other lines. Normally place it on a daily or weekly time frame, not a random intraday high. The level should be one where the stock shows volume confirmation and holds above it when it breaks. A strong pink line has been tested three or more times on elevated volume with failures that build up pressure.

### Step 2: Build Rules for When the Stock Is Below the Pink Line

When the stock is below the pink line, your default mode is to wait. Do not chase. Do not anticipate. Remember: if you know what you are looking for, you are not going to miss it. If you do not know what you are looking for, it is easy to overtrade. The pink line simplifies the decision by showing you that you are not missing any breakout opportunity below this point. Breakout opportunity only occurs above the confirmed level. Wait for confirmation that the stock holds above the level before entering.

### Step 3: Build Rules for When the Stock Is Above the Pink Line

When the stock breaks above the pink line, that is when you get involved. Do not buy the exact second it crosses. Wait for confirmation that it holds above the level for at least 5 minutes, or wait for sell orders at higher prices to be absorbed. This confirmation improves your win rate. There is a difference between buying through the level and waiting for it to hold - the latter gives you better information.

### Step 4: Consider Time of Day and Energy

Be especially careful in the first 10 minutes of the trading session. This period involves volatility with frequent fakeout moves. If a stock opens a distance of half the average true range (ATR) below the pink line and rallies to it, it has already moved half an ATR. Ask yourself: what are you giving up by waiting? If you are looking for a multi-point move in a stock, giving up a small fraction of the expected move to see if it actually holds the level is worth it. Do not buy above a level that has already run more than half an ATR - the risk/reward is reduced and it is likely to pull back.

### Step 5: Know When to Break the Rule (Nuances)

There are specific scenarios where buying below the pink line is allowed:
- **Catalysts**: A catalyst such as earnings, news, or a Fed policy announcement occurs while you are already watching the trade. The context has changed and you can anticipate the breakout because the volume will lead to it. Examples: Gold on a Fed pivot announcement; SMCI before an earnings pre-announcement.
- **Compressed Ranges**: If the stock has been compressing in a narrow range for one to two days and breaks that smaller range, you can anticipate the pink line breakout because the stock has not already run half an ATR to reach the level.
- **Strong Closes**: For swing trades, if a stock closes near the high of the day after outperforming its sector or the broader market and breaking a small prior range, you may start getting in below the pink line because the close increases the probability of a breakout on the next trading session. Many institutional traders execute large orders at the market close, providing a real read on consensus.
- **Outlier Scenarios**: When a stock is actively trading on unusually high volume with a catalyst, a lower-time-frame level can serve as an A+ pink line for that session only.

### Step 6: Grade Your Pink Lines

Not all pink lines are equal. Grade them based on:
- Time frame (daily/weekly is best, not random intraday highs)
- Number of tests (three or more tests on elevated volume that failed are A+)
- How narrow the price range is relative to the average true range near the level
- Relative strength and market context
Each additional test brings more participants into the level. A line that has been tested five times on elevated volume where participants got squeezed is stronger than a simple pivot high.

## Examples

### Example 1: Tesla False Breakouts

The traders were watching Tesla in a tight range setting up for a breakout. Every day off the open, it would move sharply higher, look like it was exhibiting relative strength, and appear to be breaking out. They chased it every morning only to watch it collapse back to the lows before finally breaking out. The losses from the false-start entries offset any gain from the confirmed breakout. The difference between the successful breakout and the failed ones? The winning day opened much closer to the pink line, while the chasing days opened a distance of half the average true range below it. They were buying above the breakout level before it held instead of waiting for confirmation.

### Example 2: The "No Trade" Decision

There was a breakout they were watching where the market was strong, internals were positive, and the stock had already moved one full average true range (ATR) to reach the pink line by midday. It looked like a strong bullish intraday setup with strong bid depth throughout the session. But it was still below the pink line and it was midday, when prior-day momentum often fades. They waited. It never closed above the level. The correct decision was not to take the trade - waiting was validated. The answer to "what am I giving up by waiting?" was zero.

### Example 3: Gold Breakout (Fed Pivot)

Gold was in the middle of a large weekly range when a Fed pivot announcement hit. Even though it was below the pink line and within the range, the catalyst combined with tape and volume confirmation made it an allowed entry. This was still a breakout trade - they were anticipating the breakout because the catalyst would drive the volume needed to break the level. The key was understanding the catalyst and seeing confirmation on the tape.

### Example 4: SMCI Pre-Announcement

SMCI issued an early earnings release and was trading at 360 before its stock split. It was below the pink line in pre-market but the catalyst was significant enough that buying the pre-market pullback made sense. The interpretation was that this catalyst was large enough to move the stock through the level. This shows that you are not ignoring the pink line - you are recognizing that the catalyst changes the conditions under which the rule applies.

### Example 5: The Q's Range Example

The Q's had been stuck in a range for multiple sessions with several days where it looked like it would break out, but it never closed above the level. If you had been trying to buy every time it looked strong within the range, you would have overtraded. The pink line rule would have kept you out until real confirmation. The answer to "what am I giving up by waiting for a close above the level?" was zero. You save capital and decision bandwidth by waiting.

## Best Practices

- Build a full methodology around the line - rules for below and rules for above
- Wait for the stock to hold above the level for at least 5 minutes before entering
- Ask yourself "what am I giving up by waiting?" when you feel urgency to enter
- Watch for sell orders at higher prices to be absorbed as confirmation
- Grade your pink lines based on time frame, number of tests, and volume
- Be patient off the open - the first 10 minutes of the trading session involve volatility with frequent fakeout moves
- Trade at the edges of ranges, not in the middle where there is less information and more noise
- Monitor whether the stock has moved more than half an ATR to reach the level - do not chase exhausted moves
- Look for closes near the high of the day and outperformance as confirmation signals
- Do not buy above a level that has already run more than half the average true range to reach the pink line
- Do not buy the exact second the stock crosses the pink line out of fear of missing out
- Do not use random intraday highs as pink lines - use daily or weekly levels tested three or more times on elevated volume
- Do not trade in the middle of ranges trying to guess direction
- Do not let fear of missing out cause you to buy above the level without waiting for confirmation
- When breaking the rule for a catalyst, apply the exception with full analysis of tape and volume

## Keep In Mind

- The pink line rule works on multiple time frames but is most powerful on daily and weekly charts
- Breakouts at the open happen but are the exception, not the rule
- A sharp move off the open can reverse quickly when the stock has not yet broken the level; a close near the high of the day carries more information about consensus
- If you know what you are looking for, you will not miss it - patience comes from clarity of rules
- The goal is not to catch every move - it is to avoid losses from entries before confirmation while catching the real breakouts
- Choosing not to take a trade is a correct decision when the stock never closes above the level
- Three or more tests and failures at a level build pressure and make the eventual breakout more significant
- Stocks in a range have frequent volatility and mean reversion - look for the shift from choppy to trending
- You can afford to wait because you are not missing any breakout opportunity below the pink line
- The open is where the most fear of missing out occurs - recognize this and do not let it drive your decisions

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Chasing stocks off the open that look strong but have not broken the pink line
  **Solution:** Wait for the breakout above the pink line with confirmation that it holds for at least 5 minutes. Strength off the open often fails when the stock has not broken the level. If it opens a distance of half the average true range below, it has already moved too far before reaching the level.

- **Problem:** Buying the exact moment the stock crosses the pink line out of fear of missing out
  **Solution:** Let it get above the level and hold for at least 5 minutes, or wait for sell orders at higher prices to be absorbed. Make the stock prove it can hold above the level before you enter.

- **Problem:** Trading in the middle of a range and interpreting noise as signal
  **Solution:** Focus on the edges of the range where there is real information. Trades in the middle of the range have less information and lead to overtrading and guesswork.

- **Problem:** Using intraday random highs as pink lines
  **Solution:** Grade your lines. Use daily or weekly levels tested three or more times on elevated volume where the stock shows volume confirmation when it breaks. An intraday high with no prior tests on high volume is not a pink line.

- **Problem:** Entering a stock that has moved more than half an ATR to reach the pink line without breaking out
  **Solution:** Monitor whether the stock has already moved more than half an ATR to reach the level. If it has moved that far without breaking out, it is often a chase to avoid.

- **Problem:** Using the catalyst exception as a reason to abandon process discipline
  **Solution:** When breaking the rule for a catalyst, confirm the catalyst's impact on the tape and volume. Not all catalysts are equal - you need a reason to believe this time is different.
