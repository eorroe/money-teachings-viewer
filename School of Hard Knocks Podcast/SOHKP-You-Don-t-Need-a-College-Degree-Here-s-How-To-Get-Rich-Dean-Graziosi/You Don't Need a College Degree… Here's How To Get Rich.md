# You Don't Need a College Degree… Here's How To Get Rich ｜ Dean Graziosi

## Overview

This money teaching distills the key principles from Dean Graziosi's journey into actionable steps you can apply to build wealth without a college degree. Graziosi ran and operated over 13 businesses and did over a billion dollars in business throughout his career, despite barely graduating high school and struggling with dyslexia. His core message is that formal education is not a prerequisite for wealth — what matters is finding the right money-making vehicle, attaching yourself to mentors who have already achieved what you want, building an unshakable mindset, and modeling proven practices instead of reinventing the wheel. He emphasizes that success leaves clues, and the fastest way to get where you want to go is to follow the footsteps of someone who has already been there.

## Steps

### Step 1: Decide That Money Is a Tool for Solving Problems, Not a Status Symbol

Graziosi's earliest motivation came from watching his mother work three jobs — cutting hair, painting houses, cleaning houses — and come home exhausted every night at 9:00 PM. His parents split when he was three, and his father was not there to help support the family. Watching his mother struggle taught him that money solves problems, and anyone who says otherwise has not given enough away. He made a commitment at age 12 or 13 that he would not live that life, and that commitment became the foundation of everything he built. Find your "why" — the specific person or problem that makes earning money personal — and let it drive you when motivation fades.

### Step 2: Choose a Money-Making Vehicle That Sparks Your Heart, Not Just Your Wallet

Graziosi bought a Tony Robbins course called "Personal Power" 27 years ago, and it changed his life by showing him that he could change his own story. The course also made him realize he wanted to enter the personal development industry — not because it was the most profitable at the time, but because it lit him up. He was already doing well in real estate, but he created courses on real estate and shifted into selling information and proven practices. His advice: do not pick a business just for the money, because there will come a day when you want to quit. Pick something that sparks your interest and has no glass ceiling — something that aligns with your heart and your lifestyle goals.

### Step 3: Find a Mentor and Become Their Best Apprentice

Graziosi's number one piece of advice for someone without money, connections, or mentors is to find someone who is already doing what you want to do and attach yourself to them. Offer to work for free or for minimal pay. Become the greatest employee they have ever had. Make it so they cannot live without you. This apprenticeship model condenses decades of learning into days — you get to see how decisions are made, how deals are structured, and how problems are solved in real time. Read the book "Mastery" by George Leonard to understand this model deeply. Your mentor does not need to be a billionaire — they just need to be a few steps ahead of you and willing to teach.

### Step 4: Build an Unstoppable Mindset by Rewiring Your Beliefs

Graziosi believes mindset is 90% of success and the vehicle is only 10%. Most people fail not because they picked the wrong business, but because they have stories running in their heads that tell them they cannot succeed. These stories — "I did not come from a good area," "I do not have a college degree," "I am not smart enough," "I do not have money to start" — are lies. Prove them wrong by finding 20 people who started with your exact circumstances and built something extraordinary. Replace your old stories with new ones every single day. Even Graziosi still has an inner voice telling him he is not that smart — he has just learned to overpower it.

### Step 5: Find a Purpose Bigger Than Your Fear of Failure

Graziosi says the reason most people do not take action is not that they are procrastinators — it is that they do not have a big enough "why." He uses a vivid analogy: if your brother is dying in New Mexico and needs a kidney, you will sprint across the desert even if you have no car, no money, and no plan. That is the level of purpose you need to succeed in entrepreneurship. Fear of failure, fear of judgment, fear of losing money — these all shrink when your purpose is big enough to overpower them. Write down exactly what you are building and who it will help. Make it so big that fear becomes irrelevant.

### Step 6: Take the Uncomfortable Actions First

Graziosi observes that most people fill their days with the actions they enjoy — posting videos, making plans, reading books — while avoiding the one action that would actually move the needle. That action is usually sales: making the call, sending the email, building the landing page, asking for the deal. He calls this facing the "demon" — the thing you have been putting off because it is scary or uncomfortable. But here is the truth: the demon is guarding the door to your next level of success. If you are not doing the uncomfortable thing every single day, you are staying exactly where you are.

### Step 7: Master Leadership Before You Need a Team

Graziosi says the three most important skills for an entrepreneur are: (1) fall in love with your product so deeply that you feel bad if people do not say yes, (2) understand marketing better than anyone else, and (3) understand leadership principles before anyone else does. Most people do not start studying true leadership until they are 40 or 50 years old, and by then they have already built bad habits and burned through good people. Start studying leadership now — read books, take courses, find mentors who lead well. Leadership is the key to everything because it determines whether you can scale beyond what one person can do alone.

### Step 8: Model Proven Practices — Do Not Reinvent the Wheel

Graziosi's entire career is built on the principle that success leaves clues. He models proven practices from the people he interviews, the books he reads, and the mentors he works with. He advises young entrepreneurs to be obsessed with finding the common threads between successful people — the subtle shifts in mindset, the daily habits, the decision-making frameworks — and apply them consistently. The fastest way to get where you want to go is to start off where someone else left off. Do not try to invent a new path when there is already a well-worn road to the destination you want.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Dyslexic High School Dropout to Billion-Dollar Career

Graziosi had dyslexia, barely got out of 12th grade, and never considered college. His mother's struggle — working three jobs and coming home exhausted every night — was the fire that drove him. He started in the car business, had a collision shop, auto sales, tow trucks, apartments, raw land, and flipped houses. Then he found real estate education and personal development, and everything shifted. He realized that all the bad things that happened in his life were actually meant to make him stronger. His story is proof that your starting point does not determine your endpoint.

### Example 2: The $100,000 Course Purchase That Changed Everything

Graziosi bought Tony Robbins' "Personal Power" course 27 years ago, and it completely reframed his understanding of himself and his potential. He realized he could change his own story, focus on solutions, change his state of mind, and enhance his confidence. He was so transformed that he bought 10 different courses and sent them to everyone in his family — and not one person listened. They should have, he says. The lesson: invest in your own education first, and do not wait for your family or friends to validate your growth. They may not be ready to see what you see.

### Example 3: The Power of Mentorship and the Apprentice Model

Graziosi's daughter was about to go to 12th grade, and he gave her a choice: go to college, or find something she is passionate about and apprentice under someone who is already doing it. She chose interior design, researched the top three designers in Scottsdale, had a phone call with one, and was scheduled to meet for coffee. His advice to her — and to every young person watching — is to sell yourself as the best intern they have ever had, offer to work for free, and absorb everything. Apprenticeship is not a step down; it is the fastest acceleration available.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always do your own research before making any financial decision — even if a mentor recommends it
- Consult with a qualified attorney before signing any partnership, franchise, or real estate agreement
- Never invest more than you can afford to lose, especially in early-stage ventures
- Be cautious of "gurus" who charge high prices for courses without verifiable results — verify track records before paying for mentorship

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Choosing a money-making vehicle based on what seems profitable rather than what you love
  **Solution:** Ask yourself: if I had to do this for five years without a payoff, would I still enjoy it? If the answer is no, find a different vehicle.

- **Problem:** Waiting for the "right" mentor to find you instead of going after them
  **Solution:** Research the top performers in your industry, find their contact information, and send a specific, value-first message offering to help them for free.

- **Problem:** Letting old stories about your past block your future
  **Solution:** Write down every story that tells you that you cannot succeed, and then find one person who proved that story wrong. Do this for every limiting belief.

- **Problem:** Spending all your time learning and none of it doing
  **Solution:** For every hour you spend consuming content, spend one hour executing. Knowledge without action is just entertainment.

- **Problem:** Trying to build a business while working a job you hate
  **Solution:** Use the urgency of your dissatisfaction as fuel. The best entrepreneurs are not the ones who love risk — they are the ones who cannot stand their current situation enough to stay in it.
