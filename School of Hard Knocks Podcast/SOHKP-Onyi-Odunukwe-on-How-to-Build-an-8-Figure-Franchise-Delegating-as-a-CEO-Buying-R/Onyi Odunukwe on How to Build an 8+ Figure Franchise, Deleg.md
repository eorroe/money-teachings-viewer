# Onyi Odunukwe on How to Build an 8+ Figure Franchise, Delegating as a CEO, & Buying Real Estate

## Overview

This money teaching distills the key business principles from Onyi Odunukwe's journey into actionable steps you can apply to build a franchise empire, scale as a CEO, and invest in real estate. Odunukwe is the founder and CEO of Glow Tanning, one of the largest sun spa and tanning franchises in the United States. Born to Nigerian doctor parents, raised across multiple countries, and educated in a military high school, he built his empire by treating entrepreneurship as a personality trait rather than a career choice. His story demonstrates that gratitude, relentless self-reliance, and the willingness to delegate to strong leaders are the foundations of building an eight-figure franchise business.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When building a franchise business in the beauty, wellness, or service industries
- When transitioning from operator to CEO and learning to delegate effectively
- When buying real estate as part of your business or investment strategy
- When deciding whether entrepreneurship is right for you versus traditional employment
- When the user asks about Glow Tanning, franchise scaling, CEO delegation, real estate investing, or building an 8-figure service business

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That Entrepreneurship Is a Personality, Not a Career Path

Onyi makes it clear that he did not choose entrepreneurship after careful career analysis — he was born with a problem with authority. He does not like being told what to do, where to be, or when to work. His choice was simple: he would rather make $40,000 working for himself than $1,000,000 working for someone else. If you feel this same resistance to structure and direction, entrepreneurship may not be a choice for you — it may be the only viable path. Do not force yourself into a corporate career if your personality rebels against it.

### Step 2: Start Hustling as Early as Possible

Onyi's first entrepreneurial act was getting in trouble in school for selling candy bars with his brother in eighth grade. They bought candy bars at a dollar store for 60 cents each, marked them up to a dollar, and sold out within one lunch period — until they got caught with $500 and a book bag full of candy. That early experience taught him that the market will always reward someone who can supply what people want, even if the system tries to stop them. Start hustling young, get comfortable with risk and rejection, and treat every early failure as tuition.

### Step 3: Build Gratitude as Your Operating System

Onyi says gratitude is the biggest principle he talks about all the time. Not gratitude as a passive feeling, but gratitude as an active operating system: be grateful for what you have, where you are, the people in your life, and the opportunities in front of you. Gratitude is not incompatible with ambition — it is the fuel that keeps you moving forward without becoming bitter or entitled. When you operate from a place of "enough," you make better decisions, treat people better, and build businesses that last.

### Step 4: Leave Corporate Life Before It Leaves You

Onyi's turning point was the realization that he could not follow directions, did not want to answer to anyone, and would rather struggle on his own terms than succeed on someone else's. He watched his parents — both doctors — follow the traditional path: school, degrees, stable jobs, retirement accounts. That path did not appeal to him because it required surrendering control over his time, his energy, and his creativity. If you feel the same way, leave before you become comfortable enough to never leave. The cost of staying grows the longer you wait.

### Step 5: Scale Through Franchising, Not Just Corporate Ownership

Glow Tanning grew into an eight-figure franchise empire by giving other entrepreneurs the right to operate under the brand while providing them with systems, training, and support. Franchising allows you to scale capital-efficiently because franchisees invest their own money into locations. The key is building systems that are replicable and training that is thorough enough that anyone can run a location profitably. If your business model works in one location, franchising is the fastest way to multiply it without taking on all the capital risk yourself.

### Step 6: Delegate to Leaders, Not Managers

As CEO of an eight-figure franchise company, Onyi emphasizes that you cannot scale if you are still making every decision. The mistake most founders make is hiring managers who need to be told what to do. Instead, hire individual contributors who can run entire functions without building teams underneath them. These are the people who can step into a leadership role, execute autonomously, and hold themselves accountable. Your job as CEO is to set the vision, recruit the right people, and get out of their way.

### Step 7: Buy Real Estate as a Wealth Preservation Strategy

Onyi incorporates real estate into his overall wealth strategy as a way to preserve and grow the wealth generated by his business. Real estate provides cash flow, tax advantages, and a hedge against inflation that a franchise business alone cannot. The key is to buy properties that cash flow positively from day one and to use depreciation and other tax strategies to reduce your overall tax burden. Business income may be volatile, but well-located real estate compounds quietly over time.

### Step 8: Set Goals That Make You Uncomfortable — Then Chase Them

Onyi's philosophy on goal-setting is that high achievers are never content. The moment you reach one goal, you are already looking at the next one. He uses specific, tangible goals — like buying a Rolls-Royce — as milestones that prove he is progressing, but he also recognizes that the chase is more satisfying than the catch. Set goals that scare you a little, attach timelines to them, and celebrate the achievement without becoming complacent. The goalpost will always move — that is what keeps you growing.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Candy Bar Suspension to 8-Figure Franchise CEO

Onyi's entrepreneurial journey started in eighth grade when he and his brother got suspended for selling candy bars at school. They had bought 60 candy bars at a dollar store, marked them up, and sold out in one lunch — until the principal caught them with $500 in cash and a confiscated inventory. That early experience taught him that demand is everywhere, even in places that try to suppress it, and that the penalty for success is sometimes just the cost of doing business.

### Example 2: Choosing $40,000 of Freedom Over $1,000,000 of Subservience

Onyi's defining career decision was rejecting the traditional path of education, corporate employment, and slow accumulation. He watched his parents — both successful doctors — follow that path, and he knew it was not for him. His exact words: "I'd rather make $40,000 working for myself than make a million dollars working for somebody else." This mindset is what allowed him to take the early risks that built Glow Tanning. If you are staying in a job you hate because of the paycheck, ask yourself: what is the real cost of staying?

### Example 3: Gratitude as the Foundation of Sustainable Ambition

Onyi returns to gratitude constantly — not as a self-help cliché, but as a practical tool for maintaining perspective. He points out that a billionaire in Germany killed himself during the 2008 downturn even though he still had $2 billion. To that billionaire, losing $5 billion felt like losing everything. To someone making $50,000 a year, that same billionaire's remaining $2 billion would feel like unimaginable wealth. Everything is about perspective: frame your life correctly, practice gratitude for what you have, and the chase for more becomes sustainable rather than desperate.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Consult a qualified business attorney before drafting franchise agreements or operating agreements
- Maintain strict separation between personal and business finances across all entities
- Understand the regulatory requirements for franchising in your state before offering franchise opportunities
- Conduct thorough due diligence on all real estate acquisitions — cash flow projections are only as good as the assumptions behind them

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Staying in a corporate job because the paycheck is safe, even though you hate it
  **Solution:** Calculate the real cost of staying — not in dollars, but in lost autonomy, creativity, and time. If you would rather be free than rich, make the move.

- **Problem:** Hiring managers instead of leaders, creating a dependency on your direction
  **Solution:** Hire people who have already done the job you are hiring them for. They should be able to execute the entire function without your input on day one.

- **Problem:** Scaling a franchise before your systems are replicable
  **Solution:** Document every process, train every trainer, and run at least one profitable location for 12 months before you franchise. If you cannot teach it, you cannot scale it.

- **Problem:** Buying real estate for appreciation rather than cash flow
  **Solution:** Cash flow is oxygen. If a property does not pay for itself from day one, it is a speculation, not an investment.

- **Problem:** Becoming complacent after hitting a major milestone
  **Solution:** The goalpost always moves. Celebrate the win for 24 hours, then set the next goal before the complacency sets in.
