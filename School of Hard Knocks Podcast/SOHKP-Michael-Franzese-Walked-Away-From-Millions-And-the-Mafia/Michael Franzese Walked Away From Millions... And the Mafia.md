# Michael Franzese Walked Away From Millions... And the Mafia

## Overview

This money teaching distills the key principles from Michael Franzese's journey from a high-ranking mobster to a legitimate entrepreneur into actionable lessons on calculated risk-taking, negotiation, redemption, and building a business on integrity. Franzese was inducted into the Colombo crime family in 1975 at age 20, became one of the most influential mobsters in America, and then walked away from the life to become a Christian, a motivational speaker, and the founder of multi-million dollar legitimate businesses including Franzese Wine and pizza vending machines. His core message is that the same skills that made him successful in organized crime — negotiation, relationship-building, risk assessment, and relentless execution — are the same skills that build legitimate empires, but they must be applied within the bounds of law and morality.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When considering how to transition from a questionable or illegal income source to legitimate business
- When learning advanced negotiation tactics that work in any context
- When trying to understand how to assess and manage calculated risk
- When rebuilding your life and reputation after making serious mistakes
- When the user asks about Michael Franzese, mafia stories, redemption arcs, wine business, or building legitimate businesses after a criminal past

## Steps

### Step 1: Master Negotiation — It Is Business, Not Personal

Franzese's most transferable skill from his mafia years is negotiation. He emphasizes that negotiation is not about aggression or intimidation — it is about finding outcomes that work for both parties. He was a phenomenal negotiator because he approached every deal as a business problem, not a personal conflict. The practical application is: in any business deal, remove your ego, focus on the interests of both sides, and look for the zone of possible agreement. The best negotiators do not win arguments; they solve problems.

### Step 2: Take Calculated Risks, Not Reckless Ones

Franzese describes himself as a risk-taker, but he is careful to qualify that: his risks are always calculated. He does not gamble on outcomes he cannot influence; he bets on situations where he has done enough research and built enough leverage to shift the odds in his favor. The difference between a successful entrepreneur and a reckless one is not the size of the risk — it is the depth of the analysis behind it. Before you take any major risk, ask yourself: what is the worst-case scenario, and can I survive it? If the answer is yes, the risk may be worth taking.

### Step 3: Find a Purpose Bigger Than Yourself

Franzese's turning point was the worst night of his life — a moment when he was out of answers, surrounded by the consequences of his choices, and wanted to lay his head on a cot and not wake up. He found God in that darkness, but the deeper principle is universal: you need a purpose that outweighs fear, outweighs the opinions of others, and outweighs the discomfort of hard work. Whether your purpose is providing for your family, leaving a legacy, or serving a cause bigger than yourself, it must be strong enough to pull you through the days when you do not feel like showing up.

### Step 4: Rebuild Your Reputation Through Consistent Action, Not Words

After leaving the mafia and serving time in prison, Franzese had to rebuild every relationship and every shred of credibility from zero. He did this by showing up consistently, delivering on his promises, and living a life that matched his new values. Reputation is not built by telling people you have changed — it is built by being the kind of person who shows up when it is inconvenient, who delivers results when no one is watching, and who treats people fairly even when they have every reason to expect otherwise.

### Step 5: Leverage Your Story as a Unique Competitive Advantage

Franzese's past — which most people would hide — became his greatest asset as a speaker, author, and entrepreneur. His story of walking away from millions in mob income to pursue redemption is so unusual that it commands attention in a way that a standard success story never could. The principle here is not that you should make mistakes on purpose, but that if you have a unique story, you should find a way to turn it into value. Your perceived weakness can become your most differentiated strength.

### Step 6: Build Businesses That Solve Real Problems

Franzese's legitimate business ventures — Franzese Wine and pizza vending machines — were not built on his reputation as a former mobster. They were built because they solved real problems: he created a wine brand with quality and value, and he invented a pizza vending machine that delivers fresh pizza as good as what comes out of an oven. The formula is the same in any industry: identify a frustration, build a solution that is genuinely better, and market it with authenticity. Your past may get people's attention, but your product keeps them.

### Step 7: Surround Yourself With Young, Innovative People

Franzese explicitly chooses to surround himself with young people because they keep him innovative, energetic, and connected to what is happening in the world. He says this was one of the most innovative interviews he has ever done because the hosts were young and asked questions from a fresh perspective. The principle is universal: if you want to stay relevant and avoid getting stuck in old patterns, spend time with people who see the world differently than you do. Energy is contagious, and so is stagnation.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Mafia Induction to Wine Entrepreneur

Franzese was inducted into the Colombo crime family in 1975 at age 20, took a blood oath, and quickly became one of the most powerful figures in organized crime. At the height of his power, he was pulling in millions through illegal schemes. After a spiritual conversion and time in prison, he walked away from that life entirely and built Franzese Wine — a legitimate, multi-state wine brand sold in Costco, Ralphs, Kroger, and Total Wine. The same skills of persuasion, distribution, and relationship-building that made him powerful in the mafia made him successful in legitimate business, but now he sleeps at night.

### Example 2: The Worst Night of His Life

Franzese describes the worst night of his life as the first night he was truly out of answers. He was at his lowest point, surrounded by the wreckage of the life he had built, and all he wanted was to not wake up the next day. That moment of total surrender — of realizing he could not solve his own problems — is what opened the door to his transformation. The lesson for entrepreneurs: sometimes you have to hit a wall so hard that there is nowhere to go but up. The breakdown becomes the breakthrough.

### Example 3: The Pizza Vending Machine Innovation

Franzese's latest venture is pizza vending machines that produce fresh pizza as good as what comes out of an oven — something most people would assume is impossible. He did not let conventional wisdom stop him; instead, he invested in solving the technical problem and built a brand around it. The machines are now in nine states, and the product is available in major retail chains. This demonstrates that the same risk-taking mindset he had in the mafia — betting on unconventional solutions — can create breakthrough innovations in legitimate markets.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Master negotiation as a business skill — separate ego from deal-making and focus on mutual gain
- ✅ Take calculated risks where you can influence the outcome, not reckless bets on luck
- ✅ Find a purpose bigger than yourself that can pull you through the hardest days
- ✅ Rebuild reputation through consistent action, not through apologies or explanations
- ✅ Turn your unique story into a competitive advantage rather than hiding it
- ✅ Build businesses that solve real, verified problems — not just businesses that look good on paper
- ✅ Surround yourself with people who see the world differently to stay innovative
- ❌ Do not let your past define your future — but do not pretend it did not happen either
- ❌ Do not take risks you cannot survive if they go wrong
- ❌ Do not expect people to trust you because you say you have changed — prove it through sustained behavior
- ❌ Do not build a brand around your reputation without backing it up with a real product

## Keep In Mind

- The same skills that make you powerful in one context can make you powerful in another — but the context must change
- Your lowest point is not your permanent address — if you are willing to rebuild, you can create something better than what you lost
- Reputation is rebuilt through action, not words — show up consistently and let time do the rest
- Young people keep you innovative — seek them out instead of waiting for them to find you
- Redemption is possible, but it requires more work than the original offense

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never use intimidation, coercion, or threats in any business negotiation — these tactics are illegal and will destroy your reputation
- Maintain complete legal compliance in all business operations, especially if you have a public profile
- Disclose your full background to business partners and investors — hiding your past creates legal and reputational risk
- Consult with qualified legal counsel before entering any business venture, especially one in a highly regulated industry like alcohol

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Letting your past define how people see your future
  **Solution:** Acknowledge your history, but build a track record of legitimate success that speaks louder than any scandal.

- **Problem:** Taking reckless risks because you are used to high-stakes environments
  **Solution:** Apply the same calculation you used in high-risk situations — assess worst-case scenarios, build contingencies, and only proceed when the odds are in your favor.

- **Problem:** Using aggressive negotiation tactics that worked in one context but are illegal or unethical in another
  **Solution:** Master principled negotiation (focus on interests, not positions) and leave every deal better than you found it.

- **Problem:** Assuming your story is a liability rather than an asset
  **Solution:** Your unique journey is your most differentiated marketing tool. Frame it honestly, take responsibility for it, and show what you have built since.

- **Problem:** Building a personal brand without a real product or service behind it
  **Solution:** Attention without a product is vanity. Make sure your business solves a real problem before you invest in building your platform.
