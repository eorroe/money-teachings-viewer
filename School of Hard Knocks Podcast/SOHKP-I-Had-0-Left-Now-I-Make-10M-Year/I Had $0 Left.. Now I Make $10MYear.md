# I Had $0 Left.. Now I Make $10M/Year

## Overview

This money teaching distills actionable principles from Chris Waters' journey from rock bottom to building Waters International, one of the largest real estate companies in the country. The content covers mastering the three skill dominoes of entrepreneurship, learning from failure, and building systems that let you scale beyond your own time.

**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=lhkND-5yXio

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When learning the foundational sales skills for entrepreneurship
- When rebuilding after a major business failure or loss
- When scaling a real estate or services business
- When deciding whether to stay in a job or start a company
- When the user asks about real estate, sales skills, or bouncing back from failure

## Steps

### Step 1: Master the Three Dominoes of Sales

Entrepreneurs must knock down three skills in order: inside sales (booking appointments by phone), outside sales (converting strangers into clients face to face), and subject matter expertise (understanding the product and market deeply). Mastery of all three is required to build a scalable business.

### Step 2: Develop Subject Matter Expertise by Doing Deals

Do not wait to become an expert before getting in front of customers. You develop expertise by doing transactions, not by studying textbooks. Understand what people want, then incrementally add value to each deal. Expertise comes from the repetition of real work.

### Step 3: Learn From Rock Bottom

Chris invested all his savings into a bar and restaurant in December 2009. Nine months later, the business was evicted. He had less than $10 in his account. That failure forced him to learn humility, get a sales coach, and commit to cold calling 30 to 40 hours per week until he improved.

### Step 4: Get a Coach and Invest in Yourself

After six months of terrible phone performance, Chris hired a sales coach to teach him cadence, tonality, and empathy. Results grew 300% to 400% within weeks. Coaching accelerated his progress more than self-study ever could.

### Step 5: Hire to Replace Yourself at the Bottleneck

Once Chris became too busy servicing customers to keep prospecting, he hired his first inside sales person. That freed him to focus on higher-value activities. Productivity grew another 300% to 400%. The entrepreneurial ladder is skills-based; you must keep climbing.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Bar and Restaurant Failure

Chris put years of savings into a bar off 6th Street in Austin in December 2009, right as the market hit bottom. Nine months later, the Texas Alcohol Beverage Commission had chains on the door. He walked away with essentially nothing.

### Example 2: Cold Calling Transformation

Chris spent six months cold calling 7 days a week, 30 to 40 hours per week, booking only one or two appointments per week. After getting a sales coach, he was booking four or five appointments per week and his results grew 300% to 400%.

### Example 3: The Inside Sales Hire

After becoming too busy to prospect, Chris hired someone to take over inside sales and appointment booking. That single hire freed him up and grew overall productivity by another 300% to 400%.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Master inside sales first: phone prospecting and appointment booking
- ✅ Follow with outside sales: converting strangers into clients face to face
- ✅ Build subject matter expertise through actual deals, not just study
- ✅ Get a coach when you hit a performance ceiling
- ✅ Hire to replace yourself at the bottleneck as soon as you can afford it
- ❌ Don't wait to be an expert before getting in front of customers
- ❌ Don't let ego prevent you from learning from people ahead of you
- ❌ Don't spread yourself too thin across too many ventures

## Keep In Mind

- The entrepreneurial ladder is very skills-based, just like the corporate ladder
- Most successful people had to lose their minds a little bit to what they were working on
- Humility and coachability are prerequisites for rapid growth
- Forward progress creates happiness; comfort creates stagnation

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never invest more than you can afford to lose in a new business venture
- Keep an emergency reserve even when you feel successful
- Be wary of personal guarantees on business debt
- Always consult with a financial advisor before investing savings into a high-risk venture

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting to become a subject matter expert before meeting customers
  **Solution:** Learn by doing deals; expertise follows experience

- **Problem:** Refusing coaching because you think you already know enough
  **Solution:** A good coach can compress years of learning into weeks

- **Problem:** Trying to do everything yourself instead of delegating
  **Solution:** Hire to replace yourself at the bottleneck as soon as cash flow allows

- **Problem:** Getting comfortable after one success and spreading into too many ventures
  **Solution:** Stay focused on your core competency and keep grinding
