# I Ran Out Of Money… Now I Sell Real Estate To Billionaires ｜ Ryan Serhant

## Overview

This money teaching distills actionable principles from Ryan Serhant's journey from broke actor to founder of a $4 billion per year real estate brokerage, education business, and media company. The content covers choosing success over a specific career, manufacturing confidence through storytelling, and building digital distribution for modern sales.

**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=wkeACs3xXR0

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When pivoting from one career to another with no relevant experience
- When entering real estate sales or luxury markets
- When building a personal brand as a salesperson
- When using media and storytelling to differentiate yourself
- When the user asks about real estate, personal branding, or sales careers

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose Success, Not a Career

Ryan did not choose real estate. He chose success. He was willing to be a theater actor, a law student, or anything else that paid the bills. Detach your identity from any single career path. Success is the goal; the vehicle is interchangeable.

### Step 2: Manufacture Confidence by Becoming Future You

Ryan projected confidence he did not yet have by acting like the person he would become. He told casting directors he was the best real estate broker in the history of the known universe even though he had only been licensed for a little over a year. Position yourself as future you, not current you.

### Step 3: Facts Tell, Stories Sell

Ryan discovered that the best salespeople do not recite facts. They tell stories. His acting background taught him how to breathe, project emotion, and make people care. Use storytelling to differentiate yourself from competitors who only list features and prices.

### Step 4: Build a Digital Presence to Reach Buyers

Ryan sells multimillion-dollar properties primarily through social media and digital content. A 13-year-old girl saw a property tour on social, showed it to her mother, and they bought a $3 million home. The first point of contact happens digitally, not at an open house.

### Step 5: Practice More Than You Play

Top athletes spend 95% of their time practicing and 5% in the game. Most salespeople spend 95% in the game and 5% practicing. Invest in learning, role-playing, and skill-building every single day. The first three years in real estate are graduate school, not a paycheck.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Million Dollar Listing Audition

Ryan showed up to an open casting call with 3,000 other agents. He told the producers he was the greatest real estate broker in the history of the known universe. Nine months later, they cast him. He sold a $60 million house over video without ever meeting the buyer.

### Example 2: The Social Media Buyer

Ryan's team created a vertical property tour for a $15 million Chelsea listing. A 13-year-old girl saw it on social, showed her mother, and the mother bought the house for $3 million. The deal closed because of digital storytelling.

### Example 3: The Mercedes-Benz Miami Launch

Ryan's firm sold 100 apartments in the first four days of the Mercedes-Benz branded residences launch in Miami. They did it entirely through organic social and digital awareness without a single print ad.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Choose success as your identity, not a specific career title
- ✅ Project future success with confidence and integrity
- ✅ Tell stories instead of reciting facts
- ✅ Build a massive digital presence to reach buyers where they already are
- ✅ Practice your craft daily; treat the first years as graduate school
- ❌ Don't lie about your current status, but do project where you are going
- ❌ Don't rely on traditional advertising alone in modern markets
- ❌ Don't stop learning once you make your first big sale

## Keep In Mind

- Personal brand is your most valuable asset in sales
- Digital platforms level the playing field for new agents
- Buyers are on social media; so are their children who influence their decisions
- The market moves fast; if you are not learning daily, you are falling behind

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always represent properties truthfully; never lie about facts
- Keep client data and transaction details confidential
- Follow all real estate licensing laws in your state
- Consult with a lawyer before signing any exclusive listing agreements

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Tying your identity to a single career that fails
  **Solution:** Choose success as your goal. The vehicle can change.

- **Problem:** Competing on price instead of storytelling
  **Solution:** Facts tell, stories sell. Invest in media and narrative.

- **Problem:** Thinking traditional advertising is still the best channel
  **Solution:** Go where the buyers are. In 2026, that is digital and social.

- **Problem:** Treating the first three years as a money-making phase
  **Solution:** Treat the first three years as graduate school. Learn as much as you can.
