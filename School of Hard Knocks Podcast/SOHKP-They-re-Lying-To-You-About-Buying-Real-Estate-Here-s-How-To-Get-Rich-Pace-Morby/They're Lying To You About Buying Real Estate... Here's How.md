# They're Lying To You About Buying Real Estate... Here's How To Get Rich ｜ Pace Morby

## Overview

This teaching distills the core real estate investing principles shared by Pace Morby, a nine-figure real estate entrepreneur who built a half-billion-dollar portfolio without ever using a bank loan, into actionable steps. Morby's central insight is that real estate investing does not require your own money, your own credit, or a traditional path into the industry. You can buy property by finding motivated sellers, understanding their real problems, and using creative financing to structure deals that work for both sides. The barrier to entry is not capital — it is knowledge, persistence, and the willingness to solve problems no one else is willing to touch. There are 26 exit strategies for every property. Find the seller's problem, not just their price.

- When you are stuck in a job you do not own and want to transition to investing
- When you are a contractor or service provider who realizes you are working for someone else's wealth
- When you need to understand seller financing, subject-to deals, and creative finance structures
- When you want to learn how to find and solve motivated seller problems
- When the user asks about real estate investing, no-money-down deals, wholesaling, or creative finance

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Trading Your Time for Money — Become the Owner

Morby was a successful construction contractor making $25 million a year with 250 employees, but a customer told him the truth: "You're not in real estate. You're a slave to me. I own the house. All you're doing is working for me." That conversation shattered his identity. If you work for someone who owns the property, you are an employee no matter how big your business looks. The goal is to own the asset, not to serve the person who does. You cannot replace the owner. The contractor can be Googled. The agent can be replaced. The tenant can be replaced. The owner cannot.

### Step 2: Learn to Find the Seller's Real Problem

Every deal is solved by finding the problem the seller will not say out loud. Morby's first deal taught him this: the seller was a retiring school teacher who could not part with three giant Flemish bunnies her granddaughter had brought home. Every other investor asked about the house. Morby asked about the bunnies. He arranged for his mother to take them. Two weeks later, the seller called and said, "I'm selling the house to you." The deal was not about price — it was about solving a problem no one else cared about. In every seller conversation, ask: "What is your real problem?" The bunnies are always there. You just have to look.

### Step 3: Use Creative Finance Instead of Bank Loans

You do not need a bank to buy real estate. Morby has never applied for a bank loan and never walked into a bank to buy a property. He uses subject-to financing (buying a property while taking over the seller's existing mortgage payments), seller financing (the seller becomes your bank), and lease options. These structures require no credit check, no large down payment, and no bank approval. Every creative finance deal is negotiated across four terms: price, down payment, interest rate, and time horizon. Master these four terms and you can structure any deal without a bank.

### Step 4: Start with Wholesaling to Build Your "Find" Muscle

Before you buy anything, learn how to find deals. Morby started by sending $5,000 in direct mail postcards that said "We'll buy your house." His first lead came in, and he did not know how to value the property, set an appointment, or write an offer. But he kept going. Wholesaling — finding a deal and assigning the contract to another investor for a fee — teaches you the most important skill in real estate: recognizing a deal before you have the money to fund it. You cannot learn to evaluate properties by sitting in a classroom. You learn by going on appointments, making offers, and watching what sellers say.

### Step 5: Determine the Exit Strategy Before You Commit

There are 26 exit strategies for a house you buy — short-term rental, long-term rental, mid-term rental, section eight, assisted living, wholesale, fix-and-flip, and more. Before you commit to a deal, figure out which strategy makes more money than the mortgage payment. Use rentometer.com and AirDNA to compare rental income against the monthly cost. Do not commit to the deal and then figure out what to do with it. Figure out the exit first, then commit. If the numbers do not work on at least one exit strategy, the deal is not a deal.

### Step 6: Buy from Motivated Sellers, Not from the MLS

Properties listed on the MLS are priced for retail buyers and already shopped by every investor in town. Motivated sellers — people in pain, behind on payments, going through divorce, inheriting a house they do not want — will sell to you at a discount because they need a solution, not a highest bid. Find them through direct mail, cold outreach, driving neighborhoods, or building relationships with probate attorneys. The pain level of the seller determines the discount you get. The MLS is where deals go to die. Motivated sellers are where wealth is built.

### Step 7: Solve the Problem, Not the Price

When a seller says "I need $165,000," but your maximum is $150,000, most people walk away. Morby asks instead: "What is the real problem?" If the seller cannot retire because of three giant bunnies, solve the bunnies. If the seller is afraid of capital gains tax, show them how to avoid it. If the seller is tired of managing a property, offer to take over management. The price becomes irrelevant when you solve the underlying problem. Look for the bunnies in every transaction. The bigger the asset, the bigger the bunnies — and the bigger the opportunity.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Flemish Bunny Deal

Morby sent out $5,000 in postcards and got a lead from Janie Munson, a retiring school teacher whose house needed $150,000 but she had a $165,000 offer already on the table. Morby could not meet the price. Instead of walking away, he asked what her real problem was. She showed him three Flemish bunnies — the size of five-year-old children — that her granddaughter had brought home ten years earlier. She could not retire until she found someone to take them. Morby called his mother, who came and took the bunnies. Two weeks later, Janie called and sold him the house for $150,000. He walked away with $25,000 in 7 hours of work. The lesson: solve the problem nobody else is willing to address.

### Example 2: The RV Park Creative Finance Deal

Morby bought an RV park for $5 million without bringing any money to closing. The seller had a $400,000 capital gains tax problem and was going to net only $500,000 after commissions and taxes if he sold traditionally. Morby offered to pay $5 million over 10 years at 4% interest with no money down. The seller became his bank. Morby avoided the tax hit, the seller earned an extra $500,000 over time, and Morby owned an 85-pad RV park next to Glacier National Park. The deal worked because he solved the seller's tax problem, not just his own acquisition problem.

### Example 3: From Contractor to Full-Time Investor

Morby ran his construction company and his real estate business simultaneously for four years because he did not believe he could replace his construction income. Then his biggest construction customer filed for bankruptcy and lost $16 million. Morby lost everything tied up in those projects. He had to sell his rental properties to liquidate. But he knew how to find deals. He went full-time in real estate and never looked back. The identity shift from "I am a contractor" to "I am a real estate investor" took four years of pain to complete. Sometimes the universe forces the identity shift you were not brave enough to make yourself.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Solve the seller's real problem before negotiating price
- ✅ Use creative finance (subject-to, seller financing) instead of bank loans
- ✅ Start with wholesaling to build your deal-finding muscle
- ✅ Determine your exit strategy before you commit to a purchase
- ✅ Buy from motivated sellers, not from the MLS
- ✅ Treat every property as a series of four negotiable terms: price, down payment, interest rate, time
- ✅ Build systems so you can scale without personally managing every property
- ✅ Find a partner or integrator who handles operations so you can focus on vision and deal flow
- ❌ Do not go to a bank when you can go directly to the seller
- ❌ Do not commit to a deal before you know how you will exit it
- ❌ Do not let your current job identity block you from switching to investing
- ❌ Do not compete on price when you can compete on problem-solving
- ❌ Do not listen to people worth less than you want to be — take advice from those who have already solved your problem

## Keep In Mind

- The only non-replaceable person in real estate is the owner. The contractor can be Googled. The agent can be replaced. The tenant can be replaced. The owner cannot.
- "Find the bunnies" should be your question in every seller conversation. Every deal is solved by the problem no one else is willing to address.
- Creative finance is not illegal. Subject-to is a standard transaction that has been used for decades. The fear comes from not understanding the structure.
- You do not need credit. You do not need cash. You need a motivated seller and a structured deal. Everything else is negotiation.
- Concentration builds wealth. Diversification keeps it. Focus on one asset class — real estate — until you have mastered it, then diversify within it.
- Your network determines your net worth. If you are not around people playing the game at the level you want, your subconscious will never map the path to get there.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is educational content based on a publicly available podcast episode.
- Real estate transactions carry significant legal and financial risk. Consult a qualified attorney before using subject-to or seller financing structures.
- Subject-to deals require understanding of the due-on-sale clause in the existing mortgage. Some lenders may accelerate the loan if the property is transferred without their consent.
- Always disclose the structure of the deal to the seller and ensure they understand the implications before signing any agreement.
- Never make promises to a seller about tax outcomes without consulting a qualified CPA or tax professional.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to compete on price with institutional buyers on the MLS.
  **Solution:** Stop shopping for listed properties. Start building relationships with motivated sellers who need a solution more than they need top dollar.

- **Problem:** Waiting until you have "enough" money or credit to start investing.
  **Solution:** Start wholesaling or bird-dogging deals today. You can learn to find deals with zero capital. The skill is more valuable than the money.

- **Problem:** Buying a property before figuring out the exit strategy.
  **Solution:** Run the numbers on every exit strategy before you sign. Rentometer, AirDNA, and local market knowledge will tell you whether the deal works.

- **Problem:** Holding onto a contractor identity that prevents full-time investing.
  **Solution:** Your identity is a choice, not a fact. You can be a real estate investor today even if you were a contractor yesterday. The market does not care about your job title.

- **Problem:** Taking advice from people worth less than you want to be.
  **Solution:** A $2 million person's advice on how to reach $100 million is rarely useful. Listen to people who have already built what you are trying to build.
