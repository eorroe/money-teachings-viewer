# He Nearly Went Bankrupt… Then Built 5 Companies Worth Over $1 Billion | David Royce

## Overview

This money teaching captures the lessons from David Royce, who built and sold five companies for over a billion dollars after nearly going bankrupt in year one. The core principles are to choose boring, scalable businesses with recurring revenue, master sales through deliberate practice, and use proven models rather than chasing sexy ideas. You should apply these teachings when selecting a business model, building a sales organization, or navigating early-stage cash flow crises.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When evaluating which industry or business model to enter
- When building or scaling a sales team from scratch
- When facing cash flow crises in the early years of a company
- When deciding whether to bootstrap or raise capital
- When the user asks about sales mastery, recurring revenue, or business model selection

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose Boring Businesses With Great Margins and Recurring Revenue

Avoid industries that are trendy but fragile. Service-based businesses with recurring revenue—such as pest control, plumbing insurance, or SaaS—generate predictable cash flow and higher valuations when you sell. Look for models where customers pay regularly and churn is low.

### Step 2: Master Sales Before You Scale

Sales is the engine of every business. Study sales books, record and mimic top performers, and practice your pitch until it is reflexive. When your presentation is automatic, you can focus on body language, tonality, and reading the customer instead of worrying about what to say next.

### Step 3: Lead With Confidence and Non-Verbal Communication

Your face, tone, and posture tell customers whether you believe in your product. Smile, nod slowly, and speak like a friend rather than a hype man. Confident people do not need to shout; they let the customer feel comfortable and in control. If you look scared, you will transfer fear to the buyer.

### Step 4: Build a Door-to-Door Sales Machine If You Need Rapid Growth

A trained, commission-only sales team can grow a local service business seven to ten times faster than digital marketing alone. Hire hungry people with no sales experience, train them intensively, and pay them purely on results. Remove base pay so they are forced to perform.

### Step 5: Manage Cash Flow Like Your Life Depends on It

Revenue is vanity, profit is sanity, but cash flow is reality. When you pay sales commissions upfront before customers renew, you can run out of money even with strong sales. Keep a cash buffer, negotiate payment terms with reps if necessary, and always know your runway.

### Step 6: Copy Proven Models and Pivot Slightly

Do not reinvent the wheel. Find a business that is already working, study its model, and execute it slightly better. Copying is the first step to building a billion-dollar company. Adaptation and execution matter more than originality.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Near Bankruptcy to Sale

A college student threw all their savings into a pest control company and nearly went bankrupt in year one because they underestimated customer acquisition costs. By studying sales systems, building a door-to-door team, and focusing on recurring service contracts, they sold that first company for $13 million within a few years.

### Example 2: The Rack System for Sales

A salesperson who went five days without a sale bought half a dozen sales books and read 90 minutes per day. They developed a "rack system" that stacked rebuttals, aces, and multiple closing styles. When a customer objected, the rep had two or three responses ready, followed by a new piece of information, and then a fresh close. That system turned them into a top producer.

### Example 3: Recurring Revenue as an Insurance Policy

A pest control company treated its service like an insurance policy: customers paid a small recurring fee and received free emergency callbacks. The customers with the fewest pest problems were actually the most loyal because they feared bugs returning. That model created predictable revenue and a much higher multiple when the company was sold.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Choose businesses with recurring revenue and high margins
- ✅ Study sales deliberately and practice daily
- ✅ Train your sales team thoroughly instead of throwing them against the wall
- ✅ Keep cash reserves to cover commission cycles
- ✅ Copy successful models and improve them by 10 percent
- ❌ Do not chase trendy or low-margin businesses just because they are exciting
- ❌ Do not hire salespeople on base salary if you need peak performance
- ❌ Do not assume revenue equals safety; cash flow determines survival

## Keep In Mind

- Most businesses fail because of cash flow, not because of bad products
- A trained sales team is the single biggest competitive advantage in a local service business
- Recurring revenue businesses command much higher multiples upon exit
- Your first year will be the hardest; plan for it

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always maintain a cash buffer equal to at least several months of operating expenses
- Be wary of taking on investors before you have proven your model
- Do not overhire before you have consistent revenue
- Consult a financial advisor before structuring commission plans or exits

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Running out of cash because commissions are paid before renewal revenue arrives
  **Solution:** Model your cash cycle carefully and negotiate commission timing with sales reps

- **Problem:** Hiring salespeople without training and then blaming them for poor results
  **Solution:** Invest heavily in onboarding, scripting, and ongoing coaching before expecting performance

- **Problem:** Choosing a business based on ego or trendiness rather than economics
  **Solution:** Ask whether the model has high margins, recurring revenue, and scalability before falling in love with the idea

- **Problem:** Giving up after the first year of losses
  **Solution:** Expect year one to be painful; plan for it, and judge success by trajectory, not early profit
