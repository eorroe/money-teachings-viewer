# Invest in What You Love Rather Than Simply Doing What You Love

## Overview

Young people make the critical mistake of pursuing careers they are passionate about rather than acquiring income-generating assets. This leaves them in the employee and self-employed quadrants. Robert Kiyosaki teaches that time is your greatest asset when young, and that you must learn financial subjects you find difficult or uninteresting—such as taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits—in the short term to build the assets you are passionate about in the long term.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are a young person or early-career individual beginning to think seriously about building wealth
- When you hear yourself or others saying "I want to do what I love" as a career justification
- When you are currently in the E (Employee) or S (Self-Employed/Specialist) quadrant and want to move toward B (Big Business) or I (Professional Investor)
- When you feel pressured by peers to chase trendy investments like Bitcoin without building foundational financial intelligence first

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Cash Flow Quadrant and Identify Your Target Quadrant

Learn the four quadrants defined in the Cash Flow Quadrant framework. E stands for Employee, who works for a salary or wage. S stands for Self-Employed or Specialist, such as a doctor, lawyer, or web designer who owns a job but cannot stop working. B stands for Big Business, which means owning a business with 500 employees or more that generates income even when you are not actively working. I stands for Professional Investor, who owns income-generating assets.

Write down which quadrant you are currently in and which quadrant you want to reach. Most young people default to E or S because schools and society train them to seek stable careers. Recognize that B and I are the quadrants where true wealth is built.

### Step 2: Recognize That "Doing What You Love" Is a Trap for the E and S Quadrants

Understand that the phrase "I want to do what I love" is the defining mindset of people trapped in the E and S quadrants. When you pursue a career doing what you love as an employee or specialist, you are focusing on earned income. In the E quadrant, you pay 40% in taxes on your earned income. In the S quadrant, you own a job and cannot stop working. In the B and I quadrants, you face lawsuits.

Acknowledge that doing what you love as a career does not build lasting wealth unless the career itself is an asset that generates income independently of your labor.

### Step 3: Adopt the Mindset of "Invest in What You Love" Instead

Replace the phrase "I want to do what I love" with "I want to invest in what I love." This is a fundamental shift in mindset. Robert Kiyosaki loves real estate and has never gone past a piece of land or a building he did not love. He also loves businesses, commodities like food, oil, gold, silver, and avocado farms. He does not love stocks, bonds, mutual funds, ETFs, 401(k)s, or IRAs, and he does not invest in them.

Write down asset types you genuinely care about. Examples of these asset types are real estate properties, businesses, and commodities. Use these as your target asset classes rather than pursuing a career in an industry you find interesting.

### Step 4: Learn the Subjects You Hate So You Can Acquire Assets You Love

List the financial subjects you currently avoid or find boring, such as taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits. These are exactly the subjects you must learn to move from the E or S quadrant to the B or I quadrant.

Commit to studying each of these subjects. The goal is to reach a level of financial literacy where you can understand your own personal financial statements and make investment decisions independently.

### Step 5: Understand Liquidity and Risk Before Acquiring Each Asset

Before acquiring any asset, understand its liquidity profile. Liquid assets like stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and ETFs can be sold quickly if you make a mistake. Illiquid assets like real estate and businesses cannot be easily sold, meaning a mistake will be expensive and long-lasting. Gold is more liquid than oil. Silver is pretty easy to get out of. Oil is tough to get out of.

For each asset class you target, understand that the less liquid the asset, the more education and due diligence you need before acquiring it.

### Step 6: Avoid Chasing Shiny Objects and Trendy Investments

A "shiny object" in investing is any trendy investment that you do not fully understand and that distracts you from your chosen asset classes. Bitcoin is a shiny object. It takes no intelligence to buy Bitcoin. Kiyosaki owns four or five Bitcoin.

When you encounter a shiny object, remember that chasing shiny objects is how most people stay in the E and S quadrants their entire lives while believing they are building wealth.

### Step 7: Build a Business Before Trying to Invest in Certain Asset Classes

Understand that real estate, private businesses, and physical commodities are asset classes that are difficult to access in meaningful size without a business income stream providing the capital and cash flow. If you work as an employee, your income is heavily taxed, leaving less capital to invest. Employees cannot use other people's money the way business owners can. Alexandra's mother shifted to using other people's money to invest in multi-family properties after adopting the Rich Dad philosophy.

If your target asset class is real estate or business, build a business first. It does not need to have 500 employees. It needs to generate income that is separate from your personal labor and that can be deployed into investments.

### Step 8: Commit to Financial Education for Life

Wealth building is not a one-time event. Commit to studying financial literacy. The reason most people stay in paper assets, 401(k)s, IRAs, and chasing shiny objects like Bitcoin is because they do not want to take the risk. You have got to study.

### Step 9: Measure Progress by Assets Acquired, Not Income Earned

Stop measuring your financial success by your salary, job title, or income level. Begin measuring your financial success by the number of income-generating assets you own.

## Examples

- **Alexandra's mother using other people's money to invest in multi-family properties:** Alexandra's mother shifted to using other people's money to invest in multi-family properties after adopting the Rich Dad philosophy. This illustrates how building a business or income stream enables you to use OPM (Other People's Money) to acquire real estate assets that would otherwise be out of reach as an employee.

- **Kiyosaki's trip to Peru to learn about IPOs and gold mines:** Kiyosaki wanted to learn about IPOs (Initial Public Offerings) and asked his mentor Frank to teach him. Frank told him to be in Lima, Peru on Saturday. Kiyosaki flew to Lima that same week, paid his own way at a cost of roughly $15,000 to $20,000 in airfare, and toured three gold mines with Frank's president. Frank told him afterward that there was nothing there — the lesson was about how badly Kiyosaki wanted to learn, not about the investment itself. This demonstrates the commitment and sacrifice required to move from the E or S quadrant toward the I quadrant.

- **Liquidity comparison across asset classes:** Stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and ETFs are liquid — you can buy them, make a mistake, and sell them quickly to get out. Real estate and businesses are illiquid — if you buy a bad piece of property or get stuck in a business, you cannot easily exit and may have to ride the loss down. Among commodities, gold is more liquid than oil; silver is relatively easy to get out of; oil is tough to get out of. Understanding these differences before acquiring an asset is essential to avoid expensive, long-lasting mistakes.

## Best Practices

- **Learn the subjects you avoid.** Taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and lawsuits are the exact subjects most people find boring or difficult — and they are exactly the subjects you must learn to move from the E or S quadrant to the B or I quadrant.
- **Commit to lifelong financial education.** Wealth building is not a one-time event. The reason most people stay in paper assets, 401(k)s, IRAs, and chasing shiny objects is because they do not want to put in the study required to build real assets.
- **Invest in what you love, not just do what you love.** Replace "I want to do what I love" with "I want to invest in what you love." This mindset shift is what separates those who build lasting wealth from those who remain in the E or S quadrants.
- **Build a business before trying to acquire certain assets.** Real estate, private businesses, and physical commodities are difficult to access in meaningful size without a business income stream providing the capital and cash flow. Employees cannot use other people's money the way business owners can.
- **Match education to risk.** The higher the risk of an asset, the more education and due diligence you need before acquiring it. It takes intelligence and study to succeed in the B and I quadrants; it takes no intelligence to buy Bitcoin or chase a shiny object.
- **Treat youth as your greatest asset, not an excuse.** Being young means you have time — and time is one of your greatest assets for building wealth. Do not delay financial education because you are young; start now.
- **Measure success by assets acquired, not income earned.** Stop measuring financial success by salary, job title, or income level. Begin measuring it by the number of income-generating assets you own.
- **Choose asset classes you genuinely care about.** Write down the asset types you love — real estate, businesses, commodities, or something else — and let those be your target. Investing in what you love keeps you motivated through the hard study and work required.

## Keep In Mind

- Being young is not an excuse to delay investing in your financial education; it is your greatest advantage because time is one of your greatest assets
- The E and S quadrants are not wrong, and you can become wealthy in them, but the path is more heavily taxed and requires you to keep working to maintain income
- Most people who are rich in appearance (nice houses, nice cars) are actually broke in net worth because they have acquired liabilities disguised as assets
- Shiny objects such as Bitcoin attract people who want to get rich quickly without putting in the work.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that "doing what you love" as a career will lead to financial freedom.
  **Solution:** Replace that belief with "invest in what you love." Choose asset classes you care about and acquire them; your career can be secondary to your asset-building mission.

- **Problem:** Using youth as an excuse to delay financial education and asset acquisition.
  **Solution:** Treat youth as your greatest asset. Start studying taxes, debt, insurance, accounting, and business immediately.

- **Problem:** Chasing shiny objects like Bitcoin instead of building foundational assets.
  **Solution:** Define your chosen asset classes in writing before you make any investment. Only invest in assets that match your chosen classes. When a shiny object appears, evaluate it against your written criteria; if it does not match, do not invest.

- **Problem:** Allowing passion to override purpose, leading to years of career building with no asset accumulation.
  **Solution:** Write a purpose statement that is broader than "doing what you love." Your purpose should involve building something that generates income independently of your daily labor. Use this purpose statement as the filter for every major career and investment decision.

- **Problem:** Leaving financial education to schools or employers rather than taking personal responsibility.
  **Solution:** Take full responsibility for your own financial education by reading, studying, and finding mentors who have already achieved the results you want.

## Security & Safety Notes

- **Lawsuits are a real risk in the B and I quadrants.** In the E and S quadrants, nobody sues you. Once you move into the B and I quadrants, you open yourself up to lawsuits. Educate yourself on legal exposure and protection strategies before acquiring assets in these quadrants.
- **Using other people's money (OPM) requires caution and competence.** Alexandra's mother used OPM to invest in multi-family properties, which worked because she had the financial literacy to manage it. OPM amplifies both gains and losses. Do not use OPM before you understand the asset, the market, and your own financial statements.
- **Illiquid assets carry irreversible mistake costs.** Real estate, businesses, gold, oil, and silver are harder to exit than stocks or ETFs. If you make a mistake with an illiquid asset, you may have to ride it down. Always complete thorough due diligence and education before committing capital to illiquid assets.
- **Shiny objects like Bitcoin carry the highest risk and require the least intelligence to enter.** Kiyosaki notes that buying Bitcoin takes no intelligence, while building a business or becoming a professional investor requires deep, ongoing study. Chasing shiny objects without the education to back it up is one of the fastest ways to lose money.
- **Youth is a liability if used as an excuse to delay.** Being young can feel like a reason to postpone serious financial education and asset acquisition. The reality is that youth is your greatest asset because time compounds. Treat every day as an opportunity to build financial literacy before opportunity cost accumulates.
- **You must be able to read your own personal financial statement.** Financial literacy is not optional for anyone moving into the B or I quadrants. If you cannot read and understand your own financial statement, you cannot make independent investment decisions or protect yourself from bad advice.

## Glossary

**B Quadrant (Big Business)** — The quadrant representing owners of businesses with 500 employees or more that generate income independent of the owner's daily labor, as defined in Kiyosaki's Cash Flow Quadrant framework.

**E Quadrant (Employee)** — The quadrant representing people who work for a salary or wage as employees, as defined in Kiyosaki's Cash Flow Quadrant framework.

**I Quadrant (Professional Investor)** — The quadrant representing people who own income-generating assets such as real estate, businesses, and commodities, as defined in Kiyosaki's Cash Flow Quadrant framework.

**S Quadrant (Self-Employed/Specialist)** — The quadrant representing self-employed individuals and specialists such as doctors, lawyers, or web designers who own a job but cannot stop working, as defined in Kiyosaki's Cash Flow Quadrant framework.

**Shiny Object** — Any trendy investment that you do not fully understand and that distracts you from your chosen asset classes, causing you to stay in the E or S quadrants instead of building lasting wealth.
