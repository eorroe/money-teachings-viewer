# SMB EARNINGS TRADING

## Overview

This money teaching teaches a structured, bucket-based framework for trading earnings reports as swing trades starting on day one of the earnings reaction. SMB Capital traders use this playbook to categorize earnings reports into distinct buckets so they can identify setups, evaluate each report with prior earnings history, and recognize when prior results indicate the street will re-rate a stock. The fourth and newest bucket -- a hot theme getting rewarded -- uses early reporters within a sector as a leading indicator for later reporters in the same theme.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to trade earnings reports as day-one swing trades
- When screening and selecting which earnings reports to focus on each morning
- When trying to determine whether an earnings gap up will sustain or sell off
- When you want to understand how Wall Street will re-rate a stock based on its earnings history
- When looking for leading indicators within a sector to predict future earnings reactions

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Four Earnings Report Buckets

Categorize every earnings report you review into one of four buckets:

- First clean profitable beat: The company just turned profitable for the first time. The report proves the business model works. Creates a notable day-one price move, especially on names with elevated short interest or unexpected results. Does not indicate sustained profitability.
- Acceleration/inflection quarter: Significant acceleration in EPS growth, revenue growth, or both. This must follow a first clean profitable beat, which proves the prior acceleration was not a one-off fluke. This is the primary setup.
- Strong company continuing to crush it: You already had your inflection quarter. Earnings and revenue growth remain strong, possibly with raised guidance. The rate of change from the prior quarter is reduced because the stock has already experienced its fundamental inflection. Positive results from the prior inflection quarter are reflected in the price. These reports can gap up and sell off.
- Hot theme getting rewarded: A leading market theme is being rewarded by the street. Early reporters in that theme show continued upward movement after day one. Later reporters in the same theme receive positive reactions even when their individual numbers are not exceptional, because the street is re-rating the entire basket. This is the fourth bucket.

### Step 2: Screen for Earnings Candidates

Use three main screening methods to narrow down which reports to review before the market opens:

- Percent A vol: Look at the volume a stock does in the overnight session compared to its average daily volume. The highest percent A vol indicates the most significant reports.
- ATR gap or percent gap versus implied: Assess the magnitude of the gap relative to the implied move. Gaps that exceed the implied move signal larger-than-expected results.
- Hot themes: Maintain a list of active market themes (e.g., fiber optics, photonics, AI infrastructure) and know when stocks in those themes are scheduled to report.

### Step 3: Grade the Trade Before the Open

Before the market opens, evaluate each candidate using these factors:

- Theme: Is the stock part of a hot theme being rewarded by the street?
- Higher timeframe technicals: Is the stock breaking out into blue sky territory, or running into overhead resistance? Is the gap up actually a breakout?
- Overnight price action: Is the after-hours range holding the initial reaction? Is the premarket range holding as well?
- Volume confirmation: You need to see volume bars at least two standard deviations above average off the open to confirm institutional participation.

### Step 4: Execute the Day-One Trade

If the premarket high holds into the open and volume confirms the move, enter the trade:

- Buy above the premarket high once volume confirms.
- Add on dips, especially if the stock shows higher highs and higher lows into the open.
- Recognize the clearest momentum phase is the opening drive. Plan to manage the trade accordingly.
- For a swing trade, hold for approximately two weeks to two months if the trade thesis remains valid.

### Step 5: Use Early Theme Reporters as a Leading Indicator

For the fourth bucket specifically:

- Identify the main stocks in a leading theme and the order they report during the quarter.
- Track day-one and post-day-one reactions for each early reporter.
- If the street rewards early reporters with continued upward movement after day one, later reporters in the same theme tend to receive positive reactions even when their individual numbers are not exceptional.
- Build a table of theme leaders, their report dates, and their day-one and post-day-one reactions.

## Examples

### Example 1: Fiber Optics/Photonics Theme (Q1 Earnings Season)

SMB traders identified fiber optics and photonics as a leading AI-related theme. The main stocks tracked were LITE, COHR, AAOI, and CIEN, reporting in that order.

- LITE reported first with an acceleration/inflection quarter. Day one saw a gap up followed by selling into the open that prevented it from holding the premarket high. However, day two through day four showed strong buying and the stock moved higher until AAOI reported.
- COHR reported second with good numbers but not an acceleration quarter, and it was a bigger, more diversified company than LITE. The street compared it unfavorably to LITE and initially gapped it down. Even so, it got bought hard off the open and moved higher afterward, signaling the theme was being rewarded.
- AAOI reported third with a record revenue quarter and near profitability (first clean profitable beat imminent), 14.5% short interest, a ~60-70 million float, and a weekly/monthly chart breaking out of a base dating back to 2017. It was a momentum name within the theme. Day one saw a large gap, high volume off the open, and nearly doubled in the first two days.
- CIEN reported last and traded similarly to COHR, confirming the theme was being rewarded.

### Example 2: AAOI as a Theme Play Exception

AAOI did not cleanly fit the existing three buckets because it was not yet profitable and did not show classic acceleration in revenue growth. However, because it was in a hot theme being rewarded, had high short interest, low float, a momentum-oriented trading character, and a technical breakout from a multi-year base, traders made an exception and treated it as a fourth-bucket hot-theme play. The stock had a large day-one gain and continued moving higher after day one.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Categorize every earnings report into one of the four buckets to standardize decision-making.
- ✅ Look at earnings in the context of prior earnings history, not in isolation.
- ✅ Only consider stocks that are already profitable for the first three buckets.
- ✅ Use early reporters in a theme as a leading indicator for later reporters.
- ✅ Confirm premarket and after-hours price action holds before entering.
- ✅ Wait for volume bars at least two standard deviations above average off the open before committing.
- ✅ Maintain theme lists and earnings calendars so you know when key names report.
- ✅ Look for higher highs and higher lows into the open as confirmation of clean momentum.
- ✅ Do not short stocks in a strong, rewarded theme.

## Keep In Mind

- Not every earnings season follows the same pattern. Strong reports may run. Gap ups may be sold. The hot-theme bucket helps you identify when the street is in a risk-on mode for a specific sector.
- An unprofitable stock will show high revenue growth rates. Do not chase these unless they are approaching profitability and report within a rewarded theme.
- The catalyst is the primary factor. Theme, overnight volume, higher timeframe technicals, overnight price action, and opening volume are secondary factors.
- When you screen, start with themes and work down to individual reports rather than reading every report in isolation.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Trading earnings involves risk of loss. This teaching is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice.
- Use proper position sizing and stop losses. Earnings gaps can reverse quickly.
- Verify all earnings data and screens from reputable sources before trading.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Reading each earnings report in isolation and trying to judge it as good or bad without context.
  **Solution:** Use the four-bucket framework and always look at the previous earnings history to understand what the street is pricing in.
- **Problem:** Missing a theme play because you focus only on individual stock metrics.
  **Solution:** Track early reporters in each theme. If the street rewards them, later reporters in the same theme are likely to follow even if their numbers are not exceptional.
- **Problem:** Entering a trade without volume confirmation at the open.
  **Solution:** Wait for volume bars at least two standard deviations above average off the open to confirm institutional participation before entering.
- **Problem:** Buying a stock simply because it beat earnings without considering whether the beat was already priced in.
  **Solution:** Check whether the stock is in a "still crushing it" bucket where much of the good news is already reflected in the price, and watch for gap-up-sell-off behavior.
- **Problem:** Shorting a stock in a strong, rewarded theme.
  **Solution:** Avoid shorting names in a hot theme that the street is actively re-rating. The momentum can last longer than expected.
