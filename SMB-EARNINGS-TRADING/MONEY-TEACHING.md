# SMB EARNINGS TRADING

## Overview

This money teaching teaches a structured, bucket-based framework for trading earnings reports as swing trades starting on day one (the first trading session following an earnings report release). SMB Capital traders use this playbook to categorize earnings reports into distinct buckets so they can identify trade setups (predefined price-action and volume conditions that signal a high-probability entry), evaluate each report with prior earnings history, and recognize when prior results meet the defined threshold — average day-two-and-beyond gains of at least 5% across early reporters — that indicates the street will upgrade ratings or price targets for the entire group of stocks. The fourth and newest bucket -- a market theme where constituent stocks are receiving positive price reactions -- uses early reporters within a sector (stocks that report in the first half of an earnings season) as a leading indicator for later reporters in the same theme.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to trade earnings reports as swing trades entered on day one (the first trading session following the report) and held for two weeks to two months
- When screening and selecting which earnings reports to focus on each morning
- When trying to determine whether an earnings gap up will sustain through the first trading session and into the second day, or will sell off
- When you want to understand how Wall Street will re-rate a stock based on its earnings history
- When looking for leading indicators within a sector to predict future earnings reactions

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Four Earnings Report Buckets

Categorize every earnings report you review into one of four buckets:

- First clean profitable beat: The company just turned profitable for the first time. The report proves the business model works. Creates a day-one price move of at least 5% (the threshold rises when short interest is at least 10%, or when the stock gaps above the implied move by at least 10%), indicating the market was not expecting results that would exceed the implied move. Does not indicate sustained profitability.
- Acceleration/inflection quarter: Acceleration in earnings per share (EPS) growth, or revenue growth, or both, following a first clean profitable beat, which proves the acceleration in that prior profitable beat quarter was not a one-off fluke. This is the preferred setup.
- Strong company continuing to crush it: You already had your inflection quarter. Earnings and revenue growth continue to meet or exceed consensus estimates, possibly with raised guidance. The rate of change from the prior quarter is lower than the acceleration quarter's rate of change, though still positive because the stock has already experienced its fundamental inflection. Prior positive results from the prior inflection quarter are largely incorporated into the current share price, which is why these reports can gap up and sell off.
- Hot theme getting rewarded: A leading market theme is being rewarded by the street. Early reporters in that theme (stocks that report before the majority of their peer group in a given earnings season) hold above their day-one closing prices. Later reporters in the same theme receive positive reactions even when their individual numbers do not meet the acceleration/inflection-quarter threshold, because the street is upgrading their ratings or price targets for the entire group of stocks. This is the fourth bucket.

### Step 2: Screen for Earnings Candidates

Use three main screening methods to narrow down which reports to review before the market opens:

- Percent Above Average Volume (Percent A vol): Look at the volume a stock does in the overnight session compared to its average daily volume. Percent Above Average Volume (Percent A vol) above 50% signals reports with overnight volume exceeding 50% of average daily volume.
- Average True Range (ATR) gap or percent gap versus implied move: Assess the magnitude of the gap relative to the expected price move derived from options pricing (implied move). Gaps that exceed the implied move by at least 10% signal results that exceeded the implied move by at least 10%.
- Hot themes: Maintain a list of market themes where constituent stocks are receiving positive price reactions (e.g., fiber optics, photonics, AI infrastructure) and know when stocks in those themes are scheduled to report.

### Step 3: Grade the Trade Before the Open

Before the market opens, evaluate each candidate using these factors:

- Theme: Is the stock part of a hot theme where constituent stocks are receiving positive price reactions?
  - Higher timeframe technicals: Is the stock breaking out to new 3-month or 6-month highs, or running into overhead resistance (a prior price high where historical sell orders have previously capped the stock's advance)? Does the gap up exceed the prior multi-month high on volume, confirming a technical breakout?
- Overnight price action: Is the after-hours range holding the initial reaction? Is the premarket range holding as well?
- Volume confirmation: You need to see volume bars at least 2 standard deviations above the 20-day average daily volume off the open to indicate strong trading interest.

### Step 4: Execute the Day-One Trade

If the premarket high holds into the open and volume confirms the move, enter the trade:

- Buy above the premarket high once volume reaches at least 2 standard deviations above the 20-day average daily volume.
- Add on dips of 2% to 3% below the entry price during the opening-drive phase, especially if the stock shows higher highs and higher lows into the open.
- Recognize the highest-momentum phase is the initial opening price move (the first 5 to 15 minutes of the trading session). Have an exit plan ready to manage the trade during this phase.
- For a swing trade, hold for two weeks to two months if the trade thesis remains valid (the stock holds above its day-one closing price and the theme continues to show positive reactions) and the stock continues to hold above its day-one closing price.

### Step 5: Use Early Theme Reporters as a Leading Indicator

For the fourth bucket specifically:

- Identify the highest-market-cap and highest-volume stocks in a leading theme and the order they report during the quarter.
- Record day-one price reaction and price reaction through at least the fifth trading session for each early reporter.
- If the street rewards early reporters with average gains of at least 5% from day-one close through day five, later reporters in the same theme often receive positive reactions even when their individual numbers do not meet the acceleration/inflection-quarter threshold, because the street is upgrading their ratings or price targets for all stocks in that market theme.
- Build a table of theme leaders, their report dates, and their day-one and post-day-one reactions.

## Examples

### Example 1: Fiber Optics/Photonics Theme (Q1 Earnings Season)

SMB traders identified fiber optics and photonics as a leading AI-related theme. The main stocks tracked were LITE, COHR, AAOI, and CIEN, reporting in that order during the quarter.

- LITE reported first with an acceleration/inflection quarter. Day one saw a gap up followed by selling into the open that prevented it from holding the premarket high. However, day two through day four showed purchasing aggressively on high volume and the stock moved higher until AAOI reported.
- COHR reported second with earnings and revenue meeting or exceeding consensus estimates but not an acceleration quarter, and it was a larger, more diversified company than LITE. The street compared it unfavorably to LITE on the basis that COHR's earnings and revenue showed no acceleration quarter and initially gapped it down. Even so, it got purchased aggressively on high volume off the open and moved higher afterward, signaling the theme was receiving positive market reactions.
- AAOI reported third with a record revenue quarter and near profitability (projected to turn profitable within the next quarter), 14.5% short interest, a float of approximately 60-70 million shares, and a weekly/monthly chart breaking above the multi-year resistance level established in 2017. It was a stock with a history of single-day price moves of at least 10% following earnings reports within the theme. Day one saw a gap of approximately 50% above the prior close, high volume off the open, and gained nearly 100% in the first two days.
- CIEN reported last and traded similarly to COHR, confirming the theme was receiving positive market reactions.

### Example 2: AAOI as a Theme Play Exception

AAOI did not cleanly fit the existing three buckets because it was not yet profitable and did not show classic acceleration in revenue growth. However, because it was in a hot theme receiving positive market reactions, had 14.5% short interest, a float of approximately 60-70 million shares, a history of single-day price moves of at least 10% following earnings reports, and a technical breakout above the multi-year resistance level established in 2017, traders made an exception and treated it as a fourth-bucket hot-theme play. The stock had a large day-one gain and continued moving higher after day one.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Categorize every earnings report into one of the four buckets to standardize decision-making.
- ✅ Look at earnings in the context of prior earnings history, not in isolation.
- ✅ For the first three buckets, only consider stocks that are already profitable; for the fourth bucket, an unprofitable stock may qualify if it is projected to be profitable within one quarter and reports within a theme receiving positive price reactions.
- ✅ Use early reporters in a theme as a leading indicator for later reporters.
- ✅ Confirm premarket and after-hours price action holds before entering.
- ✅ Wait for volume bars at least 2 standard deviations above average off the open before committing.
- ✅ Maintain theme lists and earnings calendars so you know when key names report.
- ✅ Look for higher highs and higher lows into the open as confirmation of clean momentum.
- ✅ Do not short stocks in a hot theme receiving positive price reactions.

## Keep In Mind

- Not every earnings season follows the same pattern. Strong reports in a hot-theme environment tend to experience continued upward price movement for two to five trading sessions. Gap ups may be sold. The hot-theme bucket helps you identify when institutional traders are willing to take on risk for a specific sector.
- An early-stage unprofitable company that is on track to profitability often shows high revenue growth rates before turning profitable. Do not chase these unless they are projected to be profitable within one quarter and report within a theme receiving positive price reactions.
- The catalyst is the primary factor. Theme, overnight volume, higher timeframe technicals, overnight price action, and opening volume are supporting factors that confirm or invalidate the catalyst signal.
- When you screen, start with themes and work down to individual reports; read reports in theme context rather than evaluating each report's numbers in isolation.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Trading earnings involves risk of loss. This teaching is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice.
- Use position sizing that limits loss to 1% to 2% of trading capital per trade, and place stop losses at a price level that invalidates the trade thesis. Earnings gaps can reverse quickly. Risk no more than 1% to 2% of capital per trade.
- Verify all earnings data and screens from established financial data providers before trading.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Reading each earnings report in isolation and trying to judge it as good or bad without context.
  **Solution:** Use the four-bucket framework and always look at the previous earnings history to understand the earnings trajectory and what the market is likely to be pricing in.
- **Problem:** Missing a theme play because you focus only on individual stock metrics.
  **Solution:** Track early reporters in each theme. If the street rewards them, later reporters in the same theme are likely to follow even if their numbers are not exceptional.
- **Problem:** Entering a trade without volume confirmation at the open.
  **Solution:** Wait for volume bars at least 2 standard deviations above the 20-day average daily volume off the open as a quantitative confirmation threshold before entering.
- **Problem:** Buying a stock simply because a stock's EPS or revenue beat consensus estimates without considering whether the beat was already priced in.
  **Solution:** Check whether the stock is in the 'Strong company continuing to crush it' bucket where much of the good news is already incorporated into the current share price, and watch for a day-one gap up followed by failure to hold the premarket high and a volume-confirmed pullback into the close.
- **Problem:** Shorting a stock in a hot theme receiving positive price reactions.
  **Solution:** Avoid shorting names in a hot theme that the street is actively upgrading their ratings or price targets for. The momentum can last longer than expected.
