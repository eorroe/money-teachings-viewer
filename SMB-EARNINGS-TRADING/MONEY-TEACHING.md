# SMB EARNINGS TRADING

## Overview

This money teaching teaches a structured, bucket-based framework for trading earnings reports as swing trades starting on day one of the earnings reaction. SMB Capital traders use this playbook to categorize earnings reports into distinct buckets so they can consistently identify high-probability setups, avoid analyzing reports in a vacuum, and gauge when Wall Street will re-rate a stock. The fourth and newest bucket -- a hot theme getting rewarded -- uses early reporters within a sector as a leading indicator for later reporters in the same theme.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to trade earnings reports as day-one swing trades
- When screening and selecting which earnings reports to focus on each morning
- When trying to determine whether an earnings gap up will sustain or sell off
- When you want to understand how Wall Street will re-rate a stock based on its earnings history
- When looking for leading indicators within a sector to predict future earnings reactions

## Steps

### Step 1: Understand the Four Earnings Report Buckets

Categorize every earnings report you review into one of four buckets so you can process them consistently:

- First clean profitable beat: The company just turned profitable for the first time. The report proves the business model works. Creates a strong day-one reaction, especially on high-short-interest names or complete surprises. Not yet a sustained profitability story.
- Acceleration/inflection quarter: Huge acceleration in EPS growth, revenue growth, or both. This must follow a first clean profitable beat, which proves the prior acceleration was not a one-off fluke. This is the bread-and-butter play.
- Strong company continuing to crush it: You already had your inflection quarter. Numbers will be huge, maybe with raised guidance, but the rate of change is smaller because the stock has already inflected. The cat is out of the bag and much of the good news is already priced in. These often gap up and sell off.
- Hot theme getting rewarded: A leading theme in the market is being rewarded by the street. Early reporters in that theme show strong follow-through after day one. Later reporters in the same theme, even with only decent numbers, get rewarded because the street is re-rating the entire basket. This is the newest and fourth bucket.

### Step 2: Screen for Earnings Candidates

Use three main screening methods to narrow down which reports to review before the market opens:

- Percent A vol: Look at the volume a stock does in the overnight session compared to its average daily volume. The biggest overnight volume %A vol indicates the most significant reports.
- ATR gap or percent gap versus implied: Assess the magnitude of the gap relative to the implied move. Larger gaps signal bigger surprises.
- Hot themes: Maintain a list of active market themes (e.g., fiber optics, photonics, AI infrastructure) and know when the key stocks in those themes are scheduled to report.

### Step 3: Grade the Trade Before the Open

Before the market opens, evaluate each candidate using these factors:

- Theme: Is the stock part of a hot theme being rewarded by the street?
- Higher timeframe technicals: Is the stock breaking out into blue sky territory, or running into overhead resistance? Is the gap up actually a breakout?
- Overnight price action: Is the after-hours range holding the initial reaction? Is the premarket range holding as well?
- Volume confirmation: You need to see massive volume bars off the open -- ideally two or more standard deviations above average -- to confirm the market agrees with your read.

### Step 4: Execute the Day-One Trade

If the premarket and opening price action align with your thesis, enter the trade:

- Buy above the premarket high once volume confirms.
- Add on dips, especially if the stock shows higher highs and higher lows into the open.
- Recognize the cleanest momentum phase is often the opening drive. Plan to manage the trade accordingly.
- For a swing trade, hold for a couple weeks to a couple of months if the thesis holds.

### Step 5: Use Early Theme Reporters as a Leading Indicator

For the fourth bucket specifically:

- Identify the main stocks in a leading theme and the order they report during the quarter.
- Track day-one and post-day-one reactions for each early reporter.
- If the street rewards early reporters with strong follow-through, later reporters in the same theme are more likely to get rewarded even if their numbers alone are not exceptional.
- Build a simple daily table of theme leaders, their report dates, and their day-one/post-day-one reactions.

## Examples

### Example 1: Fiber Optics/Photonics Theme (Q1 Earnings Season)

SMB traders identified fiber optics and photonics as a leading AI-related theme. The main stocks tracked were LITE, COHR, AAOI, and CIEN, reporting in that order.

- LITE reported first with an acceleration/inflection quarter. Day one was messy -- it gapped up, sold into the open, and couldn't hold the premarket high. However, day two through day four showed strong buying and the stock trended up until AAOI reported.
- COHR reported second with good numbers but not an acceleration quarter, and it was a bigger, more diversified company than LITE. The street compared it unfavorably to LITE and initially gapped it down. Even so, it got bought hard off the open and trended higher afterward, signaling the theme was being rewarded.
- AAOI reported third with a record revenue quarter and near profitability (first clean profitable beat imminent), 14.5% short interest, a ~60-70 million float, and a weekly/monthly chart breaking out of a massive base dating back to 2017. It was a pure-play momentum name within the theme. Day one saw a huge gap, massive volume off the open, and a near-double in the first two days.
- CIEN reported last and traded similarly to COHR, confirming the theme was being rewarded.

### Example 2: AAOI as a Theme Play Exception

AAOI did not cleanly fit the existing three buckets because it was not yet profitable and did not show classic acceleration in revenue growth. However, because it was in a hot theme being rewarded, had high short interest, low float, a momentum-oriented trading character, and a massive technical breakout, traders made an exception and treated it as a fourth-bucket hot-theme play. This produced an explosive day-one reaction and strong follow-through.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Categorize every earnings report into one of the four buckets to remove emotion and guesswork.
- ✅ Look at earnings in the context of the story the previous reports have been telling, not in a vacuum.
- ✅ Only consider stocks that are already profitable for the first three buckets.
- ✅ Use early reporters in a theme as a leading indicator for later reporters.
- ✅ Confirm premarket and after-hours price action holds before entering.
- ✅ Wait for massive volume bars off the open (2+ standard deviations) before committing.
- ✅ Maintain theme lists and earnings calendars so you know when key names report.
- ✅ Look for higher highs and higher lows into the open as confirmation of clean momentum.
- ✅ Do not short stocks in a strong, rewarded theme.

## Keep In Mind

- Not every earnings season has the same vibe. Patterns change season to season. Sometimes strong reports run; sometimes gap ups get sold. The hot-theme bucket helps you identify when the street is in a risk-on mode for a specific sector.
- A stock that is unprofitable will show massive growth numbers. Do not chase these unless they are on the verge of profitability and fit the theme.
- The catalyst is the heaviest-weighted factor, but theme, overnight volume, higher timeframe technicals, overnight price action, and opening volume all matter.
- When you screen, start with themes and work down to individual reports rather than reading every report in isolation.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Trading earnings involves significant risk of loss. This teaching is for educational purposes only and is not financial advice.
- Use proper position sizing and stop losses. Earnings gaps can reverse quickly.
- Verify all earnings data and screens from reputable sources before trading.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Reading each earnings report in isolation and trying to decide "is it good or not" without context.
  **Solution:** Use the four-bucket framework and always look at the previous earnings history to understand the story the street is pricing in.
- **Problem:** Missing a theme play because you focus only on individual stock metrics.
  **Solution:** Track early reporters in each theme. If the street rewards them, later reporters in the same theme are likely to follow even if their numbers are not exceptional.
- **Problem:** Entering a trade without volume confirmation at the open.
  **Solution:** Wait for massive volume bars off the open (2+ standard deviations) to confirm the market agrees with your read before entering.
- **Problem:** Buying a stock simply because it beat earnings without considering whether the beat was already priced in.
  **Solution:** Check whether the stock is in a "still crushing it" bucket where much of the good news is already reflected in the price, and watch for gap-up-sell-off behavior.
- **Problem:** Shorting a stock in a strong, rewarded theme.
  **Solution:** Avoid shorting names in a hot theme that the street is actively re-rating. The momentum can last longer than expected.
