# Your Mind Is Programmed To Stay Poor (Here's How To Rewrite It)

## Overview

Myron Golden teaches that entrepreneurs and individuals stuck in a scarcity mindset do not lack abundance - they lack awareness of the abundance they already have access to. This video explains how to identify and reprogram subconscious limiting beliefs about financial limitation, persuasion, and prosperity, and replace them with empowering principles drawn from biblical foundations, personal experience, and business strategy. The core message is that wealth is a spiritual and financial outcome, selling is the most important skill for any entrepreneur, and you can decide to believe that everything happens for you rather than against you.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you experience persistent thoughts that there is never enough money or that money is inherently evil
- When you are an entrepreneur or business owner who hesitates to ask clients for payment
- When you want to replace self-limiting beliefs ("I am not enough") with self-affirming beliefs ("I am capable")
- When you are creating wealth but have not learned financial management, preservation, or protection strategies
- When you want to teach your children financial literacy and wealth-building habits
- When you are working hard but not increasing income or savings despite consistent effort
- When you need to reframe past business setbacks, losses, or challenges as opportunities for growth

## Steps

### Step 1: Recognize That People Lack Awareness, Not Abundance

Understand that there are sufficient resources for every person to have more than enough, with resources remaining after every person has enough. Most individuals who have not studied abundance principles are simply unaware of this reality, similar to walking through life thinking there is a limited amount of oxygen and restricting your own intake of oxygen so other people have enough. Choose to operate from abundance rather than scarcity. Awareness of abundance principles is the primary gap for most individuals, and all transformation begins with awareness of abundance principles.

### Step 2: Decide to Believe That Everything Happens FOR You

You have a choice in how you interpret every event or outcome in your life. When you experience adversity, loss, or unexpected setbacks, you can respond like Jacob (focusing on the pain) or like Joseph (seeing it as happening for you rather than against you). Choose now to believe that every event that happens to you happens for you. Even if this does not feel true to you in this moment, you can make this choice. Every time you emphasize your "I am nots" ("I am not smart enough," "I am not rich enough"), you misrepresent God's nature because "I am" is a present-moment statement infused with the power of eternity. Change every "I am not" statement to an "I am" statement.

### Step 3: Adopt the Be, Do, Have Framework (Not Have, Do, Be)

Most individuals who have not studied identity principles operate in reverse: they believe "if I acquire resources, then I can take action, then I will achieve a result." You must flip this order. First, define your identity (who you are BEing). Then, take action (DO). As a result, you will have the results you want. Every result you have achieved is the product of what you have done. Every result you will achieve is the product of what you will do. Every action you will take is the product of who you are choosing to become today.

### Step 4: Develop the Skill of Selling (Persuasion, Not Convincing)

Selling is the most important skill any entrepreneur or person who desires to succeed can develop. You were born a natural-born salesperson (babies persuade parents to meet their needs), but social conditioning caused you to doubt your natural ability to persuade others. Understand the critical difference:

- Selling = persuasion = guiding someone toward a choice they already want to make that benefits them
- Convincing = attempting to get someone to do what you want for your reasons (this is called "commission breath")

People love to buy AND they love to be persuaded. What they hate is being convinced.

### Step 5: Master Seamless Selling Using the Law of Averages

When you feel desperate for a specific sale, you push away potential clients. Instead, use the law of averages to emotionally detach from the outcome of any single interaction. Know your average conversion rate (for example, 1 out of 10 potential clients will buy). If you must speak to 10 potential clients today, you do not care which one buys. This frees you from needing THIS specific sale and makes you more attractive to prospects because you become the professional who operates from confidence rather than desperation. Seamless selling means you guide someone toward a purchase and leave them believing the decision was entirely their own.

### Step 6: Uncover Value From Three Sources

Before presenting any offer, understand that value comes from three sources:

1. **Past perceived voids** - Specific problems people experienced in the past (example: Myron Golden's desire for a 1968 Pontiac GTO from his youth)
2. **Present perceived virtues** - Specific benefits people currently want
3. **Future perceived visions** - Showing people how your offer delivers the specific future outcome they want

Most people in sales talk about what is valuable to them instead of what is valuable to the prospect. Have a two-way conversation, not a one-way pitch. Ask open-ended questions to uncover what the prospect values.

### Step 7: Position Your Offer Correctly

Position your offer next to a specific outcome the prospect has already purchased that delivered less value. Also position the price of your offer next to the cost of not getting the result they have been seeking, and show them which one is more expensive. An adequate presentation with strong positioning outperforms a polished presentation with weak positioning. Focus on the outcome, not the process. Do not show them the steps or the hours of work involved - show them how they will feel, what their life will look like, and what results they will experience when they have the result.

### Step 8: Create Self-Replenishing Assets (SRAs)

Transition from trading time for money to creating assets that generate income repeatedly. Wealthy creative entrepreneurs pay for purchases using the value they create, not with time exchanged for money. When you create an offer (a book, a course, a system), you still have the creative capacity and the asset after you get paid. Turn every expense into a profit center by creating an offer to pay for it. For example, if you buy a house, create a YouTube channel or podcast that will generate income to pay for the house. An SRA is an asset that replenishes itself - you sell it once but it continues generating money indefinitely.

### Step 9: Fill Your Capacity Gap With Intentionality

Every entrepreneur has a capacity gap between who they are and who they could become, what they are doing and what they could be doing, and what they have and what they could have. Fill this gap with deliberate, focused intention. Define intention as any action, thought, or decision that directly increases income or progress. Define distraction as any distraction, excuse, or unproductive habit that does not increase income or progress. Ignore distractions as aggressively as you would avoid a contagious disease. Fulfillment comes from three sources: creation, connection, and contribution.

### Step 10: Apply Price's Law to Break Through Income Ceilings

Price's Law states that 50% of the production of any domain is produced by the square root of that domain. This means mediocrity clusters among most individuals who have not committed to excellence, but excellence is achieved only by a small committed minority who are willing to pay the price. To break through income ceilings, stop serving more people - instead, serve fewer people with greater value and charge them more. Promise significant results and deliver even greater results.

### Step 11: Manage Wealth by Hiring Where You Are Weak

When you accumulate wealth, you cannot be free if you are worried about what other people think about you. Cultivate "nothing to gain, nothing to lose, nothing to hide, nothing to prove." Hire experts in areas where you lack expertise: get a CPA, a tax strategist, and an attorney. Compensate for your weaknesses with automation and delegation. Remember that knowledge is more durable than gold - you can lose assets, but you cannot lose your knowledge, skill set, or mindset. You can always rebuild.

### Step 12: Raise Children With an Abundance Mindset

Use the four levels of parenting to teach children about abundance:

- **Training (ages 0-4):** Teach complete and immediate obedience to authority for safety
- **Teaching (ages 5-8):** Explain the reasons behind rules so they learn to reason properly and keep rules for the right reasons when you are not around
- **Transitioning (ages 9-12):** Give them life skills by working alongside them, supervising their work, and letting them work in your business. Pay them for work they complete
- **Trusting (age 13+):** Trust them as adults. Let them make decisions and learn from consequences

Make "yes" your default answer when you can. Give them opportunities to earn money, not just hand them money. Teach them that they are privileged to live with wealthy parents, not that their own wealth is what matters.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Trash Man Who Became a Millionaire

Myron Golden worked as a trash man driving a trash truck for $6.25 per hour while married with a new baby. He used his breaks to read personal development books like "The Greatest Salesman in the World." While working as a trash man, he also sold insurance and investments part-time, learning financial principles like compound interest and pay yourself first. One week in April 1999, he accidentally made $6,200 from a combination of an honorarium, network marketing income, and tape sales. That single week unlocked his belief that generating substantial income in a compressed timeframe is easier than generating small amounts of money over an extended period. He immediately decided to focus only on efficient methods of generating substantial income. By July of that same year, he made $8,000 in one day in the stock market.

### Example 2: Joseph Versus Jacob: Two Responses to Suffering

When Joseph's brothers threw him in a pit, sold him into slavery, and lied to their father that he was dead, Joseph spent over a decade in Egypt as a slave and then in prison. Eventually, he became prime minister of Egypt and saved his family from famine. When his father Jacob said, "Few and evil have been the years of the days of my life," Joseph had named his sons Ephraim ("God has made me to forget all my trials") and Manasseh ("God has made me fruitful in the land of my affliction"). The same painful experience produced two completely different interpretations. You always have a choice: will you respond like Jacob (focusing on pain) or Joseph (seeing it as happening for you)?

### Example 3: Turning Every Expense Into a Profit Center

Myron bought a house for $2.4 million. Rather than seeing it as an expense, he plans to create a golf YouTube channel that will pay for the house. When he moved out of his old house, he did not sell it - he put a podcast studio in it and started a podcast with his daughter, so the house pays for itself. This is the Self-Replenishing Asset principle: you create an offer, the offer pays for the expense, and you still retain the asset and your creativity after the purchase.

### Example 4: The Wealthy Person's Shopping Strategy

Poor and middle-class individuals feel that all purchases are expensive because they buy items with money earned by trading their time. When they buy a $100,000 car, they feel they are exchanging one year of their life for that car. Wealthy creative entrepreneurs pay for purchases using the value they create. Myron Golden wrote a book 20 years ago that generated $81,000 in the most recently reported fiscal year, and another book from 2021 that generated $186,000. He paid for expenses by creating offers, so his creative output and asset base never depleted.

- When you accumulate wealth, hire a CPA, a tax strategist, and an attorney to establish proper financial structures
- Protect your assets through proper legal structures and professional advisors
- Do not engage in unethical or dishonest business practices - if you would not say something to a person's face, do not say it behind their back
- If someone works for you and is dishonest, terminate their employment but maintain the ability to help them if they need it
- Keep your word - if you commit to something, do it even if it costs you time, effort, energy, or financial resources. Or do not commit to it at all
- Teach your children financial literacy and business skills early so they are not financially dependent throughout adulthood

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing that money is evil or that accumulating wealth separates you from spiritual practice
  **Solution:** Study biblical principles using the law of first mention, context, and definitions. Wealthy biblical figures like Abraham, David, and Solomon were businessmen. God put gold in the Garden of Eden and called it good. Poverty is not piety.

- **Problem:** Selling with desperation and "commission breath" (the smell of needing a specific sale) - trying to convince instead of persuade
  **Solution:** Focus on the law of averages. You must make X sales, but this particular potential client does not have to be one of them. When you emotionally detach from any single interaction, the potential client engages more. Sell by guiding them toward a decision they already want to make.

- **Problem:** Creating what you personally value instead of what the marketplace actually values
  **Solution:** Always start with the marketplace's needs, not your personal creative preferences. Write the book the marketplace wants to read, not the book you have always wanted to write. Uncover past perceived voids, present perceived virtues, and future perceived visions before you present your offer.

- **Problem:** Chasing sales and feeling like you need THIS specific payment to survive financially
  **Solution:** Remember that if you chase, people run. If you detach, they engage. Use the law of averages to free yourself from the pressure of any single transaction. Focus on serving people rather than extracting money from them.

- **Problem:** Letting past pain define your identity (responding like Jacob instead of Joseph)
  **Solution:** Make a conscious decision to believe that every event happens for you. Reframe painful experiences as preparation for your purpose. Choose to see challenges as things that happen for you, not against you.

- **Problem:** Operating from scarcity and hoarding money instead of turning expenses into profit centers
  **Solution:** Adopt the creative entrepreneur approach. For every expense, ask "How can I create an offer to pay for this?" Turn your house into a podcast studio, your car into content, your knowledge into products. Pay for everything according to your creativity, not with time you can never get back.

- **Problem:** Reaching an income ceiling because you believe you have already achieved success
  **Solution:** Understand that there is no final destination - the journey is the destination. When you think you have arrived, you stop growing. Use Price's Law to understand that if you keep doing what got you to six figures annually, you will remain at six figures annually. To break through, you must serve fewer people with greater value and charge them more.

- **Problem:** Allowing your children to remain dependent on your wealth instead of teaching them to create it
  **Solution:** Follow the four levels of parenting: Train (0-4), Teach (5-8), Transition (9-12), and Trust (13+). Pay children for work in your business, give them opportunities to earn, and help them understand they are privileged to live with wealthy parents - not that their own wealth is what matters. Do not give children money; teach them to create it.
