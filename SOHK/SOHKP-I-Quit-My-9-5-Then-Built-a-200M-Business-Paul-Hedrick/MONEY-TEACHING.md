# I Quit My 9-5… Then Built a $200M Business ｜ Paul Hedrick

## Overview

This money teaching distills actionable principles from Paul Hedrick's journey from Harvard graduate and management consultant to founder of Toot, a $200M direct-to-consumer boot brand. The content covers rejecting the obvious path, learning from failure, maintaining brand obsession, and building a CEO playbook that scales.

**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=felq2wfkEOM

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When leaving a stable job to start a consumer brand
- When building a direct-to-consumer business
- When deciding whether to bootstrap or raise outside capital
- When scaling a retail business with physical locations
- When the user asks about entrepreneurship, consumer brands, or bootstrapping

## Steps

### Step 1: Reject the Obvious Path and Find Your Own

Paul applied to Harvard and Stanford business schools and did not even get interviews. That rejection forced him to ask what made him different. He realized he had been following the expected path instead of forging his own. Sometimes not getting what you want is the wake-up call you need.

### Step 2: Learn More From Failure Than Success

Paul was rejected from his first job three times. He signed 10 retail leases in February 2020 and watched the world shut down one month later. That failure taught him to test ideas on a small scale before betting big. He now views every setback as data that makes the next decision stronger.

### Step 3: Start With Product and Build the Brand Around It

Paul's first $100,000 in sales came before he hired anyone. He obsessed over the product because he believed in it personally. A great product is the first box you have to check. If you would not put your reputation behind it, start over.

### Step 4: Hire People Better Than You and Make Yourself Redundant

Paul initially resisted the advice to hire people better than him at specific tasks. He eventually realized that life is better when great people are around the table. Your goal should be to make yourself redundant in every function except the one only you can do.

### Step 5: Build a CEO Playbook With Strategy, Process, and Team

A CEO needs three things: a clear strategy broken into one-to-five-year plans, a process to execute it, and a great team to run it. Paul learned that the CEO playbook is simple but not easy. A five-person company needs it just as much as a 500-person company does.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Rejection That Changed Everything

Paul applied to Harvard and Stanford for business school and got rejected from both. That forced him to sit in a bar with a coworker and ask what he actually wanted. The conversation led to Toot.

### Example 2: The Pandemic Pivot

When COVID hit and all retail had to close, Paul did not cancel leases or stop marketing. He delayed openings, cut advertising by 60%, and introduced new products while competitors liquidated stale inventory. Toot grew $10 million more in profit in 2020 than in 2019.

### Example 3: The First $100,000 in Sales

Paul made his first $100,000 in sales before hiring anyone. He sold boots out of his car, at farmers markets, and through early digital advertising. He proved demand before building infrastructure.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Question the expected path and design your own
- ✅ View failure as feedback and learning data
- ✅ Obsess over product quality before chasing scale
- ✅ Hire people better than you and delegate aggressively
- ✅ Create a simple CEO playbook: strategy, process, and team
- ❌ Don't let ego prevent you from pivoting when the market shifts
- ❌ Don't raise capital you do not need just because it is available
- ❌ Don't skip the hard skills in favor of only focusing on vision

## Keep In Mind

- Direct-to-consumer is a channel, not a business model. Brands win through connection, not distribution alone
- A great product gets you in the game; a great brand keeps you there
- The fundamentals of business do not change: Profit and Loss (P&L) is P&L
- Markets go down and up; what never changes is a fundamentally healthy business

## Security & Safety Notes

- Raising outside capital dilutes ownership and adds pressure to perform
- Always consult with a securities attorney before signing term sheets
- Keep cash reserves for risky experiments and downturns
- Never sign personal guarantees for business leases without legal review

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Following the expected path without questioning it
  **Solution:** Regularly ask what makes you different and where you can compete uniquely

- **Problem:** Betting big on unproven ideas
  **Solution:** Test on a small scale first. Spend $50,000 to validate before spending $500,000.

- **Problem:** Trying to do everything yourself
  **Solution:** Hire specialists and make yourself redundant in every area except vision

- **Problem:** Ignoring the fundamentals of Profit and Loss (P&L) and unit economics
  **Solution:** Understand your numbers cold before you talk to any investor
