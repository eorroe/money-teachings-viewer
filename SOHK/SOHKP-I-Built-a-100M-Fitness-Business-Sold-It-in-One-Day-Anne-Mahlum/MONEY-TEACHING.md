# I Built a $100M Fitness Business... Sold It in One Day ｜ Anne Mahlum

## Overview

This money teaching distills actionable principles from Anne Mahlum's journey building Solid Core from a $175,000 life savings investment into a 100-plus-location fitness brand sold for nine figures. The content covers committing fully without a plan B, building community as a moat, and scaling through focused market density.

**Video:** https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=kXvWXv7ePCc

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When launching a service-based business with limited capital
- When building a community-driven brand
- When deciding whether to take outside investment or bootstrap
- When scaling brick-and-mortar locations
- When the user asks about entrepreneurship, fitness businesses, or bootstrapping

## Steps

### Step 1: Commit Fully With No Plan B

Anne went all in on Solid Core with her entire life savings. She turned down a $75,000 offer for 30% of the business before opening a single studio because taking it would signal self-doubt. When you have no fallback, you are forced to figure it out.

### Step 2: Build Community Before You Build a Brand

The magic ingredient was creating an environment where people felt seen, respected, and celebrated. Anne required every coach to learn every client's name. That emotional connection turned a workout into a community and gave the brand its moat.

### Step 3: Bootstrap Instead of Relying on Banks

Anne noted that keeping money in a savings account means the bank earns 10% on your money while paying you half a percent. She viewed that as the biggest scam in the world. Reinvest every dollar into growth, marketing, and the customer experience instead of parking cash.

### Step 4: Scale Through Market Density

Solid Core refused to open a new market unless it could open five studios within a 15 to 20-mile radius. Brand equity compounds when people see multiple locations. A saturated local market reduces marketing costs and increases lifetime customer value.

### Step 5: Use Momentum to Build Unstoppable Flywheels

Once the flywheel started, Anne opened 32 locations in one year. Work backward from the goal: determine how many leases, Letters of Intent (LOIs), and permits you need, then frontload the critical path. Move faster than competitors by starting drawings and permit processes before leases are signed.

## Examples

### Example 1: The $175,000 Start

Anne used her entire savings to launch Solid Core. She negotiated 60-day payment terms with contractors to stretch the capital and personally passed out flyers at triathlons and marathons.

### Example 2: The Rejected Offer

A board member offered $75,000 for 30% of the business before the first studio opened. Anne declined because accepting would have signaled that she doubted her own vision.

### Example 3: The Market Density Rule

In New York, Solid Core now has roughly 20 studios in Manhattan. The 21st studio opened with a wait list and generated $2 million to $3 million from day one because of existing brand equity.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Go all in without a plan B when you believe in the vision
- ✅ Build community and emotional connection before chasing scale
- ✅ Reinvest every dollar rather than parking it in low-yield accounts
- ✅ Open multiple locations in a tight radius before expanding to new cities
- ✅ Work backward from goals and frontload critical-path items
- ❌ Don't take early outside capital if it signals doubt in your own ability
- ❌ Don't spread across too many markets before dominating one
- ❌ Don't sacrifice culture for speed

## Keep In Mind

- Success often feels earned because you drove the bus every day
- Momentum is easier to maintain than to restart
- Community is a competitive advantage that is difficult to replicate
- First-mover advantage matters less than execution advantage

## Security & Safety Notes

- Bootstrapping with life savings carries personal financial risk
- Always keep an emergency reserve if possible, even when going all in
- Consult with a financial advisor before investing personal savings into a business
- Never invest money you cannot afford to lose

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking outside capital too early and diluting ownership
  **Solution:** Bootstrap as long as possible and prove unit economics first

- **Problem:** Expanding to new cities before dominating local markets
  **Solution:** Open five locations in a 15-mile radius before entering a new city

- **Problem:** Underestimating the power of community and culture
  **Solution:** Make every client feel seen and celebrated from day one

- **Problem:** Running out of cash because of slow lease and permit timelines
  **Solution:** Start drawings and permitting immediately after LOI, not after lease signing
