# Money Teaching Title

Build A $10K/Month Service Business From Zero

## Overview

This money teaching outlines the exact eight-step framework Alex Hormozi uses to build a cash-flowing service business starting from zero. It is based on a live coaching session with Morgan, a student who wants to become a graphic designer but is considering spending $260,000 on university. The framework replaces the traditional four-year degree path with a practical, client-first approach that can generate income starting with the first client.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When starting a service-based business with no upfront capital
- When trying to land your first paying client without an existing network
- When choosing a niche and positioning yourself as an expert
 - When deciding when to leave your job and operate full-time
- When you want to attach purpose to your business beyond just making money

## Steps

### Step 1: Get Your First Client

You need a client before anything else, and the only way to get one is to ask. It is not about who you know, it is about who you ask. Walk into businesses or contact companies with outdated or poorly executed branding—such as low-quality logos or inconsistent visual identity—and offer to fix it. Say, "Your brand could do some work. I can fix it for you and it will equal more business if I do." Business owners often say yes because they recognize their brand needs improvement but have not had time to find help. If they say no, you lose only the time spent asking, which is a small time investment compared to the potential upside of landing a client. Asking is the single most important action because it requires minimal effort but can lead to a paying client.

### Step 2: Ask Your Network

After direct outreach, ask your parents, friends, and their connections. Your network includes people who own companies or know business owners who need your service. One person is all it takes. Social media also acts as an introvert-friendly way to document your journey. Post photos of your process, your sketchbook, or your work in progress at least once daily or every few days on Instagram or similar platforms. One person seeing it can turn into a direct message and a paying client. You do not need to talk on camera—a photo of your work is enough.

### Step 3: Choose A Niche

Being generic means competing with every graphic designer on earth. Being niche means you become the obvious choice for one specific type of client. For example, instead of being a graphic designer for everyone, become the designer for social media influencers with purpose-driven brands. A smaller, clearer audience is stronger than a broad one. Your niche should also come from what you personally enjoy doing, because you will work in this niche for years.

### Step 4: Name Your Business

Once you know your niche, name the business. Think ahead about whether you will have a co-founder or work alone, because that affects the brand architecture. You can use your own name (e.g., "Morgan Design") or choose an abstract brand name (e.g., "Fluid"). The priority is to choose a name you can trademark that cannot be reclaimed once it becomes public. Naming it makes it real and keeps you motivated.

### Step 5: Secure Your Domain Name

A strong domain name is the home of your business and builds trust from day one. A .store domain signals immediately that you sell online, and .store domains have been shown to generate up to 87% more traffic and double Google rankings compared to standard domains. Register the domain as soon as you name the business. This is the moment the business transitions from an idea to a real entity.

### Step 6: Build Case Studies, Not Just Portfolio Pieces

Your credibility comes from showing people what you did, not just telling them. Create case studies that document the business outcome of your work, not just the logo itself. Sell the sizzle, not the steak. For example, instead of saying "I changed their logo," say "their hoodie brand launched and sold out because of the new identity" or "their drinks brand launched and they monetized in new ways." Focus on measurable business results. As a creative, think like a business person and track what happened after the design was delivered.

### Step 7: Find The Right Partners

Every business needs external support at some point. First, your family and close circle must fully back you—meaning they promote you and do not express doubt about your path or distract you with invitations that pull you away from building your business. Remove people who undermine your goals or who do not support your business goals. Second, consider a co-founder who has complementary skills. If you are the designer, find a marketing partner. If you are the marketer, find a designer. Equal partnerships can work if both partners plan their direction together and align on goals. A co-founder pushes you, supports you, and makes it harder to quit.

### Step 8: Quit Your Job At The Right Time

Do not quit your job until your costs are down and your income is predictable. Every dollar you spend is an hour you cannot work on your business. Save enough to cover three months of personal living expenses—at least $6,000 if your monthly costs are $2,000. Aim to land one client and cut your monthly costs enough that one project payment replaces your job income. For example, if a client pays $5,000 total with a 50/50 payment model, you receive $2,500 upfront. If your monthly costs drop from $2,000 to $1,500, that $2,500 upfront payment covers your monthly costs and allows you to quit safely. Cash flow management is the number one reason businesses succeed or fail.

### Step 9: Add Purpose To Your Business

Purpose is not a fluffy word - it is the reason you will keep going when it gets hard. Your pain is where your purpose lives. You could eventually return to help students who cannot afford university the way you were helped. You could also take on one pro bono project a year for a charity. Purpose helps you get through the difficult days and turns your business into something larger than yourself.

## Examples

### Example 1: Graphic Designer Starting From Zero

A student named Morgan wants to become a graphic designer but is considering spending $260,000 on university. Instead, she should walk into local businesses with outdated branding or contact companies directly and offer to redesign their brand for free or low cost with the option to pay if they like the result, document the process on social media by posting photos of her work, and use the results as case studies. Within three months of full-time focus on this framework, she can earn at least $2,000 per month and replace her job income, launching her design business without debt.

### Example 2: Service Business With A Co-Founder

If Morgan finds a marketing-minded co-founder, they can form a 50/50 partnership. The co-founder handles client acquisition and strategy while Morgan handles design. This can make the business 10 times larger because partners push each other, support each other, and make it harder to quit. If Morgan eventually hires someone to run operations, neither founder has to work every day anymore.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Ask first, perfect second. The fastest way to get a client is to ask right now.
- ✅ Pick a niche that is narrow and personal. You become the expert faster.
- ✅ Secure your domain the same day you name the business.
- ✅ Build case studies around outcomes, not just aesthetics.
- ✅ Use the 50/50 payment model to protect your cash flow.
- ✅ Save at least three months of personal living expenses (at least $6,000 if monthly costs are $2,000) before quitting your job.
- ❌ Do not spend money you do not have. Lowering costs gives you freedom.
- ❌ Do not try to be everything to everyone. Niche down.
- ❌ Do not wait until you are perfect. Launch and learn.
- ❌ Do not ignore purpose. Money alone will not sustain you.

## Keep In Mind

- Many people avoid asking for business because of sales anxiety, but asking is the fastest way to get a client.
- Niche down based on what you genuinely enjoy, not just what makes money.
- Cash flow management determines whether your business survives its first year.
- Purpose will get you through the days when motivation is low.
- Your story of working multiple jobs while studying and building your design business is a powerful marketing asset because many business owners remember being in your position.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share sensitive client information in public case studies without permission.
- Secure your domain and social media handles early to prevent impersonation or brand squatting.
- Use contracts for every client engagement to define scope, payment terms, and intellectual property rights.
- Keep personal and business finances separate from day one.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting until you have a perfect portfolio before asking for clients.
  **Solution:** Ask now and deliver well. A single happy client is worth more than a perfect portfolio.
- **Problem:** Quitting your job before having consistent income or savings.
  **Solution:** Keep your job until you have at least three months of personal living expenses saved (at least $6,000 if monthly costs are $2,000) and one client paying on the 50/50 model.
- **Problem:** Being too generic and competing with every designer in the world.
  **Solution:** Pick a niche, become the expert, and dominate a small market first.
- **Problem:** Focusing only on the design and ignoring the business outcome.
  **Solution:** Every project should have a measurable result. Sell the outcome, not the output.
