# SMB - The 7 Trading Signals For Breakout Trades

SMB TRADING SIGNALS: The 7 Must-Have Checks for Breakout Trades

## Overview

SMB Capital traders evaluate 15 criteria organized into setup, context, and timing categories. Each criterion receives one grade: must-have (required), adds-conviction (increases confidence in the setup), or extra credit (a bonus factor). The 7 must-have signals are: A+ pink line, higher time frame squeeze, blue sky, narrative, volume moment, clean tape, and hidden relative strength. Traders screen all 15 criteria before the US stock market opens to grade a trade's potential, then look for the must-haves to confirm the setup during the trading day.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When screening stocks for potential breakout trades before the US stock market opens
- When evaluating whether a breakout setup qualifies for your watchlist
- When deciding whether to enter a trade as a stock breaks out during the trading day
- When scoring trade quality and risk-reward on a breakout trade
- When trading swing or day trades based on technical breakouts

## Steps

### Step 1: Verify the A+ Pink Line (Breakout Level)

The "A+" is a quality grade assigned to the breakout level itself, not a separate label. Identify a significant breakout level where prior failed breakouts at that level reinforce its significance each time — without prior failed breakouts at that level, you cannot confirm the level is significant. Volume typically spikes at tests of the level. When the stock breaks above the breakout level, look for a change in order flow: new buyers stepping in and volume increasing. A high-quality A+ pink line is the primary visual confirmation of a breakout because the breakout is confirmed only when price breaks above this level.

### Step 2: Confirm Higher Time Frame Squeeze

Check that the stock is in a compression pattern on a higher time frame — the hourly, daily, or weekly chart. A squeeze on a daily chart takes multiple weeks to form; on a weekly chart, it takes multiple months. A tighter, longer consolidation increases the potential range of the breakout move. Alignment across all three higher time frames — weekly, daily, and hourly simultaneously — produces the strongest A+ setup.

### Step 3: Ensure Blue Sky Above

Make sure there is no resistance above the breakout level. Blue sky means there is no resistance above the breakout level, allowing the stock to advance without encountering prior resistance levels within the visible chart range. Without overhead resistance, the stock is not forced to stop rising prematurely, preserving a favorable risk-reward profile.

### Step 4: Establish a Compelling Narrative

The stock must have a reason to move beyond the chart pattern. A narrative is the reason that explains why market participants should buy the stock. Common forms include: a corporate announcement or earnings result that shifts market perception of the stock, a cohort of stocks advancing together under a shared market theme, a short squeeze in which short sellers buy back shares to close positions, a growth narrative (accelerating revenue or earnings), or a "this shouldn't be happening" narrative — where the stock is defying bearish expectations (such as negative fundamentals or a failed breakdown) yet continues to hold or climb. You do not need to calculate valuations; you only need to identify what is driving the move. Identifying the narrative helps you maintain positions through normal volatility.

### Step 5: Spot the Volume Moment

Look for a volume moment when the stock you are evaluating breaks out. Volume confirms that large traders and institutions are participating. Target 5x the average daily volume, or volume more than two standard deviations above that average, by the close of the trading day, but a volume moment can also show up in other ways: a sharp volume burst in the first 30 minutes of the trading day as the stock breaks out, followed by lower volume as the stock digests sideways above the breakout level, is still a volume moment as long as the stock holds above the price level where that volume occurred. If volume does not show a material increase as the stock clears the breakout level, the breakout lacks confirmation and should not be traded. Without a volume moment, the breakout level may not be significant enough to trade.

### Step 6: Trade a Clean Tape

Only enter when price action is orderly. Orderly price action (called a "clean tape") means the stock respects key price levels, pullbacks hold at identifiable support levels, the bid-ask spread is no more than a few cents, and buy and sell orders arrive at a consistent pace. Avoid stocks with erratic price action or wide bid-ask spreads, because erratic price action can trigger stop-losses and produce losses even when the stock ultimately rises.

### Step 7: Confirm Hidden Relative Strength

Watch how the stock behaves relative to the S&P 500 or its sector — this signal works best with stocks whose price movement correlates with the S&P 500. Hidden relative strength means the stock resists declining when the S&P 500 falls, without staging a large independent gain. Hidden relative strength is called "hidden" because the move does not register as a large rally; a gain of 10% or more over a few days is not hidden strength; that is visible relative strength. Look for hidden relative strength on the trading day before, or the morning of, the breakout. It shows that buyers are stepping in even without broad market help.

## Examples

### Example 1: SNDK (Samsung-related semiconductor breakout)

SNDK broke out from a consolidation base with a 30-minute Bollinger Band squeeze setup (a compression pattern on the 30-minute chart). The night before the breakout, Samsung raised prices in Korea, acting as a catalyst that was not widely reported. The stock tested its previous all-time high, pulled back briefly, and then broke out the next day. This trade had the A+ pink line, higher time frame compression, clear blue sky, and a catalyst, demonstrating how evaluation criteria can align.

### Example 2: Fastly (earnings play with clean tape)

Fastly was an earnings-announcement trade where the price action opened in an orderly pattern. It moved through the pre-market session high, held that level, and had a penny-wide bid-ask spread with consistent buy and sell orders arriving steadily. This orderly price action confirmed the setup's strength and showed consistent buying, making it a breakout trade despite being a stock they had not traded in years.

### Example 3: LNG (momentum name within a sector theme)

On a day when all natural-gas-related stocks were advancing together, LNG was the largest percentage gainer within that theme. The entire sector advancing together confirmed the trade's strength because the theme was driving multiple stocks simultaneously. This illustrates how stocks benefiting from rotating market themes can produce breakout setups.

### Example 4: Tesla (weekly squeeze with podcast catalyst)

Tesla had a weekly Bollinger Band squeeze and broke above a well-established breakout level. The night before the breakout, Elon Musk appeared on a podcast. While not a fundamental catalyst (an event that changes the company's perceived value) — it was enough to push an A+ chart pattern into a two-day advance. This illustrates that catalysts vary in strength, and even a low-grade catalyst (a marginal catalyst that may only add momentum to a stock already trending upward) can trigger a move when the technical setup is strong.

## Best Practices

- Grade your trade before entering by checking all 7 must-haves: A+ pink line, higher time frame squeeze, blue sky, narrative, volume moment, clean tape, and hidden relative strength
- Grade each of the 15 evaluation criteria as must-have (required), adds-conviction (increases confidence in the setup), or extra credit (a bonus factor) based on the setup, context, and timing categories
- Screen for potential setups before the US stock market opens so you know exactly what to watch for
- Wait for the volume moment confirmation; do not buy a breakout without 5x the average daily volume or volume more than two standard deviations above that average
- Only trade stocks with orderly price action; if price action is erratic or bid-ask spreads are wider than a few cents, avoid the trade even if all other signals align
- Look for hidden relative strength on the trading day before, or the morning of, the breakout.
- Understand that a narrative explains why market participants should buy the stock; identify what is driving the move.

## Keep In Mind

- Not every breakout exhibits all 7 must-have signals, but setups showing all 7 have a higher probability of success than those showing fewer.
- A favorable broader-market trend is not a must-have; top-performing stocks can break out even when the S&P 500 is in a narrow range.
- A catalyst is not required for a valid breakout, but it increases the setup's probability of success when present.
- Stocks with consistent trending behavior (momentum names) typically respond to breakouts differently than stocks with erratic, choppy price patterns (choppy names).
- Enter when the stock is at or near the breakout level; avoid chasing stocks that have already moved one full Average True Range (ATR) above the level.
- Market leadership rotates between themes, so prioritize sectors showing positive daily momentum.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never enter a breakout trade based solely on one signal; always confirm that multiple evaluation criteria are aligned
- If a stock opens half an ATR or more above the breakout level, entry carries elevated rejection risk
- Bid-ask spreads wider than a few cents and erratic price action are signs of low liquidity and can force an exit at a loss
- Be aware that a stock can gap up on news and still fail; always wait for the stock to hold above the breakout level with volume at least 2x the average daily volume.
- Trading breakouts in stocks without consistent trending characteristics can lead to worse outcomes, even if the chart pattern resembles a stock with consistent trending behavior.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a breakout without volume confirmation
  **Solution:** Always wait for a volume moment. If the stock clears the breakout level on less than 2x the average daily volume, the breakout is likely a fakeout (a false breakout that reverses). Volume confirms that large traders are buying.

- **Problem:** Chasing a stock that has already run an ATR above the breakout level
  **Solution:** Only enter when the stock is at the breakout level. If it has already moved one full ATR above the breakout level, the upward momentum has been expended, reducing the probability of continued gains.

- **Problem:** Trading stocks with messy price action
  **Solution:** Demand orderly price action. If the stock has erratic price movements, bid-ask spreads wider than a few cents, or cannot hold its most recent swing highs and swing lows, do not force the trade. Orderly price action is typical of stocks with consistent trending behavior.

- **Problem:** Ignoring hidden relative weakness
  **Solution:** Watch how the stock performs relative to the S&P 500. If the stock falls more than the S&P 500 during down days or fails to participate in rallies, it lacks the strength needed for a strong breakout. Hidden relative strength is a must-have.

- **Problem:** Overvaluing a catalyst and ignoring technicals
  **Solution:** A catalyst adds conviction but cannot replace a setup without an A+ pink line, squeeze, and blue sky. Those three must-haves are still required. Grade catalysts by strength: A+ (can produce a strong move without any technical setup), A (adds significant conviction), B (moderate support), or C (marginal; only helps a stock already trending upward).

- **Problem:** Believing every breakout needs an uptrend
  **Solution:** Breakouts from flat consolidation bases may not be in a prior uptrend. Do not discard a setup solely because the stock has no prior uptrend.
