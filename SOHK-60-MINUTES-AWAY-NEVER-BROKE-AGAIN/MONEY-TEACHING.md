# You're 60 Minutes Away From NEVER Being Broke Again

## Overview

Myron Golden shares the foundational money principles that allowed him to go from poor to wealthy by fundamentally rethinking how income is generated. The core message is that income does not follow effort — it follows the value you create — and that time is infinitely more valuable than money. By learning to make clear offers, build authority through verifiable content, price based on documented results, and shift your identity away from your circumstances, anyone who consistently applies these principles can break the cycle of trading time for money and create lasting financial freedom.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you feel stuck trading time for money at a job with no path to wealth
- When you want to start or grow a business but don't know how to generate income beyond wages
- When you struggle with sales, persuasion, or approaching potential customers
- When you want to build authority and an audience without needing millions of followers
- When you're unsure how to price your offers or services confidently
- When you want to turn expenses into profit centers instead of pure costs
- When you need to shift your identity and beliefs around money and self-worth

## Steps

### Step 1: Shift Your Mindset From Wages to Profits

Understand that profits are better than wages. You work on a job to make a living, but you work on yourself to make a fortune. Stop believing that working harder alone will make you rich — income follows the measurable, desirable outcomes you deliver to others, not how long or how hard you work. This mindset shift begins through direct exposure to people who have already achieved the results you are currently pursuing — seek mentorship from those who have solved the exact problems you face, whether through paid mastermind groups, one-on-one coaching, books written by practitioners, or in-person events where successful business owners are present.

### Step 2: Stop Trading Time for Money

Recognize that "time is money" is a lie. Time is infinitely more valuable than money. If you run out of money, you can get more. If you run out of time, it's game over. Spend money strategically to buy back your time — invest in tools, hire assistants, or automate repetitive tasks that don't require your personal decision-making or creative input, and build repeatable systems (such as automated funnels, outsourced delivery, or subscription models) that generate revenue without your continuous direct involvement. Wealthy people create offers to buy what they want; broke people trade hours for dollars.

### Step 3: Reframe Selling as Persuasion, Not Convincing

People love to buy, but they hate to be convinced. Selling is persuasion — the opposite of convincing. Convincing is getting someone to do something you want for your reasons. Persuasion is helping someone make a decision they already desire to make for their own reasons. Stop pushing products; ask questions and serve the other person.

### Step 4: Make Yourself Findable

There are already thousands, tens of thousands, or millions of people in the world who would buy from you if they knew you existed and trusted that you could solve their specific problem. Stop hunting for customers and start making yourself findable. Publish content — such as YouTube videos, social media posts, or articles — that showcases real results you have achieved for clients or in your own work, and build authority by sharing case studies, before-and-after outcomes, and direct answers to the exact problems your target audience is already searching for online. Use searchable platforms and SEO so that people actively looking for your solution can find you before they find a competitor.

### Step 5: Serve More Than You Seek to Be Served

The best way to gain access to anyone is to seek to serve more than you seek to be served. Get your focus off yourself by asking the other person about their specific goals, their most pressing challenges, and their current priorities before you talk about your own agenda. When you genuinely focus on how to improve someone else's situation — with no immediate expectation of return — people can sense your sincerity. As the scripture says, "As in water face answers to face, so does the heart of a man to a man" — meaning your true intentions are reflected back to those you engage with; people can sense whether you genuinely want to help them or are only focused on your own agenda.

### Step 6: Identify and Own Your Identity Separate From Your Circumstances

The biggest reason people stay broke is believing that their current financial situation is who they are. Get your identity from something permanent and external to your bank account — such as your values, your faith, or your long-term vision — not from your environment or your current income level. Identity produces activity, and activity produces results. If you see yourself as a broke person, you will make decisions that keep you broke. Shift your self-concept first, and your actions will follow.

### Step 7: Apply the Law of Averages and Law of Large Numbers

Everyone who sells anything has an average close rate. Most people in sales never talk to enough people to discover what their actual average is. Take consistent, systematic action with enough prospects that your average close rate becomes predictable — volume removes randomness from your results. A "qualified prospect" is someone who has the specific problem you solve, the budget to afford your price, and the authority to make a purchasing decision. If you talk to at least 100–200 such qualified prospects per month consistently, anyone can be successful regardless of their individual close rate.

### Step 8: Price Based on the Results You Deliver

Price becomes a real objection only when the buyer cannot clearly see the tangible value they will receive in return. Don't compete on price — compete on the specific, documented results you deliver. Raise your prices by recording the before-and-after outcomes for each client — such as exact dollar amounts earned, hours saved per week, or percentage increases in revenue — and communicating those results in your sales conversations. If you help someone make half a million dollars, charging $500 for that training is undervaluing your offer. Price your offers based on the value of the transformation you provide, and if nobody buys, gather specific feedback about what outcome they expected versus what you delivered before raising the price again.

### Step 9: Create Demand Instead of Just Supplying

You don't need a large audience to build a profitable business. A small, engaged audience that has already bought from you or trusts you deeply is enough. Focus on creating demand through media and content — publish case studies, client results, and direct answers to your audience's most pressing questions so that prospects trust you before they ever see your offer, making them more likely to say yes when you present it. The fastest path to establishing that authority is by publishing a book, producing a video series, or creating a podcast that demonstrates your expertise and verifiable results.

### Step 10: Turn Expenses Into Profit Centers

Look at your three largest personal or business expenses and ask how you can bring them in-house or turn them into revenue. For example, if you spend thousands annually on plumbing services for your rental properties, you can acquire a small plumbing company or form a joint venture with a plumber so that you serve your own properties at cost while serving other property owners for profit. Whether it's golf, plumbing, HVAC, or real estate commissions — if you are spending significant money in an area, find a way to own or profit from that supply chain instead of only consuming from it.

### Step 11: Partner Rather Than Build Everything Yourself

You don't need to build both an offer and an audience to make money. If you have an audience, partner with someone who already has a proven offer. If you have a proven offer, partner with someone who already has an audience. Myron Golden built his first businesses to over $10 million in revenue before spending any money on advertising by consistently matching people with offers they were already looking for, rather than trying to create both the product and the audience himself.

### Step 12: Embrace Seasons of Focus and Balance

Balance is not the objective at all times — it is a season. Focus and balance are opposites. When you are starting out, expect long seasons of intense, single-task focus and short seasons of rest or balance. As your business grows reliable systems, recurring revenue, or a team you trust, the demand for constant personal focus decreases. Accept that you cannot be in focus and in balance at the same time, and don't model someone who is in a different season than you.

### Step 13: Let Great People Lead in Their Lanes

Surround yourself with people better than you at specific tasks and let them operate in their strengths. Whether it's family, friends, or team members — assign roles based on ability, not title. Don't second-guess people in their lanes. The secret is respect for each person's role and the humility to not need to be in charge of everything.

### Step 14: Practice Gratitude as a Habit

Gratitude is something everybody can afford, but so few are willing to put in the deliberate effort to cultivate it. Recognize that everything good in your life is a gift — your abilities, skills, opportunities, and even your challenges. Control is an illusion. When you live with gratitude, you make room to receive more. If you can't be grateful for what you have, you won't be prepared for what's coming.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Golf YouTube Channel — Turning an Expense Into a Profit Center

Paul, a friend of Myron Golden, loved golf but viewed it as an expensive hobby. He started a golf YouTube channel, posting one video every week. With 9,000 subscribers — a small niche audience — he generates $1,500–$3,000 per month in AdSense revenue, receives free custom golf bags from brands, and plays golf for free through sponsorships. Golf went from being a monthly expense to a profit center — and he can write off his golf expenses on his taxes. The lesson: take something you already spend money on and build content around it.

### Example 2: The Tanning Franchise — Vertical Integration of Expenses

A client in Myron's content agency owns the second-largest sunspot tanning franchise in the country with 300 locations. He was spending hundreds of thousands of dollars annually on plumbing, HVAC, and real estate brokerage commissions. Instead of continuing to pay those costs, he bought an HVAC company, a plumbing company, and a real estate brokerage to handle those services in-house. This is the same principle as Paul's golf channel: identify your largest expenses and find a way to own or control that part of the supply chain.

### Example 3: The $350,000 VIP Day Client — Pricing Based on Results

A client from Australia initially couldn't understand how Myron could charge $100,000 for an offer. He bought a $350,000 VIP day before the price was raised. Within twelve months, he and his business partner created a million-dollar offer, closed three $1 million offers, and sold seven or eight $100,000 offers. The ROI on a $350,000 investment was enormous — proving that price is only an issue when value is absent.

## Keep In Mind

- Income follows value, not effort — a fast food worker works hard but doesn't get rich
- Time is your most finite resource — you can always make more money, never more time
- There are already millions of people who want what you sell — they just need to know you exist
- Knowledge and mentorship are widely accessible through books, podcasts, and online content — the only investment required is your willingness to seek it out and apply it
- Identity produces activity, and activity produces results — change who you see yourself as first
- Momentum compounds — consistent, repeated action toward a clear goal builds results faster over time, as illustrated by the School of Hard Knocks hosts, who took over 3 years to reach $100K/month and 1 additional year to reach $1M/month
- Direct audience attention toward media you control (such as an email list, YouTube channel, or social media account) so you can make offers on demand rather than relying on algorithms or third-party platforms to feed you customers.
- Everyone is always making offers whether they realize it or not — become intentional about it by explicitly stating what you provide, who it is for, and what action you want the other person to take in every conversation.
- Fulfillment comes from creation, connection, and contribution — not from collecting trophies or money

## Security & Safety Notes

- Be discerning about who you attach your name to — evaluate any business deal or collaboration against your core values and principles before committing, ensuring the partnership aligns with your long-term vision and integrity.
- Don't attach your name to every opportunity that comes your way — protect your reputation as your most valuable asset
- Guard your identity and don't let poverty or negative environments define who you are
- Be cautious of pride in financial success — all gifts and abilities come from above, and control is an illusion
- Gratitude is a safeguard against letting money change your character

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing "time is money" and trading hours for dollars with no path to scale
   **Solution:** Reframe your thinking — time is more valuable than money. Create offers (such as a digital course, a group coaching program, or a software tool) and systems (such as automated funnels, outsourced delivery, or recurring subscriptions) that generate income independent of your direct time input.

- **Problem:** Trying to convince people to buy rather than persuading them
  **Solution:** Shift your mindset from "I need to make a sale" to "this person already wants this and I'm helping them make the decision they already want to make."

- **Problem:** Getting discouraged by rejection in sales or content creation
   **Solution:** Use the law of large numbers to reach enough prospects that your average close rate becomes predictable. Talk to at least 100–200 prospects per month who match your ideal client profile (i.e., they have the problem you solve, the budget to pay your price, and the authority to make a buying decision), and volume negates luck and randomness.

- **Problem:** Letting your current financial situation define your identity
  **Solution:** Anchor your identity in something higher than your environment — your faith, your values, or your long-term vision. Where you are is not who you are.

- **Problem:** Thinking you need a massive audience to build a profitable business
   **Solution:** You can build a thriving business with 200–500 followers who have already bought from you, refer others, or actively engage with your content. Build authority by publishing documented case studies and client testimonials that show exactly what you helped someone achieve, build trust by delivering on small promises before large ones, and serve your specific niche by solving one clear problem rather than chasing viral fame.

- **Problem:** Competing on price instead of value
   **Solution:** Raise your prices by recording the before-and-after outcomes for each client — such as the exact revenue increase, hours saved per week, or percentage improvement they experienced — and communicating those specific results in your sales conversations. If someone pays you $350,000 and makes millions in return, your price was never the issue — the value was always there.

- **Problem:** Trying to maintain perfect balance while building a business
  **Solution:** Accept that focus and balance are opposing seasons. When you're building, lean into focus. Balance comes in due time as your business matures and systems take over.

- **Problem:** Rejecting knowledge because you think you already know everything
   **Solution:** The biggest hindrance to learning is thinking you already know. Stay curious by reading at least one business or self-improvement book per month written by someone who has actually built what they teach, listening to podcasts featuring practitioners who have achieved real results in your industry, and seeking direct proximity to people ahead of you through paid masterminds, mentorships, or by working inside successful organizations as an apprentice or employee to learn how things actually work.
