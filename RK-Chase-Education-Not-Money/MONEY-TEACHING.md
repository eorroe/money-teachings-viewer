# Don't Chase Money, Chase Education

## Overview

This Money Teaching teaches you to prioritize financial education over chasing quick profits, specifically through real estate investing using a cash flow strategy rather than a capital gains approach. It explains why buying undervalued properties with poor management, improving them through better management, and refinancing to pull out your original investment creates a return where you recover 100% of your original capital while retaining ownership and ongoing cash flow. The core message is that your financial success depends on what you know, not which asset class you choose — if you lack financial education, you will lose money in any market.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to invest in real estate without risking your own capital
- When you are considering house flipping or capital gains real estate plays
- When you need to evaluate partnership deals or structured financing for property investments
- When the market is crashing and you want to find opportunities while others panic
- When you are choosing between a cash flow strategy and a capital gains strategy
- When you need to build a team for property management and deal execution

## Steps

### Step 1: Reject the Capital Gains Mindset

Do not buy real estate with the intention of selling it for a higher price later. A capital gains strategy requires you to find a buyer and subjects you to the whims of the marketplace. Instead, commit to a cash flow strategy that does not require an exit. Your goal is to buy income-producing assets that generate monthly cash flow regardless of market conditions.

### Step 2: Buy Undervalued Properties With Poor Management

Target properties that are priced below market value specifically because of bad management. Do not buy based on appreciation potential. Purchase properties where rents are below market, tenants are poorly screened, or operations are inefficient. These are the properties where you can create real value through management improvement rather than relying on market forces.

### Step 3: Assemble the Right Team and Partnerships

Choose partners who share your sophistication level and understand the deal structure. Secure good structured financing before entering any deal. Ensure you have a property manager who has the skills to increase rents, remove bad tenants, and improve operations. Do not proceed without partners and management talent that match your level of expertise.

### Step 4: Improve Operations and Increase Rents

Raise rents by removing bad tenants and improving property management. Increase property value through better management — this is what creates real wealth. When you increase rents, the property's cash flow improves, which raises its valuation. This is a management play, not a market play.

### Step 5: Refinance to Pull Out Your Original Investment

Once the property valuation has increased due to improved cash flow, refinance the property to extract your original investment. For example, in one deal a property was purchased for approximately $11 million, improved, and refinanced at approximately $17 million. After pulling out the original $11 million, the owner kept the remaining cash flow and equity. This is how you achieve a return where you recover 100% of your original capital while retaining ownership and ongoing cash flow.

### Step 6: Repeat the Cycle With Other People's Money

After pulling out your original investment, you have no money tied up in the deal but you still own the asset and collect monthly cash flow. Repeat this cycle with additional properties. You can structure deals where you have no personal investment at all, yet control large amounts of assets and collect ongoing cash flow from tenants.

### Step 7: Choose the Right Time to Start Ventures

Do not start a new business or venture when the market is booming and everyone is euphoric. The worst time to start is when everyone is euphoric and prices are at their peak. Start when the market is down, when others are struggling, and when there is less competition. Times of crisis are when the best opportunities emerge for educated investors. The best opportunities emerge when others are selling.

### Step 8: Use Your Home as a Shelter, Not an ATM

Do not repeatedly borrow against your home equity to fund consumption. Cash extracted from home equity during market runs gets spent on goods, furniture, and lifestyle — not invested in income-producing assets. When the market corrects, you are left with debt and no cash flow. Protect your home equity and keep your personal residence separate from your investment strategy.

### Step 9: Treat Failures as Educational Opportunities

When deals go wrong or you lose money, examine what you did wrong rather than blaming the asset class, the market, or external factors. Whether it is real estate, stocks, gold, silver, or oil — you lose money when you are uneducated, not because of the asset itself. Every loss is feedback that reveals gaps in your financial education.

### Step 10: Read Financial Education Materials and Apply Them

Study materials like Rich Dad Poor Dad and pay specific attention to sections on property management. Most readers skip these sections, but they contain critical information about building a team and creating value through operations. Read with the intent to apply, not just to consume. Recognize that property managers are critical members of your investment team.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Cash Flow Property Flip

You identify a 2-bedroom, 2-bath condo that is undervalued due to poor management and below-market rents. You purchase it with a partnership structure and structured financing. Your property manager raises rents to market rate and removes bad tenants. The improved cash flow increases the property's valuation. You refinance the property, pull out your original investment, and keep collecting monthly cash flow from tenants with no personal capital remaining in the deal.

### Example 2: The Office Building Opportunity

You notice commercial office buildings sitting empty because their owners cannot support the cash flow requirements. You position yourself to acquire one of these buildings at a discount. You wait patiently while the market corrects, then step in when the previous owner cannot pay. You improve management, increase occupancy and rents, refinance to recover your investment, and hold the asset for ongoing cash flow — all without tying up your own capital.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Buy for cash flow, not for appreciation or capital gains
- ✅ Target undervalued properties specifically because of poor management
- ✅ Build partnerships with investors who share your sophistication level
- ✅ Hire skilled property managers who can increase rents and reduce bad tenants
- ✅ Refinance after improving operations to extract your original investment
- ✅ Start businesses and investments during market downturns, not during booms
- ✅ Treat every loss as feedback on your financial education
- ✅ Keep your primary residence separate from your investment strategy
- ✅ Read financial education materials and apply the management principles
- ✅ Structure deals where you control assets with minimal or no personal capital

## Keep In Mind

- The asset class does not cause losses — lack of financial education does. You will lose money in real estate, stocks, gold, silver, or oil if you are uneducated.
- Rich Dad Poor Dad teaches that your passive income must exceed your expenses — this principle, as taught in Rich Dad Poor Dad, has not changed.
- A cash flow strategy requires management skill and a good team; it is not passive in the way some investors assume.
- Being a "flipper" who buys for capital gains without cash flow to support mistakes is dangerous and will leave you broke when the market turns.
- Property management is the critical skill that separates successful cash flow investors from those who lose money.
- Infinite return on investment means you get all your original capital back while continuing to own and collect cash flow from the asset.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never invest in a deal without understanding the full financing structure and exit strategy.
- Verify the financials and management history of any property before purchasing.
- Do not rely on market appreciation to bail out a bad purchase — always ensure the property cash flows from day one.
- Protect your personal residence by not using it as collateral for speculative investments.
- Work with licensed professionals for mortgages, refinancing, and legal structuring of partnership deals.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a property for capital gains and hoping the market will make you rich.
  **Solution:** Switch to a cash flow strategy where the property pays you regardless of market direction. Buy for income, not for appreciation.

- **Problem:** Starting a business or investment when the market is booming and everyone is euphoric.
  **Solution:** Wait for market downturns. Start when others are exiting. The best deals happen when others are struggling.

- **Problem:** Using home equity as an ATM to fund lifestyle expenses.
  **Solution:** Keep your home equity intact. Borrow only to acquire income-producing assets, not to consume.

- **Problem:** Losing money and blaming the asset class instead of your own lack of education.
  **Solution:** Audit your own decisions and knowledge gaps after every loss. The asset is not the problem — your understanding of the asset is.

- **Problem:** Trying to flip houses without cash flow reserves to support mistakes.
  **Solution:** Only enter deals where you have cash flow to learn from mistakes without catastrophic loss. If you cannot afford to be wrong, do not enter the deal.
