# How to Make Money Without Working - Robert Kiyosaki

## Overview

Teach the three types of education (academic, professional, and financial) and explain why financial literacy is the only type that enables you to earn money 24/7 without trading time for income. Guide learners to master the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement so they can distinguish true assets from liabilities, avoid common financial mistakes, and build wealth through four specific asset classes. This teaching exists because a salary focus can trap people in a cycle of working harder without growing net worth. Kiyosaki teaches that financial literacy is the key to earning money 24/7 without trading time for income.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to stop relying solely on a salary or paycheck
- When you need to understand what actually makes someone wealthy versus high-income
- When you are evaluating whether to buy a house, car, or other purchase
- When you want to build investments that pay you whether you work or not
- When you are learning how to read a financial statement

## Steps

### Step 1: Learn the Three Types of Education

Understand that academic education (reading, writing, arithmetic) and professional education (doctor, lawyer, pilot, sailor) are necessary but not sufficient for wealth. Financial education is the third and a key type because it teaches you how money works. Study the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement until you can read and manage them confidently. Rich Dad said: "If you can manage this, you'll always be a rich man no matter what happens to you."

### Step 2: Define Assets and Liabilities by Cash Flow

Use the Cash Flow board game or a simple diagram to memorize the exact definitions:
- Asset: Anything that puts money in your pocket, whether you work or not (rental property income, dividends, business profits).
- Liability: Anything that takes money from your pocket, whether you work or not (mortgage payments, car loans, credit card debt).

Do not rely on standard accounting definitions that classify your primary residence as an asset. Under Kiyosaki's definition, a house with a mortgage is a liability because the mortgage, taxes, insurance, and maintenance pull money out every month.

### Step 3: Map Your Cash Flow Statement

Draw your personal cash flow statement with four boxes: Income, Expense, Asset, and Liability. Track where income and expenses flow each month. If your cash inflow is only a salary and your cash outflow covers mortgage, car, boat, and lifestyle costs, you have salary income flowing in while lifestyle liabilities flow out constantly. Shift your goal so that the Asset column grows and generates cash independent of your labor.

### Step 4: Eliminate the Primary Residence Liability Trap

Stop treating your primary home as an asset. Kiyosaki teaches that "mort" in mortgage reflects how a mortgage can financially suffocate you if income stops. Buying a larger house because you think it will appreciate results in expenses that exceed income. Limit personal residence liabilities and redirect those monthly payments toward income-producing assets instead.

### Step 5: Build the Four Asset Classes That Generate 24/7 Income

Build holdings in these four asset classes so they generate income regardless of whether you show up for work:
1. Business: Own a business system that runs without your daily presence. Sales skills are essential—learn to sell well because a business without customers generates no cash flow.
2. Real Estate: Buy rental properties that generate positive monthly cash flow after expenses. Kiyosaki's wife Kim began with one rental unit in 1989 and grew the portfolio, proving that real estate investments can compound into passive income.
3. Paper Assets: Allocate capital to stocks, bonds, mutual funds, and trusts. Treat this class cautiously because it is the riskiest for investors who lack financial education; do not rely on it as your primary wealth vehicle.
4. Commodities: Hold physical precious metals (gold, silver) and energy assets (copper, oil). Kiyosaki cites physical gold and silver as investments to hold, noting that "a dollar is crashing."

### Step 6: Stop Focusing on Salary as Your Primary Wealth Driver

Salary is the line most employees and self-employed people obsess over, but it is not the line that creates wealth. Train your attention on interest, dividends, rental income, and business profits. If you stop working and income stops, Kiyosaki teaches that you do not own a cash-flow-positive asset—you own a liability that does not generate income independent of your labor. Design your cash flow so the income from your four asset classes covers all your monthly expenses.

### Step 7: Practice with the Cash Flow Board Game

Use the Cash Flow board game (kids, intermediate, and advanced versions) to practice moving money from the income/expense side to the asset side of the statement. The game trains you to convert earned income into passive investments that generate 24/7 cash flow. Practice until choosing assets over liabilities becomes your consistent decision pattern.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Rental Property Path to Passive Income

In 1989, Kiyosaki's wife Kim purchased one rental unit. She systematically acquired more units while treating them as income-producing assets. The rental payments from tenants pay the mortgage and expenses, and any surplus flows to the owner. That single asset grew into a portfolio of assets, generating income whether she works or not.

### Example 2: The Business as the Number One Asset

Bill Gates built Microsoft, Steve Jobs built Apple, and media entrepreneurs built broadcasting companies. These are businesses on the asset side of the cash flow statement. They generate profits from customers and employees, not from the owner's daily labor.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Learn to read and manage the income statement, balance sheet, and cash flow statement before buying investments
- ✅ Classify every holding as an asset or liability based solely on its monthly cash flow direction
- ✅ Build all four asset classes (business, real estate, paper assets, commodities)
- ✅ Redirect monthly savings from consumption spending (liabilities) to income-producing investments (assets)
- ✅ Learn sales skills if you want to own a business
- ✅ Use the Cash Flow board game to practice until the asset/liability pattern becomes automatic
- ❌ Do not count your primary residence as an asset while you still owe a mortgage on it
- ❌ Do not believe that house price appreciation alone creates wealth if the property drains monthly cash
- ❌ Do not treat salary increases as the path to wealth; they lead only to higher expenses

## Keep In Mind

- Financial education is the third and a key type of education for building wealth.
- Assets put money in your pocket whether you work or not; liabilities take money from your pocket whether you work or not.
- A mortgage on a personal residence is a liability, not an asset, under the cash flow definition.
- Salary income enters while lifestyle expenses exit, which can drain money without building net worth.
- Salary is not the key line on your financial statement; focus on interest, dividends, rental income, and business profits.
- The Cash Flow board game was created to teach people to move money from the income/expense side to the asset side of the statement.
- Business is a top asset class because it can generate income independent of your labor; real estate, paper assets, and commodities provide diversification.
- Kiyosaki cites physical gold, silver, and energy commodities among the four asset classes, noting that "a dollar is crashing."
- If you stop working and your income stops, Kiyosaki teaches that you do not own a cash-flow-positive asset—you own a liability that does not generate income independent of your labor.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not invest based solely on price appreciation expectations (e.g., buying a house because it "will go up in value"); always evaluate the monthly cash flow impact.
- Commodities and paper assets carry market risk; allocate capital and diversify across all four asset classes.
- Avoid borrowing for consumption (boats, luxury cars, oversized homes) because these create liabilities that grow even if your income stops.
- Verify that any investment claimed to be an asset actually delivers positive monthly cash flow independent of your labor.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Counting your primary home as your main asset while carrying a mortgage.
  **Solution:** Reclassify the home as a liability on your cash flow statement and redirect the money you would spend on mortgage payments toward rental properties or other cash-flow-positive assets.
- **Problem:** Working harder and earning more salary but never increasing the asset column because every raise funds a liability.
  **Solution:** Freeze lifestyle expenses and commit every income increase to purchasing assets that generate passive monthly cash flow.
- **Problem:** Believing that mutual funds and unit trusts are safe core holdings.
  **Solution:** Treat paper assets as a diversification layer, not your primary wealth engine; build business and real estate assets first because they give you control and leverage.
- **Problem:** Quitting a job or retiring before asset income covers all monthly expenses.
  **Solution:** Calculate the exact monthly passive income required to cover all expenses and do not stop working until your four asset classes generate income that exceeds that threshold.
