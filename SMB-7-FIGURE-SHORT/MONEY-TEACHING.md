# SMB 7-FIGURE-SHORT - Secrets Behind a Short Trade Earning 7 Figures ($1,000,000+ Monthly Profit)

## Overview

This money teaching distills the SMB Capital playbook exercise and the specific overextended short trade on gold (GLD) from October 21st. It teaches how to build a systematic trading playbook by deconstructing high-conviction trades, identify parabolic overextensions using technical and fundamental confluences, and execute short entries with disciplined risk management. The core lesson is that consistent profitability comes from doing daily playbook work so you can recognize and act on A+ trades in real-time without hesitation.

- When looking for overextended short setups after parabolic rallies

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Your Daily Playbook Exercise

At the end of each trading day, deconstruct one trade that really worked well. This can be a trade you made, a trade you spotted on the desk, or a trade you could have taken. Write down the setup, the catalyst, the technical levels, and why it made sense. Collect these playbooks into a database. Over time, this database becomes your trading business foundation because it trains you to recognize A+ setups in real-time without second-guessing.

### Step 2: Identify Parabolic Overextension

Look for stocks or commodities that have made a parabolic move. Gold in this example rallied 27% in a month and a half across three measured moves from a four-month wedge consolidation. Signs of overextension include: price gapping above the 5 Simple Moving Average (SMA), highest volume day of the move, and consecutive gap-ups with expanding range. When the price gaps above key moving averages and the volume accelerates at the top, a correction is coming.

### Step 3: Confirm Macro and Fundamental Context

Do not trade technicals in isolation. For this gold trade, four macro factors aligned: Trump remarks that a full-scale tariff war with China is unsustainable gave markets certainty; the S&P 500 and QQQ were testing all-time highs indicating returning risk appetite; the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) was declining after tariff announcements signaling reduced fear; and the DXY (dollar index) was snapping back which historically pressures gold. When the DXY (dollar index) snaps back, it historically pressures gold lower. For this gold trade, the dollar had been weakening which supported gold, but the DXY snapback signaled a potential gold top. Gold gapped up after the Friday sell-off, triggering stops on existing swing short positions and forcing liquidations. The gap-down on the trade day reset the structure and provided the new short entry.

### Step 4: Mark Key Technical Levels

Identify the critical price levels before the market opens. For this gold short: 390 was the October 21st pre-market support level, 385 was the prior day low (first touch in five days after acceleration), and 373 was higher-timeframe support. Also note the psychological $4,000 level per ounce. A break below these levels with volume confirms the short thesis. The daily chart was overbought, and the intraday price action was negative, giving a two-timeframe alignment to the short side.

### Step 5: Scan for Similar Setups

Build a stock scanner to find new candidates. Use criteria like: price closed higher than the previous day for consecutive days; increasing volume over the last four days; and ATR on the prior day at least 1.5 times the ATR of the day before. This scanner should be toggleable between pre-market and regular hours. Running this scan identifies stocks exhibiting expanding daily range and rising volume on consecutive up days as the gold trade did.

### Step 6: Execute Two-Part Entry

Enter the position in two parts for better risk management and to gather insight into buying and selling pressure while in the trade. The first entry is on the initial rejection or breakdown (such as the Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) rejection on the first candle that fails to hold above VWAP). The second, larger entry is on confirmation that the price is holding below the key level (such as a two-bar break below pre-market low). This gives you a position with a tight stop but also lets you average in as conviction builds. Use 15% of your daily stop loss as position size for entries on a confirmed breakdown below the pre-market low.

### Step 7: Set Stop and Profit Targets

Place your initial stop above the October 21st high of the day or above VWAP if entering on the VWAP breakdown. For profit targets, use a combination of measured moves and trailing stops. Take one-third of the position at the first measured move target. Trail the rest with the 90-minute moving average (MA) or the 9-period exponential moving average (EMA) on the 2-minute chart. Watch the intraday time-and-sales for the highest-volume candle of the day as your primary capitulation exit signal. If volume contracts on the downside, the bid stack thins out, and sellers step away, exit the remaining position immediately.

### Step 8: Review the Trade Objectively

After the trade, conduct a thorough review. Identify what you did well (early spotting, entries, tape reading) and what went wrong (exits at 384 and 381 before the clear capitulation at 377). For this gold trade, the team scored 84.5 out of 100, rounded to 85. Mistakes included exiting at 384 and 381 before the clear capitulation at 377, which represented the majority of the total P&L for the trade. Acknowledge errors honestly and update your playbook for next time.

## Examples

The trade was a short on gold as it broke down from a parabolic rally. Gold had risen 27% in a month and a half with three measured moves from a four-month wedge consolidation. The day before the big unwind, the range was the largest of the move and volume was the highest. On the unwind day, gold dropped more than 5% in a single day, the largest decline in over a decade, with volume twice the 20-day average. The trade was triggered when price failed at VWAP and broke the pre-market low. Key levels were 390 (pre-market support), 385 (prior day low), and 373 (higher-timeframe support). The team entered at VWAP rejection and at the two-bar break below pre-market low. The stop was above the October 21st high of the day. Although the team exited too early at 384 and 381, the clear capitulation came at 377 where the largest volume candle of the day appeared.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Do the playbook exercise every single day to build your database of A+ trades
- ✅ Use two timeframes: daily charts to spot overextensions, intraday charts to time entries
- ✅ Enter in two parts - a smaller initial entry plus a larger confirmation entry
- ✅ Trail stops with the 90-minute moving average or the 9-period exponential moving average (EMA) on the 2-minute chart to capture larger moves
- ✅ Look for capitulation volume (highest volume candle) as your primary exit signal
- ✅ Review every trade honestly and update your playbook with lessons learned
- ✅ Use a scanner to systematically find stocks matching the overextension pattern
- ❌ Do not try to predict where a stock will go - focus on what market participants are doing
- ❌ Do not exit early out of fear - let a portion run until the tape shows exhaustion
- ❌ Do not ignore the difference between short covering and real buying

## Keep In Mind

- Gold is a terrible product to trade intraday due to global hedging activity that creates messy price action unrelated to gold fundamentals. However, when global macro events are more in the news, gold can produce A+ setups. Only trade it when you see A+ trades.
- You do not need to know where price is going. Focus on prior positioning, who is buying and selling, and what is likely to happen next based on that positioning.
- A $400 stock needs more than a 4-point move to justify the trade. Ensure your reward-to-risk ratio is defined before entering.
- The best trades on the desk come when daily charts show an opportunity and intraday charts confirm it in the same direction.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always use a stop loss. Never enter a position without a predefined exit level.
- Size positions appropriately - use 15% of your daily stop loss as position size for entries triggered by a confirmed breakdown below the pre-market low at the opening drive so a single loss has limited impact on your capital.
- Gold trades 24/7 across Asian, London, and New York sessions. Hedging activity from institutional portfolio rebalancing can cause sudden, unpredictable moves that are not related to gold supply or demand.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Exiting too early and missing the bulk of the move
  **Solution:** Take one-third at the first measured move target (equal to the height of the prior consolidation range projected from the breakdown level) and trail the remainder with the 90-minute moving average or the 9-period exponential moving average on the 2-minute chart. Only exit fully when the time-and-sales data shows the highest single volume bar of the day.
- **Problem:** Second-guessing entries in real-time because the work was not done beforehand
  **Solution:** Complete the playbook exercise daily so that when you spot an A+ trade, you execute without doubt. Preparation eliminates hesitation.
- **Problem:** Confusing short covering with real buying after a gap-up
  **Solution:** Compare the volume on the gap-up day to the unwind day. If the gap-up has lower volume, it is short covering, not real buying, and the price will reverse down again.
- **Problem:** Taking profits out of fear after a quick flush
  **Solution:** Have a systematic exit plan before entering. Decide in advance how much you will take at the first target and how much you will trail. Let the trade play out according to the plan.
- **Problem:** Not using measured move targets to size profit expectations
  **Solution:** Study your best trades after the fact and calculate the measured move target (equal to the height of the prior consolidation range projected from the breakdown level) for each trade. Record the measured move target alongside your actual exit so you learn to recognize the pattern in future setups.
