# SMB 7-FIGURE-SHORT - Secrets Behind 7-figure Short Trade

## Overview

This money teaching distills the SMB Capital playbook exercise and the specific overextended short trade on gold (GLD) from October 21st. It teaches how to build a systematic trading playbook by deconstructing high-conviction trades, identify parabolic overextensions using technical and fundamental confluences, and execute short entries with disciplined risk management. The core lesson is that consistent profitability comes from doing daily playbook work so you can recognize and act on A+ trades in real-time without hesitation.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to build a repeatable trading playbook from real trades
- When looking for overextended short setups after parabolic rallies
- When you want to improve trade entries, exits, and position sizing
- When scanning for similar setups using volume, ATR, and consecutive higher closes
- When reviewing your own trades to identify mistakes and missed opportunities

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Your Daily Playbook Exercise

At the end of each trading day, deconstruct one trade that really worked well. This can be a trade you made, a trade you spotted on the desk, or a trade you could have taken. Write down the setup, the catalyst, the technical levels, and why it made sense. Collect these playbooks into a database. Over time, this database becomes your trading business foundation because it trains you to recognize A+ setups in real-time without second-guessing.

### Step 2: Identify Parabolic Overextension

Look for stocks or commodities that have made a parabolic move. Gold in this example rallied 27% in about a month and a half across three measured moves from a wedge consolidation. Signs of overextension include: price gapping far above the 5 SMA, highest volume day of the move, and consecutive gap-ups with expanding range. When the price is far above key moving averages and the volume accelerates at the top, a correction is likely coming.

### Step 3: Confirm Macro and Fundamental Context

Do not trade technicals in isolation. For this gold trade, several macro factors aligned: Trump remarks that a full-scale tariff war with China is unsustainable gave markets certainty; the S&P 500 and QQQ were testing all-time highs indicating returning risk appetite; VIX was declining after tariff announcements signaling reduced fear; and the DXY (dollar index) was snapping back which historically pressures gold. When the dollar weakens, gold rises, but a snapback in the DXY hints at a gold top. Gold gapped up after a Friday sell-off, which reset the trade and gave new short entries.

### Step 4: Mark Key Technical Levels

Identify the critical price levels before the market opens. For this gold short: 390 was pre-market support, 385 was the prior day low (first touch in five days after acceleration), and 373 was higher-timeframe support. Also note the psychological $4,000 level per ounce. A break below these levels with volume confirms the short thesis. The daily chart was overbought, and the intraday price action was negative, giving a two-timeframe alignment to the short side.

### Step 5: Scan for Similar Setups

Build a stock scanner to find new candidates. Use criteria like: price closed higher than the previous day for multiple consecutive days; increasing volume over the last four days; and ATR on the prior day significantly greater than the day before. This scanner should be toggleable between pre-market and regular hours. Running this scan identifies stocks exhibiting the same acceleration and exhaustion behavior as your reference trade.

### Step 6: Execute Two-Part Entry

Enter the position in two parts for better risk management and information. The first entry is on the initial rejection or breakdown (such as the VWAP rejection on the first candle that fails to hold above VWAP). The second, larger entry is on confirmation that the price is holding below the key level (such as a two-bar break below pre-market low). This gives you a position with a tight stop but also lets you average in as conviction builds. Use B-risk sizing (about 15% of your daily stop) for the opening range break.

### Step 7: Set Stop and Profit Targets

Place your initial stop above the high of the day or above VWAP if entering on the VWAP breakdown. For profit targets, use a combination of measured moves and trailing stops. Take one-third of the position at the first measured move target. Trail the rest with the 90-minute MA or 9 EMA. Watch the tape for capitulation candles (highest volume candle of the day) as the ultimate exit signal. If the tape shows acceleration and the bid disappears, exit the remaining position immediately.

### Step 8: Review the Trade Objectively

After the trade, conduct a thorough review. Identify what you did well (early spotting, entries, tape reading) and what went wrong (exits too early, fear of giving back profits, inexperience). For this gold trade, the team scored 85 out of 100. Mistakes included exiting at 384 and 381 before the clear capitulation at 377, which represented half of the total P&L. Acknowledge errors honestly and update your playbook for next time.

## Examples

### Example 1: Gold (GLD) Overextended Short on October 21st

The trade was a short on gold as it broke down from a parabolic rally. Gold had risen 27% in a month and a half with three measured moves from a four-month wedge consolidation. The day before the big unwind, the range was the largest of the move and volume was the highest. On the unwind day, gold dropped more than 5% in a single day, the largest decline in over a decade, with volume twice the 20-day average. The trade was triggered when price failed at VWAP and broke the pre-market low. Key levels were 390 (pre-market support), 385 (prior day low), and 373 (higher-timeframe support). The team entered at VWAP rejection and at the two-bar break below pre-market low. The stop was above the high of the day. Although the team exited too early at 384 and 381, the clear capitulation came at 377 where the largest volume candle of the day appeared.

### Example 2: Scanner Setup for Finding Similar Trades

Using scans.com, the team built a scanner with three criteria: price closed higher than the previous day for multiple consecutive days; increasing volume on the last four days; and ATR on the prior day much greater than the day before. Toggling these filters narrowed the universe to a single stock that matched the overextension profile. While that particular stock had low volume and was not traded, the scan demonstrated how to systematically find candidates exhibiting the same acceleration and exhaustion behavior as the gold trade.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Do the playbook exercise every single day to build your database of A+ trades
- ✅ Use two timeframes: daily charts to spot overextensions, intraday charts to time entries
- ✅ Enter in two parts - a smaller initial entry plus a larger confirmation entry
- ✅ Trail stops with the 9 EMA or 90-minute MA to capture larger moves
- ✅ Look for capitulation volume (highest volume candle) as your primary exit signal
- ✅ Review every trade honestly and update your playbook with lessons learned
- ✅ Use a scanner to systematically find stocks matching the overextension pattern
- ❌ Do not try to predict where a stock will go - focus on what market participants are doing
- ❌ Do not exit early out of fear - let a portion run until the tape shows exhaustion
- ❌ Do not ignore the difference between short covering and real buying

## Keep In Mind

- Gold is almost always a terrible product to trade intraday due to global hedging activity that creates messy price action unrelated to gold fundamentals. However, during major macro events, gold can produce exceptional A+ setups. Only trade it on special days.
- You do not need to know where price is going. Focus on prior positioning, who is buying and selling, and what is likely to happen next based on that positioning.
- A $400 stock needs more than a 4-point move to justify the trade. Ensure your reward-to-risk ratio is meaningful before entering.
- The best trades on the desk come when daily charts show an opportunity and intraday charts confirm it in the same direction.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always use a stop loss. Never enter a position without a predefined exit level.
- Size positions appropriately - use B-risk or C-risk sizing for opening range breakouts so a single loss does not significantly impact your capital.
- Gold trades 24/7 across Asian, London, and New York sessions. Hedging activity from institutional portfolio rebalancing can cause sudden, unpredictable moves that are not related to gold supply or demand.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Exiting too early and missing the bulk of the move
  **Solution:** Take one-third at the first measured move target but trail the remainder with the 90-minute MA or 9 EMA. Only exit fully when the tape shows clear capitulation with highest volume.
- **Problem:** Second-guessing entries in real-time because the work was not done beforehand
  **Solution:** Complete the playbook exercise daily so that when you spot an A+ trade, you execute without doubt. Preparation eliminates hesitation.
- **Problem:** Confusing short covering with real buying after a gap-up
  **Solution:** Compare the volume on the gap-up day to the unwind day. If the gap-up has lower volume, it is likely short covering, not real buying, and the price will probably reverse down again.
- **Problem:** Taking profits out of fear after a quick flush
  **Solution:** Have a systematic exit plan before entering. Decide in advance how much you will take at the first target and how much you will trail. Let the trade play out according to the plan.
- **Problem:** Not having imagination for how big a move can be
  **Solution:** Study your best trades after the fact and note the measured moves. Train yourself to visualize the full potential of a setup so you do not cut winners short.
