# How to Trade Stocks Like the Longest-Standing NYSE Trader

## Overview

Peter Tuchman, the longest-standing trader on the New York Stock Exchange floor with over four decades of experience, teaches disciplined, rules-based stock market trading that survives every crash. He trades between half a billion and one billion dollars per day and reveals that the key to long-term success is trading stocks—not consumer products—using risk management, stop orders, and consistent strategy. This Money Teaching distills his core methods so any responsible investor can apply them.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to build a stock portfolio that survives market crashes
- When learning to day trade or execute trades with discipline
- When deciding what stocks to buy as a beginner or retail investor
- When the user asks how to avoid the mistakes of greedy traders who blow up accounts

## Steps

### Step 1: Buy Stocks, Not Stuff

Every time you buy a consumer product you love—an iPhone, Nikes, or a phone plan—buy one share of the company that makes it instead of replacing the product. Redirect discretionary spending into shares of brands you already trust and use. Over time, those small recurring purchases compound into a portfolio worth tens of thousands of dollars instead of a drawer full of depreciated gadgets.

### Step 2: Build an S&P 500 Habit Early

Put $250 per month into the S&P 500 starting at age 18. By age 60, that habit alone grows to approximately $1.4 million without any stock-picking skill. The S&P 500 has survived every crash because it owns 500 leading companies across sectors. Even smaller amounts—$5 to $10 per week—build wealth if you start early and stay consistent.

### Step 3: Use Stop Orders and Take Partial Profits

When you buy a stock, immediately set a stop order below your entry price to cap your downside. For example, if you buy at $50, place a stop at $49.50 so you cannot lose more than $0.50 per share. If the stock rises to $50.50, take a partial profit, then move your stop to break-even so you cannot lose money on the position. This "hit singles and doubles" approach preserves capital across all market conditions.

### Step 4: Focus on Secondary and Tertiary AI and Tech Plays

Don't chase the headline stock that has already moved. Research the components inside popular products—rare earth minerals, chips, data-center equipment, GPU manufacturers—because those smaller public companies often offer ground-floor upside. Peter cites Thomas Healey as an example: he pivoted from EV batteries into data-center generators after recognizing that AI infrastructure needs energy, and his stock moved from $2 to $8 within months.

### Step 5: Avoid FOMO, Hype, and Meme Stock Mania

FOMO, hype, and hope are not sustainable trading strategies. Peter observed thousands of new retail traders who bought meme stocks like GameStop at the top—some still holding shares bought at $483. The rule is simple: nobody got broke taking a profit. Sell when a stock has moved, don't hope for one more dollar, and never let greed turn a winning trade into a loss.

## Examples

### Example 1: Turning iPhone Upgrades Into Apple Shares

Instead of buying a new iPhone every year at $1,000, buy five shares of Apple stock with that money and keep your current phone. Repeat every upgrade cycle. After 15 to 20 years, those accumulated shares would be worth tens of thousands of dollars instead of owning a drawer of obsolete devices.

### Example 2: Surviving Black Monday with Discipline

Peter was a clerk on the NYSE floor during Black Monday in 1987, when the Dow sold off 608 points—the biggest percentage drop in history. He processed thousands of paper orders under chaos, but because he had stop orders and followed his rules precisely, he did not panic. Traders who ignored risk management went broke. Those who stuck to process survived to trade another day.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Buy stocks in companies whose products you already use and love
- ✅ Set a stop order on every trade before you enter the position
- ✅ Take partial profits immediately when a stock moves in your favor
- ✅ Contribute to the S&P 500 every month starting as early as possible
- ❌ Do not chase meme stocks at all-time highs
- ❌ Do not let greed override your exit plan
- ❌ Do not invest money you cannot afford to lose

## Keep In Mind

- The market always crashes from a record high—never after a long decline. That pattern repeats every cycle.
- Peter never owned a single share of stock in his own name for most of his career because regulations prevented him from front-running customer orders. His profit and loss is not tied to the market direction.
- Accessibility to the market expanded dramatically during COVID, but 80 to 90% of new retail traders blew up their accounts because they skipped risk management.
- "Nobody got broke taking a profit" is not just a saying—it is the core discipline that separates survivors from victims.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never front-run customer orders or use non-public information. Jordan Belfort's scheme recruited eight brokers to illegally buy ahead of customer orders; all were arrested by the FBI.
- Avoid pump-and-dump schemes on social media. Bad actors buy penny stocks, hype them on Twitter, then sell to unsuspecting followers.
- Use regulated platforms and verified sources for market data. Most "insider tips" on social media are pump-and-dump setups.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a stock because everyone on social media says it is going to the moon.
  **Solution:** Verify the company's fundamentals yourself. If you heard about it on social media, the easy money has already been made. Research components, read SEC filings, and wait for the unlock period to pass before evaluating.
- **Problem:** Holding a winning stock too long until it gives back all profits.
  **Solution:** Take partial profits at predetermined levels and move your stop to break-even. If the stock continues higher, you still have a position. If it reverses, you do not lose money.
- **Problem:** Using money you need for living expenses to trade.
  **Solution:** Only trade with disposable income. Peter stresses that you do not quit your job to feed your family through trading. If losing 100% would hurt your standard of living for five years, you are not qualified to trade it.
