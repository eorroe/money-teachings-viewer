# From 9th Grade Dropout to $90M Business: The Joshua Crisp Playbook

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills the key principles from Joshua Crisp's journey of dropping out in 9th grade, working a $7.25/hour recycling plant job, going to jail, and eventually building a $90M+ e-commerce empire. It focuses on how to reframe rock bottom as a foundation, find a mentor, study obsessively, ask the right questions, solve the biggest problem in your niche, and create irresistible offers that scale.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are starting from nothing (or less than nothing) and need a roadmap to escape
- When you feel stuck at a income ceiling and need to break through to the next level
- When you want to build an e-commerce or digital marketing business
- When you need to find a mentor and extract maximum value from the relationship
- When you are crafting offers and want to make them irresistible
- When you need to hire and delegate as you scale from six to seven to eight figures

## Steps

### Step 1: Reframe Rock Bottom — It Is Happening For You, Not To You

Joshua was working a temp job sorting trash for $7.25/hour, living in a one-bedroom apartment with his wife and son, on food stamps, and had just gotten out of jail. His kids asked why Santa never brought them what they wanted. That pain became his fuel. The mindset: whatever does not break you, you break through. Tough times do not last, tough people do. Easy choices create a hard life; hard choices create an easy life. When you hit rock bottom, you have nowhere to go but up — use that urgency.

### Step 2: Get Exposed to New Possibilities — You Cannot Grow Beyond What You See

Joshua had no idea normal people could be business owners until he visited a mentor's office and saw a regular person running an e-commerce business from a normal desk. He says: "You cannot grow to what you are not exposed to." If you hang around people who are broke, you will be broke. If you hang around people who are building businesses, you will start building businesses. Seek out environments, podcasts, events, and mentors that show you what is possible.

### Step 3: Find a Mentor and Study Them Obsessively

Joshua's mentor gave him a "breadcrumb" — a small piece of actionable advice. Joshua studied everything the mentor did, watched every sales call, and asked methodical questions. He became so obsessed that he slept in the office. The mentor eventually said, "Hey Josh, I want to make you a millionaire." Within a year, Joshua was a millionaire. Find someone who has the results you want, study them like your life depends on it, and ask specific, potent questions.

### Step 4: Ask the Right Question — The One Thing That Changes Everything

Joshua's superpower is asking the right question. He does not waste time with small talk. He acts as if he only has one question — because he does. If you could ask a billionaire only one question, you would think long and hard about it. Joshua's questions are premeditative, open-ended, and specific. Example: "I studied your funnel. Why do you do this specific thing and what is the conversion increase?" That one question got him a mentor's funnel data and made him multiple six figures.

### Step 5: Take Action NOW — Do Not Become Addicted to Education

Too many people consume content forever and never execute. Joshua's rule: "Now means no opportunities wasted." Find one thing, take action on it, and implement it. Do not fall victim to analysis paralysis. If you are stuck at sub-$10M/year, the problem is not knowledge — it is execution. Emotion creates motion. Get upset about your current situation and let that drive you to act.

### Step 6: Solve the Biggest Problem in Your Niche — Find the Big Domino

Joshua's mentor sold bath bombs, but no one could beat Lush because Lush had a patent. Once the patent expired, Joshua saw the #1 complaint: bath bomb sets all had the same scents. He created a subscription box with 12 new exclusive bath bombs every month. Sales shot up. Then he added a free random gift worth 20%+ of the box value. This solved the biggest problem (selection) and created recurring revenue (the Rolls-Royce of marketing). Find the one problem that, if solved, makes everything else easy.

### Step 7: Build a Community Before You Sell — Cultivate Herds

Joshua created a Facebook community called "I love bath bombs" before he sold a single product. He cultivated multiple communities around the niche. Why? Because communities are multi-dimensional for marketing. You can survey them, learn from them, and sell to them. He asked one specific question: "What is your biggest problem with bath bombs?" The answer was selection. He built the product around the community's feedback, not his own assumptions.

### Step 8: Survey Your Audience Before Building — Let Data Drive Decisions

Joshua's #1 piece of advice: start surveying your audience today. Use Google Forms (free) or ChatGPT to create a survey. Ask: "What is your biggest problem? What does the marketplace not have? How can I serve you at the highest level?" Then build the product around the answers. Do not create what you think they want — create what they ask for. This is free, quick to implement, and will change your business trajectory.

### Step 9: Create Irresistible Offers — Speed + Size of Result

An irresistible offer has two components: speed to result and size of result. How fast can you get them results? How big are those results? Combine both and you have an offer people cannot refuse. Example: "I will help you generate 7-8 figures within 12 months" (size + speed). Amateurs monetize the front end; experts monetize the back end through value stacking. But do not use false urgency or fake scarcity — people see through it.

### Step 10: Never Sacrifice Quality for Margin — Do Not Profit Off Diminished Value

Joshua's first product on Amazon was a USB car charger. He beat the supplier to death on price, made the cheapest version possible, and got massacred. Lesson: never decrease the quality of your product, team, or ecosystem to increase margin. If you do, you will lose customers and reputation. Negotiate hard on price, but never sacrifice quality.

### Step 11: Understand Lifetime Value (LTV) and the Law of Reciprocity

Chewy refunds customers for their last bag of dog food after their pet passes away, and sends flowers within 24 hours. Why? Because pet owners will get another dog, and they will come back to Chewy. Joshua learned that LTV is everything — a customer who stays for years is worth 10x a one-time buyer. He also learned the law of reciprocity: if you give value first, people feel indebted to give back. Give free gifts, bonuses, and surprises to create loyalty.

### Step 12: Delegate Using DISC Assessment — Hire Yang to Your Ying

When Joshua was scaling, he took a DISC assessment and discovered he is a high D (dominance) — he gets things done immediately, but he is not an integrator or manager. So he hired people who were the opposite: operators, integrators, managers. He also recommends married couples take the DISC assessment to understand compatibility. Hire for what you lack, not what you already have.

## Examples

### Example 1: From $7.25/Hour Recycling Plant to $11.3M Year

Joshua was hand-sorting trash at minimum wage, living in a one-bedroom apartment with his wife and son, on food stamps. He got exposed to e-commerce through a mentor, studied obsessively, asked the right questions, and launched bath bombs on Amazon. One product, one SKU, $1M in sales in less than 12 months made him a millionaire. He eventually scaled to over $11.3M in a single year. The entire journey started with reframing a miserable job as the foundation for his comeback.

### Example 2: The Bath Bomb Subscription Box That Crushed Lush

Lush had a patent on bath bombs and was the #1 brand. Once the patent expired, Joshua did not try to compete on price or quality. He asked his Facebook community what they wanted most. The answer: variety. He created a subscription box with 12 new exclusive scents every month, a free random gift, and recurring delivery. Sales shot up, Subscribe & Save (recurring revenue) skyrocketed, and he became the #1 bath bomb seller on Amazon. He solved the biggest problem in the space.

### Example 3: Asking One Question and Getting a Mentor's Entire Funnel

At a 3,000-person event, Joshua asked Cody Sperber one specific question about his funnel and conversion rate. Everyone else asked generic questions like "What do you think about the green Lamborghini?" Cody got frustrated. Joshua's question was so specific and showed he had done his homework that Cody walked across the room, asked for his email, and sent him the entire funnel and data. Joshua implemented it and made multiple six figures on a webinar. The right question at the right time can change your life.

## Security & Safety Notes

- When scaling e-commerce, protect your supplier relationships and do not burn manufacturers by undercutting them
- If using third-party platforms (Amazon, eBay), understand their terms of service — account bans can destroy your business overnight
- Do not share proprietary funnels, ad strategies, or product research publicly — competitors will copy you
- When hiring, run background checks if you are handling sensitive customer data or large sums of money
- Protect your brand with trademarks and patents where possible — Joshua's first product succeeded because Lush's patent expired

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Consuming endless content without ever executing.
  **Solution:** Pick one thing, take action now, and implement it. Education without execution is worthless.
- **Problem:** Building a product nobody wants because you did not survey customers.
  **Solution:** Ask your audience what they want before you build. Use Google Forms, phone calls, and communities.
- **Problem:** Choosing a trending product that crashes as fast as it rises.
  **Solution:** Use Google Trends to check historical data. Sustainability matters more than viral spikes.
- **Problem:** Hiring people just like you instead of complementary skills.
  **Solution:** Take a DISC assessment. Hire the ying to your yang — operators, integrators, and managers for your vision and strategy.
- **Problem:** Making emotional business decisions instead of data-driven ones.
  **Solution:** Survey your audience, analyze market data, and let numbers guide you. If the data says no, do not do it.
