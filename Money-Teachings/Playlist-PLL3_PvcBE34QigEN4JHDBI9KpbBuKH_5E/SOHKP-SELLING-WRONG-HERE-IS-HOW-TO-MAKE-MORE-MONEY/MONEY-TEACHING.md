# How To Sell More By Disarming Prospects And Mastering Questioning Skills

## Overview

This teaching covers how to become a top salesperson by mastering questioning, tonality, and objection prevention rather than relying on pushy tactics. It explains why no one is born a natural salesperson and how to build the skills needed to consistently close deals at high levels.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to improve your sales closing rate
- When prospects give you surface-level answers or objections
- When you want to build genuine trust instead of sounding like every other salesperson

## Steps

### Step 1: Master The Five Types Of Tonality

Learn and practice the five essential tonalities: curious, confused, challenging, playful, and concerned. Your tone determines how prospects interpret your intention behind every question. Use a curious tone to uncover real situations, a confused tone to get prospects to expand on their pain, a challenging tone to trigger them to defend why they need change, a playful tone to lower their guard, and a concerned tone to reveal hidden objections.

### Step 2: Disarm Prospects With ABDs (Always Be Disarming)

Replace the old "always be closing" mindset with "always be disarming." Avoid sounding like every other salesperson by using playful, non-threatening language and unexpected responses that make prospects relax and lower their defenses. When you sound like a typical salesperson, prospects immediately go into fight-or-flight mode.

### Step 3: Ask Consequence Questions To Create Emotional Pain

Move prospects from surface-level answers to deep emotional awareness by asking consequence questions that make them visualize the future pain of inaction. Pace your questions verbally rather than rushing them, because a fast question gets a fast knee-jerk response. People buy emotionally and justify with logic, so you need them to feel the pain before they will want to change.

### Step 4: Prevent Objections Instead Of Handling Them

Focus on objection prevention rather than objection handling. Every objection stems from a lack of certainty in the prospect's mind, and you control certainty through the questions you ask, the tonality you use, and whether you get them to open up emotionally. If you eliminate uncertainty before it becomes an objection, you drastically reduce the need to handle pushback later.

### Step 5: Build Rapport Through Genuine Listening And Reframing

Listen actively but know when to interject with clarifying questions to bring prospects back on track when they go off on tangents. Make prospects feel like the most important person in the room by talking about their experiences and interests rather than your own achievements. Trust and the ability to get results matter more than being liked.

### Step 6: Scale From Individual Sales To Leading An Organization

Transitioning from being a top salesperson to running a company requires a completely different skill set. Hire people with strengths in areas where you have weaknesses, find a business partner with complementary skills, and build an executive team that can handle operations, marketing, and revenue. Focus on your strengths and let others run the business.

### Step 7: Invest In Continuous Skill Development

Acquire high-level skills through deliberate practice, coaching, and real-world experience. Confidence follows skill level, not the other way around. Study tonality from acting coaches, rehearse your conversations, and treat sales like a craft that requires years of dedicated improvement. Start in high-volume environments like door-to-door sales or cold calling to build speed and adaptability.

## Examples

### Example 1: Using A Confused Tone To Expand On Pain

A prospect mentions they are stressed about their current situation. Instead of moving on, respond with a confused tone: "Hold on, stress? How do you mean by stress?" This triggers their brain to explain the problem in greater detail, revealing deeper pain points that create urgency to buy.

### Example 2: Pacing A Consequence Question For Maximum Impact

When selling life insurance, instead of asking quickly "What happens if you don't do anything and lose Dan's income?" pace it out slowly: "So what happens if you just keep the work policy you have now, but then you lose Dan's income like years before you thought... how would you be able to pay for the house and the kids' school without Dan's income?" The slow pace forces the prospect to actually feel the consequences.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Use the ABDs of selling: Always Be Disarming, not always be closing
- ✅ Master all five tonalities and know when to apply each one
- ✅ Ask consequence questions that make prospects feel future pain
- ✅ Prevent objections by eliminating uncertainty early in the conversation
- ✅ Listen actively but interject with reframing questions to stay on track
- ✅ Build trust by demonstrating you can get the prospect the best result
- ❌ Don't sound like every other salesperson with generic openers
- ❌ Don't push for a close before the prospect has emotionally decided to buy
- ❌ Don't rely on being liked as the primary factor in closing deals
- ❌ Don't hire people with the same skill sets as you in business partnerships
- ❌ Don't expect to master sales without deliberate, sustained practice

## Keep In Mind

- People buy from people they trust can get them the best results, not necessarily people they like
- The best salespeople never make prospects feel like they were sold; they feel like they bought a solution
- Confidence is a direct reflection of your skill level—raise your skills and confidence follows
- Closing happens throughout the entire conversation, not in the final question
- AI will likely replace lower-ticket sales but not high-ticket, high-trust sales

## Security & Safety Notes

- Avoid manipulative tactics that rely on pressure or deception, as these damage long-term reputation
- Verify the credentials and real results of sales coaches before investing in training
- Be cautious with customer data and never share private prospect information in training materials

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Prospects give surface-level answers because they feel pressured
  **Solution:** Disarm them first with unexpected, non-salesy language and slower-paced questions
- **Problem:** Too many objections derail the conversation
  **Solution:** Focus on preventing objections by building certainty through emotional connection and clear value
- **Problem:** Scaling from individual contributor to business owner fails
  **Solution:** Hire people stronger than you in your weaknesses and build an executive team before scaling
- **Problem:** Relying on "natural" sales ability without developing real skills
  **Solution:** Treat sales as a learned craft requiring deliberate practice, coaching, and continuous improvement
