# Money Teaching: SOHKP Lying About Buying Property How To Get Rich

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills Ari Rastegar's approach to building wealth through real estate, with a focus on why conventional wisdom about property investing is misleading for most people. It covers how to use zoning law as a value-creation tool, why leverage from banks and strategic partnerships matters more than personal cash, and why reframing failure as a necessary step—rather than a setback—is critical for long-term success. The teaching also explains how to build a vertically integrated real estate business across multiple asset classes and cities.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are evaluating real estate as a path to wealth and want to avoid common beginner mistakes
- When you want to understand how zoning changes can turn inexpensive land into high-value development sites
- When you need guidance on using leverage (bank financing) to amplify real estate returns without risking all your personal capital
- When you are considering whether to build a real estate business or invest in properties passively
- When you want to build relationships with banks, accountants, and legal professionals who can fund and protect your deals
- When you need a mindset framework for handling failure, setbacks, and early business losses

## Steps

### Step 1: Reframe Failure as a Sign You Are on the Right Path

Stop treating failure as a bad thing. Ari Rastegar looks in the mirror every morning and calls himself a failure—because that label reminds him he is still hungry, still learning, and still in the game. Write yourself a symbolic check for a large target amount (for example, $1 billion) and carry it with you every day as a reminder of the goal. Separate your current net worth from your target valuation. Having a $1 billion platform is not the same as having $1 billion in your bank account—both are levels of operation on the same path.

### Step 2: Master Contracts and Law Before Making Your First Deal

All business deals are contracts, and all relationships are pacts. Understanding the law helps you understand order, structure, and risk before you sign anything. If you come from a legal background, use that training to identify what could go wrong—then design the deal to protect against it. If you do not have legal training, hire a phenomenal accountant and a competent real estate attorney before making your first acquisition. Example: Before buying a 318-acre piece of agricultural land, understand the current zoning classification, what rezoning would require, and how long the city council approval process typically takes in that municipality.

### Step 3: Buy Undervalued Land or Properties and Rezone for Higher-Value Use

One of the most reliable ways to create wealth in real estate is to buy property with restrictive or low-value zoning (such as agricultural land) and petition the city to rezone it for residential, commercial, or mixed-use development. This is a legal and civic exercise—you are making the case that your project benefits the community. Example: Purchase a 318-acre farm zoned for agriculture at a low price. Hire a land use attorney to present a case to the city council showing how rezoning for residential apartments benefits the community by adding housing and tax revenue. Once rezoned, the land value increases significantly.

### Step 4: Build a Vertically Integrated Real Estate Business

Instead of only buying and holding properties, build capabilities across the full real estate lifecycle—acquisition, development, construction, leasing, and property management. This gives you control over margins and timelines and creates a compounding business rather than a passive investment portfolio. Example: Rather than buying a building and hiring a third-party manager, develop the project yourself, manage construction directly, and lease units through your own in-house team.

### Step 5: Use Leverage from Banks to Amplify Returns Without Risking All Your Capital

Leverage is the word that defines real estate wealth. Build relationships with banks early so they are willing to lend you money for acquisitions and development projects. A phenomenal accountant is also essential—accounting is the language of business, and your CPA should understand both risk and opportunity in the context of real estate deals. Example: If you have $500,000 of your own capital, a bank willing to lend $2 million at a low interest rate gives you $2.5 million in buying power—five times what you could deploy with your own money alone.

### Step 6: Add Value to the Community First, Profit Second

The best real estate investments improve the community where they are located. Before buying any property, evaluate how your project will benefit the neighborhood—whether through housing, commercial space, public amenities, or tax revenue. This approach also helps when petitioning city councils for zoning changes, because you can demonstrate civic benefit rather than purely private gain. Example: When proposing a new apartment building in a neighborhood that currently has no housing, frame the project as solving a community need for housing rather than just maximizing your own return.

### Step 7: Invest in Relationships Before You Need Them

The non-negotiable relationships for real estate success are: a strong accountant who understands real estate tax and deal structures, a competent real estate attorney, and a banking relationship that can provide leverage. Build these relationships before you need them, not in the middle of a deal. The personality of a great accountant (risk-averse, reflective, precise) complements the bold, fast-moving personality of an entrepreneur—together they create better decisions.

## Examples

### Example 1: From $80,000 Loss to a Billion-Dollar Real Estate Empire

Ari Rastegar's first property deal cost him $80,000 and sold for $15,000—an immediate and painful loss. Rather than quitting, he analyzed the mistake and concluded that real estate was still the right path, but he needed to understand the legal and financial structures better. He went on to build a vertically integrated real estate firm with investments in 38 cities, 13 states, and seven different asset classes.

### Example 2: Using Zoning to Create Value Where Others See None

Rastegar's firm bought agricultural land and used zoning expertise to convert it to residential and commercial use. The ability to read zoning laws, understand city council priorities, and present a civic-benefit case turned cheap farmland into a high-value development site. This legal and strategic skill—not construction expertise—was the core of the value creation.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Call yourself a failure every morning to stay hungry and avoid complacency
- ✅ Write yourself a symbolic check for your target net worth and keep it visible
- ✅ Master contracts and zoning law before making your first real estate deal
- ✅ Buy undervalued land with rezoning potential rather than already-developed properties at market price
- ✅ Build vertically integrated capabilities so you control the full deal lifecycle
- ✅ Build relationships with banks and accountants before you need financing
- ✅ Frame every real estate project as a community benefit to accelerate zoning approvals
- ❌ Do not treat failure as permanent—treat it as feedback for your next deal
- ❌ Do not rely on personal cash alone—use bank leverage to amplify your buying power
- ❌ Do not skip the legal and zoning analysis—this is where most real estate deals fall apart
- ❌ Do not enter real estate without a CPA who understands deal structures and tax implications

## Keep In Mind

- A billion-dollar valuation and a billion dollars in your bank account are different levels of operation—both are milestones on the same path
- Zoning is a legal exercise that requires petitioning city councils and demonstrating community benefit, not just filing paperwork
- The lawyer's mind is trained to see what could go wrong; the entrepreneur's mind is trained to see what could go right. Both perspectives are necessary for deal success
- Adding more value than you are paid for in the early stages is how you build the reputation and track record that leads to larger deals

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share deal terms, acquisition prices, or zoning strategies publicly—competitors can use this information against you in future bids
- Protect banking relationship details and financing structures as confidential commercial information
- When petitioning city councils, avoid making public statements that could undermine your case for rezoning before the vote

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Quitting after a single failed real estate deal
  **Solution:** Analyze every loss as a learning event. Rastegar lost $80,000 on his first property and used that lesson to build a billion-dollar portfolio.
- **Problem:** Buying already-zoned properties at market price with no value-creation upside
  **Solution:** Target undervalued land with rezoning potential or properties where you can add value through renovation or repositioning
- **Problem:** Relying on personal cash instead of bank leverage
  **Solution:** Build banking relationships early so you can amplify your buying power with borrowed capital
- **Problem:** Ignoring zoning law and city council politics
  **Solution:** Hire a land use attorney and study the zoning map of every market before buying. Zoning is the difference between a $50,000 parcel and a $5 million development site
