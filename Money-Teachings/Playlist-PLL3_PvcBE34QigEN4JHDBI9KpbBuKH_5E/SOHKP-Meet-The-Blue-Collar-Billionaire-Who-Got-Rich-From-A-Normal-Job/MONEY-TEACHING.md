# Money Teaching: Meet The Blue Collar Billionaire Who Got Rich From A Normal Job

## Overview

Tommy Melo built A1 Garage Door into a $300M revenue company with over 700 employees, starting from working the jobs himself and writing checks off his equity line. This Money Teaching covers his principles for becoming a specialist rather than a generalist, hiring people based on character and competitive drive rather than sales experience, building an equity culture that gives every employee — including technicians and installers — an ownership mindset, delegating operational tasks to work on the business rather than in it, investing in personal development and coaching, and building a brand rooted in trust, reliability, and genuine care for customers and team members.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are in a blue-collar or home service industry and want to scale from a local operation to a national brand
- When you have hit a revenue ceiling and need to shift from a generalist to a specialist
- When you want to build a team of high-integrity people without relying on traditional sales hiring practices
- When you are ready to implement an equity incentive program to align your team's interests with company growth
- When you need to shift from working in the business to working on the business
- When you want to invest in your own personal development and leadership capabilities

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose a Niche and Become a Specialist, Not a Generalist

The riches are in the niches. Tommy became a specialist in garage doors when every competitor was a generalist. A specialist command higher margins, stronger customer trust, and clearer positioning. Do not spread your energy across multiple unrelated business lines early on — focus on one trade, one service, or one product category and become the absolute best at it before expanding. When a friend told Tommy to put all his eggs in one basket for three years and see if it overflowed, it changed his trajectory entirely.

### Step 2: Hire Based on Character and Competitive Drive, Not Sales Experience

Do not hire salespeople. Hire good people who smile and do the right thing, then teach them the technical skills. Tommy uses four criteria for every hire: (1) Would I work for them? (2) Would I buy from them? (3) Would my grandmother buy from them? (4) Would I have a beer with them and laugh the whole time? He prioritizes former athletes because they understand practice, repetition, and showing up prepared — the same traits that build winning companies. People who have competed in sports understand that you practice 10 times more than you play, and excellence is a habit, not an outcome.

### Step 3: Fire Fast, Fire Even Faster — Cancer Spreads Quickly

A top-performing employee who disrespects peers, managers, or the culture is a net negative for the organization even if their numbers are strong. Tommy has fired over a dozen top producers for toxic behavior, and every time the rest of the team respected the decision more. If you allow disrespect to persist, it erodes trust across the entire company. Cut bad actors fast — do not let them spread their toxicity to the wider team.

### Step 4: Implement an Equity Incentive Program That Gives Every Employee an Ownership Mindset

Move from ordinary income (salary and bonus) to equity-based compensation (profit units and stock options) so every employee thinks and acts like an owner. Tommy's company went from $12M to $27.5M in EBITDA in 19 months after implementing an equity incentive program that gave technicians and installers a stake in the business. When people own a piece of the outcome, they show up differently — they recruit on their days off, they ask for reviews, they treat company equipment like their own. Every publicly traded company and private equity firm uses this model; apply it all the way down to frontline workers.

### Step 5: Delegate Operational Work So You Can Work on the Business

Use an executive assistant to handle email, scheduling, and administrative tasks. Time-block your day and calculate your hourly value — anything that can be done for less than your hourly rate should be delegated. The only tasks you should keep are the ones that require your personal touch: leading orientation, running VP-level interviews, conducting acquisition conversations, and telling your team how much you appreciate and value them. If you are still answering your own emails, you are working in your business, not on it.

### Step 6: Read the Right Books and Re-Read Them at Different Stages of Life

You do not need to read a hundred different business books. Read the same books repeatedly and apply one or two lessons at a time. Tommy's must-reads include "Go for No" for every employee, "How to Win Friends and Influence People" by Dale Carnegie, "Buy Back Your Time" by Dan Martell, "Think and Grow Rich" by Napoleon Hill, "The Ultimate Sales Machine" by Chad Holmes, and "The Richest Man in Babylon." The lessons in these books land differently at 25, 35, and 45 — re-read them as you grow.

### Step 7: Invest Millions in Coaching, Personal Development, and Health

Tommy spends millions per year on coaches for himself and his executive team — not just business coaches, but confidence coaches, breathing coaches, and performance coaches. He cold plunges four days a week not because he enjoys it but because discipline is a muscle that must be flexed to stay strong. Surround yourself with people who are better than you at what you do. Pay to play with the best — extract 80% of what they know and build a lifelong connection.

### Step 8: Build a Personal Brand That Attracts Talent, Customers, and Opportunity

Your personal brand is the most scalable marketing asset you own. Forty of Tommy's last 50 new hires found him because they were already following his personal brand. When you show up consistently — online and in person — with authenticity and a clear message, you attract people who want to work with you before you ever post a job opening. Invest in content that communicates your values, your story, and your vision. Visibility is no longer optional for leaders — it is a business strategy.

### Step 9: Create a "Dream Manager" System That Helps Employees Achieve Their Personal Goals

Hire a full-time Dream Manager whose job is to understand every team member's personal goals — financial, family, health, spiritual — and help them build a plan to get there faster. When your company is known for helping people achieve their dreams, not just paying them a wage, you attract and retain the best talent in your industry. Loyalty is a two-way street: if you genuinely care about your team's lives outside of work, they will give you everything they have inside of it.

### Step 10: Build Systems That Allow You to Scale Without Becoming the Bottleneck

Read "The E-Myth Revisited" by Michael Gerber to understand the difference between working in the business and working on it. Create scorecards for every position, set clear performance metrics, and give your team the tools to operate independently. As you scale from 100 to 200 to 400 to 800 employees, the only way you grow is by building systems that work without you in the room. Your job as the founder is to set the vision and remove obstacles — not to manage day-to-day operations.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Writing Checks Off an Equity Line to $300M Revenue

Tommy started doing garage door jobs himself, writing checks off his equity line when cash was tight, and dealing with employees stealing, cars catching fire, and cops showing up at the office. He never gave up — he kept showing up, kept fixing problems, and kept building systems. Today A1 Garage Door generates over $300M in revenue with over 700 employees and is one of the largest home service companies in the country.

### Example 2: Using an Equity Incentive Program to 2x EBITDA in 19 Months

A1 Garage Door went from $12M to $27.5M in EBITDA in 19 months after rolling out an equity incentive program that gave every employee — including technicians and installers — a stake in the business's success. When people think like owners, they act like owners: they recruit on their days off, they ask for reviews, and they treat company assets with care. Private equity firms use profit units; public companies use stock options — A1 gave this same incentive to the people who actually do the work.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Choose one trade, service, or product and become the specialist — do not diversify early
- ✅ Hire for character, competitive drive, and coachability — not for industry experience or sales track record
- ✅ Fire top performers who disrespect peers or damage culture — cancer spreads fast if you do not cut it out
- ✅ Implement an equity incentive program that reaches all employees, not just executives
- ✅ Delegate everything below your hourly rate — spend your time on high-leverage leadership activities
- ✅ Re-read the same 5–10 business books throughout your career — they mean something different at every stage
- ✅ Invest in coaching and personal development for yourself and your leadership team
- ✅ Build a personal brand that attracts talent and customers before you ever post a job opening
- ✅ Use a Dream Manager system to help your team achieve their personal goals alongside company goals

## Keep In Mind

- There are no participation trophies in entrepreneurship — if you are not first, you are last
- The hustler has to die for the leader to be born — you cannot keep doing every job yourself and scale
- Your people are your most important asset — hire slowly, train continuously, and fire fast when it is not working
- You have 168 hours in a week — sleep 50, work 60, and invest the remaining 58 in your future self
- Most people are not cut out for entrepreneurship — very few are willing to do the work and accept the risk
- The greatest ROI on your money is investing in yourself — you cannot outsource your growth to anyone else

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share exact employee headcount, revenue figures, or financial metrics publicly — this information can be used by competitors
- When running background checks on hires, use licensed providers and comply with all local employment laws
- Protect customer data collected by your service teams — garage door installation often involves access to private homes and sensitive information
- Keep your personal brand and company brand separate enough that you are not personally liable for business debts or legal issues

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trying to do everything yourself and becoming the bottleneck
  **Solution:** Read "The E-Myth," hire an executive assistant, time-block your day, and delegate every task below your hourly value
- **Problem:** Hiring toxic top performers because their numbers are strong
  **Solution:** Fire them fast — their numbers do not outweigh the damage they do to team culture and long-term performance
- **Problem:** Staying in the technician role as the company grows
  **Solution:** Force yourself off the trucks and out of the field — your job is to build the system, not to be the best technician
- **Problem:** Underinvesting in personal development and coaching
  **Solution:** Pay to work with the best coaches in the world — the ROI on a great coach is infinite because it compounds across every decision you make
- **Problem:** Building a business that only makes you money but does not serve your family or personal values
  **Solution:** Write down your "Six Fs" — faith, family, friends, fitness, fun, and finance — and make sure your business serves all of them, not just the last one
