# Money Teaching: I Lost Everything Then Got Rich in Dubai

## Overview

Dariush Soudi built and lost a multi-million dollar health club empire in the UK, surviving a violent home invasion, a heart attack, and financial ruin before arriving in Dubai with $750 and rebuilding from scratch. This Money Teaching covers his principles for taking full personal accountability for every outcome, identifying shared business fundamentals across industries, building a business through value-driven sales rather than capital investment, using the 5630 content method to find your business niche, pricing based on the value you deliver rather than competitor benchmarks, and building a team and culture rooted in explicit written values.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you have lost everything financially and need to rebuild from zero
- When you are considering relocating to a more business-friendly jurisdiction with lower taxes
- When you want to build a consulting or services business without upfront capital or a physical location
- When you need to hire and retain top talent in a competitive market
- When you are setting pricing for your services and tend to undercharge out of insecurity
- When you want to establish a clear set of personal and business values that guide every decision

## Steps

### Step 1: Accept Full Personal Accountability for Every Outcome

Take full responsibility for everything that has happened in your business and life — including the people you let in. Dariush accepted that the franchisee who betrayed him was attracted into his life because he ignored red flags while focused only on growth. Victims blame external forces; owners look inward first. When you stop blaming the government, the economy, taxes, or other people, you reclaim the only thing that matters: your ability to control your next decision. This mindset is the foundation of all resilience.

### Step 2: Identify the 5–6 Things All Businesses Have in Common and Solve for Them

Every successful business shares a small number of core fundamentals. Dariush knew the health industry and saw that most clinic and salon owners underinvested in soft skills — specifically the ability of their receptionists to convert visitors into paying customers. He walked in and offered to train their front-line staff for 30% of the incremental revenue generated. This single insight grew into a multi-company empire across social media, SEO, web development, and training — all because he identified a universal bottleneck and solved it first.

### Step 3: Start With a Services Offer Before You Can Afford a Formal Business

When you have no capital and no company structure, sell your time and expertise as a consultant first. Dariush door-knocked clinics in Dubai with no car and no office, doing half-day consulting engagements for cash. Under-the-table cash work funded him for three and a half years while he built toward a proper company setup. Never refuse money when it is offered — sell first and figure out delivery second. Perfection is not required on day one; a paying customer is.

### Step 4: Use the 5630 Method to Discover Your True Business Direction

Before committing to a specific niche, create five content or service buckets that interest you, produce six pieces of content or service offers in each bucket, and post or offer them for 30 days without looking at the data. At the end of the month, evaluate which bucket energized you the most and which one generated the strongest response. Focus month two on the winning buckets. This method prevents analysis paralysis and lets real market feedback — not your assumptions — decide your direction.

### Step 5: Price Based on the Problem You Solve, Not on What Competitors Charge

Never price your services based on what others in your industry charge. Price based on how much your solution costs the client in lost revenue if they do not act. When Dariush pitched a government deal at 15x his normal rate, he was rejected for being too cheap — the low price signaled low value to a buyer with deep pockets. When he raised his prices and focused on branding and social proof, the same clients started approaching him asking "Can I afford you?" instead of him justifying his worth. Fire clients who cannot afford your premium pricing — they consume energy without generating meaningful revenue.

### Step 6: Hire for Drive and Values, Not for Resumes and Experience

Recruit using a 7-step process that evaluates motivation and cultural fit before reviewing the CV. Place ads based on values — honesty, drive, happiness — not job responsibilities. Run group interviews where candidates interview each other; the people who ask thoughtful questions reveal their curiosity and initiative. Use a spy in the room to observe how candidates behave while waiting — 50% of people disqualify themselves before the formal interview begins due to poor attitude when plans go off-schedule. CVs are unreliable; motivation is not.

### Step 7: Build Your Company Culture Around Explicit Written Values

Create a written values document that every team member reads and agrees to uphold before they are hired. When someone violates those values, the firing conversation is straightforward — they know what they signed up for, and they failed themselves, not you. This approach means people often thank you when you fire them because the values agreement was clear from the start. Your values become your brand, your hiring filter, and your decision-making compass all at once.

### Step 8: Shift From Selling Time to Building an Asset That Does Not Rely on You

Selling your time caps your income at the number of hours in a day. From day one, build toward a business model that can operate without your direct involvement. Dariush spent three and a half years as a one-person band before he had enough credibility and a formal company structure to hire. If you cannot step away for a week without the business stopping, you do not have a business — you have a job.

### Step 9: Build a Personal Brand on Social Media to Command Premium Pricing

Before having a social media presence, Dariush had to justify his rates to every prospect. After building a credible social media presence, prospects approach him asking "Can I afford you?" rather than him defending his worth. Invest in content that demonstrates your expertise, showcases client results, and communicates your values. Your social media profile is the new business card — it shapes how people think about you before you ever speak to them.

### Step 10: Prioritize Health and Relationships Alongside Business Ambition

The shock of a heart attack forced Dariush to confront that he had been building a life he did not actually enjoy — an industry he did not care about, in a country that made him unhappy. Burnout and chronic stress are silent killers that undermine every other financial goal. Write down your non-negotiable values and life requirements before you are forced to by a health crisis. You can build wealth and protect your health simultaneously — abundance is not a zero-sum game.

## Examples

### Example 1: Rebuilding From $750 and No Company to a Multi-Company Empire in Dubai

Dariush arrived in Dubai with $750 in his pocket and no legal business entity. He door-knocked clinics, offered performance-based training, and parlayed that cash flow into a social media company, then added websites, SEO, recruitment, and a training school — all built on the same foundational value system. Within roughly 16 years, he had four companies, was a Google Premier Partner, and was charging $10,000 per hour for consulting.

### Example 2: Rejecting Cheap Work to Build a Premium Brand

Dariush multiplied his normal rate by 15 for a lucrative government deal and was rejected — not for being too expensive, but for being too cheap. The low price made the buyer question the quality. He shifted his positioning to a premium brand focused on results and perception, and the same clients began approaching him with confidence rather than skepticism. The lesson: charging too little signals low value and attracts low-quality clients who consume disproportionate time and energy.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Accept full personal accountability for every business outcome — stop blaming external factors
- ✅ Identify universal business bottlenecks and sell solutions to them before building a full company
- ✅ Sell first, figure out delivery second — never refuse money when it is offered
- ✅ Use the 5630 content method to discover your real niche before overcommitting
- ✅ Price based on client ROI, not competitor pricing — fire clients who underpay
- ✅ Hire using a values-first, CV-last process; motivation is teachable, skills are not
- ✅ Write down explicit values and have every team member agree to them before hiring
- ✅ Build a personal brand on social media so prospects come to you asking your price
- ✅ Prioritize health and personal fulfillment alongside financial ambition — they are not mutually exclusive

## Keep In Mind

- You attract the people and circumstances that reflect your own standards — if you hire a bad partner, that is on you
- In the UK, Dariush worked 29 out of 30 days just to pay the landlord, staff, and tax man — leaving one day for himself. In Dubai, what you earn you keep, making entrepreneurship far more accessible
- People only take action when they feel pain — prevention messaging does not sell as effectively as solving an acute problem
- Every employee should have a stake in the business — equity thinking turns workers into owners
- If you are selling your time exclusively, you will never build real wealth — build an asset that scales beyond your hours

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share exact revenue figures, property addresses, or personal financial details publicly — this information can be exploited by bad actors
- When entering new markets, use formal contracts with clear performance clauses and termination rights to protect your brand and distribution
- Be cautious about who you bring into your inner circle as a business partner — trust must be earned through consistent behavior, not assumed from a good initial impression
- Keep your values document updated and review it with every new hire before onboarding

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Partnering with the wrong people because you are desperate to scale fast
  **Solution:** Pause and evaluate every potential partner against your written values — if there are red flags, trust them and walk away
- **Problem:** Undercharging because you are new to the market and lack confidence
  **Solution:** Price based on the value you deliver and the pain you solve — if a client balks at a fair price, they were never going to be a good client anyway
- **Problem:** Becoming the bottleneck in your own company because you cannot delegate
  **Solution:** Hire people who are 80% as good as you, build systems around them, and use independent recruiters so your team leaders do not dilute quality over time
- **Problem:** Hiring based on impressive resumes that turn out to be inaccurate
  **Solution:** Run a technical assessment as the final step of your interview process — it quickly reveals who is all talk and who can actually do the job
- **Problem:** Burning out by prioritizing business over health until a crisis forces a reset
  **Solution:** Write down your values and life requirements now, before you are forced to by a health emergency — protect your health proactively, not reactively
