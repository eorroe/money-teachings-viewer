# How Edwin Arroyave Built a $40M/Year Empire From Poverty

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills the strategies Edwin Arroyave used to go from sleeping with cockroaches in a windowless room to building a $40 million per year security company. It focuses on how pain-driven motivation, identity-building through discomfort, mentorship by modeling top performers, and disciplined action can create exponential success even from the most difficult starting circumstances.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you feel stuck in a low-income job with no clear path forward
- When you need to build unshakeable self-belief to pursue big goals
- When you want to accelerate your career by modeling top performers
- When you are afraid to take risks that demand more than you think you are worth
- When you need a framework for turning pain into productive action

## Steps

### Step 1: Use Pain as Fuel for Action

Identify the specific pains in your life that are driving you to change. Edwin became the head of household at 15 because he was tired of roaches waking him up, tired of not having enough food, and tired of living in a dark room. Write down exactly what you are trying to escape and what you are trying to build. That clarity creates urgency.

### Step 2: Focus on What You Can Control

When Edwin worked at the call center, he focused only on his activity and attitude instead of the fact that others had seniority, education, and natural talent. Control your daily actions and mindset. Ignore the noise of people ahead of you who seem more qualified.

### Step 3: Model the Best Person in the Room

Edwin recorded his company's top salesperson's pitch, studied it verbatim, and copied his tonality and body language. Find the person who is already crushing it in your field. Record their pitch, their presentation style, their closing techniques. Study until their approach becomes reflexive.

### Step 4: Step Into Uncertainty

Edwin left a stable $70,000/year job without having figured out everything. He bought a minivan before he had salespeople to create urgency. If you wait until you have everything figured out, you will never start. Sell first, then figure it out. Faith is taking action before you have what you need.

### Step 5: Build Your Identity Through Small Wins

Every accomplishment builds belief. Edwin became manager at 18, which made him believe he deserved $100k/year. Set targets that are just ahead of your current belief level. Each target you hit unlocks a new level of self-worth. You will never out-earn your self-worth, so invest in it daily.

### Step 6: Find Someone With a Vested Interest in Your Success

Edwin says the two things you need are an opportunity that pays what you want and someone who has done it before and has a vested interest in your success. Find a mentor, coach, or partner who will actually help you, not just give generic advice.

### Step 7: Build Infrastructure Before Scaling Sales

Once you have a proven product, build your operations and systems before pushing massive sales volume. If operations cannot support the sales, you will grow too fast and go out of business. Invest in strong foundations before scaling.

### Step 8: Keep the Promises You Make to Yourself

Your subconscious mind tracks every promise you break. Each time you keep a promise, you build confidence. Each time you break one, your subconscious sabotages you back to what you think you are worth. Be intentional about keeping commitments to yourself.

## Examples

### Example 1: Edwin Becomes the Youngest Manager at 18

Edwin was a shy, timid kid working at a call center. He showed up to every leadership class when top salespeople skipped them. He recorded the VP of Sales' pitch and memorized it word for word. He became the youngest manager in company history at 18, earning $60,000/year. This single win built enough belief to chase $100k/year by 21.

### Example 2: Burning the Boats on the Call Center Job

Edwin worked at the call center from 7am to 3pm and his own business from 4pm to 10pm. He had no urgency because the call center paid $1,500/week. The moment he resigned, his urgency spiked. He learned his entire presentation in one day and got five sales on his first full day. He made $1,500 in one day, which was more than a full week at the call center working 70 hours.

### Example 3: Buying the Minivan Before Hiring Salespeople

Edwin bought a minivan before he had any salespeople working for him. The minivan created urgency: he had to fill it. He went out and recruited salespeople immediately. This is the principle of touching the dream before you have what you need. He also stayed at mid-tier hotels before he could afford his dream house to program his subconscious mind for the lifestyle he wanted.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Focus on activity and attitude, not external circumstances you cannot control
- ✅ Model the best person in your field by recording and studying their pitch, tonality, and body language
- ✅ Step into uncertainty and sell first, then figure it out
- ✅ Set micro-targets that are just ahead of your current belief level
- ✅ Keep every promise you make to yourself to build subconscious confidence
- ✅ Touch the dream before you have what you need to create urgency
- ✅ Build infrastructure before scaling sales when you have a proven product
- ✅ Invest back into your business instead of sitting on cash

## Keep In Mind

- Your unlimited potential is limited to where you are today, but every accomplishment expands it
- Pain is not something to run away from; it is what builds grit, fortitude, and resiliency
- The pursuit of greatness is not a destination; it is the continuous potential to become a better version of yourself
- Problems are a good thing because big dreams come with big challenges
- When you get comfortable, you become vulnerable to being passed by someone hungrier

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not confuse urgency with recklessness. Edwin kept $200,000 in reserves when he invested $800,000 back into his company in 2009. Always maintain a financial safety net.
- When modeling a top performer, ensure you are not copying unethical or illegal behavior. Focus on their process and presentation, not shortcuts that could damage your reputation or lead to legal trouble.
- Burning the boats means committing fully, but only after you have validated there is real demand for what you are selling.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Waiting for the perfect time to start.
  **Solution:** There is no perfect time. Sell first, then figure it out. Action creates clarity.
- **Problem:** Getting discouraged because your big dream seems too far away.
  **Solution:** Set micro-targets. Every small win builds belief and keeps you moving forward.
- **Problem:** Playing it safe and avoiding opportunities that demand more than you think you are worth.
  **Solution:** If your dreams are big enough, step into the uncertainty. That is where growth happens.
- **Problem:** Focusing on the "how" instead of the "why."
  **Solution:** The "how" is the killer of all dreams. Start with your why, and the how will reveal itself through action.
