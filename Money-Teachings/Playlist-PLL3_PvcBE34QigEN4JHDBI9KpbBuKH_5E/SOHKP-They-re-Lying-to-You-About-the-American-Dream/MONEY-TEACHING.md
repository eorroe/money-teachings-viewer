# They're Lying to You About the American Dream: How to Build Wealth Without Losing Your Soul

## Overview

This Money Teaching challenges the traditional narrative of the American Dream by showing how true wealth comes from building systems, protecting relationships, and maintaining integrity - not just chasing money. Abdullah "AK" Cudra, an ER doctor with 13 businesses, explains that money amplifies who you already are, and that the path to real success requires being kind but firm, strategic in partnerships, and focused on leaving institutions that outlast you.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you feel pressure to conform to traditional definitions of success
- When entering partnerships or business relationships
- When you want to build wealth without sacrificing your values
- When the user asks how to protect themselves from bad partnerships or corruption

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Systems, Not Just Income

Real wealth comes from creating institutions and organizations that continue to generate value even when you are not there. As one person, you can only do so much. But if you build a system, you can serve many and leave a legacy. Focus on scalable models rather than trading time for money.

### Step 2: Be Kind But Firm

There is a difference between being kind and being "nice." You can be kind to people without being Mr. Nice Guy and allowing them to take advantage of you. In business, you must be willing to put your foot down, create boundaries, and make decisions that are best for the company even when they are unpopular.

### Step Step 3: Vet Partners Like You Vet a Business Deal

Never enter a 50/50 partnership without a clear charter and roadmap. Define exactly what each person is responsible for and what happens if they do not deliver. Look at their history - if they had toxic relationships before, they may bring that pattern into your partnership. Show me your friends and I will tell you who you are.

### Step 4: Protect Against Corruption

Money amplifies who you are. If you are a good person, money will make you more generous. If you are corruptible, money will accelerate your decline. Have a healthy fear of corruption and check yourself as you gain success. Easy come, easy go - the curse of the lottery is real.

### Step 5: Eliminate Nepotism

Never bring family or friends into your company if they are not qualified for the role. If you want to give them money, write them a check. But do not put them in a position they are not ready for. Spoiling people creates bad situations just like spoiling a pet. Leaders must be willing to make hard decisions even with family.

### Step 6: Create Strategic Distance

Employees are not friends. If you blur those lines, they will walk all over you. Maintain strategic distance for the good of the company. You must wear different hats and not blend certain things. The person who is your friend should not also be the employee you have to fire.

## Examples

### Example 1: The 50/50 Partnership Mistake

Early in his career, AK made the common mistake of doing a 50/50 partnership with a friend without defining roles. His partner slacked off, but still made 50% of the profits. When it came time to expand to a second location, the friend assumed he was coming along, and AK had to create a new company just to escape the bad deal. The lesson: equity should be tied to actual contribution.

### Example 2: The Amazon Jungle Pivot

While on a mission trip to Uganda and Kenya, AK saw 250-300 patients per day with a team of only nine people. He realized that as one person, he could only do so much. But if he built an institution, he could serve many even after he was gone. That trip changed his entire perspective from being a doctor to being a system builder.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Build systems that outlast you
- ✅ Be kind but not "nice" - enforce boundaries
- ✅ Vet partners by looking at their history and friends
- ✅ Define roles and equity before entering partnerships
- ✅ Keep employees and friends in separate categories
- ✅ Give family money directly, not unearned positions
- ✅ Maintain strategic distance in business relationships
- ❌ Don't do 50/50 partnerships without clear role definitions
- ❌ Don't bring unqualified family into the business
- ❌ Don't let historical relationships blind you to bad behavior
- ❌ Don't create positions people are not ready for

## Keep In Mind

- Money is the great equalizer - when you lack it, it dominates your thoughts; when you have it, you can focus on becoming the person you are meant to be
- The greatest gift money can give you is not luxury - it is the freedom to work on yourself
- Hard times build character - every great person went through difficult times
- There are two types of people: those who think they can and those who think they can't

## Security & Safety Notes

- Contracts are law, not words - what is signed matters more than what was promised verbally
- Don't be afraid to walk away from a wrong partnership within 90 days if it is not reciprocal
- Heavy is the head that wears the crown - leaders must do what is right even when it is hard

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Partnering with friends without defining roles or equity
  **Solution:** Create a full charter and roadmap before signing anything. Tie equity to specific deliverables, not relationships
- **Problem:** Hiring family members who are not qualified
  **Solution:** Write them a check if you want to help them financially, but hire based on merit
- **Problem:** Allowing employees to become too comfortable because they are friends
  **Solution:** Keep strategic distance. You can be kind without being complicit in underperformance
- **Problem:** Believing money will solve all problems
  **Solution:** Money amplifies who you are. Work on your character before you work on your net worth
