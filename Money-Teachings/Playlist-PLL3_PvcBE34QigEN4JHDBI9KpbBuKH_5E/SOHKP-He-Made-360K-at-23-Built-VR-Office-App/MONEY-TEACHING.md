# Money Teaching Title: He Made $360K at 23, Then Built the #1 VR Office App

## Overview

Renji Bijoy earned $360,000 annually at 23 as a lead software architect but felt unsatisfied and left to build Immersed, now the most used AR/VR virtual office app on the planet. This Money Teaching distills how he solved his own problem first, bootstrapped through crowdfunding instead of traditional VC, growth-hacked his way to $10 million in days, and built a product so strong that major tech companies sought him out. Use these teachings when launching a tech product in an unproven market, fundraising without giving away leverage, or scaling a startup with limited resources.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When building a product for a future market before mainstream adoption
- When seeking capital without sacrificing equity or control
- When you need to compete against well-funded incumbents
- When building a team of high-caliber engineers on a lean budget
- When your product is ahead of its time and faces skepticism

## Steps

### Step 1: Solve Your Own Problem First

Build a product you actually need. Renji wanted multiple virtual screens while working remotely, so he bought a $20 Google Cardboard and built a proof of concept for himself. Do not guess what users want if you can solve a pain point you personally experience. This keeps you motivated and ensures you build something real.

### Step 2: Bootstrap With Crowdfunding, Not VC

Traditional VCs take large equity stakes and offer little leverage. Use equity crowdfunding or community rounds instead. Renji raised $2 million in 2 days and $8 million in 2 weeks through crowdfunding, compared to the typical 3 to 6 months for VC. Post the campaign publicly, recruit a "growth squad" of early supporters to amplify the message, and let FOMO bring in additional investors.

### Step 3: Growth-Hack Your Fundraising

Treat your crowdfunding campaign like a product launch. Recruit 40 to 700+ people to comment and share immediately upon launch. Create daily content, run giveaways, and stay on phones helping investors complete transactions. Scenario-plan for low-performing days so you are never caught off guard.

### Step 4: Prioritize Product Over Everything Else

Renji states explicitly that no amount of funding, team, or partnerships can save a bad product. Focus on user experience, onboarding, and feature completeness before seeking scale. Major companies like TikTok and Meta came to Immersed because the product outperformed competitors, not because of hustle.

### Step 5: Hire for Belief, Not Just Salary

Weight compensation toward equity rather than high salaries. This attracts people who believe in your vision and will stay even if a larger company offers more cash. Use equity to build a team that will execute through difficult early years.

### Step 6: Be Open About Your Values

Renji is openly Christian in the tech world and credits his faith with giving him the courage to take bigger risks. Hiding core values creates friction later. Being transparent from day one filters out people who would oppose your values and attracts those who respect them.

### Step 7: Plan for the Long Haul

Renji advises that most startups fail and only a tiny fraction become enduring companies. Accept that building something real takes longer than expected. Use your current job to save aggressively, learn adjacent skills, and align incentives toward your eventual launch.

## Examples

### Example 1: Pre-Seed Crowdfunding for a Hardware Startup

You have a prototype for a smart home device but no manufacturer relationships. Instead of pitching VCs who will own 20-30% of your company, run a Wefunder or StartEngine campaign. Recruit 200 beta users to invest $1,000 each to raise $200,000. Use their network to attract press and additional investors. This gives you capital and proof of demand without giving up board seats or control.

### Example 2: Growth-Hacking a B2B SaaS Launch

You are launching a project management tool. Build a private Slack community of 100 founders before launch. Have them post about your tool on launch day. Offer early adopters lifetime discounts in exchange for testimonials. This creates the same chain-reaction effect Renji used to turn $2 million into $8 million in two weeks.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Solve a problem you personally experience before building for others
- ✅ Use crowdfunding to maintain leverage and control
- ✅ Growth-hack your crowdfunding campaign with a pre-recruited squad
- ✅ Prioritize product-market fit above all other concerns
- ✅ Weight compensation toward equity to attract believers
- ✅ Be transparent about your values and faith from day one
- ✅ Plan for a longer timeline than you expect
- ❌ Do not rely on VC money if you can avoid it
- ❌ Do not outsource learning about your customer
- ❌ Do not hire for salary alone; hire for belief
- ❌ Do not hide your values to appeal to everyone

## Keep In Mind

- Renji was 23 when he earned $360K and still felt unsatisfied; money alone is not the goal
- The VR market took 3 years to gain traction; patience is required when building ahead of the curve
- Immersed was rejected by the Oculus store initially because it was not a game; persistence opens doors
- Intel CEO Pat Gelsinger gave Renji time despite being 1/1000th of his company size; character builds lasting relationships
- Being a solo founder is extremely difficult but possible with the right accelerator support

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not commingle business and personal funds; maintain separate accounts
- When raising crowdfunding, use reputable platforms and understand securities laws
- Protect your product formula and IP from manufacturers by keeping production in-house or with trusted partners
- Vet investors carefully; some angels and VCs will push for unfair equity terms

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Running out of cash before reaching product-market fit
  **Solution:** Bootstrap longer by crowdfunding and keeping salaries lean
- **Problem:** Hiring people who are only in it for salary
  **Solution:** Weight compensation toward equity and screen for belief in the vision
- **Problem:** Building a product no one wants
  **Solution:** Start with your own pain point and validate with real users before scaling
- **Problem:** Giving away too much equity early
  **Solution:** Use crowdfunding to keep more ownership and maintain leverage in future rounds
