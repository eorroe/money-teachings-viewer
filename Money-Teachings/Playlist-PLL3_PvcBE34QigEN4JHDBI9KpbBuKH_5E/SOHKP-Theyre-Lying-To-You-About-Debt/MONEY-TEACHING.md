# How to Get Rich Using Good Debt Cash Flow and Real Assets

## Overview

Robert Kiyosaki, author of Rich Dad Poor Dad, teaches that the system is designed to keep the middle class poor through bad debt, fake fiat currency, and schoolteachers who do not practice what they preach. He became wealthy by choosing his teachers wisely, using debt to buy cash-flowing real estate, and operating as a team with attorneys and accountants. This Money Teaching gives you the exact framework to shift from a poor-dad mindset—work hard, save money, get out of debt—to a rich-dad mindset that uses leverage, real assets, and perpetual education.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When evaluating whether a house is an asset or a liability
- When deciding how to use debt to build wealth instead of destroy it
- When choosing teachers, mentors, or financial advisors
- When the user asks why working hard and saving money does not make people rich

## Steps

### Step 1: Choose Your Teachers Wisely

Your brain is your greatest asset, and whatever you put into it from ages zero to eighteen determines who you become. If your parents, teachers, and financial advisors are employees who need job security and pensions, they will program you for a life of trading time for money. Kiyosaki's rich dad taught him to buy assets. His poor dad—a PhD and head of education for Hawaii—told him to get a job, save money, and get out of debt. Choose teachers who have the result you want, not people who are giving advice from a position of security.

### Step 2: Understand That a House Is Not an Asset Unless It Pays You

An asset puts money in your pocket. A liability takes money out. If you live in a house and pay a mortgage, property taxes, insurance, and maintenance every month, it is a liability—not an asset—even if it appreciates in value. Kiyosaki owns 15,000 rental properties that pay him approximately $200 per unit per month. That is $3 million per month in cash flow from real assets. Buy properties for cash flow, not for capital gains, and use the 1031 tax-deferred exchange to compound without paying taxes on every sale.

### Step 3: Use Good Debt to Buy Income-Producing Assets

After 1971, when Nixon took the US dollar off the gold standard, the dollar became fiat currency created from debt. Savers are losers because they save fake money that loses purchasing power. The rich do not work for money, save money, or get out of debt. They use debt—leveraged through real estate—to buy assets that pay them monthly cash flow. Learn how to talk to bankers about investment loans, raise capital from partners, and structure deals where the asset pays the debt service for you.

### Step 4: Learn to Sell and Raise Capital

Kiyosaki says the four essential skills for entrepreneurs are: sell, use debt, raise capital, and maintain integrity under pressure. He nearly got fired from Xerox after three months because he made zero sales. His rich dad told him to step up his failure rate. He went to a nonprofit and made 30 cold calls per night until he could not fail. His Xerox sales then skyrocketed. If you are afraid of failing, you will never be successful. Fail fast, learn faster.

### Step 5: Operate as a Team Sport

Kiyosaki does not play soccer—he plays rugby. Find the players for your sport and do not let soccer players tell you how to play rugby. Hire the smartest attorneys, accountants, and partners you can find and operate as a team. He studies with his team every Thursday morning on a different subject. Most professors teach things they learned 50 years ago and have never practiced. Surround yourself with practitioners who are still in the game.

## Examples

### Example 1: Buying 15,000 Rental Properties Through Cash Flow

Kiyosaki's formula is simple: buy properties that generate positive monthly cash flow after all expenses, including debt service. At 15,000 units averaging $200 per month, he earns $3 million per month without lifting a finger. He does not sell properties for capital gains; he holds, collects, and uses 1031 exchanges to roll proceeds into larger assets tax-free.

### Example 2: Turning Failure Into a $100,000 Lesson

Kiyosaki's first nylon wallet business was going broke. He needed $100,000 to survive. His rich dad told him to get the hell out of the office. His poor dad—a socialist who mortgaged his own house—gave him the $100,000. The accountant stole the money and ran. Kiyosaki had to save his father's house from foreclosure. That hard knock taught him to never trust an accountant or attorney blindly and to verify every professional before hiring them.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Never blindly trust an attorney or accountant because they have a license. Kiyosaki lost $30 million to a dishonest attorney. Verify integrity through referrals and track records.
- Do not falsify government records. Kiyosaki learned the hard way that lying to prosecutors ends badly. Tell the truth even when it costs you.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Believing a primary residence is your biggest asset.
  **Solution:** Calculate your monthly cash flow from every property. If your house costs you money every month, it is a liability. Rent it out or buy a different property that pays you.
- **Problem:** Listening to schoolteachers or parents who have never built wealth about how to build wealth.
  **Solution:** Your poor dad's advice—go to school, get a job, save money, get out of debt, diversify—is a prescription for middle-class stagnation. Find rich mentors who have built the life you want.
- **Problem:** Being afraid to fail so you never start.
  **Solution:** Step up your failure rate. If you make ten sales calls per day and close zero, make 30 per day. Volume negates luck. The math will eventually work if you show up enough times.
