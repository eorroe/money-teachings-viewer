# How to Master the Art of Pitching and Selling Anything

## Overview

Most people over-talk the sale and fail to create desire before presenting the solution. Forbes Riley, the pitch queen who generated $1.2 million in a single day, reveals that people hate to be sold to but love to buy. This teaching breaks down her proven infomercial pitch formula, mindset shifts, and relationship strategies that transform anyone into a persuasive communicator.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you have a product or service but struggle to get leads and close sales
- When preparing for a live pitch, webinar, or one-on-one sales conversation
- When networking and introducing yourself to high-value contacts
- When you need to reframe how you communicate value without sounding desperate

## Steps

### Step 1: Stop Telling People What They Need—Make Them Want What You Have

The biggest mistake sellers make is explaining the product before the prospect cares. Forbes Riley says most people talk past the sale. Instead, set up the problem first. Remind them of a pain point they already have. Build curiosity. Create want. Only then reveal the solution.

### Step 2: Use the Four-Part Pitch Formula

Forbes Riley's formula works on every product every time. First, get them to want what you have by setting up a relatable problem. Second, use third-party social proof—let someone else edify the product. Third, overdeliver on the offer (two for the price of one). Fourth, create urgency and scarcity so they have no choice but to act now.

### Step 3: Master Assumptions and Read Your Audience

Before you speak, make assumptions about who is listening. At 9 a.m. on a Tuesday, your audience is tired and rushing. At 2 a.m., they are lonely and browsing. Tailor your pitch to meet them exactly where they are. The worst approach is to talk only about yourself and ignore who is on the other side.

### Step 4: Reframe Your Identity From What You Do to What You Can Do For Them

Nobody cares what you do when they ask. They care what you can do for them. Instead of saying "I run a media company," say "I can give you access to the richest people in the world and help level up what you are doing." This turns any conversation into a value exchange.

### Step 5: Remember Names Using the Rule of Three

Create a naming convention for every person you meet. Link their name to something you already know. "Jack, as in my mom's name was Jackie." Say it three times in your head. Remembering someone's name makes them feel seen, special, and valued—and it costs you nothing.

### Step 6: Turn Problems Into Solutions

You do not really have a product or service. You have a solution to a problem someone else has. If you sell dog walking, the problem is not dogs—it is time, trust, and energy. Frame every offer around the problem it solves, not the features it offers.

## Examples

### Example 1: Selling 2.8 Million Units of a Fitness Product

Forbes Riley created SpinJim, a fitness tool, and sold 2.8 million units using pure pitch psychology. She never led with product features. She asked "Do you want to see something cool?" embedded a command, got a yes, and let the prospect experience the value. By the time she explained what it was, they already wanted it.

### Example 2: Generating $1.2 Million in One Day

Forbes Riley launched a webinar and sold $1.2 million in 24 hours. She did it by packaging her lifetime pitch knowledge into a system, recruiting her daughter to help structure the offer, and selling to a warm audience that already knew her. The key was not the product—it was the delivery of a proven formula.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Set up the problem before showing the product
- ✅ Build curiosity and want before revealing the solution
- ✅ Use third-party validation instead of talking about yourself
- ✅ Create urgency and scarcity naturally
- ✅ Assume your audience's situation and speak to it directly
- ✅ Reframe every answer around what you can do for the other person
- ✅ Remember names using the rule of three
- ❌ Do not over-explain or over-talk the sale
- ❌ Do not lead with product features before creating desire
- ❌ Do not make the conversation about you
- ❌ Do not skip assumptions about your audience

## Keep In Mind

- People hate to be sold to, but they love to buy
- The most valuable piece of real estate is the graveyard—billion-dollar ideas die there because people never took action
- Free has huge value if you put value in free
- If you have a product or service and are not getting leads, you are over-talking

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not use high-pressure tactics or false scarcity to manipulate buyers
- Deliver on every promise you make during a pitch
- Protect customer data and payment information with the same rigor you apply to closing deals

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Talking past the sale and over-explaining the product.
  **Solution:** Ask questions, set up the problem, build want, then reveal the solution. Stop talking once they say yes.
- **Problem:** Making the pitch all about you and your credentials.
  **Solution:** Nobody cares what you do. Reframe every statement around what you can do for them.
- **Problem:** Skipping assumptions and delivering a generic pitch.
  **Solution:** Know your audience's situation before you speak. Meet them where they are, not where you are.
