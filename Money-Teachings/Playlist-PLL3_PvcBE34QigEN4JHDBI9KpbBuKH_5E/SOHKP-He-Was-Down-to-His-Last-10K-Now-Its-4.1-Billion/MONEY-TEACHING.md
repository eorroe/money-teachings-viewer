# How to Build a Billion-Pound Business From Scratch

## Overview

Richard Harpin built HomeServe from a failed plumbing business into a company that sold for 4.1 billion pounds, taking investment only twice. He discovered at age four that he wanted to be an entrepreneur, then spent decades mastering the art of copying what works, bootstrapping until proven, and scaling with local teams. His nine-step blueprint is a masterclass in disciplined, long-term wealth creation.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When building a service-based business from the ground up
- When deciding whether to bootstrap or raise capital
- When expanding a proven business model into new countries
- When seeking a mentor or business coach to accelerate growth
- When you need to regain focus amid too many ideas

## Steps

### Step 1: Copy and Pivot—Find What Works and Make It Slightly Better

Do not reinvent the wheel. Richard Harpin says if somebody is already doing it, it works. Copy their model, improve it, and think bigger. He copied a plumbing insurance model, added blocked drain cover and internal plumbing emergency cover, and scaled it into a billion-pound business. Step one to building a billion-pound business is copying and pivoting.

### Step 2: Bootstrap Until Your Model Is Proven

Bring on investors only when you are ready to scale, not when you are trying to prove your model. Richard put 50,000 pounds of his own money into his first business. When he brought in an investor too early, he gave away 52 percent of the equity for half a million pounds and still ran out of money. Bootstrap first. Prove the model. Then take capital.

### Step 3: Get a Coach and Mentor With Gray Hairs

Every successful person has had many mentors. Richard sought out Nigel Morris, co-founder of Capital One, who gave him two pieces of advice that transformed HomeServe America: move from Miami to the East Coast, and hire an American CEO because Americans buy from Americans. Find someone who has made the mistakes you are about to make.

### Step 4: Combine Bricks and Clicks With Paper

Direct mail still works because inboxes are overcrowded with email. Richard combined physical presence with online marketing and mail shots. A 3.8 percent take-up from a direct mail shot to 1,000 customers saved his business from bankruptcy. Combine old-school outreach with modern digital channels.

### Step 5: Hire Your Replacement

Entrepreneurs have too many ideas and need to focus. Hire a proven chief executive officer to run the business, then step up to work on the business rather than in it. Richard hired Tom Rusin to run HomeServe America, and 16 years later the business was making 300 million dollars in annual profit instead of 10 million.

### Step 6: Go Global With Locals

When expanding to a new country, keep your business model within 10 percent of the original. Do not change it by more than 10 percent or you will create complexity and disaster. Hire locals to run the business because people buy from people who look and sound like them.

### Step 7: Choose Evolution Over Revolution

Businesses that do not keep evolving die. Blockbuster Video and BlackBerry refused to adapt. Richard says you must keep your model evolving or you will die. Small, continuous improvements beat radical reinvention every time.

### Step 8: Maintain a Not-To-Do List

A not-to-do list is more important than a to-do list. Entrepreneurs have too many ideas and need to focus on executing one thing really well. Richard learned this the hard way. If an opportunity does not fit your core strategy, it goes on the not-to-do list.

### Step 9: Hone Your Character

Focus on your strengths and hire people to do the things you are not good at. Richard used to conduct performance reviews and discovered he was bad at most things and only good at a few. Master your character, know your strengths, and surround yourself with people who complement your weaknesses.

## Examples

### Example 1: From Bailiffs to 4.1 Billion Pounds

Richard Harpin and his business partner put 50,000 pounds into A1 Fast Fix, an emergency plumbing business. Six months later, bailiffs arrived to take their furniture and computers. A friend lent them 10,000 pounds at 20 percent interest instead of taking 10 percent equity. Richard paid back the loan with 3,000 pounds profit. If the friend had taken the equity, it would have been worth 400 million pounds when HomeServe sold. Richard pivoted, copied a better model, and built a 4.1 billion-pound empire.

### Example 2: The Hedgehog Strategy

Richard trained with Jim Collins and adopted the hedgehog strategy: answer three questions in one sentence of no more than 20 words. What are you passionate about? What can you be the best at? How will you make money? At Business Leader, his strategy is "We inspire our founder and CEO members to fast forward their growth through new unique content and facilitated learning." Every decision that does not fit that sentence goes on the not-to-do list.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Copy what works, then improve it slightly
- ✅ Bootstrap until your model is proven before raising capital
- ✅ Get a mentor who has made the mistakes you will make
- ✅ Combine direct mail with online marketing
- ✅ Hire a proven CEO and step back to work on the business
- ✅ Expand globally with local teams and minimal model changes
- ✅ Keep evolving your business model continuously
- ✅ Maintain a not-to-do list to protect your focus
- ✅ Focus on your strengths and hire for your weaknesses
- ❌ Do not take investment before proving your business model
- ❌ Do not give away majority equity early
- ❌ Do not reinvent the wheel when a working model already exists
- ❌ Do not expand internationally without testing in a smaller market first

## Keep In Mind

- 97 percent of businesses set up in the UK fail within 5 years—buying a proven small business is less risky than starting from zero
- It is easier to buy revenue than to create revenue
- Americans buy from Americans—hire locals when going global
- If you need to change your business model by more than 10 percent for a new country, do not go there
- The best time to take capital off the table is when your business is profitable and proven

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not overextend financially by taking on too much debt or equity too early
- Protect your business model and competitive advantages before expanding
- Verify any advisor or mentor has real experience before taking their capital or counsel

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Taking investment before proving the business model.
  **Solution:** Bootstrap until you have proof. Richard lost control and nearly lost his business by raising too early.
- **Problem:** Expanding globally with a model that is too different from the original.
  **Solution:** Keep the model within 10 percent of the original. Test in a smaller country before going to America.
- **Problem:** Trying to do everything yourself without a team.
  **Solution:** Hire a proven CEO, get a mentor, and focus on the highest-leverage activities only.
