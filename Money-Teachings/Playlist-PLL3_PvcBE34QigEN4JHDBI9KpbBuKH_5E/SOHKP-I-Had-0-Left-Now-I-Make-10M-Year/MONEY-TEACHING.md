# Money Teaching Title: I Had $0 Left, Now I Make $10M/Year

## Overview

Chris Waters went from $0 after a failed bar investment to building a real estate empire generating hundreds of millions of dollars. This teaching outlines the three essential skill dominoes for entrepreneurs—inside sales, outside sales, and subject matter expertise—and the power of coaches, humility, and resourcefulness over resources.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When rebuilding after a major business failure or rock bottom moment
- When entering real estate or any sales-driven industry
- When you lack capital but need to generate revenue quickly
- When learning to master phone sales, face-to-face selling, and industry expertise
- When choosing between staying an employee and becoming an entrepreneur

## Steps

### Step 1: Knock Down the First Domino: Inside Sales

Learn to sell over the phone. Book appointments from cold calls. This is the foundational skill every entrepreneur must master. Study tonality, cadence, objection handling, and empathy. You can hire someone to do this later, but you must understand it deeply first.

### Step 2: Knock Down the Second Domino: Outside Sales

Convert complete strangers into clients face to face. Build rapport, establish yourself as an expert, differentiate your value, and close the deal. In real estate, this means mastering the art of the listing appointment and the buyer consultation.

### Step 3: Knock Down the Third Domino: Subject Matter Expertise

Become the authority in your field. Contrary to popular belief, you do not need expertise before selling; you develop expertise by doing deals. The more transactions you complete, the more credibility you earn. Knowledge compounds with experience.

### Step 4: Hire a Coach and Stay Coachable

Chris hired a sales coach after six months of terrible results. His weekly output jumped 300-400 percent. Coaches provide accountability, sharpen skills, and shorten the learning curve. Surround yourself with people who will tell you the truth, not just yes-men.

### Step 5: Stay Humble and Resourceful

After his bar-restaurant failed, Chris was broke, owing money to everyone. Humility forced him to learn from others who had already succeeded. He read books like The Millionaire Real Estate Agent, modeled successful people, and avoided the ego that had caused his earlier failure.

### Step 6: Test Small, Scale What Works

Chris advises testing ideas on a small scale before betting big. Do not spend half a million or a million dollars testing an untested concept. Develop an MVP—minimum viable product—and validate demand before expanding. Resourcefulness matters more than resources.

### Step 7: Build an Army of Specialists

As the company grows, hire specialists, not generalists. The top Inc 500 companies succeed because every person focuses on one thing they do exceptionally well. Let inside sales handle appointments, outside sales handle closings, and operations handle logistics.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Bar and Restaurant Failure

Chris invested all his savings into a bar and restaurant in 2009. Nine months later, it was padlocked. He had less than $10 in his account. That failure taught him to respect risk, stay focused, and avoid emotional decisions. It also gave him the hunger to never be in that position again.

### Example 2: From Cold Calls to Hundreds of Millions

Chris started in real estate with no leads, no software, and no money. He cold-called 7 days a week, 30-40 hours per week, and booked approximately one appointment a week. After coaching, he booked 4-5 appointments weekly. He hired an inside sales team to take over prospecting, freeing him to close deals and scale.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Master phone sales before anything else
- ✅ Get a coach to accelerate your learning curve
- ✅ Stay humble enough to model people who have already solved your problem
- ✅ Test ideas on a small scale before investing heavily
- ✅ Build a team of specialists rather than expecting one person to do everything

## Keep In Mind

- Rock bottom is frequently the foundation for massive success
- You do not need money to start; you need resourcefulness
- The corporate ladder exists in entrepreneurship too—it is skills-based
- Forward progress in career, fitness, and relationships creates happiness

## Security & Safety Notes

- Keep business and personal finances completely separate
- Do not commingle funds or use business accounts for personal expenses
- Hire financial advisors and CFO support as you scale to avoid cash flow surprises

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Getting comfortable after early success and losing edge
  **Solution:** Set new goals immediately after hitting old ones. Avoid the Audi R8 moment—treat comfort as a warning sign, not a destination
- **Problem:** Spreading yourself too thin across multiple ventures
  **Solution:** Focus on your core business. Diversification is for later, after mastery
- **Problem:** Thinking you need more resources to start
  **Solution:** Resourcefulness beats resources every time. Start with door hangers, phone calls, and sweat equity
