# How To Build A Nine-Figure Brand By Leveraging Disproportionate Skills And Multiple Sales Channels

## Overview

This teaching reveals the exact framework for scaling a business from seven to nine figures, with emphasis on assembling elite talent, mastering multiple distribution channels, and building a brand that can compete at the highest retail levels. It shows why entrepreneurship is a marathon requiring continuous reinvention, and how to survive setbacks by treating every challenge as a new game to win.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When scaling a business from seven to eight or nine figures
- When hiring and retaining top-tier talent in a competitive market
- When expanding into retail and wholesale distribution channels
- When facing doubters, naysayers, or business stagnation
- When building a personal brand that outlasts any single product

## Steps

### Step 1: Build A Seven-Figure Business On A Disproportionate Skill

Identify a skill where you are significantly stronger than the average competitor and use it to build initial revenue. Self-taught web design, SEO, media buying, and editing gave Josh Snow a compounding advantage. Master one core competency deeply before expanding, because that skill becomes the foundation every other part of the business relies on.

### Step 2: Scale To Eight Figures By Recruiting A-Team Players

Eight-figure growth requires recruiting people who are better than you in their respective domains. Use LinkedIn, spy tools, and direct outreach to find top performers at competing brands. Offer paid consultations to extract insights, then recruit the best ones. Triple-train your people so they can adapt during contractions, and move them to higher-value work by outsourcing lower-level tasks.

### Step 3: Reach Nine Figures By Diversifying Sales Channels

Nine figures requires multiple scalable sales channels. Start with the digital channel you know best, then add retail, wholesale, TikTok, YouTube, SEO, and other platforms. Test each channel for profitability and scalability before fully committing. In consumer products, retail is unavoidable because people buy toothpaste when they buy toilet paper—meet customers where they already are.

### Step 4: Protect Brand Equity By Growing At The Right Pace

Sacrifice short-term revenue to protect long-term brand reputation. If you expand into a new channel before you have the right team to support it, you will deliver poor results and damage your brand. It is better to grow slower and get client results than to scale fast and burn the reputation it took years to build.

### Step 5: Handle Naysayers By Building Internal Validation

Ignore external doubters and build a community that supports your vision. Use negative feedback as fuel or simply remove it from your environment. Surround yourself with high-performing friends and mentors who understand the entrepreneurial journey. Your inner circle should act like antivirus software—remove viruses, malware, and spyware that slow down your mental processing.

### Step 6: Fall In Love With New Challenges To Avoid Burnout

Entrepreneurship is a series of games. When one challenge becomes routine, find a new one to unlock. Whether it is breaking into retail, launching a new product line, or acquiring a business, keep the journey interesting by constantly upgrading your character and seeking bigger puzzles to solve.

### Step 7: Take Care Of Your Mental And Physical Health Along The Way

Build a supportive community, ask for help when needed, and prioritize mental health. Failure and setbacks are inevitable, but burnout destroys more businesses than market conditions. Give yourself grace, recognize that recessions last one to four years, and remember that you are running a marathon, not a sprint.

## Examples

### Example 1: Recruiting Top Talent With LinkedIn Outreach

To build the TikTok team for Snow, the founder searched for brands crushing it on TikTok, found their employees on LinkedIn, used contact tools to get their phone numbers, and texted them directly with a personalized compliment and a paid consultation offer. This non-intrusive, respectful approach fills the pipeline with high-quality candidates who feel recognized rather than spammed.

### Example 2: Bootstrapping To $100M Without Outside Capital

Josh Snow built Snow to over $100 million in revenue without taking outside capital. He reinvested profits, controlled the brand vision completely, and avoided the pressure of investor timelines. This gave him the freedom to make slow, deliberate moves that protected brand equity rather than chasing growth at any cost.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Find a skill where you are disproportionately better than competitors and build from there
- ✅ Recruit people stronger than you in your weaknesses and build an executive team
- ✅ Add sales channels one at a time, testing profitability and scalability before scaling
- ✅ Protect brand equity by refusing to grow faster than your team and infrastructure can support
- ✅ Remove negative influences from your environment immediately
- ✅ Fall in love with new challenges regularly to avoid burnout
- ✅ Prioritize mental health and build a supportive community of peers
- ❌ Don't hire people with the same skill sets as your business partner—complementary strengths are essential
- ❌ Don't scale into retail or new channels before hiring the right people to support them
- ❌ Don't let external validation become your primary source of self-worth
- ❌ Don't micromanage high performers—give them autonomy and get out of their way
- ❌ Don't neglect your health while building the business—you need to survive the marathon

## Keep In Mind

- It takes the same amount of time to build a $1 million business as a $100 million business; the difference is timing, addressable market, and the product or service chosen
- The cream rises to the top: consistent, professional creators and team members will be recognized and recruited regardless of platform changes
- Being the smartest person in the room is the wrong room to be in—listen twice as much as you talk
- Every business has a shelf life; recognize when it is time to pivot or sell before the decline drains your energy

## Security & Safety Notes

- Do not share proprietary business metrics or financials publicly, as competitors can use them against you
- Vet influencers and partners thoroughly before entering long-term brand deals
- Maintain control of core brand assets such as trademarks, social media handles, and customer data

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Hiring friends or people with the same strengths as you creates blind spots
  **Solution:** Seek partners and team members whose skills complement yours, not duplicate them
- **Problem:** Micromanaging high performers drives them away
  **Solution:** Move to higher-value work yourself and trust your team to deliver outcomes
- **Problem:** Brand damage from scaling too fast into channels you cannot support
  **Solution:** Hire the right people first, then expand—protect brand equity over short-term revenue
- **Problem:** Burnout from working on a business you no longer love
  **Solution:** Regularly assess whether you are falling in love with the challenge or just going through the motions; be willing to pivot or sell
