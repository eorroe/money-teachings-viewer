## Steps

### Step 1: Transition from LLC to S-Corp

If you are operating as an LLC and taking significant income, transitioning to an S-Corp is often the first move that saves money. This entity change allows you to split income into salary and distributions, potentially reducing self-employment taxes on the distribution portion.

### Step 2: Use Real Estate to Offset W2 or Business Income

Real estate is both an investment and a business. If you own rental properties or flip houses, you can use losses and depreciation to offset income from your W2 job or active business. This is the loophole that many high earners use to wipe out their tax bills.

### Step 3: Leverage Depreciation Strategies

Depreciation allows you to deduct the cost of your real estate over time, creating paper losses that reduce your taxable income. Learn the depreciation rules for residential and commercial property, and work with a tax professional who understands how to maximize these deductions.

### Step 4: Implement Income Shifting Strategies

Income shifting involves moving income from high-tax situations to lower-tax situations. This includes timing income and expenses, using retirement accounts, or structuring your businesses in ways that legally reduce your taxable income each year.

### Step 5: Read the Tax Code Daily

Karlton reads the tax code almost every single day. The tax code is full of legitimate deductions and strategies that most people never discover because they do not study it. Commit to learning the rules so you can operate within them to your advantage.

### Step 6: Start Tax Planning at the Beginning of the Year

Do not wait until tax season to figure out your strategy. Some of the moves that reduce your tax bill require you to be active in real estate throughout the year. Plan your acquisitions, improvements, and sales with tax implications in mind from January 1.

### Step 7: Work With a Tax Strategist, Not Just a Tax Preparer

A tax preparer files your returns after the year is over. A tax strategist helps you plan throughout the year to legally minimize what you owe. Find someone who specializes in real estate and business owners, not just individual W2 employees.

## Keep In Mind

- The tax code is written for people who read it. Most people overpay because they do not know the rules.
- These strategies require active involvement in real estate. They are not passive, and they are not a "set it and forget it" solution.
- Karlton lives in California and still pays state taxes. $0 federal tax is achievable, but state taxes may still apply depending on where you live.
- Tax laws change. Strategies that work today may need to be adjusted when laws change. Stay informed.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always work with a qualified tax professional who understands your specific situation. Do not implement tax strategies based solely on what you hear on a podcast.
- Aggressive tax avoidance can cross into illegal tax evasion. The line is thin. Make sure every strategy you use is clearly supported by the tax code.
- Keep all documentation and receipts. If you are ever audited, you need to prove that your deductions are legitimate.
- Do not mix personal and business expenses. This is one of the fastest ways to lose an audit and face penalties.
