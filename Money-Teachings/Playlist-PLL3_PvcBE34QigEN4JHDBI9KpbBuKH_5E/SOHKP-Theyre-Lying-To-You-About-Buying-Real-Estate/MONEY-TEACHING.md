# How to Buy Real Estate Creative Finance No Bank No Credit

## Overview

Pace Morby went from contractor to nine-figure real estate entrepreneur with half a billion dollars in property and 2,000 doors by mastering creative finance and seller financing. He teaches that buying real estate the conventional way—through banks, agents, and large down payments—is a trap, and that you can acquire properties with no money down and no credit by solving the seller's actual problem. This Money Teaching breaks down his exact subject-to and wholesaling framework so you can build a real estate business without traditional financing.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When a motivated seller needs to sell a property but cannot do so through the MLS
- When you want to invest in real estate without a bank loan or credit check
- When you are deciding which exit strategy to apply to a property
- When the user asks how to wholesale, assign contracts, or use seller financing

## Steps

### Step 1: Find the Seller's "Bunnies"

Every seller has an emotional or logistical problem they are not stating on the MLS. Your job is to uncover it. Pace's first deal closed because he asked a retiring school teacher if he could help with anything, discovered she could not rehome three giant Flemish rabbits, and arranged for his mother to take them. The seller then trusted him with the house when no other investor would. Always ask: "What is your biggest problem with this property right now?"

### Step 2: Offer Four Terms Before Mentioning Price

Instead of leading with a price, structure a creative finance deal using four terms: price, down payment, interest rate, and length of time. For example, offer $600 for a phone with a $50 down payment, zero percent interest, and 12 monthly payments of $50. Apply this same structure to real estate by offering to take over the seller's existing payments, pay them a premium, and give them peace of mind. The seller keeps the house as collateral if you default, so they are secured.

### Step 3: Use Subject-To and Wholesaling as Entry Strategies

Subject-to means you take over the seller's existing mortgage payments without qualifying for a new loan. Wholesaling means you get the house under contract at a discount and assign that contract to another buyer for a fee—no money, no credit, no risk required. Pace's first deal earned him $25,000 for seven hours of work simply by assigning a contract he never had to fund. Learn both models and apply whichever fits the seller's situation.

### Step 4: Evaluate Exit Strategies Before Committing

Before you commit to a property, run every possible exit strategy through rentometer.com, AirDNA, and local Section 8 rates. Calculate whether short-term rental, mid-term rental, Section 8, sober living, assisted living, or traditional rental income covers the payment you are taking on. If the numbers do not work on at least one strategy, do not buy the property. Know your exit before you sign.

### Step 5: Build a Personal Brand and Network Simultaneously

Pace attributes his nine-figure success to running multiple revenue streams while always building a personal brand. He posts daily YouTube content, spends $60,000 per month on distribution, and treats his YouTube channel as the modern storefront sign—if nobody knows you exist, nobody will invest with you. Simultaneously, audit your five closest friends. Their average income predicts your own. Surround yourself with people playing the game at a higher level than you.

## Examples

### Example 1: Wholesaling a House With No Money Down

Pace sent $5,000 in direct-mail postcards, got a lead from a retiring teacher, could not meet her price, but arranged for his mother to take three rabbits off her hands. The seller trusted him and accepted his lower offer two weeks later. Bethany, his mentor, bought the contract from him for $25,000 more than he paid the seller. Pace walked away with $25,000 profit and never had to fund the purchase.

### Example 2: Buying an RV Park With 0% Interest Seller Financing

Pace bought an RV park next to Glacier National Park for $5 million without bringing money to the table. The seller faced $400,000 in capital gains tax and real estate commissions. Pace offered 0% interest, a 10-year payback, and showed the seller how to avoid all tax liabilities. The seller earned an extra $500,000 over time and retired stress-free. Pace now controls a cash-flowing asset with no bank involvement.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always find the seller's bunnies before pitching a deal
- ✅ Structure four terms—price, down payment, interest rate, length—before agreeing to anything
- ✅ Run exit strategy numbers before you commit to a property
- ✅ Build a personal brand as aggressively as you build your deal pipeline
- ✅ Audit your five closest friends and replace anyone not playing at your target level
- ❌ Do not buy a property until you know which exit strategy covers the payment
- ❌ Do not go to a bank when you can negotiate directly with the seller
- ❌ Do not focus on only one revenue stream while waiting on projects

## Keep In Mind

- Real estate values have never declined over the full history of the world. Even during the 2008 crash, investors who held and rented recovered and surpassed previous highs.
- Your identity as a contractor or employee will sabotage your real estate investing. Pace took four years to quit construction because he had to dismantle his old identity and rebuild a new one.
- 96% of entrepreneurs never reach one million dollars in annual revenue because they refuse to get a polar-opposite partner and they hire poorly.
- Seasons matter. When your business is not producing, dig your roots deeper—study your industry, hang out with competitors, and wait for the next harvest. Do not mistake an offseason for failure.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always verify the seller's ownership and lien status before signing anything. Use a title company or attorney to protect both parties.
- Never tell a seller to stop making payments to their bank without ensuring the deed is transferred and the transaction is recorded properly.
- Keep every agreement in writing. Verbal promises in real estate lead to lawsuits.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Buying a property because it looks like a good deal without verifying exit strategy income.
  **Solution:** Run every exit strategy through rentometer, AirDNA, and local market data before signing. If no strategy covers the payment, walk away.
- **Problem:** Going to a bank for financing because you think that is the only way.
  **Solution:** Go directly to the seller. Offer to take over payments, provide them with security through the property collateral, and structure four clear terms. Banks add unnecessary friction and credit requirements.
- **Problem:** Letting the wrong people influence your ambition.
  **Solution:** Take an audit of your five closest friends. Add up their incomes and divide by five. That number is likely your current income level. If they are not playing at your target level, you must upgrade your circle.
