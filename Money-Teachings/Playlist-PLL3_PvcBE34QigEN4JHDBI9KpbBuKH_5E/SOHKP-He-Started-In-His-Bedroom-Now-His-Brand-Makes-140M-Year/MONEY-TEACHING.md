# Money Teaching: He Started In His Bedroom Now His Brand Makes 140M Year

## Overview

George Heaton started the Represent streetwear brand from his bedroom, living on zero salary for the first four years, while his brother Mike joined him in grinding through nights and mornings to fulfill orders. After a multi-million dollar trademark dispute forced the brand underground for two years, he emerged with renewed energy and grew the company from $8M to $140M in revenue. This Money Teaching covers his principles for starting with no salary and reinvesting everything, owning your intellectual property from day one, building a brand identity that is bigger than any single founder, launching product lines based on genuine personal use and need, creating hero products that break through the $30M revenue plateau, and building a team with a "hire fast, fire faster" mentality.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When starting a brand or product company with limited capital and no financial runway
- When you need to decide whether to pay yourself a salary or reinvest every dollar back into the business
- When launching a brand name that can conflict with existing trademarks or intellectual property
- When you have hit a revenue ceiling (typically around $30M) and need to break through to the next level
- When expanding a brand from a single market into global retail distribution
- When building a fashion or apparel brand that requires global market adaptation

## Steps

### Step 1: Start With No Salary and Reinvest Every Dollar Back Into the Business

For the first four years — 2012 through 2015 — George paid himself nothing. He and his brother Mike fulfilled orders, shipped packages, and ran the brand out of their bedroom, then their parents' shed, then their parents' entire house. The accountant told them if they did not have $100,000 stashed, it was not worth continuing. They had $150,000 in PayPal after two years and decided to go all in. Delayed gratification is the price of admission for building a real brand — live far below your means until you can live far above them.

### Step 2: Own Your Intellectual Property From Day One

The brand name is the most valuable asset you will ever build — protect it before you have proven the concept. George did not trademark "Represent" early on, and when a small European company that owned the name in a key category discovered Represent's growth, they demanded millions to change their name. George paid multiple millions to resolve the dispute. Trademark your brand name, your logo, and any product names or slogans at the earliest possible stage — before you have revenue, before you have proof of concept, before you have anything to lose. Defense always beats regret.

### Step 3: Build a Brand Identity Bigger Than Any Single Founder

Never want to be the sole person behind your brand — it creates key-man risk that kills companies when the founder steps away. George pushed his personal story and visibility to grow the brand, but his stated goal is for Represent to be a legacy brand that outlives him and his brother. The brand must stand on its own identity — its aesthetics, its values, its product quality — not on the fame of its founder. Build systems and culture that make the brand great regardless of who is at the helm.

### Step 4: Create Products You Genuinely Want to Wear Yourself

Build the wardrobe you wish existed. George launched the 247 activewear line because he personally could not find athleticwear that matched Represent's design DNA when he got into fitness. Launching product lines that you personally use and believe in ensures authenticity and prevents you from chasing trends that do not align with your brand's core identity. If you would not wear it, your customers will not either.

### Step 5: Build a Hero Product That Breaks the $30M Revenue Ceiling

Most apparel brands plateau around $30M in revenue. The companies that break through have a hero product — one SKU that drives disproportionate revenue and becomes synonymous with the brand. Represent's Owners Club hoodie (small logo front, large logo back) drove nearly half the business from 2022 to 2025. Identify your hero product early, invest in making it the highest-quality version of itself, and let it carry the brand while you diversify into other categories.

### Step 6: Enter Global Markets With Conviction, Not Just Ambition

Going global requires more than shipping product internationally. You must understand cultural differences in how people shop, what they value, and what aesthetics resonate. In the UK, group identity and shared branding drives adoption — everyone wearing the same brand is a badge of honor. In America, especially LA, individual uniqueness matters — being the only person in a room wearing your brand is cooler than being one of many. Adapt your marketing, product silhouettes, and retail strategy to each market rather than applying a single global template.

### Step 7: Hire Fast, Fire Faster, and Bring in Experts to Scale Infrastructure

Bring in senior operational leaders from established companies when you are hitting your scaling ceiling. George brought in a CEO from Puma at $20M ARR when the team was stretched to 15 people working nights — he was not ready to manage 50 people, but the CEO was. Do not let ego prevent you from hiring people better than you at what you need. When someone is not working out, fire them fast — you will know within an hour of meeting someone whether they fit the brand, and you will know within weeks whether they can actually do the job.

### Step 8: Use Retail Placement as a Top-of-Funnel Brand Signal

Getting your brand into premium retailers — Selfridges, Harrods, Nordstrom — is not primarily a revenue play; it is a credibility play. A placement at Harrods signals to every other buyer, every customer, and every media outlet that your brand has arrived. Wait until you are undeniable before approaching buyers — let them come to you. Build demand through content, social proof, and direct-to-consumer sales first. When buyers at fashion weeks start talking about your brand, the floodgates open.

### Step 9: Stay True to Your Pricing Even When Competitors Discount

Never race to the bottom on price. If you drop your price to match a competitor, you permanently re-anchor your brand's perceived value in the mind of your customer — and you will never be able to raise it again. George stayed disciplined on pricing while competitors discounted aggressively, building a brand associated with quality and value rather than cheapness. Hard decisions now create an easy life later; easy decisions now create a hard life later.

### Step 10: Build a Business That Outlasts You

Build systems, culture, and brand equity so strong that the business thrives without you. George wants Represent to be a legacy brand that his children and their children can run. Document your values, invest in your team's growth, and treat every decision as if you are building a 100-year company, not flipping a quick profit. The brands that survive generations are the ones built by people who thought in decades, not quarters.

## Examples

### Example 1: Growing Represent From $0 to $140M in Revenue Starting From a Bedroom

George and his brother started with no salary, no outside investment, and a PayPal account. They shipped orders themselves, grew through Facebook, got Mac Miller wearing their brand on Twitter, and slowly built a global streetwear brand. After paying a multi-million dollar trademark settlement, they emerged stronger and grew from $8M to $140M in five years.

### Example 2: Using a Hero Product to Break the $30M Revenue Ceiling

Represent's Owners Club hoodie — a simple logo hoodie and t-shirt — drove nearly half the company's revenue between 2022 and 2025. Rather than spreading product development across hundreds of SKUs, they identified one hero product that resonated with their core audience, invested in making it exceptional, and let it carry the brand through the most critical growth phase.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Pay yourself nothing for the first several years and reinvest every dollar into inventory and growth
- ✅ Trademark your brand name, logo, and product names on day one — before you have revenue to protect
- ✅ Build a brand identity that is bigger than any single founder to eliminate key-man risk
- ✅ Create products you personally want to use — authenticity is your strongest competitive advantage
- ✅ Identify a hero product early and invest disproportionately in making it exceptional
- ✅ Hire fast but fire faster — bring in senior operational experts when you hit your scaling ceiling
- ✅ Stay true to your pricing — discounting re-anchors your brand's perceived value permanently
- ✅ Use premium retail placement as a credibility signal, not just a revenue channel — let buyers come to you
- ✅ Adapt your product and marketing to each global market rather than applying a single template
- ✅ Build systems and culture that allow the business to outlive you as the founder

## Keep In Mind

- You will not feel like you are missing out by not taking a salary when you know you will eventually have everything — self-belief makes delayed gratification possible
- The biggest hidden cost in apparel is overstocking — fashion demand is fragile and unpredictable, so lean inventory management is critical
- Your brand must be bigger than you as a person — if you are the sole face of the brand, you are a single point of failure
- Retail placement is a marketing investment, not a profit center — flagship stores may lose money but build the brand awareness that drives online sales
- The 5630 method applies to brand building too: test five product ideas, make six versions of each, and let real customer response decide your direction

## Security & Safety Notes

- Trademark your brand name and logo in every market you plan to enter — delay gives competitors or opportunistic actors time to register your name first
- Do not share exact revenue figures, trademark settlement amounts, or growth timelines publicly — this data can be used by competitors to benchmark against you
- When hiring senior executives from large corporations, have clear contracts that protect your creative vision and brand control
- Secure your supply chain by building relationships with multiple factories across different geographies — over-reliance on a single manufacturer creates fragility

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Running out of cash because you paid yourself a salary too early instead of reinvesting
  **Solution:** Commit to zero personal salary for at least the first three to four years — live below your means until you can live above them
- **Problem:** Losing the rights to your brand name because you did not trademark it early enough
  **Solution:** Trademark everything — brand name, logo, product lines, slogans — before you have any revenue; it costs very little upfront and can save you millions later
- **Problem:** Hitting a $30M revenue ceiling with no clear path to the next level
  **Solution:** Identify and invest in a hero product that can carry the brand through the ceiling — one exceptional SKU often outperforms dozens of mediocre ones
- **Problem:** Discounting to compete with cheaper brands and permanently damaging your perceived value
  **Solution:** Stay disciplined on pricing — discounting is a short-term fix that creates a long-term brand problem
- **Problem:** Becoming the bottleneck because you hired slower than you needed to
  **Solution:** Hire fast for attitude and values, train for skills, and fire fast when it is not working — bring in a COO or senior operator when you are stretched beyond your capabilities
