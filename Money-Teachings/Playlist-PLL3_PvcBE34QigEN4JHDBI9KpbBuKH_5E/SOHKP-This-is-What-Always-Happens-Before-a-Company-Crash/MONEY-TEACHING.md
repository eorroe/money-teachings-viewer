# This is What Always Happens Before a Company Crash: Leadership Lessons from a Fortune 500 CEO

## Overview

This Money Teaching distills leadership principles from Jim Keyes, who became CEO of not one but two multi-billion dollar Fortune 500 companies. Keyes emphasizes that the most valuable resource of any company is its human capital, and that avoiding yes-men, maintaining humility, and putting people in positions where they can succeed are critical to long-term survival and growth.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you are leading a team or company of any size
- When you feel surrounded by people who only tell you what you want to hear
- When you need to rebuild trust and culture after a period of success
- When the user asks about preventing company crashes or toxic leadership

## Steps

### Step 1: Eliminate Yes-Men Immediately

Surround yourself with people who will tell you the truth, not what you want to hear. Yes-men will drag you down and "yes you to death." The Bible says do not be afraid. Yoda said do not be afraid. You need knowledge, but you also need the force - the courage to hear hard truths and make difficult decisions.

### Step 2: Protect Human Capital Above All Else

The most valuable resource of any company is not equipment or assets - it is the human resource. Invest in your people, listen to them, and create an environment where they can thrive. When you treat employees with respect and give them real ownership, they will take the company further than any asset ever could.

### Step 3: Lead With Humility and Courage

Do not let success compromise your values or your humility. You cannot be humble and fly a private jet, but you can be grateful and grounded. The greatest leaders acknowledge that they are not the smartest person in the room and that they need a team of people who complement their weaknesses.

### Step 4: Build a Legacy Beyond Profit

Education is freedom. Jim Keyes wrote a book called Education is Freedom to help inspire millions. Build institutions and systems that outlast you. Create organizations that do good things everywhere you go. Success is not just about the money - it is about what you leave behind for the next generation.

## Examples

### Example 1: The 7-Eleven Turnaround

When Jim Keyes became CEO of 7-Eleven, the company was doing about 12-13 billion in revenue with 90,000 stores worldwide. He focused on rebuilding the culture, listening to franchisees, and refocusing on the core mission of serving communities. Under his leadership, the company grew to 60-70 billion in revenue.

### Example 2: Going Global With Impact

Jim Keyes has been in 14 countries in the last year alone, and young people everywhere approach him saying they learned from School of Hard Knocks. He proves that doing good things and sharing knowledge creates a legacy that transcends borders and business success.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Remove yes-men from your inner circle immediately
- ✅ Treat human capital as your most valuable asset
- ✅ Lead with courage and humility
- ✅ Build institutions that outlast you
- ✅ Create systems that serve many, not just one
- ✅ Invest in education and freedom for others
- ❌ Don't let success make you arrogant
- ❌ Don't surround yourself with people who only agree with you
- ❌ Don't prioritize equipment and assets over people

## Keep In Mind

- Leadership is a game of pattern recognition, but too many mental models will make you miss opportunities
- The best way to predict the future is to create it
- Humility does not mean you cannot enjoy success - it means you stay grounded in why you achieved it

## Security & Safety Notes

- When you are the leader, you must do what is right even when it is hard
- Heavy is the head that wears the crown - leadership comes with responsibility to your people

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Surrounded by yes-men who hide problems until they become crises
  **Solution:** Hire and promote people who have the courage to challenge you
- **Problem:** Success breeds complacency and arrogance
  **Solution:** Stay grounded by remembering where you came from and who helped you get there
- **Problem:** Focusing on short-term gains over long-term culture
   **Solution:** Build systems and institutions that prioritize people over profits
