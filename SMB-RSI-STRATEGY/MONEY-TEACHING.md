# SMB Capital's Overextended Parabolic RSI Short

## Overview

This money teaching describes SMB Capital's overextended parabolic RSI short: a consistently profitable strategy the SMB desk uses for overextended parabolic setups. It teaches how to identify parabolic moves (price moves with six or more consecutive up days in which daily gains accelerate from one day to the next) that have become extended beyond normal trading ranges for large-cap US equities, use a customized RSI setup with specific settings and thresholds, confirm signals across time frames, and execute mean reversion shorts with disciplined risk management.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

  - When a stock or commodity has risen in a parabolic move (as defined in Step 1: six or more consecutive up days with accelerating daily gains) and is showing signs of accelerating gains that exceed the prior day's gain
 - When you want to filter for parabolic extension situations rather than trade normal trending stocks
 - When building a mean reversion short playbook for day-trading pullbacks
  - When you need a specific indicator-based framework (RSI, Keltner channels, opening range analysis) to avoid entering before the parabolic peak
  - When trading commodities such as silver (SLV) or gold (GLD) that can extend beyond normal trading ranges for large-cap US equities before reversing

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup Environment

Look for a stock or commodity that is overextended on daily and intermediate charts. A parabolic move is a price move with six or more consecutive up days in which daily gains accelerate from one day to the next (not just a typical equity grind higher). The preferred scenario is a parabolic move where price has extended beyond normal trading ranges for large-cap US equities. Examples include silver (SLV) breaking out of a weekly base, stocks such as MSTR or SMCI that went parabolic, or any instrument hitting RSI levels well above 70 (the standard overbought threshold for normal RSI settings). Use an environment filter (red light / yellow light / green light) to confirm you are in a regime where this setup is valid: red light means market conditions do not meet the setup requirements and no trade should be taken, yellow light means proceed only with a parabolic setup with the majority of the five confirming factors aligned, and green light means all setup requirements are met. Avoid entering before the parabolic peak - the trader risks suffering losses if entry occurs before the peak is confirmed.

### Step 2: Customize Your RSI Settings

Do not use the default RSI settings (14-period, overbought 70, oversold 30). Instead, apply the SMB desk's custom configuration:

 - Change the RSI length/period from 14-period to 20-period. A 20-period lookback on a daily chart uses 20 trading days of data and filters out normal price action of large-cap US equities.
  - Change the overbought threshold from 70 to 80. You want the RSI to show a reading above 80 only when the move is parabolic (as defined in Step 1: six or more consecutive up days with accelerating daily gains).
  - (Optional) Some SMB desk traders use even higher thresholds such as 85 or 90 for instruments with similar volatility characteristics (such as silver, gold, or comparable commodities) that can extend further than the normal trading ranges for large-cap US equities before reversing.

These custom settings ensure more discerning signals that represent parabolic (as defined in Step 1: six or more consecutive up days with accelerating daily gains) and extended conditions rather than normal price action of large-cap US equities. The default 14-period, 70/30 settings are designed for normal price action of large-cap US equities; you need settings that trigger only on parabolic moves.

### Step 3: Confirm Across At Least Three Time Frames

Do not take the trade based on a single time frame alone. You must see the RSI exceed your custom overbought threshold (RSI above 80) on daily, intermediate, and short-term charts simultaneously:

  - Daily chart (the highest time frame): confirms the instrument is overbought with RSI above 80.
- Intermediate time frame: such as a 130-minute chart or hourly chart.
  - Short time frame (such as a 30-minute chart): When the shorter time frame also shows RSI above 80 at the same time as the daily and intermediate time frames, it suggests that the parabolic move (daily gains increasing in magnitude) is accelerating rather than moving at a steady pace.

If the weekly chart also shows RSI above your custom overbought threshold (RSI above 80), that adds further confirmation. If only the lower time frame is overbought but the daily chart is not, that is a setup with fewer than all five confirming factors aligned and should be traded with half the planned position size or avoided.

### Step 4: Validate With Additional Context Checks

Before entering, gather confirming factors. Indicators alone are not complete strategies; they are tools that describe market variables. Combine the multi-timeframe RSI signal with these additional context checks: Keltner channels, historical comparison, consecutive up days, gap up, and opening range breakdown:

 - Keltner channels (Average True Range-based bands above a moving average) to visualize price extension beyond the moving average.
 - Historical comparison: look at prior parabolic (six or more consecutive up days with accelerating daily gains) moves in the same instrument over the 12 months preceding the setup, or in instruments with similar volatility characteristics (such as silver (SLV) or gold (GLD)), to see what the indicators looked like at those prior peaks.
  - Consecutive days up: at least three consecutive up days leading into the trade day adds additional confirmation.
  - Gap up into the day: a gap up from the prior close into the trading session increases your statistical edge by confirming the move is continuing to accelerate. A gap up of at least 1% from the prior close is the minimum threshold for this factor.
  - Opening range breakdown (a downward break of the first 15 minutes of the trading session): wait for the opening range to break down to confirm intraday direction. The highest-conviction entry is an opening range breakdown that occurs after a gap up from the prior close.

### Step 5: Size and Execute the Trade

This is a day trade, not a swing trade. You are capturing the downside reversal of a blowoff top (an overextended price peak followed by a decline of at least 2% within the trading session), not making a multi-week directional bet. Size the trade based on how many of the five confirming factors are present: (1) multi-timeframe RSI alignment, (2) gap up, (3) at least three consecutive up days, (4) opening range breakdown, and (5) Keltner channel extension:

 - If all five confirming factors are present (multi-timeframe RSI alignment, gap up, at least three consecutive up days, opening range breakdown, and Keltner channel extension), allocate full size (100% of the planned position).
  - If one or more of the five confirming factors are absent (e.g., RSI is present but other context factors are absent), take a reduced position (50% of planned size). You can add size if all five confirming factors are present before the setup expires at the close of the trading session.
 - Do not hold the position beyond the trading session. If the trade invalidates and price closes above the opening range high (the high of the first 15 minutes of the session) or breaks back above the 50-day and 200-day moving averages, exit immediately. These blowoff tops can produce reversals of at least 2% within the session in both directions; stubbornness can turn a winner into a loser.

### Step 6: Manage Risk and Review Performance

After each trade, review whether you entered before the parabolic peak (the point at which the accelerating gain sequence ends and price begins to reverse). A major risk is losses from entering before the parabolic peak is confirmed. Key risk management rules:

 - If you enter early and the setup does not play out, get out without hesitation.
 - Do not add size into a trade that is not working; wait for the next clean setup with all five confirming factors present.
  - Ensure you have a positive statistical edge before entering by reviewing win rate and average win versus average loss across prior qualifying setups (setups where all five confirming factors are present). 1–2 fakeouts (1–2 per quarter) are normal; the goal is to have a positive statistical edge across all qualifying setups per quarter.

## Examples

### Example 1: Silver (SLV) Week of December 22 Short Trade

SMB Capital's desk executed a silver short via SLV in the week of Christmas (Monday the 22nd). Silver had broken out of a weekly base during Thanksgiving week and continued rising every single day leading into Christmas week. By that point, SLV was trading above 60. On the Friday before the trade, silver closed at the highs after a gap up - the candle immediately preceding the final candle on the daily chart showed clear extension. At that point, the custom RSI (20-period, 80 threshold) was showing RSI above 80 on the daily chart, the 130-minute chart, and the 30-minute chart simultaneously. The weekly chart was also showing overbought readings (RSI above 80). The desk recognized this as a setup with all five confirming factors present. They shorted the opening range breakdown on Monday after silver futures gapped up overnight, and silver declined throughout the session (a decline of at least 2% from the opening range high). Traders were executing from laptops in living rooms and over Discord, with the team providing input even while members were traveling. The trade was profitable because all time frames aligned and the acceleration was confirmed across every chart.

### Example 2: Gold (GLD) Mean Reversion Short

SMB Capital also traded a similar setup in gold (GLD). The pattern was nearly identical to the silver trade: the daily RSI was showing RSI above 80, intermediate time frames confirmed, and the move was parabolic. The trade was a day trade that reversed sharply (a decline of at least 2% within the session). Gold later consolidated and made a new all-time high two months afterward. This illustrates that the strategy is a short-term day trade based on technical dynamics, not a long-term directional call. The desk does not need to know where silver or gold will be in six months; they only need to capture the rapid reversal when the move has become extended beyond normal trading ranges for large-cap US equities.

### Example 3: MSTR and SMCI Parabolic Shorts

SMB Capital has cataloged trades within this same setup, including MSTR and SMCI. In each case, the pattern was consistent: the stock went parabolic, the default 14-period RSI at 70 would have fired before the parabolic peak and produced false signals, but the custom 20-period RSI at 80 only triggered when all three time frames (daily, 130-minute, 30-minute) aligned at the peak. With SMCI, the opening range breakdown offered a clean entry after a final gap up, and options (zero days to expiration, 0DTE) were used to capture the move with size. These trades are rare (1–2 per quarter), but they contributed to quarterly P&L.

## Best Practices

 - ✅ Change RSI period from the 14-period to the 20-period and the overbought threshold from 70 to 80 to filter for parabolic (six or more consecutive up days with accelerating daily gains) conditions.
 - ✅ Require multi-timeframe confirmation: daily + intermediate (130-min or hourly) + short-term (30-min) all three time frames must show RSI above 80 as part of all five confirming factors being present.
- ✅ Combine RSI with price action, Keltner channels, and historical regime comparison.
 - ✅ Treat this as a day trade - capture the rapid reversal, do not try to swing it for weeks.
  - ✅ Size based on how many of the five confirming factors are present; allocate full size (100% of planned position) when all five confirming factors (multi-timeframe RSI alignment, gap up, at least three consecutive up days, opening range breakdown, and Keltner channel extension) are present, or take a reduced position (50% of planned size) if one or more factors are absent.
 - ✅ Use indicators as checks to keep emotion and FOMO (fear of missing out) in check.
 - ✅ If an early entry attempt fails and price reclaims above the opening range high (the high of the first 15 minutes of the trading session), exit immediately without stubbornness.
 - ✅ Review each trade to evaluate whether you entered before the parabolic peak and risk allocation.

## Keep In Mind

  - This setup is rare. You can get 1–2 qualifying setups per quarter. If you are taking one of these trades every week, you are using the wrong settings or forcing the trade.
  - The default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) are designed for normal trading ranges of large-cap US equities. They will fire before the parabolic peak for parabolic, extreme moves.
  - Commodities with similar volatility characteristics (silver, gold) can extend beyond normal trading ranges for large-cap US equities before snapping back, but the reversal is still a decline of at least 2% within the trading session.
 - Stocks with no fundamental basis for being overvalued (e.g., a zero-revenue, high-sentiment stock) are also valid for this setup - the extended move can be driven by a short squeeze (a rapid price increase caused by short sellers covering their positions) rather than fresh buying.
 - Indicators are not strategies. They are mathematical formulas that generate numerical values. They provide data points and context, but they do not replace price action analysis, chart reading, or tape reading (order flow analysis).
  - A discretionary trader's intuition can complement the indicator framework, but indicators give you checks that prevent early entries and help you follow a process.

## Security & Safety Notes

 - This is a day-trading strategy. Do not hold these positions overnight as a swing trade unless you have a separate fundamental thesis (a reasoned view about the instrument's intrinsic value or supply-demand outlook independent of the technical setup) for the position independent of the technical setup. The SMB desk treats these as single-session trades.
 - Size appropriately. These are high-volatility, low-frequency setups. Taking a position of more than 1–2% of your trading capital on a single trade can devastate capital if you are wrong.
 - Always use a defined stop. If price closes above the opening range high or breaks back above the 50-day and 200-day moving averages, exit the position immediately. These blowoff tops (overextended price peaks followed by rapid declines) can produce reversals of at least 2% within the session in both directions.
 - Do not trade this strategy in illiquid instruments. A reversal of at least 2% within the session requires the ability to enter and exit a position sized appropriately for the instrument's liquidity at market.

## Common Pitfalls

 - **Problem:** Using default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) and entering as soon as RSI hits 70.
  **Solution:** Change to 20-period and 80 overbought threshold. The default settings generate false signals and cause you to enter before the parabolic peak, incurring avoidable losses.
- **Problem:** Trading based on a single time frame (e.g., daily RSI only) without confirming on shorter time frames.
  **Solution:** Always require the RSI signal on at least three time frames: daily, intermediate (130-min or hourly), and short-term (30-min). Alignment across time frames suggests acceleration.
  - **Problem:** Entering before the parabolic peak because of FOMO while the move is still rising steadily without acceleration (daily gains that are not increasing in magnitude).
   **Solution:** Wait for the acceleration and the multi-timeframe confirmation. If the parabolic peak is obvious to all market participants, the peak has already occurred. The highest-conviction entries happen when the move has become parabolic, not when it is just beginning to extend.
 - **Problem:** Holding the short after the setup invalidates, hoping the market agrees with you.
   **Solution:** Cut losers immediately if price closes above the opening range high or breaks back above the 50-day and 200-day moving averages. Do not let ego turn a day trade into a swing trade. "Blowoff tops" in this context refers to the overextended parabolic setups described in Step 1. As the transcript states: "these types of blowoffs are what you would stay in if you tried to just hold it and be stubborn."
 - **Problem:** Taking the trade too frequently, treating every overbought reading as a setup with all five confirming factors present.
     **Solution:** These setups are rare (1–2 per quarter). If you are shorting every RSI overbought signal, you are not following the process. Wait for the full context: acceleration, multi-timeframe alignment, gap up, and at least three consecutive up days.
 - **Problem:** Believing an indicator alone will solve all trading problems or, conversely, dismissing all indicators as unreliable lagging indicators.
   **Solution:** Indicators are tools, not strategies. Use them to quantify context and provide checks. Combine with price action, tape reading (order flow analysis), and an overall environment filter. Relying on indicators alone or dismissing all indicators is equally incorrect.
