# SMB Capital's Most Profitable Setup: The Overextended Parabolic RSI Short

## Overview

This money teaching describes SMB Capital's overextended parabolic RSI short: a setup the desk has identified as its most historically profitable strategy. It teaches how to identify parabolic moves that have become extended beyond typical equity ranges, use a customized RSI setup with specific settings and thresholds, confirm signals across time frames, and execute mean reversion shorts with disciplined risk management.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

 - When a stock or commodity has risen in a parabolic move (a sequence of accelerating gains, not just a typical equity grind higher) and is showing signs of parabolic acceleration
 - When you want to filter for parabolic extension situations rather than trade normal trending stocks
 - When building a mean reversion short playbook for day-trading pullbacks
 - When you need an indicator-based framework to avoid entering before the parabolic peak
 - When trading commodities such as silver (SLV) or gold (GLD) that can extend beyond typical equity ranges before snapping back

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup Environment

Look for a stock or commodity that is overextended on daily and intermediate charts. The preferred scenario is a parabolic move where price has extended beyond typical ranges. Examples include silver (SLV) breaking out of a weekly base, stocks such as MSTR or SMCI that went parabolic, or any instrument hitting RSI levels well above standard equity thresholds. Use an environment filter (red light / yellow light / green light) to confirm you are in a regime where this setup is valid: red light means market conditions do not meet the setup requirements and no trade should be taken, yellow light means proceed only with a parabolic setup with most confirming factors aligned, and green light means all setup requirements are met. Avoid entering before the parabolic peak - the trader risks suffering losses if entry occurs before the peak is confirmed.

### Step 2: Customize Your RSI Settings

Do not use the default RSI settings (14-period, overbought 70, oversold 30). Instead, apply the SMB desk's custom configuration:

 - Change the RSI length/period from 14-period to 20-period. A 20-period lookback on a daily chart uses 20 trading days of data and filters out typical equity price action.
 - Change the overbought threshold from 70 to 80. You want the RSI to show a reading above 80 only when the move is extreme and parabolic.
 - (Optional) Some SMB desk traders use even higher thresholds such as 85 or 90 for instruments such as silver, gold, or comparable commodities that can extend further than typical equities before snapping back.

These custom settings ensure more discerning signals that represent parabolic and extended conditions rather than typical equity behavior. The default 14-period, 70/30 settings are designed for typical equity trading conditions; you need settings that trigger only on parabolic moves.

### Step 3: Confirm Across Multiple Time Frames

Do not take the trade based on a single time frame alone. You must see the RSI exceed your custom threshold (e.g., above 80) on daily, intermediate, and short-term charts simultaneously:

 - Daily chart: confirms the instrument is overbought with RSI above 80 on the highest time frame.
- Intermediate time frame: such as a 130-minute chart or hourly chart.
 - Short time frame: such as a 30-minute chart. When the shorter time frame also shows RSI above your threshold at the same time as the higher time frames, it suggests that the parabolic move is accelerating rather than moving at a steady pace.

If the weekly chart also shows RSI above your custom threshold, that adds further confirmation. If only the lower time frame is overbought but the daily chart is not, that is a lower-probability setup and should be traded with half the planned position size or avoided.

### Step 4: Validate With Additional Context Checks

Before entering, gather confirming factors. Indicators alone are not complete strategies; they are tools that describe market variables. Combine the multi-timeframe RSI signal with:

- Keltner channels (Average True Range-based bands above a moving average) to visualize extension.
- Historical comparison: look at prior parabolic moves in the same instrument over the prior 12 months, or in comparable instruments such as silver (SLV) or gold (GLD), to see what the indicators looked like at those tops.
 - Consecutive days up: at least three consecutive up days leading into the trade day adds additional confirmation.
 - Gap up into the day: a gap up from the prior close into the trading session increases your statistical edge by confirming the move is continuing to accelerate.
 - Opening range break: wait for the opening range (first 15 minutes of the trading session) to break down to confirm intraday direction. A strong entry is an opening range breakdown after a gap up.

### Step 5: Size and Execute the Trade

This is a day trade, not a swing trade. You are capturing the downside reversal of a blowoff top, not making a multi-week directional bet. Size the trade based on how many confirming factors are present:

- If all five confirming factors align (multi-timeframe RSI alignment, gap up, three consecutive up days, opening range break, and Keltner channel extension), allocate full size (100% of the planned position).
 - If at least one confirming factor is missing (e.g., RSI is present but other context factors are absent), take a reduced position (50% of planned size). You can add size if all five confirming factors later align before the setup expires at the end of the trading session.
- Do not hold the position beyond the trading session. If the trade invalidates and price closes above the opening range high or breaks back above the 50-day and 200-day moving averages, exit immediately. These blowoff tops are sharp in both directions; stubbornness can turn a winner into a loser.

### Step 6: Manage Risk and Review Performance

After each trade, review whether you entered before the parabolic peak. A major risk is losses from entering before the parabolic peak is confirmed. Key risk management rules:

- If you enter early and the setup does not play out, get out without hesitation.
- Do not add size into a trade that is not working; wait for the next clean, highest-quality setup (all five confirming factors aligned).
 - Ensure you have a positive statistical edge before entering by reviewing win rate and average win versus average loss across prior setups. One or two fakeouts (one or two per quarter) are normal; the goal is to have a positive statistical edge across all qualifying setups per quarter.

## Examples

### Example 1: Silver (SLV) Week of December 22 Short Trade

SMB Capital's desk executed a silver short via SLV in the week of Christmas (Monday the 22nd). Silver had broken out of a weekly base during Thanksgiving week and continued rising every single day leading into Christmas week. By that point, SLV was trading above 60. On the Friday before the trade, silver closed at the highs after a gap up - the candle immediately preceding the final candle on the daily chart showed clear extension. At that point, the custom RSI (20-period, 80 threshold) was showing RSI above 80 on the daily chart, the 130-minute chart, and the 30-minute chart simultaneously. The weekly chart was also showing overbought readings (RSI above 80). The desk recognized this as a highest-quality overextended short setup. They shorted the opening range breakdown on Monday after silver futures gapped up overnight, and silver declined throughout the session. Traders were executing from laptops in living rooms and over Discord, with the team providing input even while members were traveling. The trade was profitable because all time frames aligned and the acceleration was confirmed across every chart.

### Example 2: Gold (GLD) Mean Reversion Short

SMB Capital also traded a similar setup in gold (GLD). The pattern was nearly identical to the silver trade: the daily RSI was showing RSI above 80, intermediate time frames confirmed, and the move was parabolic. The trade was a day trade that reversed sharply. Gold later consolidated and made a new all-time high two months afterward. This illustrates that the strategy is a short-term day trade based on technical dynamics, not a long-term directional call. The desk does not need to know where silver or gold will be in six months; they only need to capture the sharp snapback when the move has become extended beyond typical equity ranges.

### Example 3: MSTR and SMCI Parabolic Shorts

SMB Capital has cataloged trades within this same setup, including MSTR and SMCI. In each case, the pattern was consistent: the stock went parabolic, the default 14-period RSI at 70 would have fired before the parabolic peak and produced false signals, but the custom 20-period RSI at 80 only triggered when all three time frames (daily, 130-minute, 30-minute) aligned at the peak. With SMCI, the opening range breakdown offered a clean entry after a final gap up, and options (zero days to expiration, abbreviated zero DTE, meaning days to expiration) were used to capture the move with size. These trades are rare (one or two per quarter), but they contributed to quarterly P&L (profit and loss).

## Best Practices

 - ✅ Change RSI period from the 14-period to the 20-period and thresholds from 70/30 to 80/20 to filter for extreme and unsustainable conditions.
- ✅ Require multi-timeframe confirmation: daily + intermediate (130-min or hourly) + short-term (30-min) all three time frames must show RSI above 80.
- ✅ Combine RSI with price action, Keltner channels, and historical regime comparison.
- ✅ Treat this as a day trade - capture the snapback, do not try to swing it for weeks.
 - ✅ Size based on how many confirming factors are present; allocate full size (100% of planned position) when all five confirming factors (multi-timeframe RSI alignment, gap up, three consecutive up days, opening range break, and Keltner channel extension) align, or take a reduced position (50% of planned size) if context factors are absent.
 - ✅ Use indicators as checks to keep emotion and FOMO (fear of missing out) in check.
 - ✅ If an early entry attempt fails and price reclaims above the opening range high, exit immediately without stubbornness.
 - ✅ Review each trade to evaluate whether you entered before the parabolic peak and risk allocation.

## Keep In Mind

 - This setup is rare. You can get one or two high-quality opportunities per quarter. If you are taking one of these trades every week, you are using the wrong settings or forcing the trade.
 - The default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) are designed for typical equities. They will fire before the parabolic peak for parabolic, extreme moves.
 - Commodities (silver, gold, natural gas) can extend beyond typical equity ranges before snapping back, but the snapback is still sharp.
 - Stocks with no fundamental basis for being overvalued (e.g., a zero-revenue, high-sentiment stock) are also valid for this setup - the extended move can be driven by a short squeeze rather than fresh buying.
 - Indicators are not strategies. They are mathematical formulas that generate numerical values. They provide data points and context, but they do not replace price action analysis, chart reading, or tape reading (order flow analysis).
 - A discretionary trader's intuition can complement the indicator framework, but indicators give you checks that prevent early entries and help you follow a process.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is a day-trading strategy. Do not hold these positions overnight as a swing trade unless you have a separate fundamental thesis for the position independent of the technical setup. The SMB desk treats these as single-session trades.
- Size appropriately. These are high-volatility, low-frequency setups. Taking a position that is too large relative to your capital on a single trade can devastate capital if you are wrong.
- Always use a defined stop. If price closes above the opening range high or breaks back above the 50-day and 200-day moving averages, exit the position immediately. These blowoff tops can be sharp and fast in both directions.
- Do not trade this strategy in illiquid instruments. The sharp snapback requires the ability to enter and exit with size at market.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) and entering as soon as RSI hits 70.
  **Solution:** Change to 20-period and 80/20 thresholds. The default settings generate false signals and cause you to enter before the parabolic peak, suffering losses.
- **Problem:** Trading based on a single time frame (e.g., daily RSI only) without confirming on shorter time frames.
  **Solution:** Always require the RSI signal on at least three time frames: daily, intermediate (130-min or hourly), and short-term (30-min). Alignment across time frames suggests acceleration.
 - **Problem:** Entering before the parabolic peak because of FOMO while the move is still moving steadily without acceleration.
  **Solution:** Wait for the acceleration and the multi-timeframe confirmation. If the parabolic peak is obvious to all market participants, the peak has already occurred. Strong entries happen when the move has become extreme, not when it is just beginning to extend.
- **Problem:** Holding the short after the setup invalidates, hoping the market agrees with you.
  **Solution:** Cut losers immediately if price closes above the opening range high or breaks back above the 50-day and 200-day moving averages. Do not let ego turn a day trade into a swing trade. As the transcript states: "these types of blowoffs are what you would stay in if you tried to just hold it and be stubborn."
- **Problem:** Taking the trade too frequently, treating every overbought reading as a highest-quality setup.
  **Solution:** These trades are rare. If you are shorting every RSI overbought signal, you are not following the process. Wait for the full context: acceleration, multi-timeframe alignment, gap up, and consecutive days up.
- **Problem:** Believing an indicator alone will solve all trading problems or, conversely, dismissing all indicators as lagging trash.
  **Solution:** Indicators are tools, not strategies. Use them to quantify context and provide checks. Combine with price action, tape reading (order flow analysis), and an overall environment filter. Neither relying on indicators alone nor dismissing all indicators is correct.
