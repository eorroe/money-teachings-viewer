# SMB - The #1 Most Profitable RSI Strategy Used by Our Traders

## Overview

This money teaching describes SMB Capital's #1 most profitable RSI strategy: the overextended parabolic short. It teaches how to identify outlier situations where a stock or commodity becomes unsustainably extended, use a customized RSI setup with specific settings and thresholds, confirm signals across multiple time frames, and execute mean reversion shorts with disciplined risk management. The strategy has generated millions for the SMB desk by focusing on rare, high-conviction setups rather than frequent low-quality signals.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When a stock or commodity has run up dramatically and is showing signs of parabolic acceleration
- When you want to filter for outlier overextension scenarios rather than trade normal trending stocks
- When building a mean reversion short playbook for day-trading pullbacks
- When you need an objective indicator-based framework to avoid chopping up the front side of a trade
- When trading commodities like silver (SLV) or gold (GLD) that can stretch further than typical equities before snapping back

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup Environment

Look for a stock or commodity that is overextended on multiple time frames. The ideal scenario is an outlier situation where price has moved extraordinarily and unusually - not just a normal grind higher, but a parabolic move with accelerating velocity. Examples include silver (SLV) breaking out of a weekly base, memestocks like MSTR or SMCI going parabolic, or any instrument hitting RSI levels well above normal. Context is everything: use an environment filter (yellow light / green light) to confirm you are in a regime where this setup is valid. Avoid attempting this on the front side of a move - it is very easy to get run over and chopped up if you enter too early.

### Step 2: Customize Your RSI Settings

Do not use the default RSI settings (14-period, overbought 70, oversold 30). Instead, apply the SMB desk's custom configuration:

- Change the RSI length/period from 14 to 20. A 20-period lookback on a daily chart equals approximately one month of trading days and makes it harder for the oscillator to hit extreme values, reducing noise.
- Change the overbought threshold from 70 to 80. You want the RSI to light up only when the move is genuinely extreme and unsustainable.
- (Optional) Some desk traders use even higher thresholds such as 85 or 90 depending on the instrument and historical regime comparison.

These custom settings ensure you receive fewer, more discerning signals that represent true outlier conditions rather than normal stock behavior. The default 70/30 settings are designed for normal children; you need settings that describe a devious child getting kicked out of kindergarten.

### Step 3: Confirm Across Multiple Time Frames

Do not take the trade based on a single time frame alone. You must see the RSI exceed your custom threshold (e.g., above 80) on multiple time frames simultaneously:

- Daily chart: confirms the instrument is deeply overbought on the highest time frame.
- Intermediate time frame: such as a 130-minute chart or hourly chart (65-minute if you want one bar per session). This measures the acceleration of the move.
- Short time frame: such as a 30-minute chart. When the shorter time frame also lights up at the same time as the higher time frames, it confirms that the parabolic move is accelerating and not just grinding linearly.

If the weekly chart also lights up, that is an even stronger signal. If only the lower time frame is overbought but the daily is not, that is a different, lower-conviction setup.

### Step 4: Validate With Additional Context Checks

Before entering, gather multiple checks in favor. Indicators are not strategies; they are tools that describe variables. Combine the multi-timeframe RSI signal with:

- Keltner channels (ATR-based bands above a moving average) to visualize extension.
- Historical comparison: look at prior parabolic moves in the same instrument or related instruments (silver, gold, commodities) to see what the indicators looked like at those tops.
- Consecutive days up: three or more consecutive up days into the trade day adds conviction.
- Gap up into the day: a gap up from the prior close into the session you plan to trade raises expected value significantly.
- Opening range break: wait for the opening range to break to confirm intraday direction. Often the best entry is an opening range breakdown after the gap up.

### Step 5: Size and Execute the Trade

This is a day trade, not a swing trade. You are capturing the backside of a blowoff top, not making a multi-week directional bet. Size the trade based on how many checks in favor are present:

- If all variables align (RSI all time frames, gap up, three days up, opening range break), allocate full size.
- If some variables are missing (e.g., RSI is there but other context is off), take a smaller position knowing you can add size if the setup improves.
- Do not overstay. If the trade invalidates and price reclaims above the opening range or breaks back above key levels, exit immediately. These blowoff tops are violent on the way down, but stubbornness can turn a winner into a loser.

### Step 6: Manage Risk and Review Performance

After each trade, review your patience on the front side. The biggest risk is getting chopped up by trying to front-run the top. Key risk management rules:

- If you take an early stab and the setup does not play out, get out without hesitation.
- Do not add size into a trade that is not working; wait for the next clean A+ setup.
- Calculate expected value in your head before entering. A few fakeouts are normal; the goal is to have positive expectancy over a small number of high-quality trades per quarter.

## Examples

SMB Capital's desk executed a massive silver short via SLV over Christmas week. Silver had broken out of a huge weekly base during Thanksgiving week and continued grinding higher every single day leading into Christmas. On the Friday before the trade, silver closed at the highs after a massive gap up - the second-to-last candle on the daily chart showed extreme extension. At that point, the custom RSI (20-period, 80 threshold) lit up in pink on the daily chart, the 130-minute chart, and the 30-minute chart simultaneously. The weekly chart was also flashing overbought. The desk recognized this as an A+ overextended short setup. They shorted the opening range breakdown on Monday after the futures gap up overnight, and silver flushed hard throughout the session. Traders were executing from laptops in living rooms and over Discord, with the team providing input even while members were traveling. The trade was highly profitable because all time frames aligned and the acceleration was confirmed across every chart.

### Example 2: Gold (GLD) Mean Reversion Short

SMB Capital also traded a similar setup in gold (GLD) at another point in time. The pattern looked nearly identical to the silver trade: the daily RSI lit up, intermediate time frames confirmed, and the move was parabolic. The trade was a day trade that eventually came off sharply, though gold later consolidated and made a new all-time high a couple of months afterward. This illustrates that the strategy is a short-term day trade based on technical dynamics, not a long-term fundamental call. The desk does not need to know where silver or gold will be in six months; they only need to capture the violent snapback when the rubber band is stretched too far.

SMB Capital has cataloged multiple large trades within this same setup, including MSTR and SMCI. In each case, the pattern was identical: the stock went parabolic, the default 14-period RSI at 70 would have fired much too early and produced noise, but the custom 20-period RSI at 80 only triggered when all three time frames (daily, 130-minute, 30-minute) aligned at the top. With SMCI, the trade was particularly large - the opening range breakdown offered a clean entry after a final gap up, and options (zero DTE) were used to capture the move with size. ## Best Practices

- ✅ Change RSI period from 14 to 20 and thresholds from 70/30 to 80/20 to filter for true outliers.
- ✅ Require multi-timeframe confirmation: daily + intermediate (130-min or hourly) + short-term (30-min) all must show RSI above 80.
- ✅ Combine RSI with price action, Keltner channels, and historical regime comparison.
- ✅ Treat this as a day trade - capture the snapback, do not try to swing it for weeks.
- ✅ Size based on how many checks in favor are present; reduce size if context is incomplete.
- ✅ Use indicators as objective checks to keep emotion and FOMO in check.
- ✅ If a stab at the trade fails and price reclaims, exit immediately without stubbornness.
- ✅ Review each trade to evaluate patience on the front side and risk allocation.

## Keep In Mind

- This setup is rare. You might get one or two A+ opportunities per quarter. If you are taking one of these trades every week, you are likely using the wrong settings or forcing the trade.
- The default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) are designed for normal stocks. They will fire too early and too often for parabolic, outlier moves.
- Commodities (silver, gold, natural gas) can stretch further than equities before snapping back due to macro drivers and squeeze dynamics. The rubber band can stretch more, but the snapback is still violent.
- Stocks with no fundamental basis for being elevated (e.g., a zero-revenue AI hype stock) are also valid for this setup - the unsustainable move is often driven by a short squeeze rather than fresh buying.
- Indicators are not strategies. They are math and statistics. They provide data points and context, but they do not replace price action, chart reading, or tape feel.
- A discretionary trader's intuition is valuable, but indicators give you objective checks in favor that prevent early entries and help you follow a process.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is a day-trading strategy. Never hold these positions overnight as a swing trade unless you have explicit fundamental conviction separate from the technical setup. The SMB desk treats these as single-session trades.
- Size appropriately. These are high-volatility, low-frequency setups. Over-sizing on a single trade can devastate capital if you are wrong.
- Always use a defined stop. If price reclaims above the opening range or breaks back above key moving averages, exit the position immediately. These blowoff tops can be sharp and fast in both directions.
- Do not trade this strategy in illiquid instruments. The violent snapback requires the ability to enter and exit with size at market.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) and entering as soon as RSI hits 70.
  **Solution:** Change to 20-period and 80/20 thresholds. The default settings generate noise and cause you to short too early, getting chopped up on the front side of the move.
- **Problem:** Trading based on a single time frame (e.g., daily RSI only) without confirming on shorter time frames.
  **Solution:** Always require the RSI signal on at least three time frames: daily, intermediate (130-min or hourly), and short-term (30-min). Alignment across time frames confirms acceleration.
- **Problem:** Entering too early because of FOMO while the move is still grinding linearly.
  **Solution:** Wait for the acceleration and the multi-timeframe confirmation. If it was easy to see, you are too late. The best entries happen when the rubber band is stretched to its limit, not when it starts stretching.
- **Problem:** Holding the short after the setup invalidates, hoping the market agrees with you.
  **Solution:** Cut losers immediately if price reclaims the opening range or breaks back above key levels. Do not let ego turn a day trade into a swing trade. As the transcript states: "these type of blowoffs are what you would stay in if you tried to just hold it and be stubborn."
- **Problem:** Taking the trade too frequently, treating every overbought reading as an A+ setup.
  **Solution:** These trades are rare. If you are shorting every RSI overbought signal, you are not following the process. Wait for the full context: acceleration, multi-timeframe alignment, gap up, and consecutive days up.
- **Problem:** Believing an indicator alone will solve all trading problems or, conversely, dismissing all indicators as lagging trash.
  **Solution:** Indicators are tools, not strategies. Use them to quantify context and provide checks in favor. Combine with price action, tape reading, and an overall environment filter. Neither extreme - indicator worship nor indicator dismissal - is correct.
