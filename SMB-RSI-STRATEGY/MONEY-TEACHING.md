# SMB - A Profitable RSI Strategy Used by SMB Capital Traders

## Overview

This money teaching describes SMB Capital's RSI strategy: the overextended parabolic short. It teaches how to identify extreme and unsustainable situations where a stock or commodity becomes unsustainably extended, use a customized RSI setup with specific settings and thresholds, confirm signals across time frames, and execute mean reversion shorts with disciplined risk management.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When a stock or commodity has risen sharply and is showing signs of parabolic acceleration
- When you want to filter for extreme and unsustainable extension scenarios rather than trade normal trending stocks
- When building a mean reversion short playbook for day-trading pullbacks
- When you need an objective indicator-based framework to avoid entering before the parabolic peak
- When trading commodities like silver (SLV) or gold (GLD) that can extend further than equities before snapping back

## Steps

### Step 1: Identify the Setup Environment

Look for a stock or commodity that is overextended on time frames including daily and intermediate charts. The ideal scenario is an extreme and unsustainable situation where price has moved unsustainably - not just a normal grind higher, but a parabolic move. Examples include silver (SLV) breaking out of a weekly base, memestocks like MSTR or SMCI going parabolic, or any instrument hitting RSI levels well above normal. Use an environment filter (red light / yellow light / green light) to confirm you are in a regime where this setup is valid, where red light means do not trade, yellow light means proceed with caution, and green light means conditions are favorable. Avoid entering before the parabolic peak - it is likely to suffer losses if you enter too early.

### Step 2: Customize Your RSI Settings

Do not use the default RSI settings (14-period, overbought 70, oversold 30). Instead, apply the SMB desk's custom configuration:

- Change the RSI length/period from 14 to 20. A 20-period lookback on a daily chart uses 20 trading days of data and filters out normal price action.
- Change the overbought threshold from 70 to 80. You want the RSI to show a reading above 80 only when the move is genuinely extreme and unsustainable.
- (Optional) Some traders use even higher thresholds such as 85 or 90 for instruments such as silver, gold, or other commodities that can extend further than equities.

These custom settings ensure more discerning signals that represent true extreme and unsustainable conditions rather than normal stock behavior. The default 70/30 settings are designed for ordinary trading conditions; you need settings that trigger only on genuinely extreme moves.

### Step 3: Confirm Across Multiple Time Frames

Do not take the trade based on a single time frame alone. You must see the RSI exceed your custom threshold (e.g., above 80) on time frames including daily, intermediate, and short-term charts simultaneously:

- Daily chart: confirms the instrument is deeply overbought on the highest time frame.
- Intermediate time frame: such as a 130-minute chart or hourly chart (65-minute for one bar per session).
- Short time frame: such as a 30-minute chart. When the shorter time frame also shows RSI above your threshold at the same time as the higher time frames, it confirms that the parabolic move is accelerating and not just grinding linearly.

If the weekly chart also shows RSI above your custom threshold, that is an even stronger signal. If only the lower time frame is overbought but the daily is not, that is a different, lower-conviction setup.

### Step 4: Validate With Additional Context Checks

Before entering, gather confirming factors. Indicators are not strategies; they are tools that describe variables. Combine the multi-timeframe RSI signal with:

- Keltner channels (ATR-based bands above a moving average) to visualize extension.
- Historical comparison: look at prior parabolic moves in the same instrument or related instruments (silver, gold, commodities) to see what the indicators looked like at those tops.
- Consecutive days up: at least three consecutive up days into the trade day adds conviction.
- Gap up into the day: a gap up from the prior close into the session you plan to trade raises expected value.
- Opening range break: wait for the opening range to break to confirm intraday direction. The best entry is an opening range breakdown after a gap up.

### Step 5: Size and Execute the Trade

This is a day trade, not a swing trade. You are capturing the backside of a blowoff top, not making a multi-week directional bet. Size the trade based on how many confirming factors are present:

- If all five confirming factors align (RSI across all time frames, gap up, three consecutive up days, opening range break, Keltner channel extension), allocate full size.
- If some confirming factors are missing (e.g., RSI is present but other context is incomplete), take a reduced position. You can add size if all five confirming factors later align before the setup expires.
- Do not overstay. If the trade invalidates and price reclaims above the opening range or breaks back above key moving averages, exit immediately. These blowoff tops are sharp in both directions; stubbornness can turn a winner into a loser.

### Step 6: Manage Risk and Review Performance

After each trade, review your patience before the parabolic peak. The biggest risk is suffering losses by entering before the parabolic peak. Key risk management rules:

- If you take an early stab and the setup does not play out, get out without hesitation.
- Do not add size into a trade that is not working; wait for the next clean highest-quality setup.
- Calculate a positive expected value in your head before entering. A few fakeouts are normal; the goal is to have a positive expected value across a set of high-quality setups per quarter.

## Examples

### Example 1: Silver (SLV) Christmas Week Short Trade

SMB Capital's desk executed a large silver short via SLV in the week of Christmas. Silver had broken out of a weekly base during Thanksgiving week and continued rising every single day leading into Christmas week. On the Friday before the trade, silver closed at the highs after a gap up - the second-to-last candle on the daily chart showed extreme extension. At that point, the custom RSI (20-period, 80 threshold) was showing RSI above 80 on the daily chart, the 130-minute chart, and the 30-minute chart simultaneously. The weekly chart was also showing overbought readings. The desk recognized this as a highest-quality overextended short setup. They shorted the opening range breakdown on Monday after the futures gapped up overnight, and silver declined throughout the session. Traders were executing from laptops in living rooms and over Discord, with the team providing input even while members were traveling. The trade was profitable because all time frames aligned and the acceleration was confirmed across every chart.

### Example 2: Gold (GLD) Mean Reversion Short

SMB Capital also traded a similar setup in gold (GLD). The pattern looked nearly identical to the silver trade: the daily RSI was showing RSI above 80, intermediate time frames confirmed, and the move was parabolic. The trade was a day trade that reversed sharply, though gold later consolidated and made a new all-time high approximately two months afterward. This illustrates that the strategy is a short-term day trade based on technical dynamics, not a long-term directional call. The desk does not need to know where silver or gold will be in six months; they only need to capture the sharp snapback when the move has become unsustainably extended.

### Example 3: MSTR and SMCI Parabolic Shorts

SMB Capital has cataloged many trades within this same setup, including MSTR and SMCI. In each case, the pattern was identical: the stock went parabolic, the default 14-period RSI at 70 would have fired much too early and produced noise, but the custom 20-period RSI at 80 only triggered when all three time frames (daily, 130-minute, 30-minute) aligned at the peak. With SMCI, the trade was large - the opening range breakdown offered a clean entry after a final gap up, and options (zero DTE) were used to capture the move with size. These trades were rare (one or two per quarter), but they accounted for a significant percentage of quarterly P&L.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Change RSI period from 14 to 20 and thresholds from 70/30 to 80/20 to filter for true extreme and unsustainable conditions.
- ✅ Require multi-timeframe confirmation: daily + intermediate (130-min or hourly) + short-term (30-min) all three time frames must show RSI above 80.
- ✅ Combine RSI with price action, Keltner channels, and historical regime comparison.
- ✅ Treat this as a day trade - capture the snapback, do not try to swing it for weeks.
- ✅ Size based on how many confirming factors are present; reduce size if context is incomplete.
- ✅ Use indicators as objective checks to keep emotion and FOMO in check.
- ✅ If a stab at the trade fails and price reclaims, exit immediately without stubbornness.
- ✅ Review each trade to evaluate patience before the parabolic peak and risk allocation.

## Keep In Mind

- This setup is rare. You might get one or two high-quality opportunities per quarter. If you are taking one of these trades every week, you are likely using the wrong settings or forcing the trade.
- The default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) are designed for normal stocks. They will fire too early for parabolic, extreme and unsustainable moves.
- Commodities (silver, gold, natural gas) can extend further than equities before snapping back due to macro drivers and squeeze dynamics. The move can extend further, but the snapback is still sharp.
- Stocks with no fundamental basis for being elevated (e.g., a zero-revenue AI hype stock) are also valid for this setup - the unsustainable move is typically driven by a short squeeze rather than fresh buying.
- Indicators are not strategies. They are mathematical formulas that generate numerical values. They provide data points and context, but they do not replace price action analysis, chart reading, or tape reading.
- A discretionary trader's intuition is valuable, but indicators give you objective checks that prevent early entries and help you follow a process.

## Security & Safety Notes

- This is a day-trading strategy. Do not hold these positions overnight as a swing trade unless you have a separate fundamental thesis for the position independent of the technical setup. The SMB desk treats these as single-session trades.
- Size appropriately. These are high-volatility, low-frequency setups. Over-sizing on a single trade can devastate capital if you are wrong.
- Always use a defined stop. If price reclaims above the opening range or breaks back above key moving averages, exit the position immediately. These blowoff tops can be sharp and fast in both directions.
- Do not trade this strategy in illiquid instruments. The sharp snapback requires the ability to enter and exit with size at market.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Using default RSI settings (14-period, 70/30) and entering as soon as RSI hits 70.
  **Solution:** Change to 20-period and 80/20 thresholds. The default settings generate noise and cause you to enter too early, suffering losses before the parabolic peak.
- **Problem:** Trading based on a single time frame (e.g., daily RSI only) without confirming on shorter time frames.
  **Solution:** Always require the RSI signal on at least three time frames: daily, intermediate (130-min or hourly), and short-term (30-min). Alignment across time frames confirms acceleration.
- **Problem:** Entering too early because of FOMO while the move is still grinding linearly.
  **Solution:** Wait for the acceleration and the multi-timeframe confirmation. If it was easy to see, you are too late. The best entries happen when the move has become extreme, not when it is just beginning to extend.
- **Problem:** Holding the short after the setup invalidates, hoping the market agrees with you.
  **Solution:** Cut losers immediately if price reclaims the opening range or breaks back above key moving averages. Do not let ego turn a day trade into a swing trade. As the transcript states: "these type of blowoffs are what you would stay in if you tried to just hold it and be stubborn."
- **Problem:** Taking the trade too frequently, treating every overbought reading as a highest-quality setup.
  **Solution:** These trades are rare. If you are shorting every RSI overbought signal, you are not following the process. Wait for the full context: acceleration, multi-timeframe alignment, gap up, and consecutive days up.
- **Problem:** Believing an indicator alone will solve all trading problems or, conversely, dismissing all indicators as lagging trash.
  **Solution:** Indicators are tools, not strategies. Use them to quantify context and provide objective checks. Combine with price action, tape reading, and an overall environment filter. Neither extreme - relying on indicators alone nor dismissing all indicators - is correct.
