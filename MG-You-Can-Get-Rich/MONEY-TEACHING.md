# Regardless Of Your Income You Can Get Rich – The I Can Can System

## Overview

This money teaching outlines Myron Golden's "I Can" can system, a structured six-account money management framework that allocates every dollar of income toward a specific purpose. The system helps you build wealth regardless of income level by ensuring disciplined, systematic money management. It works because more money amplifies existing money habits — managing small amounts well prepares you for managing larger amounts.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you want to systematically manage your money and build wealth regardless of income
- When you are struggling to save or feel broke despite making money
- When you need a practical allocation system for every dollar you earn

## Steps

### Step 1: Open Six Separate Bank Accounts

Open one dedicated bank account for each of the six "I Can" cans: Tithe, Pay My Bills, Finish Free, Have Fun, Save For What I Want, and Educate Myself. Label each account clearly so you can manage them like physical cans.

### Step 2: Allocate Your Income Using the 50/10/10/10/10/10 Split

When income hits your primary account, immediately transfer it across the six accounts using this percentage allocation:

- 10% to I Can Tithe Can
- 50% to I Can Pay My Bills Can
- 10% to I Can Finish Free Can (Legacy)
- 10% to I Can Have Fun Can
- 10% to I Can Save For What I Want Can
- 10% to I Can Educate Myself Can

### Step 3: Start With What You Can Manage

If you cannot live off 50% of your income, start with smaller percentages in each can — even 1% each — and work toward the ideal 50/10/10/10/10/10 split over time. If even 1% is too much, put a fixed dollar amount such as $10 into each can. Cut unnecessary expenses such as cable television, dining out, and non-essential subscriptions to close the gap.

### Step 4: Spend Your Fun Money Each Month

Withdraw and spend every dollar in the I Can Have Fun Can every month. This can inspires you to earn more and prevents you from stealing from your future to pay for your present.

### Step 5: Automate Transfers and Be Detail-Oriented

Set up automatic transfers so the money moves into each can account as soon as income hits your primary account. If your bank does not support automatic transfers, perform the transfers manually on the same day each pay period. Track the ledger from each account meticulously. Being detail-oriented with your money is what creates financial strength and opens opportunities.

## Examples

### Example 1: Starting on a Low Income

You bring home $3,000 per month but cannot live off 50% ($1,500). You cut cable and dining out and determine you can live off 80%. You keep the 10% tithe, reduce each remaining can to 1% ($30 each), and direct the rest to bills. Over time, as your income grows or your fixed costs shrink, you increase the percentages back toward the ideal split.

### Example 2: Using the Fun Can to Build Motivation

You put $200 per month into the I Can Have Fun Can. Instead of wasting it, you spend it on something you genuinely enjoy — such as a golf trip or a hobby — because you are required to spend it all. This reinforces positive money habits and gives you a taste of what is possible when you manage your money well.

### Example 3: Using Debt Strategically for Large Purchases

You want a new car. You save in the I Can Save For What I Want Can for your down payment, then take a car loan if the bank offers favorable terms quickly. If the bank is slow, you may pay cash to avoid frustration. Either way, you fund the purchase from the correct can rather than borrowing against your future.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Open a separate bank account for each of the six cans
- ✅ Automate transfers immediately after income hits your primary account, or perform manual transfers on payday if automation is unavailable
- ✅ Cut unnecessary expenses (cable, restaurants, subscriptions) before declaring you cannot live off 50%
- ✅ Spend every dollar in the I Can Have Fun Can each month
- ✅ Use debt strategically when it makes more financial sense than using your own cash
- ✅ Be detail-oriented and track the ledger from each account meticulously
- ❌ Do not leave money sitting in the I Can Have Fun Can at the end of the month
- ❌ Do not rob from future cans to fund current spending
- ❌ Do not assume making more money will fix poor money management habits

## Keep In Mind

- More money amplifies your existing money habits — whether they are good or bad
- Managing small amounts well prepares you to manage larger amounts later
- Financial discipline in the present creates opportunities that only financially strong people can recognize
- You can start at any income level; the system is designed to scale from where you are now
- Even if you can only afford $10 per can at first, the discipline matters more than the amount

## Security & Safety Notes

- Only use banks or financial institutions you trust for your can accounts
- Do not share online banking credentials or account access with anyone
- Regularly review your account statements for unauthorized transactions
- Keep records of all transfers between cans to maintain accurate ledgers

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** You cannot live off 50% of your income and abandon the system.
  **Solution:** Start with smaller percentages in each can and gradually reduce expenses until you can reach the 50% target.
- **Problem:** You let money accumulate in the I Can Have Fun Can.
  **Solution:** Set a calendar reminder to spend the full balance each month on something that truly brings you joy.
- **Problem:** You mix can funds or spend from the wrong account.
  **Solution:** Automate transfers immediately after payday and review each ledger separately every month.
- **Problem:** You believe a higher income will solve your money problems.
  **Solution:** The system works at any income level; more money without better management habits will only amplify your problems.
