# SMB - The Trading Floor: SMB Capital Podcast (Episode 2)

## Overview

This money teaching covers how traders at SMB Capital's trading desk identify, analyze, and execute day-one catalyst breakout trades using the example of QURE on September 24, 2024. The teaching focuses on finding high-conviction setups through pre-market volume analysis, catalyst valuation, and precise intraday entry/exit execution.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When a biotech or high-catalyst stock gaps up at least 188% pre-market
- When pre-market volume exceeds 5x average daily volume (A vol)
- When looking for intraday setups in in-play stocks where the potential reward is 5 to 10 times the defined risk per share (e.g., risking $1–2 per share for a potential 10-point move to the $50 target, producing a 5:1 to 10:1 risk-reward ratio)
- When you need to evaluate whether a catalyst justifies the current valuation by estimating potential revenue (e.g., patient pool x treatment price x market share scenarios) and comparing to comparable company valuations

## Steps

### Step 1: Scan for High-Volume Pre-Market Activity

  Use a percent of average daily volume (A vol, or average daily volume) pre-market scanner to identify stocks trading at least 5x their average daily volume in pre-market. For QURE, average daily volume was 1.3 million and it did over 7 million in pre-market.   High relative volume of at least 5x average daily volume signals strong institutional interest and ensures liquidity for your trade.

### Step 2: Verify the Catalyst Significance

  Confirm there is a differentiated catalyst driving the move by checking if it addresses an unmet medical need with no existing competition or represents a clinically meaningful improvement in treating the underlying disease rather than only alleviating symptoms. For QURE, the AMT-130 gene therapy showed a 75% slowing of Huntington's disease progression when no existing drugs addressed the underlying disease. Check if the catalyst is first-in-class or just incremental/priced in.

### Step 3: Build a Preliminary Valuation Model

Estimate potential revenue to gauge if the current price is reasonable. For QURE:
- Identify the patient pool (100,000 eligible for the therapy)
- Estimate treatment price ($2-3 million per patient, referencing their approved 2022 therapy priced at $3.5 million)
- Calculate market share scenarios (10% = 10,000 patients = $25 billion revenue at $2.5M per treatment)
- Compare to comparable biotechs trading at 2-4x peak sales
- SMB estimated QURE's market cap at $3–4 billion post-gap, while the conservative revenue scenario (10% market share at $2.5M per treatment) suggested $25 billion in annual revenue potential, indicating the stock was undervalued based on SMB's revenue model

### Step 4: Find Historical Analog Trades

Look for comparable historical setups to add conviction. For QURE, the team compared it to ABBVX, which gapped up over 500% in mid-July after being added to a medical conference. Like QURE, ABBVX held its gap day one, consolidated, then trended for 4–5 days afterward. Historical analogs help you understand how a similar catalyst might play out, though outcomes can differ based on specific catalyst details and market conditions.

### Step 5: Wait for the Opening Range and Setup

Do not chase the initial gap at the open. Instead, let the stock establish a 10-30 minute opening range and watch for:
- The stock to hold above VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price)
- A failed breakdown below VWAP (Volume-Weighted Average Price) that immediately reclaims within 1–2 minutes
- Price to tighten (range contracts) as buyers and sellers compete
- Formation of a higher low after the initial volatility

For QURE, traders placed small starter-size orders at support and offered into resistance to test liquidity and gauge the tape before the real setup emerged.

### Step 6: Identify the Breakout Entry

  The primary entry comes when the stock breaks its established range with strength. Key signals:
- Price breaks above the pre-market high or intraday consolidation high on increased volume
- Volume on the breakout bar exceeds the average volume per minute in the consolidation range
- Price holds above the breakout level for one 1–2 minute bar rather than rejecting within one minute
- For QURE at 11:30, the stock tightened after a higher low formation above VWAP, then broke the trend line and the day's high

### Step 7: Use TWAP or Scale Into the Position

Use a TWAP (Time-Weighted Average Price) program to accumulate shares as long as price stays above the pullback low. For QURE, traders set the TWAP to buy every 30 seconds as long as price stayed above the pullback low from the most recent higher low. This smooths entry price and builds size without chasing.

### Step 8: Set a Tight Stop Below the Range

  Place your stop $1–2 per share below the breakout level or the range low. For QURE, the risk was $1–2 per share below the breakout point. This defined risk of $1–2 per share allows for a potential 10-point move to the $50 target, creating a risk-reward ratio of 5:1 to 10:1.

### Step 9: Trail Exits as Momentum Peaks

  Take partial profits into strength (e.g., sell 30-50% of position when price reaches target), then trail the remainder using trend lines, moving averages, or price action signals. When volume drops below 50% of the average volume per minute in the consolidation range and the stock struggles to push higher for 5-10 minutes, tighten the stop and exit. For QURE, traders sold some at the $50 highs and trailed the rest until the bounce weakened.

### Step 10: Evaluate Overnight Hold Carefully

  Before holding a position overnight, assess:
  - Offering risk (secondary offerings can reduce the price by 10–30%, as happened with QURE)
  - Whether you understand what the catalyst means for future revenue and market impact
  - If the ticker is within your usual trading focus (e.g., if you typically trade large-cap biotechs, avoid swinging low-float small caps)
For QURE, an offering was announced after the market close on September 24th, validating the decision to take the intraday profit rather than swing overnight.

## Examples

### Example 1: QURE Day-One Catalyst Breakout

On September 24, 2024, QURE gapped up 188% to open at $40 after AMT-130 gene therapy data showed 75% slowing of Huntington's disease. Pre-market volume was 7 million shares, 5x the 1.3 million average. SMB traders built a valuation model estimating 10% market share at $2.5M per treatment equating to $25B in revenue. Comparable biotechs traded at 2-4x peak sales, suggesting QURE's $3-4B market cap was undervalued based on SMB's revenue model. The stock held above VWAP, formed a higher low, and broke the range at 11:30. Traders used TWAP to accumulate near the $40 opening price, set stops $1–2 below the breakout point, and targeted $50. The stock ran to $50 before volume dropped and traders exited.

### Example 2: ABBVX Historical Analog

ABBVX gapped up over 500% in mid-July 2024 after being added to a medical conference. Like QURE, it held its gap day one, consolidated, then trended for 4–5 days afterward. This analog gave SMB traders additional conviction that QURE could follow a similar path, reinforcing the watch and the long bias.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Use pre-market volume scanners to surface stocks with relative volume of at least 5x the average daily volume
- ✅ Build a valuation model using comparable companies and market share estimates (e.g., patient pool x treatment price x market share)
- ✅ Find historical analogs to understand how comparable setups with similar catalysts played out
- ✅ Let the stock establish an opening range (the first 10–30 minutes) before entering
- ✅ Use small starter sizes (feelers) initially to test liquidity and gauge the tape
- ✅ Use TWAP programs to accumulate without chasing, with a defined price floor (e.g., above the pullback low)
- ✅ Place stops $1–2 below the consolidation or breakout level
- ✅ Take partial profits (30-50% of position) into strength and trail the remainder
- ✅ Assess offering risk and catalyst significance before swinging overnight

## Keep In Mind

- Traders are not sell-side analysts; they do not need to be exactly right about long-term business fundamentals, just need to gauge what other market participants will do in their intraday or multi-day timeframe
- Higher volume (e.g., 5x average daily volume) correlates with higher volatility and more readable price action because more participants are actively trading
- A catalyst does not need to be fully understood, but it should be graded on how differentiated the catalyst is (e.g., first-in-class vs. incremental improvement) and its potential impact on future revenue (e.g., estimated market share scenarios)
- Even without knowing the complete fundamental picture, good risk-reward setups (e.g., at least 5:1) can be traded successfully
- When experiencing a period of inconsistent results (e.g., at least three consecutive losing trades), slow down and let trades come to you rather than forcing them

## Security & Safety Notes

- Always verify the catalyst is real and public (check SEC filings, company press releases, or clinical trial registries) before trading
- Be aware of offering risk (secondary offerings can reduce the price by 10–30%) when holding biotech or small-cap names overnight
- Confirm the stock has adequate liquidity for your position size (e.g., can exit a full position within two minutes at market without moving the price by more than 2%)
- Use stops to limit downside on any single trade to a predefined dollar amount per share
- Do not rely solely on social media sentiment or single sources for catalyst validation; cross-reference with primary sources

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Chasing the initial gap (buying within the first 5-10 minutes of the open at any price) without waiting for a setup
  **Solution:** Let the stock establish a range (the first 10–30 minutes) and show direction before entering
- **Problem:** Buying without understanding the catalyst significance
  **Solution:** Build a revenue estimate (patient pool x treatment price x market share) and check comparable valuations
- **Problem:** Taking too large a position too early (e.g., full position size on the first feeler)
  **Solution:** Use small starter sizes (feelers) and add on confirmation (breakout with volume)
- **Problem:** Holding a low-float, high-catalyst name overnight without checking for offering risk
  **Solution:** Evaluate whether the ticker is within your usual trading focus and if secondary offerings are likely before swinging
- **Problem:** Letting a profitable position turn into an unprofitable position by not trailing stops
  **Solution:** Take partial profits (30-50%) into strength and tighten stops when volume drops below 50% of the average per-minute volume in the consolidation range
