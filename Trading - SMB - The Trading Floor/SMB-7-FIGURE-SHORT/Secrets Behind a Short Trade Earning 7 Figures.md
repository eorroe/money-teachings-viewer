# Secrets Behind a Short Trade Earning 7 Figures ($1,000,000+ Monthly Profit)

## Overview

This money teaching distills the SMB Capital playbook exercise and the specific overextended short trade on gold (GLD) from October 21st. It teaches how to build a systematic trading playbook by deconstructing high-conviction trades, identify steep, accelerating overextensions using technical and fundamental confluences, and execute short entries with disciplined risk management. The core lesson is that daily playbook work enables you to recognize and act on high-conviction trades in real-time without hesitation.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When you need to build a repeatable trading playbook from actual trades
- When looking for overextended short setups after steep, accelerating rallies
- When you want to refine trade entries, exits, and position sizing
- When scanning for similar trade setups using volume, Average True Range (ATR), and consecutive higher closes
- When reviewing your own trades to identify mistakes and missed opportunities

## Steps

### Step 1: Build Your Daily Playbook Exercise

At the end of each trading day, deconstruct one trade that had a clear positive outcome. This can be a trade you made, a trade you spotted on a trading desk, or a trade with a setup you understood. Write down the setup, the catalyst, the technical levels, and why the setup was valid. Collect these playbooks into a database. Over time, this database becomes your trading business foundation because it trains you to recognize high-conviction setups in real-time without second-guessing.

### Step 2: Identify Parabolic Overextension

Look for stocks or commodities that have made a steep, accelerating move. Gold in this trade rallied 27% in a month and a half across three projected price moves from a four-month wedge consolidation. Signs of overextension include: price gapping above the 5 Simple Moving Average (SMA), highest volume day of the move, and consecutive gap-ups with expanding range. When the price gaps above key moving averages and the volume accelerates at the top, a correction is coming.

### Step 3: Confirm Macro and Fundamental Context

Do not trade technicals in isolation. For this gold trade, four macro factors aligned: President Trump's remarks that a full-scale tariff war with China is unsustainable increased market certainty; the S&P 500 and QQQ were approaching all-time highs from below indicating returning risk appetite; the CBOE Volatility Index (VIX) was declining after tariff announcements indicating reduced market fear; and the DXY (dollar index) was snapping back, which can pressure gold prices. We compare the US dollar index (DXY) with GLD; a snapback in DXY signaled a potential price top. Gold gapped up after the Friday sell-off on October 17th, triggering stop-loss orders and resetting the setup for new short entries. The gap-down on October 21st provided the new short entry.

### Step 4: Mark Key Technical Levels

Identify the critical price levels before the market opens. For this gold short: 390 was the October 21st pre-market support level, 385 was the prior day low (first touch in five days after acceleration), and 373 was daily-chart support level. Also note the psychological $4,000 level per ounce. A break below these levels with volume confirms the short thesis. The daily chart was overbought, and the intraday price action was negative, giving a two-timeframe alignment to the short side.

### Step 5: Scan for Similar Setups

Build a stock scanner to find new stock candidates. Use criteria like: price closed higher than the previous day for consecutive days; increasing volume over the last four days; and ATR on the prior day at least 1.5 times the ATR of the day before. This scanner should be toggleable between pre-market and regular hours. Running this scan identifies stocks exhibiting expanding daily range and rising volume on consecutive up days as the gold trade did.

### Step 6: Execute Two-Part Entry

Enter the position in two parts for more disciplined risk management and to observe the flow of buy and sell orders while in the trade. The first entry is on the initial rejection or breakdown (such as the Volume-Weighted Average Price (VWAP) rejection on the first candle that fails to hold above VWAP). The second, larger entry is on confirmation that the price is holding below the key level (such as a two-bar break below pre-market low). This gives you a position with a tight stop but also lets you average in as conviction builds. Use 15% of your daily stop loss as position size for entries on a confirmed breakdown below the pre-market low.

### Step 7: Set Stop and Profit Targets

Place your initial stop above the October 21st high of the day, or above VWAP if VWAP was your entry trigger. For profit targets, use a combination of projected price moves and trailing stops. Take one-third of the position at the first projected price-move target. Trail the rest with the 90-minute moving average (MA) or the 9-period exponential moving average (EMA) on the 2-minute chart. Watch the intraday order flow data for the highest-volume candle during that session as your primary capitulation exit signal. If volume contracts on the downside, buy orders at current prices decrease, and fewer sell orders are placed, exit the remaining position immediately.

### Step 8: Review the Trade Objectively

After the trade, conduct a thorough review. Identify what you did well (early spotting, entries, tape reading) and what went wrong (exits at 384 and 381 before the clear capitulation at 377). For this gold trade, the team scored 84.5 out of 100, rounded to 85. Mistakes included exiting at 384 and 381 before the clear capitulation at 377. Acknowledge errors objectively and update your playbook for next time.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Do the playbook exercise every single day to build your database of high-conviction trades
- ✅ Use two timeframes: daily charts to spot overextensions, intraday charts to time entries
- ✅ Enter in two entries of unequal size - a smaller initial entry plus a larger confirmation entry
- ✅ Trail stops with the 90-minute moving average or the 9-period exponential moving average (EMA) on the 2-minute chart to capture more of the move
- ✅ Look for capitulation volume (highest volume candle) as your primary exit signal
- ✅ Review every trade objectively and update your playbook with lessons learned
- ✅ Use a scanner to systematically find stocks matching the overextension pattern
- ❌ Do not try to forecast where a stock will go - focus on what market participants are doing
- ❌ Do not exit early prematurely - let a portion run until the order flow data shows declining volume and weakening momentum
- ❌ Do not ignore the difference between short-position closing and genuine buying interest

## Keep In Mind

- Gold is a challenging market to trade intraday due to global hedging activity that creates noisy price action unrelated to gold supply-and-demand fundamentals. However, when global macro events dominate market attention, gold can produce high-conviction setups. Only trade it when you see high-conviction trades.
- You do not need to know where price is going. Focus on prior positioning, who is buying and selling, and what is likely to happen next based on that positioning.
- A $400 gold stock needs more than a 4-point move to make the trade worthwhile. Calculate your profit potential relative to risk before entering.
- The best trades on the desk come when daily charts show an opportunity and intraday charts confirm it in the same direction.

## Security & Safety Notes

- Use a stop loss on every trade. Avoid entering a position without a predefined exit level.
- Size each position as a percentage of your daily stop loss, using 15% of your daily stop loss as position size for entries triggered during the opening drive by a confirmed breakdown below the pre-market low, so a single loss has contained impact on your capital.
- Gold trades 24/7 across Asian, London, and New York sessions. Hedging activity from institutional portfolio rebalancing can cause sudden volatile moves that are not related to gold supply or demand.

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Exiting too early and missing the majority of the move
  **Solution:** Take one-third at the first projected price-move target (equal to the height of the prior consolidation range projected from the breakdown level) and trail the remainder with the 90-minute moving average or the 9-period exponential moving average on the 2-minute chart. Only exit fully when the order flow data shows the highest single volume bar of the day.
- **Problem:** Second-guessing entries in real-time because the work was not done beforehand
  **Solution:** Complete the playbook exercise daily so that when you spot a high-conviction trade, you execute without doubt. Preparation eliminates hesitation.
- **Problem:** Confusing short-position closing with genuine buying interest after the October 20th gap-up
  **Solution:** Compare the volume on the October 20th gap-up day to the unwind day. If the October 20th gap-up has lower volume, it is short-position closing, not genuine buying interest, and the price will reverse down again.
- **Problem:** Taking profits prematurely after a quick flush
  **Solution:** Have a systematic exit plan before entering. Decide in advance how much you will take at the first target and how much you will trail. Let the trade play out according to the plan.
- **Problem:** Not using projected price-move targets to size profit expectations
  **Solution:** Study your best trades after the fact and calculate the projected price-move target (equal to the height of the prior consolidation range projected from the breakdown level) for each trade. Record the projected price-move target alongside your actual exit so you learn to recognize the pattern in future setups.
