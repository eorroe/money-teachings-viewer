# SMB-MARKET-CRASHES

## Overview

This teaching covers how to survive and profit from market crashes and volatility events. It focuses on trading strategies during news-driven sell-offs and risk management principles. The content is based on SMB Capital's trading desk debrief of the "Armageddon Friday" market crash — their nickname for the October 2025 event — and how their trading desk positioned themselves to profit while managing risk.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When the market experiences a breaking news event causing a drop of at least 3%
- When trading at all-time highs and facing policy announcements or executive actions
- When you need to manage risk during headline-driven market conditions with sharp price swings
- When you want to capitalize on crypto liquidation events (trading sessions where leveraged positions are automatically closed due to margin calls)
- When adjusting your trading strategy
- When dealing with fear of missing out (FOMO) and comparison bias (comparing your performance to other traders) in trading

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare for Breaking News Events

Monitor news feeds and social media platforms such as X for catalysts (events that move markets). Pay attention to headlines, policy announcements, and influential tweets. Build a watchlist of potential movers including indices (Qs), VIX (CBOE Volatility Index), and stocks in sectors such as quantum, robotics, rare earth, batteries, drones, nuclear energy, AI data centers, and crypto mining that could be impacted by news.

### Step 2: Execute Immediate Risk Management

When a breaking news event hits, action is risk management. Review your current positions and determine what needs to be reduced or eliminated. If the news involves policy announcements or executive actions, consider shorting before evaluating your long portfolio — this short-selling recommendation assumes your account has margin and short-selling permissions. Abandon any pre-market plans as they become irrelevant during news events.

### Step 3: Identify Market Direction Plays

Interpret the news to determine market direction. Look for volume spikes, distance from all-time highs, and the time of day. On a Friday with policy announcements or executive actions and the market near all-time highs, the risk of a downside move of at least 3% is elevated as traders avoid holding long positions over the weekend.

### Step 4: Use Appropriate Instruments

For market-wide moves, trade the indices rather than individual stocks — this assumes access to index instruments such as Qs or VIX. Use Qs (NASDAQ-100 ETF) or VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) to express your market view. If you want to trade individual stocks, only do so after establishing your core market position. This prevents the mistake of picking a stock that underperforms the broader market during a sell-off.

### Step 5: Manage Positions Through Trend Days

A trend day is a session where the market moves consistently in one direction with counter-trend bounces that fail to reclaim the trend level. During a trend day, hold a position as long as the market closes below the intraday high (on a down trend day) without reclaiming that level — a failure to reverse the trend direction. A key principle attributed to Linda Raschke: never short a weak market on a Friday. As the day progresses and bounces fail to reclaim the prior high, the likelihood increases that the trend will continue into the close because no one wants to hold over the weekend. Consider using weekly options for trend days rather than 0 DTE (zero days to expiration) options if you expect the move to continue past the session close.

### Step 6: Maintain Psychological Discipline

Avoid comparing your performance to other traders. Morgan Housel describes the hardest financial skill as getting the goalpost to stop moving. Focus on your own defined trading process, follow your own rules, and let price action confirm your trades. In market environments where quantum, robotics, rare earth, batteries, drones, nuclear energy, AI data center, and crypto mining stocks are making strong moves, it is easy to get overwhelmed. Keep your focus narrow, use price alerts, and avoid chasing trades based on fear of missing out (FOMO).

## Examples

### Example 1: The Trump China Tariff Announcement

On a Friday morning, Trump announced 100% additional tariffs on China and export controls on critical software. The market immediately sold off, with Qs moving lower. The SMB desk exited long positions, then shorted the market. Since it was Friday and the market was near all-time highs, they bought 0 DTE (zero days to expiration) puts and puts expiring the following week. The market closed near lows and continued selling into the close. Over the weekend, Trump walked back the comments, and the market reversed on Monday.

### Example 2: The Musk-Trump Feud / Tesla Sell-off

When Trump and Musk engaged in a public battle on X, Tesla stock sold off. Traders who monitored X posts in the days leading up to the event observed Musk's increasingly critical messages toward Trump, which signaled the relationship was deteriorating. Once Trump joined the public exchange, the market reacted to the confirmed breakdown of the relationship.

### Example 3: The Crypto Liquidation Event

The crypto market experienced the largest single-session liquidation event recorded at that time, per the Trading Floor podcast recording of the October 2025 event, erasing $19 billion in minutes. This was roughly 10 times the size of the 2020 COVID crash liquidation event or the FTX collapse liquidation event. The cause was over-leverage at all-time highs. According to Unusual Whales, a trader opened a levered Bitcoin short 30 minutes before Trump's tariff announcement and made $192 million in two hours. This demonstrates both the danger of leverage and the opportunity for prepared traders.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always have stops in place on every trade
- ✅ Use market instruments (Qs, VIX (CBOE Volatility Index)) for market-wide plays before picking individual stocks
- ✅ Never short a weak market on a Friday
- ✅ Let price action and the tape tell you what the market wants to do
- ✅ Keep your focus narrow and concentrate on your priority watchlist
- ✅ Use price alerts to manage multiple moving stocks
- ✅ Compare yourself to yourself, not to other traders' profit and loss (P&L)
- ✅ Shorten your time frame when volatility increases and the market becomes headline-driven
- ✅ Have a plan but be ready to abandon it when breaking news hits
- ✅ Anticipate moves by tracking developing news stories before they peak

- ❌ Don't trade without stops
- ❌ Don't over-leverage, especially at all-time highs
- ❌ Don't pick individual stocks to express a market view without trading the index first
- ❌ Don't let FOMO drive you into trades after stocks have already moved
- ❌ Don't assume retail is always the "dumb money"
- ❌ Don't let comparison bias (comparing your performance to other traders) override your process and rules

## Keep In Mind

- Morgan Housel writes in The Psychology of Money that one of the hardest financial disciplines is recognizing when your goals are based on moving benchmarks rather than fixed ones
- Traders have strategies and playbooks that perform differently in different environments
- Market character changes over time and your trading must adapt accordingly
- Psychology can override even the best risk systems when the mind starts overriding them
- Markets at all-time highs are vulnerable to sharp reversals
- During severe market sell-offs, correlations tend to rise as assets move together, making indices the best instruments to trade
- A 3% drop can feel extreme — the SMB Capital team nicknamed their October 2025 event "Armageddon Friday" because of the dramatic intraday movement, high leverage, and extreme sentiment — when leverage is elevated (crypto traders were levered up at all-time highs, per the podcast) and sentiment is at an extreme (quantum stocks described by podcast hosts as unable to go down)

## Security & Safety Notes

- Your top priority is to preserve your trading capital so that you can continue trading tomorrow
- SMB Capital team leader K Fitz says, "Stay alive and stay in the game above all else"
- Risk management is more important than any single trade or opportunity
- Never let greed or envy of others' profits drive your trading decisions
- The goal is consistency and longevity, not large gains on every trade
- If you find yourself comparing your profit and loss (P&L) to others, you are already off track

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trading over-leveraged without stops during market crashes
  **Solution:** Always maintain stops and avoid excessive leverage. The October 2025 crypto liquidation event — which erased $19 billion in leveraged positions within minutes — was caused by over-leverage at all-time highs.

- **Problem:** Picking individual stocks instead of market instruments for market-wide moves
  **Solution:** Trade Qs or VIX (CBOE Volatility Index) first to express your market view. The SMB Capital trading desk notes that they learned this lesson on a prior sell-off day when AMD underperformed the Qs, illustrating that even a strong stock can lag the index.

- **Problem:** Comparing yourself to other traders and letting FOMO drive decisions
  **Solution:** Measure your performance against your own defined trading plan and rules, not against other traders' profit and loss (P&L). Morgan Housel writes in The Psychology of Money that one of the hardest financial disciplines is recognizing when your goals are based on moving benchmarks rather than fixed ones.

- **Problem:** Abandoning your trading plan during breaking news events
  **Solution:** Have a plan, but also build the foundation to follow it. Prepare mentally so you do not freeze during volatility.

- **Problem:** Assuming the market will keep going up because some traders described certain stocks as "unable to decline"
  **Solution:** Recognize that sentiment can flip instantly. When most traders are complacent at all-time highs — the SMB Capital podcast hosts described some market participants as viewing quantum stocks as "unable to go down" — the stage is set for a sharp reversal.
