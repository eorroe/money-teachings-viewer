# SMB-MARKET-CRASHES

## Overview

This teaching covers how to survive and profit from market crashes and extreme volatility events. It focuses on trading strategies during breaking news-driven sell-offs, risk management principles, and the psychological discipline required to navigate headline-driven market turbulence. The content is based on SMB Capital's analysis of the "Armageddon Friday" market crash and how their trading desk positioned themselves to profit while managing risk.

## When to Follow These Money Teachings

- When the market experiences a sudden breaking news event causing sharp declines
- When trading at all-time highs and facing unexpected negative catalysts
- When you need to manage risk during extreme volatility
- When you want to capitalize on trend days and market panic
- When adjusting your trading strategy for changing market conditions
- When dealing with FOMO and comparison bias in trading

## Steps

### Step 1: Prepare for Breaking News Events

Monitor news feeds and social media for catalysts that could move the market. Pay attention to escalating headlines, policy announcements, and influential tweets. Build a watchlist of potential movers including indices (Qs), VIX, and sector-specific stocks that could be impacted by news.

### Step 2: Execute Immediate Risk Management

When a breaking news event hits, your first action is risk management. Immediately review your current positions and determine what needs to be reduced or eliminated. If the news is negative, consider shorting first before evaluating your long portfolio. Abandon any pre-market plans as they become irrelevant during major news events.

### Step 3: Identify Market Direction Plays

Quickly interpret the news to determine market direction. Look for key signals: volume spikes, distance from all-time highs, and the time of day. On a Friday with negative news and the market near all-time highs, the probability of further downside increases as traders avoid holding long positions over the weekend.

### Step 4: Use Appropriate Instruments

For market-wide moves, trade the indices rather than individual stocks. Use Qs (NASDAQ-100 ETF) or VIX to express your market view. If you want to trade individual stocks, only do so after establishing your core market position. This prevents the mistake of picking a stock that underperforms the broader market during a sell-off.

### Step 5: Manage Positions Through Trend Days

During a trend day, hold your position as long as the market continues in your direction and fails to reverse. A key principle: never fade a weak market on a Friday. As the day progresses and bounces fail, confidence in the trend grows because no one wants to hold over the weekend. Consider using weekly options for trend days rather than 0 DTE options if you expect the move to continue.

### Step 6: Maintain Psychological Discipline

Avoid comparing your performance to other traders. The hardest financial skill is getting the goalpost to stop moving. Focus on your own process, follow your rules, and let price action confirm your trades. In markets where everything is doubling, it is easy to get overwhelmed. Keep your bandwidth tight, use price alerts, and avoid chasing trades based on FOMO.

## Examples

### Example 1: The Trump China Tariff Announcement

On a Friday morning, Trump announced 100% additional tariffs on China and export controls on critical software. The market immediately sold off, with Qs dropping like a rock. The SMB desk first exited long positions, then shorted the market. Since it was Friday and the market was near all-time highs, they bought 0 DTE puts and puts expiring the following week. The market closed near lows and continued selling into the close. Over the weekend, Trump walked back the comments, and the market reversed on Monday.

### Example 2: The Musk-Trump Feud / Tesla Sell-off

When Trump and Musk engaged in a public battle on X, Tesla stock sold off sharply. Traders who paid close attention in the days leading up to the event could have anticipated the move. Musk had been "mouthing off" for days, and once Trump joined in, the market realized the relationship was deteriorating. This was a breaking news setup that could be anticipated by watching the developing story.

### Example 3: The Crypto Liquidation Event

The crypto market experienced the largest liquidation event in history, erasing over $19 billion in minutes. This was 10 times larger than the 2020 COVID crash or the FTX collapse. The cause was massive over-leverage at all-time highs. A trader reportedly opened a multi-million dollar levered Bitcoin short 30 minutes before Trump's tariff announcement and made $192 million in two hours. This demonstrates both the danger of leverage and the opportunity for prepared traders.

## Best Practices

- ✅ Always have stops in place on every trade
- ✅ Use market instruments (Qs, VIX) for market-wide plays before picking individual stocks
- ✅ Never fade a weak market on a Friday
- ✅ Let price action and the tape tell you what the market wants to do
- ✅ Keep your bandwidth tight and focus on your priority watchlist
- ✅ Use price alerts to manage multiple moving stocks
- ✅ Compare yourself to yourself, not to other traders' P&L
- ✅ Shorten your time frame when volatility increases and the market becomes headline-driven
- ✅ Have a plan but be ready to abandon it when breaking news hits
- ✅ Anticipate moves by tracking developing news stories before they peak

- ❌ Don't trade without stops
- ❌ Don't over-leverage, especially at all-time highs
- ❌ Don't pick individual stocks to express a market view without trading the index first
- ❌ Don't let FOMO drive you into trades after stocks have already moved
- ❌ Don't assume retail is always the "dumb money"
- ❌ Don't let comparison bias override your process and rules

## Keep In Mind

- The hardest financial skill is getting the goalpost to stop moving
- Everyone has different strategies and playbooks that perform differently in different environments
- The market's nature changes over time and your trading must adapt accordingly
- Psychology can override even the best risk systems when the mind starts overriding them
- Markets at all-time highs with zero worries are vulnerable to sudden sharp reversals
- Correlations go to 1 during major sell-offs, making indices the best instruments to trade
- A 3% drop can feel like Armageddon when leverage is high and sentiment is extreme

## Security & Safety Notes

- Your number one job is to make sure that you have a job tomorrow
- Stay alive and stay in the game above all else
- Risk management is more important than any single trade or opportunity
- Never let greed or envy of others' profits drive your trading decisions
- The goal is consistency and longevity, not home runs on every trade
- If you find yourself comparing your P&L to others, you are already off track

## Common Pitfalls

- **Problem:** Trading over-leveraged without stops during market crashes
  **Solution:** Always maintain stops and avoid excessive leverage. The 19 billion dollar crypto liquidation was caused by over-leverage.

- **Problem:** Picking individual stocks instead of market instruments for market-wide moves
  **Solution:** Trade Qs or VIX first to express your market view. As the traders note, they learned this lesson when AMD underperformed the market on a sell-off day.

- **Problem:** Comparing yourself to other traders and letting FOMO drive decisions
  **Solution:** Keep it "you versus you." The hardest financial skill is getting the goalpost to stop moving. Focus on your own process.

- **Problem:** Abandoning your trading plan during breaking news events
  **Solution:** Have a plan, but also build the foundation to follow it. Prepare mentally so you don't freeze like a deer in headlights when volatility hits.

- **Problem:** Assuming the market will keep going up because "these stocks can't go down"
  **Solution:** Recognize that sentiment can flip instantly. When everyone is complacent at all-time highs, the stage is set for a sharp reversal.
